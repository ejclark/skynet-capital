## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Stand aside — nothing tracked is exposed to this print — but the number is more predictable than the headline suggests: Tokyo core should come back *below* September's +2.7%.** The Bureau's own September flash (read directly this session, not press) shows that more than half of September's jump in Tokyo inflation came from two policy-timing items, not broad price pressure: **water bills (+0.24pp)** — Tokyo's summer fee waiver depressed the September-2025 base and had already ended in the September-2026 reading, so it reverses in October — and **childcare fees (+0.29pp)**, a base shift that persists and may add a little more. October-2025 was the other side of that coin: water charges snapped back to full price that month (y/y −34.6% → 0.0%) and Tokyo core jumped **+0.90% m/m, the biggest September→October move in the 24 years of the Bureau's file**. So this October's y/y has to climb a hill the water item no longer helps with. A centre of about **+2.4%** (likely **+2.2% to +2.6%**) follows from three methods that disagree by a few tenths; a print at or above **+2.7%** needs October core to rise about **0.8% m/m**, which has happened once in 24 Octobers. The date is **estimate**; it widens caution and licenses nothing.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Tokyo's September flash broke high (+2.7% core vs 2.4% consensus, 1.8% prior). The sibling ledger [`japan-cpi-2026-10-23`](japan-cpi-2026-10-23.md) asked what that meant for the *national* print. This one asks the next question: **does Tokyo itself keep going in October, on the morning of the BoJ decision?** The proposal that created this entry ([`proposals/japan-cpi-tokyo-flash-2026-10-30.from-japan-cpi-2026-10-23.json`](../../../src/domain/market-events/proposals/japan-cpi-tokyo-flash-2026-10-30.from-japan-cpi-2026-10-23.json), read first) tracked it only as "the freshest datapoint a hold-or-hike conversation can cite" and named October's base as "the next thing to read". This is that read.

**One-line verdict: September's jump was largely two policy-timing items that do not repeat, and October's base is the steepest in the file, so Tokyo core most likely prints around +2.4% — below September — without that meaning anything about underlying inflation.**

### Conviction legs, tested

**Leg 1 — More than half of September's jump is policy timing, not momentum: SUPPORTED (Bureau primary).** Core went 1.8% → 2.7% (+0.9pp); core-core 2.0% → 3.0%; headline 1.9% → 2.7%. The Bureau's own "contributions to the change in y/y" list for the headline's +0.8pp: **childcare fees +0.29pp** (the line is still −100% y/y, but its drag shrank from −0.50pp to −0.21pp), **water +0.24pp** (water charges **+65.6% y/y** against a base the 2025 waiver had cut), lodging +0.12pp, household durables +0.04pp, recreational durables +0.03pp, overseas package tours +0.04pp, energy +0.01pp, and fresh food **−0.15pp**. Water plus childcare is **0.53pp of the 0.8pp**. They behave differently from here: **water reverses in October** (leg 2), while **childcare is a base shift that persists** (leg 4). Reuters names the same two ("water bill and childcare support ending") plus energy, chip-driven PC prices and wages.

**Leg 2 — October's base is the steepest on record, and its main driver does not repeat: SUPPORTED.** Tokyo core Sep→Oct 2025: **99.8 → 100.7 = +0.90% m/m**. Over 2002-2025 (n=24) that is the largest September→October move; next are 2023 (+0.73%), 2024 (+0.62%) and 2022 (+0.54%); only 2025 exceeds +0.8%. Mechanism, from the Bureau and press: Tokyo's summer water-fee waiver pulled Sept-2025 water down **34.6%** y/y (Adnkronos, 2025-09-26); in Oct-2025 it was back to **0.0%** y/y (MNI / Trading Economics, 2025-10-31). Sept-2026 water is already at full price (+65.6% y/y), so **October 2026 carries no water rebound and the water item falls out of y/y: about −0.24pp** (same weight as the Bureau's September contribution). That is a mechanical subtraction from September's +2.7%, before any new price news.

**Leg 3 — where the print lands: MIXED, three estimators, one ordering.** The map from October's core index to the published y/y, against the Oct-2025 base of **100.7** (the Bureau computes from unrounded indices, so ±0.1pp): 102.6 → 1.9% · 102.8 → 2.1% · 103.0 → 2.3% · 103.1 → 2.4% · 103.2 → 2.5% · 103.3 → 2.6% · **103.4 → 2.7%**. (a) *Arithmetic:* y/y change ≈ this October's m/m − 0.90; take October m/m of 0.35-0.8% and the print is 2.15-2.6%, centre 0.6% → **2.4%**. (b) *Seasonal mean of Sep→Oct m/m:* 2002-2025 mean +0.22% → 2.1%; 2013-2019 mean +0.26% → 2.1%; 2022-2024 mean +0.63% (the inflation regime, no water rebound in those years) → **2.5%**. (c) *Regression of October m/m on September's own y/y:* 2013-2025 (n=13) slope 0.147, corr 0.67, residual sd 0.20 → +0.60% → **2.5%**; 2002-2025 (n=24) corr 0.62 → +0.53% → 2.4%; 2018-2025 (n=8, corr 0.86, sd 0.16) → +0.75% → **2.65%**. The estimators span 2.1-2.65%; the high one is the shortest, hottest window and also contains 2025's water rebound. Centre **~2.4%**, likely 2.2-2.6%; ≥2.7% needs m/m ≳ +0.8%, ~**20%** on a blend of those fits; ≤2.1% ~**10%**. A stated base rate, not a market-implied probability.

**Leg 4 — the childcare item is the unresolved ±0.2pp: MIXED.** The Bureau shows the line at −100% y/y in both August and September 2026 but with a contribution that moved from −0.50pp to −0.21pp, which means the Sept-2025 base had already started to fall. Whether Oct-2025 fell further (a bigger drag in the base, so a further *lift* to this October's y/y of up to ~0.21pp) is not in anything fetched; the item-level October-2025 history is not in the Table 1-2 file. It widens the top of the band by up to a fifth of a point and is the reason the high end is 2.6-2.7% rather than 2.5%.

**Leg 5 — a sub-2.7% Tokyo print is not a national signal either way: SUPPORTED, small samples.** [`japan-cpi-2026-11-20`](japan-cpi-2026-11-20.md) already registered (FT-japan-cpi-2026-11-20-2) that in October the Tokyo flash has *no edge* over simply reusing the prior national print (Tokyo vs naive, mean absolute error — 2018-2025: 0.171 vs 0.175pp, a tie; 2000-2025: 0.214 vs 0.160pp, naive wins). This ledger does not re-derive it. What it adds is the direction: a dip in Tokyo's y/y that this ledger predicts from the water base should not be read through to the national October print on 2026-11-20.

**Leg 6 — what the BoJ does with it: context, not a call.** The BoJ hiked to 1.25% on 2026-09-18 (7-2). [`boj-decision-2026-10-30`](boj-decision-2026-10-30.md) is hold-modal; its 2026-10-14 row logs October-hike pricing of roughly 20-25%, and the Reuters 10-16 poll quoted in the national sibling says many officials are wary of October. The flash lands hours before the decision. A Tokyo print below 2.7% is the *expected* shape and should not move that ledger; a print at or above 2.7% would be a surprise to the base arithmetic, not necessarily to the Bank. One view this ledger leans against: Dai-ichi Life's Shinke (Reuters, 10-02) saw national core "exceed 3% in coming months"; the Bureau decomposition says that is hard to reach through water and childcare, which are both one-offs. The first test is this print.

**Leg 7 — no tradeable channel: SUPPORTED.** `symbols: []`. The 2026-10-29/30 window is the most crowded on the calendar (58 tracked events within 5 days; FOMC, PCE, GDP advance, ECI, the mega-cap prints, ECB). Any tracked-name move cannot be attributed to a Japanese CPI line.

### Honest limits

- **No consensus** exists for this print at D-14; every probability above is a historical-model base rate.
- **October core m/m is the whole uncertainty**, and a sd of ~0.2pp on it is a sd of ~0.2pp on the print. The plausible range is therefore 2.1-2.7%, wider than the 2.2-2.6% "likely" range.
- **The historical Sep→Oct file spans two consumption-tax hikes and several base revisions**; the Bureau splices rather than recomputes. Small windows (n=8, n=13) are directions, not separated results.
- **Water in 2026:** a web-search summary (undated, unfetched) says Tokyo ran a second summer waiver in 2026; the CPI nonetheless shows full-price water in September, so the waiver's CPI months moved. A new October-2026 waiver or rate change would break leg 2.
- **Release time unsourced:** the schedule page states none. The Sept-flash PDF's 08:31 JST `Last-Modified` is a proxy.
- The schedule page is ~9 months stale; the date is `estimate`.
