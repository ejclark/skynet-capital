## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **This event was put on the calendar to explain the biggest restatements in the TIC panel, and
the first thing this session did was prove it cannot explain half of them.** The proposal's premise —
that the preliminary annual survey's benchmark is what makes February and March monthly vintages the
largest revisions on record — fails a piece of arithmetic nobody had run. The February *monthly* release
publishes on Treasury's "11th business day plus 0 to 3 days" rule; the survey publishes on the **last
business day** of the same month. Computed over **every February from 2003 to 2050**, the minimum gap is
**7 calendar days**, and there is no arrangement of the calendar that closes it. **A February vintage can
never contain this survey** — which removes the panel's single largest restatement (**$745.9bn**,
2024-02) from the survey's account entirely. Treasury's own note (d) then removes most of the rest: the
survey-into-March convention is written in the **past tense** ("*Before September 2011… usually released
in March to include preliminary data from the annual survey*"), superseded for Major Foreign Holders by
note (c)'s Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct schedule since December 2011. The one feedback path Treasury still documents
in the present tense is the **quarterly gross external debt** release on the last business day of June —
proposed here as a new calendar entry. The April final report and the 2027-03-18 monthly vintage — the
only one able to settle the March half — were already proposed by a sibling sweep the same day. **The tape leg is a cleaner null than any
sibling has produced.** The session after a preliminary survey moves the 10-year by **4.96bp** (n=24,
back to 2003) against **5.11bp** for a non-February month-end session — **p=0.85**. Month-end is the
noisy thing (**5.11bp vs 4.27bp** for an ordinary session, **p=0.002**); the survey adds nothing to it.
The one subsample that looks significant (2015-2026, 6.83bp, p=0.002 vs all sessions) collapses to
**p=0.062** against the month-end control and fails 0.05 in **9 of 12** drop-one-year jackknives. **What
this print is actually good for is a number, and one is registered.** Mapping the survey's all-securities
total onto Treasury's own monthly SLT long-term panel gives a stable ratio (**1.0484, sd 0.0080**, n=6),
and June 2026 SLT long-term holdings are already published at **$39.19tn** — so the 2027-02-26 headline
should print near **$41.1tn**, against **$35.3tn** at end-June 2025. The date is now **`confirmed`**: the
page's last-business-day rule reproduces **all eight** published 2026-27 annual dates **exactly**.

## Initial research

### The question

The proposal that created this event asserted that the preliminary annual survey is *"the single largest
input to the revisions of the monthly series this calendar already tracks."* Is that true — and if it is
not, what is this release actually worth to a calendar that cannot trade it?

### One-line verdict

The attribution is **half impossible and half undocumented**, the tape effect is **month-end and not the
survey**, and what survives is a **forecastable headline** — a benchmark total near **$41.1tn** whose
value to this calendar is as a dated, checkable number rather than as a cause.

### Leg 1 — the date · **SUPPORTED**, and promoted `estimate` → `confirmed`

Treasury's 2027 table puts February's row at **18** in the *Monthly* column and **"26 - prelim."** in the
column headed *Annual: Foreign portfolio holdings of U.S. securities at end-June of previous year* — two
separate releases, eight days apart, a fact that becomes leg 2. April's row reads **"30 - final"**. The
page's note block states the rule in its own words: *"Preliminary data for the survey are released on the
last business day of February; the final report is released on the last business day of April,"* under
*"The time of release is normally at 04:00 p.m. in Washington D.C."*

The mechanical corroboration here is stronger than anything the monthly siblings could produce, and for a
structural reason: the annual rule has **no window**. The monthly rule is *"the 11th business day plus 0
to 3 days"* — a four-day band that a published date can sit anywhere inside, which is why the January
sibling's exact hit was worth a paragraph. *"Last business day"* is a point. Recomputed from scratch
against all **eight** published 2026 and 2027 annual dates:

| Published | Column | Computed last business day | Offset |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-02-27 | prelim (U.S. liabilities) | 2026-02-27 | **+0** |
| 2026-04-30 | final (U.S. liabilities) | 2026-04-30 | **+0** |
| 2026-08-31 | prelim (U.S. claims) | 2026-08-31 | **+0** |
| 2026-10-30 | final (U.S. claims) | 2026-10-30 | **+0** |
| **2027-02-26** | **prelim (U.S. liabilities) — this event** | **2027-02-26** | **+0** |
| 2027-04-30 | final (U.S. liabilities) | 2027-04-30 | **+0** |
| 2027-08-31 | prelim (U.S. claims) | 2027-08-31 | **+0** |
| 2027-10-29 | final (U.S. claims) | 2027-10-29 | **+0** |

**Eight for eight, exactly.** 2027-02-28 is a Sunday and 2027-02-27 a Saturday; 2027-10-31 is a Sunday and
10-30 a Saturday. Washington's Birthday is the third Monday of February and can fall no later than
**February 21**, so it can never collide with the last business day of the month — the annual rule needs
no holiday handling at all, which is part of why it scores perfectly where the monthly rule misses twice.

Promotion follows [`mts-october-2026-11-12`](mts-october-2026-11-12.md) and
[`tic-monthly-2027-01-19`](tic-monthly-2027-01-19.md): one named Treasury primary plus independent
mechanical corroboration. The `estimate` ground the proposal cited was this lane's **no-self-confirm**
limit — the entry was discovered by the `tic-monthly-2027-01-19` sweep, not researched. That is cleared
here by this event's own session re-fetching the page. As on every sibling, `confirmed` describes **the
published schedule and nothing else**; the same page carries the standing notice that a date *"will be
revised after offices reopen"* if Washington D.C. offices are closed.

### Leg 2 — is the survey the cause of the February/March monthly restatements? · **REFUTED for February, UNDOCUMENTED for March**

This is the claim the event was proposed under, and it is the one this session was built to test.

**The February half is impossible, and the proof is arithmetic rather than evidence.** Two Treasury rules
on the same page fix the two dates. The monthly release is *"the 11th business day plus 0 to 3 days."*
The survey is *"the last business day of February."* Take the **latest** date the monthly rule can
produce and the **earliest** the survey rule can produce, in the same February, and compute the gap for
every year from **2003 to 2050**:

```
minimum gap, latest-possible monthly release → preliminary survey   =  7 calendar days   (February 2003)
2026:  11th business day 02-17,  published monthly 02-18,  survey 02-27   →  9 days
2027:  11th business day 02-16,  published monthly 02-18,  survey 02-26   →  8 days
```

Washington's Birthday, the only federal holiday in the month, falls on the third Monday and so lands no
later than February 21 — it can push the monthly release later but can never reach the last business day.
**The February monthly vintage always publishes before the survey.** It cannot contain it. That single
fact removes the panel's largest restatement from the survey's account: the sibling ledger's
**$745.9bn** move (vintage **2024-02**, line 30, closed 2021 and 2022 totals) was published on or about
**2024-02-14**, and the 2024 preliminary survey published **2024-02-29**, fifteen days later. The sibling
already suspected as much on different grounds — it noted the figure sits across Treasury's documented
*"Series break at February 2023"* — and that suspicion is now the only remaining explanation, because the
survey has been ruled out on timing.

**The March half is not refuted; it is undocumented, which is a different and weaker finding.** Treasury's
note (d), quoted in full because the tense is the whole point:

> *"Before September 2011, revised data back to the previous June was usually released in March to
> include preliminary data from the annual survey of foreign holdings of U.S. securities at end-June of
> the previous year. The May release usually included the final results from that survey."*

The sentence before it says the Major Foreign Holders data are, *"Beginning December 2011,"* based on
monthly Form SLT data *"and revised as described in (c) above"* — that is, on the **Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct**
schedule. So Treasury describes the survey-into-March mechanism as a **superseded** convention. That does
not prove the March restatements the siblings measured (**$127.9bn** at 2025-03, **$68.5bn** at 2026-03)
are unrelated to the survey; it proves nobody is entitled to assert they are. The honest state is an open
question with a named observation that would settle it. That observation is the **2027-03-18** monthly
vintage — the first one published *after* a survey — and it is already on the board, proposed the same
day by the `tic-monthly-2027-02-18` sweep as `proposals/tic-monthly-2027-03-18.from-tic-monthly-2027-02-18.json`.
This session did not duplicate it; what this leg adds is the reason it is worth researching, which that
proposal did not have.

**What Treasury *does* still document, in the present tense, points somewhere else entirely.** From the
same note block, under *Quarterly U.S. Gross External Debt*:

> *"Revised data for the previous five quarters (back to the first quarter of the previous year) are
> normally released on the last business day of June. Revisions include the results of the Survey of
> Foreign Holdings of U.S. Securities at end-June of the previous year."*

That is the survey's one currently-documented destination, and it is a **June quarterly release** that
this calendar does not track. It is proposed as `tic-quarterly-external-debt-2027-06-30`. The
relocation is the leg's real output: the proposal asked the calendar to watch February and March; the
documentation says watch June.

**One limit, stated plainly.** This leg refutes an attribution, not a magnitude. The February and March
vintages really are the largest restatements in the sibling's 29-vintage panel — that measurement is not
in question here and this session did not reproduce it (the archived press-notice PDFs were not
re-fetched; the filename pattern the sibling used was not recorded in its ledger and five guessed paths
under `home.treasury.gov/system/files/136/` all returned 404). What changed is the *cause* on offer, and
the change is in the direction of admitting we do not know.

### Leg 3 — does the release move the tape? · **REFUTED**, and the confound is the finding

Every prior tape test in this series compared TIC release next-sessions to *all* sessions or to a
mid-month control. Neither is the right control here, for a reason specific to this event: **the
preliminary survey always publishes on the last business day of the month.** It is structurally a
month-end print, and month-end sessions are not ordinary sessions.

Next-session |Δ10Y| on Treasury's daily par curve, 5,925 sessions from **2003-01-02** to **2026-09-08**:

| Sample | n | mean \|Δ10Y\| | vs all sessions | vs non-Feb month-end control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Preliminary survey, full history 2003-2026** | **24** | **4.96bp** | t=+0.94, **p=0.35** | t=−0.19, **p=0.85** |
| Non-February month-end control | 254 | 5.11bp | t=+3.13, **p=0.002** | — |
| All sessions | 5,924 | 4.27bp | — | — |
| Preliminary survey, 2015-2026 only | 12 | 6.83bp | t=+3.15, **p=0.002** | t=+1.87, **p=0.062** |

Three readings, in order of what they cost:

1. **The full-sample answer is a clean null, and it is the cleanest null this series has produced.**
   p=0.85 against the control is not a borderline pass — it is the survey session and an arbitrary
   month-end session being the same distribution to two decimal places.
2. **Month-end is real, and it is the whole effect.** 5.11bp against 4.27bp, **p=0.002**, on n=254. Anyone
   who tested a month-end release against all sessions and found significance would have found the
   calendar, not the release. That is a reusable caution for every other last-business-day event on this
   board.
3. **The subsample that looks significant is the trap, and it is named rather than buried.** Restricting
   to 2015-2026 gives 6.83bp and p=0.002 against all sessions — a result any sibling's methodology would
   have reported. Against the month-end control it is **p=0.062**, and the drop-one-year jackknife runs
   **p=0.007 to p=0.138**, failing 0.05 in **9 of 12** drops. The per-year series explains why: the twelve
   pre-2015 observations run 0-6bp and the twelve post-2015 run 1-11bp, which is the level of yields
   changing, not the release.

The signed test adds nothing either: **+0.13bp** on the full sample, against the January sibling's
**+1.14bp** signed drift for monthly releases. Whatever that monthly drift is, this release does not
carry it.

### Leg 4 — what the print will actually say · **SUPPORTED**, and a number is registered

The survey measures foreign holdings of **all** U.S. securities at end-June. Treasury's monthly SLT panel
measures foreign holdings of **long-term** U.S. securities, monthly, and is already published through
reference month **2026-06** — the survey's own reference date. The two are the same population on
different definitions, so the ratio between them is the thing to model.

From `shlhistdat.csv` (survey totals, primary) and `slt_table1.txt` (Grand Total holdings, primary), at
every June both files cover:

| End-June | Survey, all securities | SLT, long-term | Gap | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2020 | $21.95tn | $20.70tn | $1.25tn | 1.0606 |
| 2021 | $27.19tn | $26.18tn | $1.01tn | 1.0387 |
| 2022 | $24.89tn | $23.87tn | $1.02tn | 1.0427 |
| 2023 | $26.87tn | $25.53tn | $1.34tn | 1.0526 |
| 2024 | $30.88tn | $29.57tn | $1.31tn | 1.0445 |
| 2025 | **$35.30tn** | $33.57tn | $1.73tn | 1.0514 |
| **2026** | **— (this event)** | **$39.19tn** | — | — |

The 2025 survey total is read from `shl2025r.pdf`'s own key takeaways (*"reaching $35.3 trillion at
end-June 2025"*); every other row is `shlhistdat.csv`'s "Total" line. Mean ratio **1.0484**, sd
**0.0080** — a 0.76% spread across six years with no visible trend.

```
forecast = 39.19tn × 1.0484 = $41.09tn        (gap method: 39.19 + 1.28 = $40.47tn)
```

**The composition behind it, from the same file.** June 2025 → June 2026, SLT long-term: total
**$33.57tn → $39.19tn** (+16.7%); U.S. corporate equity **$19.67tn → $24.50tn** (+24.5%); U.S. Treasuries
**$7.67tn → $7.87tn** (+2.6%). This is the same shape the last two surveys described in prose — *"higher
U.S. equity prices"* driving the total while Treasury holdings creep — so a $41tn print would be the
continuation of a mechanism the survey itself has named twice, not a new claim.

**The one bias in this method, stated because it is not small.** Every historical June SLT figure in the
table above is a **post-benchmark** vintage: the survey has already fed back into it. The June-2026
figure is **pre-benchmark**. If the benchmark systematically *raises* the monthly panel, the ratio is
fitted on inflated denominators and applied to an uninflated one, and the forecast is biased **low**. The
registered window is therefore tilted upward — **$40.3tn to $42.0tn** rather than the ±2σ interval of
$40.5tn-$41.7tn the ratio alone implies. Quantifying that bias needs an archived pre-benchmark SLT
vintage, which this session did not obtain.

### Leg 5 — the source that was sought and not found · recorded, not substituted

The most valuable single measurement for this event would be the **preliminary-to-final revision**: how
much the February headline moves by April. Treasury publishes the **final** report as
`ticdata.treasury.gov/…/Documents/shl<year>r.pdf` (both `shl2024r` and `shl2025r` fetched, HTTP 200), but
serves **no preliminary artefact** at any of the eight paths tried (`shl2025p`, `shl2025pr`, `shl2024p`,
`shl2024pr`, `shlprelim2025`, `shl2024_prelim`, `shl2024pre`, `preliminary2024` — all HTTP 404), and
neither final report contains the word *"preliminary"* anywhere in its text. Treasury's own press-release
index for the TIC topic returned **404** at the path tried.

Per the honesty rules this is **recorded rather than worked around**: the prelim-to-final revision is not
measured here, no proxy is substituted for it, and `probe-ref.blocked` stays empty because nothing
*failed* — the artefact appears not to be published at a stable path at all, which is a different
finding from a blocked fetch. The `tic-annual-survey-final-2027-04-30` entry — already proposed the same
day by the `tic-monthly-2027-02-18` sweep, so not duplicated here — is where that measurement belongs: a
future sweep with the February headline in hand can take the revision from both endpoints directly.

One revision *is* visible without it, and it sizes the question: `shl2024r.pdf` reported that foreign
investors *"own 21 percent of all U.S. securities outstanding"* at end-June 2024; `shl2025r.pdf` restates
the same year as **20.5 percent**. A half-point on a $30.9tn base is ~$150bn, on a figure a year settled.

### What plays the conditions support

None. `symbols: []`, `low` tier, 16:00 ET publication after the equity cash close, reference data eight
months old at print, no house playbook keyed to a macro release, and a measured next-session tape effect
that is indistinguishable from an ordinary month-end. The value of this document is that it makes a
**dated, checkable prediction** about a number and about two mechanisms, and that it corrects a premise
three sibling ledgers were about to inherit.

### Honest limits

- **The February refutation is arithmetic; the March finding is documentary.** The first cannot be wrong
  unless Treasury changes a published rule. The second is an argument from tense in a note block, and it
  is the weaker of the two. `tic-monthly-2027-03-18` is proposed precisely because the March question is
  open, not closed.
- **This session did not reproduce the 29-vintage press-notice panel** the sibling built. Leg 2 argues
  about the cause of measurements it takes on the sibling's authority, and says so.
- **n=6 on the ratio in leg 4** is a small basis for a forecast, and the pre-benchmark bias runs in a
  known direction that the registered window widens for rather than corrects.
- **n=24 on the tape** is small in absolute terms even though it is the entire published history of this
  release. The null is stated as a null, not as evidence of equality.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stand aside, permanently, on the trade — and treat this document as a correction to the calendar's
model of itself rather than as a view on a print.** The date is **`confirmed`** (Treasury primary, the
last-business-day rule at 8-for-8), so the schedule statement is not hedged; every other statement here
is about mechanism and measurement, and none of them is trading-adjacent in the sense the honesty rules
police. The single behavioural instruction this ledger issues to its own successors: **do not test a
last-business-day release against all sessions**, because month-end alone clears p=0.002 and will
manufacture a finding.

**Kill switches:**

1. **The date.** No preliminary survey published on **2027-02-26** — a Washington D.C. closure, a funding
   lapse, or a schedule revision. Kills leg 1 and makes FT-2 and FT-3 unscoreable.
2. **The tape null.** |Δ10Y| on **2027-03-01** above **16bp** (~3σ of the n=24 distribution, sd 3.61bp).
   That would not by itself resurrect the survey as a driver — a 16bp month-end can come from anywhere —
   but it would put the null back in play and is registered as the kill.
3. **The forecast.** A preliminary headline outside **$40.3tn-$42.0tn**. Below is a survey-to-monthly
   mapping less stable than six years suggest; above is the pre-benchmark bias being larger than the
   window's tilt allows.
4. **The February refutation.** Treasury changing either published rule — moving the monthly release off
   "11th business day plus 0 to 3 days," or the survey off "last business day of February" — such that
   the two can meet. Nothing on the 2026-27 schedule does this.
5. **The March relocation.** The **2027-03-18** monthly vintage restating a closed-year total by more
   than **$14.3bn** (the sibling panel's ordinary-vintage ceiling) *and* the **2027-02-18** vintage not
   doing so, which would be direct evidence the survey does still feed the March monthly and would move
   leg 2's March half from "undocumented" back to "supported."
