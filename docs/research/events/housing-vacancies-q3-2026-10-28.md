## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This event exists because the `new-home-sales-2026-10-27` lane's adjacency sweep proposed it, and
the proposal was specific about why: that print reads the housing market as **flow** (units sold,
units for sale), and this one reads the same market as **stock** (what share of the standing
inventory sits empty, and who owns the rest). The proposer's standing `This quarter` call is an
inventory-overhang argument, and HVS is the independent survey that either corroborates it or does
not. So there are two questions, and they have opposite answers:

**Does the occupied stock confirm the overhang the flow side is showing — and can a reader do
anything with this release on the day it prints?**

**One-line verdict:** yes to the first and emphatically no to the second — the stock side
corroborates the overhang across three series at once, while the print itself is the most
unreadable macro row on this board, colliding with the FOMC by *structure* rather than by accident
and carrying a publisher-stated margin of error larger than its own typical quarterly move.

**Statistics.** Session range = `(high − low) / open × 100`. Every p-value is a **20,000-iteration
two-sided permutation test on the difference of medians**, the same procedure the sibling ledgers
use. Because each family is tested across twelve instruments at once, every family also carries a
**Holm–Bonferroni** adjustment, and *only the adjusted number is allowed to support a claim* — the
raw p's are reported so the correction can be checked, not so a nominal hit can be quoted.

### Leg 5 — what the data actually says **SUPPORTED, in trend only**

CB26-116 (Q2 2026): rental vacancy **7.3%**, homeowner vacancy **1.2%**, homeownership **65.0%**,
median asking rent **$1,531**, median asking sales price **$343,800**. Against the full published
history:

| Series | Q2 2026 | Full-history range | Where it sits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rental vacancy | **7.3%** | 4.8–11.1 (298 quarters, 1956→) | 49.7th percentile of all history, but **no quarter since 2017Q3 (7.5%) has printed above it** |
| Homeowner vacancy | **1.2%** | 0.7–2.9 (298 quarters) | 22.5th percentile, **joint highest since 2019Q4 (1.4%)** |
| Homeownership | **65.0%** | 62.9–69.2 (262 quarters, 1964→) | 53.4th percentile, **no quarter since 2019Q3 (64.8%) has printed below it** |

Over the last eight quarters all three drift together and consistently: rental vacancy
**6.6 → 7.3 (+0.7pp)**, homeowner vacancy **0.9 → 1.2 (+0.3pp)**, homeownership
**65.6 → 65.0 (−0.6pp)**. More empty rentals, more empty owner units, fewer owners. That is the
occupied stock telling the same story the for-sale side is telling — and the regional split lines up
too: **South rental vacancy 9.5%**, the highest of the four regions and well clear of the Midwest
(6.9%), Northeast (5.9%) and West (5.3%), which is the same Sunbelt geography where the for-sale
overhang sits.

**But nothing here can be read off one print.** Two arithmetic facts, both from the release itself:

- Census states the standard error on the rental vacancy rate is **0.149pp**, giving a 90% margin of
  error of **±0.245pp** on the *level*. The median absolute quarter-over-quarter move over the last
  40 quarters is **0.20pp** — smaller than the margin of error on the level, before any allowance
  for the difference. **25 of 40 (62.5%)** quarterly moves fall inside ±0.245pp; **30 of 40
  (75.0%)** inside a naive ±0.347pp difference band. (Naive because CPS quarterly estimates share
  sample and are positively correlated, which makes the true difference band *tighter* than ±0.347pp
  — the honest direction, since it makes more moves insignificant, not fewer.)
- CB26-116's own language: **"not statistically different" 16 times** and **"virtually the same" 3
  times**, against **4** "was higher than" and **1** "was lower than". Nineteen of its twenty-four
  comparative statements are explicit non-findings, including all three headline quarter-over-quarter
  comparisons.

Two more honest caveats on the level readings. The series is **not seasonally adjusted** (CB26-116
states so), and Q3 carries a tilt: across 2006–2025 the Q2→Q3 rental-vacancy change was up in
**14 of 20** years, median **+0.25pp** — so a higher Q3 2026 print is the seasonal default. And the
series has a live scar: Q4 2025 is asterisked "based only on data from November and December 2025"
because of the funding lapse, which is also what pushed the Q3 2025 release to **2025-12-11**, the
only break in 28 quarters of schedule.

### Honest limits

- **`symbols: []` is the binding limit.** Every tape measurement here is on instruments the app does
  not hold for this event; they establish what the day looks like, not what we would do.
- **n=3 on the headline configuration.** Only two prior HVS releases have hit a decision day. Those
  two sessions illustrate the attribution trap; they cannot measure it, and this ledger never treats
  them as evidence.
- **The instrument set is a choice.** Twelve names, chosen as two broad indices, two homebuilder
  ETFs, four builders and four residential REITs — the last group specifically because rental
  vacancy is the fundamental those names trade on. A different twelve would give different nominal
  p's; the Holm discipline is what makes the conclusion robust to that choice, and the conclusion
  (null on release day, loud on decision day) does not depend on any single instrument.
- **The "clean releases are wider" thread is unresolved.** DHI/PHM read wider on the fifteen
  non-collided release days at raw p<0.05 and neither survives correction. It is registered as an
  open thread, not a finding, and a later pulse with more sessions should retest it.
- **The archive of prior press releases was not reachable.** Census overwrites
  `currenthvspress.pdf`, and no quarterly archive path was discoverable from `histtabs.html`,
  `rates.html` or `prevann.html`. So the "not statistically different" phrase count is **n=1
  edition**; the durable version of that claim is the arithmetic one (MOE vs median q/q move),

- **No 2027 successor is proposable.** `calendar-listview-2027.html` resolves (HTTP 200, 75,643
