## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **The FOMC does nothing to this auction, and that is the finding — what it does is move
the 7-Year, which is the one thing that would let us finally measure this calendar's most-cited rates
result cleanly.** The proposing sweep named "sells on FOMC day one" as this leg's distinguishing
feature; measured, it is a **null**. Across all **17** 5-Year sales since 1998 that priced on the eve
of a statement, the auction-day 5Y-minus-avg(3Y,7Y) move is **−0.59bp against −0.42bp** for the other
273 (Welch t = −0.41, **permutation p = 0.72**), and bid-to-cover is **2.50 vs 2.47** (t = 0.39). The
real consequence is **schedule displacement**. The same schedule fetch puts the block at 2Y Mon 01-25,
**5Y Tue 01-26**, 2Y FRN Wed 01-27 (statement day), **7Y Thu 01-28** — the 7-Year pushed **off** the
5-Year's D+1. That matters because the [11-24 sibling](treasury-5y-note-2026-11-24.md)'s headline —
the **+0.56bp D+1 "give-back"** it used to refute the par-curve re-anchoring story — is **confounded**:
in 2023+, **41 of 43** 5-Year auctions (95%) have the block's own 7-Year selling the very next session,
at one of the two tenors the measure subtracts. Measured where the confound is only 61% (pre-2023),
D+1 runs **−0.61bp (t = −4.69, n=151)** with the 7Y there against **−0.24bp (t = −2.52, n=96)** without
— Welch t = −2.26, **perm p = 0.039** — a **~0.37bp downward bias**, with the D+0 control showing no
such split. The bias runs **against** the sibling's conclusion, so that conclusion was **understated**.
Only **9 of 290** auctions on the whole record, and **2 of 43** in the current regime, put the 7Y at
D+2 the way 2027-01-26 does. And the measurement **survives the statement**: on 2023+ statement days
the outright 5Y sd is **9.13bp vs 6.46** ordinary, but the neighbour-subtracted measure is *quieter*
than an ordinary session (**sd 0.77 vs 0.97**) — a policy statement moves the belly near-parallel and
the subtraction cancels it. Date `estimate`, `symbols: []`, nothing here is a trade.

## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** the proposing sweep filed this event because it sells on the FOMC's first
day, and named that as its distinguishing feature. Two things follow if that is true — the auction
should price differently, and the [11-24 sibling's](treasury-5y-note-2026-11-24.md) event-time study
should be unreadable here, because its D+1 is a statement day. This session tests both, and finds the
first is false and the second is backwards.

**One-line verdict:** **FOMC-eve is a measured null on this auction; what the FOMC actually does is
displace the 7-Year off D+1, which makes 2027-01-26 one of the very few sessions on record able to
measure the "give-back" leg of this calendar's most-cited rates result without the confound that
contaminates 95% of the current regime's observations.**

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The date and terms are right, re-read from the primary, and the entry stays `estimate` —
SUPPORTED.** The schedule carries the row verbatim: `5-Year NOTE · Thursday, January 21, 2027 ·
Tuesday, January 26, 2027 · Monday, February 01, 2027`, with no `R` against the PDF's own
`R --denotes reopening` legend. That is a new issue, and the record agrees emphatically — **all 374**
nominal 5-Year auctions on the fiscaldata dataset since 1991 are new issues; this tenor has never been
reopened in the modern era. The FOMC position is independently confirmed, not carried: the Fed's own
calendar page, fetched direct this session, lists 2027's meetings as `January 26-27`, so the 1:00pm
ET bidding deadline lands on **day one**, with the statement 26 hours away. The entry stays
**`estimate`** on the two limits that apply at D−139: a tentative schedule is tentative, and this lane
may not self-confirm an event a sibling sweep discovered. The confirming primary is the **2027-01-21**
announcement, 134 days out.

**2. The size is not published for January 2027, and this doc will not assert one — SUPPORTED (as a
negative), and sharper than the sibling could state it.** `sb0590` (2026-08-05), read direct today,
says verbatim that its table "presents, in billions of dollars, the actual auction sizes for the May
to July 2026 quarter and the anticipated auction sizes for the **August to October 2026** quarter" —
so a refunding statement publishes exactly **one quarter forward**. The 5-Year column reads **70** in
all six rows. Two verbatim sentences bracket what that is worth: Treasury "anticipates maintaining
nominal coupon and FRN auction sizes for at least the next several quarters," and "continues to
evaluate potential future changes." So **$70B is an expectation** — 28 consecutive $70B prints
(2024-04-24 → 2026-08-26, zero deviations) plus flat guidance — and on that one-quarter-forward
cadence the **Jan-27 anticipated size is first published at the 2026-11-04 refunding**, with the
binding number at the 2027-01-21 announcement. Every band in this doc is computed on the $70B era and
is void if January deviates.

**3. "Sells on FOMC day one" is a NULL — REFUTED as this event's distinguishing feature, and this is
the first half of the finding.** Define the condition operationally as *the next session carries an
FOMC statement*, which for 2027-01-26 is also literally meeting day one. Seventeen 5-Year auctions
since 1998 qualify:

| Population | n | D+0 relative | t |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eve of a statement | **17** | **−0.59bp** | −1.53 |
| Every other 5Y auction | 273 | −0.42bp | −3.74 |

Welch **t = −0.41** (df 18.9), permutation **p = 0.72** on 20,000 draws. The pre-2023 half alone
(n=15) reads −0.40 vs −0.20, **p = 0.67**. Demand is equally indifferent: bid-to-cover **2.50**
(sd 0.27) on statement eves against **2.47** (sd 0.28) elsewhere, Welch **t = 0.39**; dealer takedown

**4. The 7-Year confounds the sibling's headline, and the bias runs against the sibling —
SUPPORTED, and this is the load-bearing result.** The [11-24 sibling](treasury-5y-note-2026-11-24.md)
refuted the "par-curve re-anchoring" story with a reversal: 2023+ D+1 **+0.55bp** (t = +5.1),
cumulating **+1.33bp** over D+1…D+3. The measure is `Δ5Y − avg(Δ3Y, Δ7Y)`. Where does the block's own
7-Year auction fall?

| Window | n | 7Y at D+1 | 7Y at D+2 | no 7Y within 8 sessions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pre-2023 | 247 | **151 (61%)** | 7 | 89 |
| 2023+ | 43 | **41 (95%)** | 2 | 0 |

So in the current regime the give-back is measured almost entirely on sessions carrying a *different*
auction at one of the two subtracted tenors — **n=2** is the entire out-of-confound in-regime sample.
That is not, by itself, a refutation; it is a statement that the regime's own schedule cannot separate
the two. The pre-2023 sample can, because the confound is only 61% there:

| pre-2023 D+1 relative | n | mean | t |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7Y auctions at D+1 | 151 | **−0.61bp** | −4.69 |
| 7Y does not | 96 | **−0.24bp** | −2.52 |

Welch **t = −2.26** (df 244), permutation **p = 0.039**. The **control is clean**: the same split on
**D+0**, where the 7Y cannot matter, reads −0.07 vs −0.45 with Welch **t = +1.42** — no split, and if
anything the opposite sign, so this is not a general difference between the two block layouts. The
bias is therefore about **−0.37bp per session, downward**, and it is exactly the sign a 7-Year
concession predicts (the 7Y cheapens → `avg(Δ3Y, Δ7Y)` rises → the measure falls). **Which means the
sibling under-stated its own case:** if the pre-2023 estimate transfers, the true unconfounded 2023+
give-back is nearer **+0.95bp** than +0.56. The honest caveat on that transfer is that it is out of
sample across the very regime break the sibling identified.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (estimate-dated event; no standalone play):** watch-only. The contribution is **two
corrections and one opportunity**. First correction: this event's named distinguishing feature —
selling on FOMC day one — is a **measured null** on both price (−0.59 vs −0.42bp, perm p = 0.72,
n=17) and demand (b/c 2.50 vs 2.47, t = 0.39), so the sale itself inherits the ordinary base rate and
nothing else. Second: the [11-24 sibling's](treasury-5y-note-2026-11-24.md) D+1 give-back is
**confounded by the block's own 7-Year auction in 41 of 43 in-regime observations**, and the bias is
measurable where the confound is weaker (pre-2023: −0.61 with vs −0.24 without, Welch t = −2.26,
perm p = 0.039, with a clean D+0 control at t = +1.42) — worth about **−0.37bp**, running *against*
the sibling's conclusion, which was therefore understated rather than overstated. The opportunity is
that the FOMC **displaces the 7-Year to D+2**, so **2027-01-27 is a decontaminated read on the
give-back** — a configuration present in only 9 of 290 auctions ever and 2 of 43 in-regime — and the
neighbour-subtracted measure is **quieter on a statement day than an ordinary one** (sd 0.77 vs 0.97,
against outright sd 9.13 vs 6.46), so the statement does not drown it. Confidence on that last claim
is **low**, and deliberately so: the one in-configuration, in-regime, in-size precedent
(**2026-01-27**) printed D+1 **+0.50**, at the confounded base rate rather than above it. Grade the
print itself on **dealer takedown** ($70B era p25 11.00% / median 12.35% / p75 13.24%). Size is
**unpublished for January 2027** — `sb0590` publishes one quarter forward and stops at Oct-26 — and
$70B is an expectation, not a number. Nothing here is directional and no house playbook applies.

**Kill switches (what would change this stance):**

- **The base rate failing on 2027-01-26.** If the 5Y-minus-avg(3Y,7Y) move is **greater than −1.0bp**
