## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Two ledgers now point at this print as "the last datum before the December vote". It is
closer than that, and it is set up to go the other way.** The Bank of England's own minutes headers
put the announcement day *after* the meeting-end day — the July 2026 minutes say, verbatim, *"meeting
ending on 16 September will be published on 17 September 2026."* A **17 December** announcement
therefore follows a meeting **ending 16 December** — the morning this print publishes at 07:00. That
configuration has occurred in **four of the last five Decembers**, and in the most recent one the MPC
voted **5–4 to cut** and opened its summary with *"CPI inflation has fallen since the previous
meeting, to 3.2%"* — the number ONS had published at 07:00 that same morning. So the channel is real.
**The problem is the sign.** November 2025's monthly rate was **−0.215%** (ONS D7BT, 139.8 → 139.5),
the **second-softest November in thirteen years** against a 13-year mean of **+0.11%**, and ONS named
**food** and **alcohol and tobacco** as the two largest downward monthly contributions. Both reverse.
The **2026 Budget is 28 October** against **26 November** in 2025, so the duty uprating lands in the
**November** index instead of December — CPI division 02 rose **+1.26% / +2.59% / +1.42%** m/m in the
late-October-Budget Novembers of 2018 / 2021 / 2024 and **−0.45%** in November 2025. Add food's
reversion and a little energy drift and the attributable push is **+0.11 to +0.19pp** on the annual
rate; full reversion to the November norm would be **+0.33pp**. **Expect the annual rate to RISE into
the vote — and do not read that as reacceleration.** Services (**D7NN**) fell **4.4% → 3.4%** across
2026 and core (**DKO8**) **3.2% → 2.6%**; neither carries duty, VAT or cap distortion. **None of it is
ours to trade** — `symbols: []`, and this calendar's whole tracked universe is eight US mega-caps.
Date is **estimate** on a taxonomy gap, not on the evidence; it widens caution and licenses nothing.

this index instead of the next one.

### Leg 2 — "It is 29 hours before the vote." **REFUTED. The print lands INSIDE the final meeting day.**

The BoE does not announce on the day it decides. Its minutes headers state the meeting-end date and
the publication date separately, and they are one day apart. From the July 2026 minutes (fetched):
*"At its meeting ending on **29 July 2026**, the Monetary Policy Committee voted…"*, published **30
July**; and, in the same document, the forward notice: *"meeting ending on **16 September** will be
published on **17 September 2026**."*

Apply the convention to December: an announcement on **Thursday 17 December 2026** follows a meeting
**ending Wednesday 16 December 2026**. The print publishes at **07:00 that morning**. It is not a
datum the committee saw the day before; it is a datum that arrives *during the final day of the
meeting*, ahead of the vote taken that day.

This is not a novelty of 2026. Pairing each ONS release page's `Released:` field against the matching
BoE minutes header, all fetched today:

| Year | November CPI published | MPC meeting ending | Announced | Coincident? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | **15 December 2021** | **15 December 2021** | 16 December 2021 | **yes** |
| 2022 | **14 December 2022** | **14 December 2022** | 15 December 2022 | **yes** |
| 2023 | 20 December 2023 | 13 December 2023 | 14 December 2023 | no |
| 2024 | **18 December 2024** | **18 December 2024** | 19 December 2024 | **yes** |
| 2025 | **17 December 2025** | **17 December 2025** | 18 December 2025 | **yes** |

**Four of the last five.** And the most recent instance is the one that gives this entry stakes. The
December 2025 summary reads, verbatim: *"At its meeting ending on 17 December 2025, the Monetary
Policy Committee voted by a majority of **5–4 to reduce Bank Rate by 0.25 percentage points, to
3.75%**. Four members voted to maintain Bank Rate at 4%. **CPI inflation has fallen since the previous
meeting, to 3.2%.**" The number cited in the summary's first substantive sentence is the number ONS
published at 07:00 that same morning, and the vote carried by **one member**.

That is not proof the print moved a vote — nothing short of a counterfactual could be — but it is the
tightest coupling this calendar has between a foreign statistical release and a policy decision, and
it is the reason the rest of this document bothers with the sign of the number.

### Leg 5 — "Nothing special distinguishes November 2026." **REFUTED. The Budget moved a month earlier, and the duty signature is unmistakable.**

The 2026 Budget is **28 October** (HM Treasury, per [`uk-autumn-budget-2026-10-28`](uk-autumn-budget-2026-10-28.md),
which reads the Chancellor's own letter to the Treasury Select Committee). The 2025 Budget was
**26 November**. ONS collects prices around the middle of the month, so a late-October Budget's duty
uprating lands in the **November** index and a late-November Budget's lands in **December**.

CPI division 02 (alcohol, tobacco and narcotics — series **D7BV**, fetched) prints that timing
cleanly:

| Autumn fiscal event | Nov m/m | Dec m/m |
|---|---|---|
| **27 Oct 2021 (Budget)** ✓ | **+2.59%** | −0.82% |
| **30 Oct 2024 (Budget)** ✓ | **+1.42%** | −0.20% |
| **26 Nov 2025 (Budget)** ✓ | **−0.45%** | **+1.02%** |
| 29 Oct 2018 (Budget) | +1.26% | −1.07% |
| 17 Nov 2022 (Autumn Statement) | +0.63% | −1.10% |
| 22 Nov 2023 (Autumn Statement) | −0.07% | **+1.35%** |
| no autumn Budget held (2019) | −0.17% | −1.39% |
| no autumn Budget held (2020) | −0.17% | +0.09% |

**Provenance, stated rather than blurred.** Every m/m figure is ONS D7BV, fetched today. The three
Budget dates marked **✓** are the ones corroborated from gov.uk's own topical-event timestamps, as
recorded in [`uk-autumn-budget-2026-10-28`](uk-autumn-budget-2026-10-28.md)'s source line (fetched
today); the other five rows are from the general record and were **not** verified from a primary this
session. **The claim rests on the ✓ rows alone and survives on them:** the two late-October Budgets
produced November steps of **+2.59%** and **+1.42%**; the one late-November Budget produced
**−0.45%** and pushed **+1.02%** into December. The unverified rows corroborate the split — every
late-October year is ≥ +1.26%, every late-November year ≤ +0.63% — and none contradicts it.

**The 2026 print therefore compares a duty-bearing November against a duty-free one.** Taking 2024's
**+1.42%** as the central case against the **−0.45%** base, the swing is **+1.87pp** on a 2026 weight
of **37.1650/1000** (CHZS) = **+0.070pp** on the annual rate. The 2018 analogue gives +0.064pp; the
2021 analogue gives +0.113pp. Call it **+0.06 to +0.11pp**.

**And food.** D7BU, fetched: November m/m ran +0.413 / +0.497 / +0.099 / +0.875 / −0.290 / +0.955 /
+1.150 / +0.299 / +0.440 across 2016–2024, a ten-year mean of **+0.423%**, against **−0.209%** in
2025. Food's annual rate has since collapsed from **4.2%** (Nov-2025) to **1.3%** (Jul-2026), so a
below-average November is the fair assumption rather than the mean — at **+0.20%** the swing is
+0.409pp on a weight of **109.6059/1000** (CHZR) = **+0.045pp**. At the ten-year mean it would be
+0.069pp. Call it **+0.04 to +0.07pp**.

**And a little energy.** The Ofgem cap does not step between 1 October and 31 December, but the
fixed-tariff rolling-cohort drift [`uk-cpi-2026-11-18`](uk-cpi-2026-11-18.md) documented (+0.43%/month
electricity, +0.55%/month gas through Apr–Jun 2026) is not zero, and November 2025 was near-flat
(electricity 193.9 → 194.1 = **+0.103%**; gas 148.6 → 148.6 = **0.000%**). Weighted at 20.6065 and
10.3002 per 1000 that is **+0.012pp**.

| Push | Contribution to the change in the annual rate |
|---|---|
| Alcohol and tobacco — the Budget moved from 26 Nov to 28 Oct | **+0.06 to +0.11pp** |
| Food — reversion from a −0.209% November | **+0.04 to +0.07pp** |
| Energy — fixed-tariff drift, no cap step | **+0.012pp** |
| **Attributable total** | **+0.11 to +0.19pp** |
| *Memo: full reversion of the whole index to the 13-yr November mean* | *+0.33pp* |

The attributable figure is the honest one — it counts only what has a named mechanism. The +0.33pp
memo is what the arithmetic would give if the rest of the basket also behaved like a normal
November, and it is **not** claimed: three of the last six Novembers printed negative m/m, so the
recent norm is softer than the 13-year one. **The confident part of this call is the sign, not the
size.** For the annual rate to *fall*, November 2026 would have to come in below **−0.215%** — a
level reached once in thirteen years (2014, −0.299%) — in a year carrying a duty uprating that every
comparable November has priced at **+1.26% or more** in division 02.

### Leg 6 — "So the underlying is reaccelerating." **REFUTED, and this is the attribution rule.**

It is not. The two series the MPC actually votes on have been falling all year, and neither carries
duty, VAT or cap distortion (ONS, fetched):

| | Nov 2025 | Jul 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| CPI services annual rate (**D7NN**) | 4.4% | **3.4%** |
| CPI core annual rate (**DKO8**) | 3.2% | **2.6%** |
| CPI headline (**D7G7**) | 3.2% | 2.9% |

Services is the majority's stated disinflation premise, and the electricity VAT zero-rating is a
**goods** measure: services are untouched by it, by the Ofgem cap and by alcohol and tobacco duty.
So the honest reading of a higher 16 December headline is **duty timing plus an easy base**, and the
committee — which sets the duty calendar's fiscal counterpart and publishes the look-through
convention — is the reader least likely to be fooled by it. The risk this entry names is the
*commentary*, not the MPC.

**What would change that:** services printing at or above **3.8%** on 16 December. Then the rise is
in the series that votes, and this leg is wrong.

### Leg 7 — "The VAT cut makes this print the cleaner read." **MIXED — true about the level, irrelevant to the change.**

The `uk-cpi-2026-11-18` proposer's claim is that this print is cleaner because the SI 2026/987 notch
sits in the base without an October cap step on top. On the **level** that is exactly right: the
−0.098pp entered in October and stays in the annual rate until the Order sunsets, so November's
headline is 0.098pp lower than a no-cut world's.

But this document is about the **change** from October's print to November's, and there the notch
appears on **both sides** and cancels. The VAT cut contributes **0.000pp** to the Oct→Nov move in the
annual rate. "Cleaner" is true of attribution and false of dynamics — and it is the dynamics that
arrive on the meeting day.

The mirror confirms the mechanism rather than the reassurance: December 2026's print (**2027-01-20**,
ONS release page fetched, proposed below) faces a base that *did* carry the duty rise — division 02
rose **+1.02%** m/m in December 2025 — so whatever November gains, January's release gives back.

### Honest limits

- **The 16 December meeting-end date is an inference, not a publication.** The BoE has published the
  *announcement* date and its own convention (four of five Decembers, plus the July minutes' explicit
  forward notice for September); it has not published "meeting ending 16 December 2026." Registered
  as the mechanism behind **FT-…-1** rather than asserted, and the 16–17 September pair tests the
  same convention eight days from now.
- **The duty rise is an assumption about a Budget that has not happened.** Every recent Budget has
  uprated tobacco duty and the escalator is standing policy, but the 28 October 2026 measures are not
  known today. If that Budget defers or freezes duty, Leg 5's largest single term goes to zero and
  the call weakens to food and drift alone (**≈ +0.06pp**, still positive, no longer confident).
- **The Budget date is itself `estimate`** — HM Treasury's own letter, filed `NEWS:` on the same
  taxonomy gap. The date is not in doubt; the label is.
- **October's print has not happened.** The absolute landing zone quoted anywhere below (roughly
  3.0–3.5%) chains off `uk-cpi-2026-11-18`'s **2.9–3.2%** base case, which itself assumes two prints
  that do not exist yet. **The call registered here is about the direction of the change, not a
  level** — deliberately, because the level compounds three assumptions and the change compounds one.
- **The 5–4 December 2025 cut is a precedent, not a causal claim.** The summary cites the morning's
  CPI figure; it does not say the figure changed a vote, and one meeting is n = 1.
- **No consensus to be surprised against.** No published Reuters/Bloomberg consensus for the November
  2026 UK print was found today, and no OIS pricing for the December meeting was accessible from this
  session — so the base case is this ledger's arithmetic, the same gap both siblings recorded.
- **Weights are 2026's and will be re-based for 2027.** The division weights used here (CHZR
  109.6059, CHZS 37.1650) are the current year's and are correct for this print; they are not the
  weights that will apply to the mirror print in January 2027's basket update.
- **The taxonomy gap, now at nine entries.** ONS, HM Treasury, Ofgem, MOF Japan, S&P Global,
