## Initial research

### The question

What will the October-2026 PPI show, will it be published at all, and — landing three days after CPI,
twelve days after the midterms, and 26 days before an FOMC that carries an SEP and a dot plot — how
much rate-path signal does it actually carry for our nine tracked AI-infra / mega-cap names?

### One-line verdict

The most *structurally* consequential of the four tracked PPI prints and the most fragile: it is the
last wholesale read the December FOMC sees and an input to the pre-vote PCE, but its reference month is
the exact one an appropriations lapse deleted in 2025 — so the honest posture is a guard on the corridor
and a watch on the House floor, with the number itself a stand-aside.

### Leg 2 — THE material finding: this is the one PPI slot a lapse *deletes*. SUPPORTED (BLS primary).

BLS's own "Revised news release dates following the 2025 and 2026 lapses in appropriations" page,
fetched today, is unambiguous about what the 2025 lapse did to each PPI reference month:

| Reference month | Originally scheduled | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| September 2025 | Thu 2025-10-16 | Delayed to Tue **2025-11-25** (40 days) |
| **October 2025** | **Fri 2025-11-14** | **Canceled** — data folded into the November release |
| November 2025 | Thu 2025-12-11 | Delayed to Wed **2026-01-14** |
| December 2025 | Wed 2026-01-14 | Delayed to Fri **2026-01-30** |
| January 2026 | Thu 2026-02-12 | Delayed to Fri **2026-02-27** |
| February 2026 | Thu 2026-03-12 | Delayed to Wed **2026-03-18** |

Two things follow, and they are the reason this doc is not a copy of its siblings. First,
`ppi-2026-11-13` **is** that October slot — same reference month, same mid-November Friday, one year on
— so the framing [`ppi-2026-10-15`](ppi-2026-10-15.md) correctly carries for *its* slot ("a lapse
delays this print rather than deleting it") is the wrong one here, and
[`ppi-2026-12-15`](ppi-2026-12-15.md)'s 40-day-slip precedent describes the September slot, not this
one. Second, the recovery ran **five months**: PPI's schedule was not back to its ordinary cadence
until the February-2026 reference month. A lapse is not a one-print event for this series.

The mechanism is that the reference month whose *collection window* sits inside the lapse is the one
that loses its release, and October 2026's collection window opens **10-01** — the first day of a
possible lapse. The concrete branch, if funds lapse: no October-data PPI news release; the October data
appears inside the **November-data release**, which itself slips; and the **12-09 FOMC sees no PPI at
all after 10-15**. That last consequence is dated and checkable, and it also voids **FT-26** (the
base-effect test registered on the 12-15 print).

**The honest contrast with CPI, which cuts the other way.** [`cpi-2026-11-10`](cpi-2026-11-10.md)
records that October-2025 CPI was cancelled because price collection needs in-person and phone visits
that cannot be done retroactively — the *data* was destroyed. PPI is a mail/electronic establishment
survey, and the 2025 outcome proves the difference: the October-2025 PPI data **was** collected and
**was** published, two months late, alongside November's. So for this print a lapse deletes the
*release*, not the *series*. That is a materially smaller loss than CPI's, and it should not be
conflated with it.

### Leg 3 — the role inverts again, and this time in our favour. SUPPORTED (structural, confirmed dates).

Each of the four tracked PPI prints does a different job, and copying a sibling's framing is the error
this leg exists to prevent:

| Print | Position | Job |
|---|---|---|
| [09-10](ppi-2026-09-10.md) | PPI → CPI 09-11 | **Setup** — pre-loads CPI-day positioning; the whipsaw hazard |
| [10-15](ppi-2026-10-15.md) | CPI 10-14 → PPI | **Scoring** — confirms or contradicts a reaction already spent |
| **11-13** | CPI 11-10 → **PPI** → PCE 11-25 → **FOMC 12-09** | **Input** — the last wholesale read before a live SEP meeting |
| [12-15](ppi-2026-12-15.md) | FOMC 12-09 → CPI 12-10 → PPI | **Post-decision** — lands in a 43-day policy vacuum |

This is the only one of the four with a decision genuinely downstream of it. The 12-09 FOMC carries an
SEP and a dot plot (the 10-28 meeting has neither), and the inflation reads it sees are CPI 11-10, this
print, and PCE 11-25 — CPI 12-10 arrives the *morning after* the vote. So the December meeting's
inflation picture is essentially fixed by **11-25**, and this print is one of its three inputs. That is
the strongest argument on this calendar for a PPI mattering beyond its `medium` tier.

### Leg 4 — but the pipe to PCE is narrowed 44 days before the print. SUPPORTED (carried, dated).

The transmission in Leg 3 runs partly through PCE, and that channel is being cut. From BEA's
**2026-09-30** annual update, applied retroactively, BEA stops deflating PCE portfolio-management fees
with the PPI and extrapolates quantity from a CES hours-based indicator instead (BEA *Survey of Current
Business*, "Preview of the 2026 Annual Update", June 2026; sourced size ~−13bp on a May-2026 example,
~−20bp per GS/JPM — the finding is [`ppi-2026-10-15`](ppi-2026-10-15.md)'s, carried here rather than
re-derived, and it is consistent with the −10 to −30bp the [`pce-2026-09-30`](pce-2026-09-30.md) entry
already holds). Portfolio management was the whole "hot underneath" story of the July print (**+6.5%
m/m**, driving core-ex-trade to +0.4%). By 11-13 that line has no rate-path transmission left.
**Health-care services and airfares are the PPI→PCE lines that survive** — read those, and read the
headline and core-ex-trade as wholesale-pressure measures in their own right.

### Leg 5 — the base arithmetic splits the two headline measures in opposite directions. SUPPORTED as arithmetic, MIXED as a forecast.

From Table A of the July release (BLS primary, fetched today), seasonally adjusted m/m and the
unadjusted 12-month rates:

| | 2025 Aug | Sep | Oct | 12-mo, Jul-2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Final demand | −0.2 | +0.6 | +0.1 | **4.7%** |
| Ex foods, energy, trade services | +0.3 | +0.2 | +0.6 | **4.7%** |

Those three 2025 months are exactly what rolls out of the window between the July-data print and this
October-data print. The headline base totals **+0.5%**; the core base totals **+1.1%**. The two
measures are level-tied today at 4.7% — and a **0.6pp** mechanical wedge opens between them by 11-13 if
the incoming Aug–Oct 2026 months are the same for both. Put concretely: holding the headline 12-month
at 4.7% needs Aug–Oct 2026 to total no more than **+0.5%** (~0.17%/mo); holding the core rate needs
**+1.1%** (~0.37%/mo). July's core-ex-trade ran +0.4% in a single month.

So the base-effect expectation into this print is **headline up, core flat-to-down** — a split that
will read on the tape as a contradictory print and is nothing of the kind. This is the most useful
thing in the doc for reading the release: watch the m/m, not the y/y.

**Two caveats, stated because they matter.** Table A's monthlies are seasonally adjusted while the
12-month rates are unadjusted, so this is an arithmetic approximation of *direction*, never a forecast
of a level. And the base month itself is unusual: **October 2025 is a lapse artifact** — its news
release was cancelled and its data first published on 2026-01-14 with November's, so the year-ago
comparison for this print rests on a month that never had its own release.

### Leg 6 — the trend it extrapolates from. SUPPORTED (BLS primary).

12-month final demand through 2026: Jan 3.1 · Feb 3.4 · Mar 4.3 · Apr 5.7 · May 5.9 · Jun 5.5 · Jul
**4.7**. Ex-foods/energy/trade: 3.5 · 3.5 · 3.6 · 4.4 · 5.0 · 5.0 · **4.7**. The hump is the oil shock
passing through the goods leg and unwinding — final-demand energy ran +10.5% (Mar), +7.2% (Apr), +8.2%
(May), −6.5% (Jun), −3.1% (Jul), with July's flat headline held down by gasoline **−5.7%**. The core
measure peaked at 5.0% and has given back only 0.3pp, so the disinflation is overwhelmingly an energy
artifact rather than broad cooling. Wholesale at 4.7% still sits ~130bp above CPI headline (3.4%) and
~140bp above core PCE (3.3%).

**And the energy leg is firming again, freshly.** Brent settled **$87.84** on 08-26 and trades
**$90.03** today (08-31), WTI **$84.94** — up ~2.5% in three sessions and above the ~$87–88 the sibling
ledgers carry from last week's Iran–Oman Hormuz corridor framework. That is the swing factor for a hot
headline path, and it moved *against* the disinflationary read since the siblings were written.

### Leg 7 — the reaction function, and why the midterms sit in front of it. MIXED (estimate).

This cycle's only clean PPI precedent is 08-13: a soft headline (0.0% vs +0.2% consensus, core +0.2%
vs +0.3%) was bought — S&P 500 to a record close, yields down, September hike odds pared below 40%
(contemporaneous press, 08-13). PPI trades through the same yield channel as CPI with less force. Two
things argue for a *larger* reaction here than at either the 10-15 or 12-15 print: a live SEP meeting is
downstream (Leg 3), and by 11-13 the market has had exactly one full week to price the **11-03 midterm**
result, whose main channel into wholesale prices is tariff and fiscal policy. Three argue smaller: CPI
prints three days earlier and sets the narrative; the chair pre-committed on 08-28 to reading "trends
rather than isolated data points," which flattens the dovish-relief channel the 08-13 reaction ran on;
and the print is a Friday, with monthly opex the following Friday rather than the same week.

**No forecast is offered on the midterm outcome or its price effect.** The honest statement is only
that this is the first PPI of the post-election Congress and that its policy channel is tariffs; the
[`midterm-elections-2026-11-03`](midterm-elections-2026-11-03.md) doc owns that event.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `confirmed`; two BLS primaries fetched direct 2026-08-31).** No position and no
directional lean on this print at any horizon. The deployable content is a **corridor guard** — no new
unhedged overnights held across **2026-11-06 → 2026-11-20**, and entries deferred past the 11-13 open
per E1 — and, above that, a **conditionality flag**: until an FY2027 CR is signed, nothing should be
held whose payoff requires this print to occur, because the measured 2025 precedent for this exact slot
is **cancellation**, not delay. Working read (**estimate**, no consensus exists at D-74): the 12-month
headline rate rises from 4.7% on the light **+0.5%** base while the 12-month core rate holds or eases
against its heavy **+1.1%** base — a split that should be read as base arithmetic, not as a change in
wholesale pressure, with energy (Brent back to ~$90 today, up from ~$88 last week) the swing factor on
the goods leg. Read **health-care services and airfares** as the surviving PPI→PCE lines; from BEA's
09-30 update, portfolio management carries no rate-path signal.

**Kill switches:**

- **The existence branch, and it is the headline** — no CR signed by **2026-09-30** → expect this
  release **cancelled** with October data folded into the 12-15 print (BLS lapse page: Oct-2025 PPI,
  scheduled 2025-11-14, Canceled). Retired by a CR or full-year bills enacted before 09-30. Check on
  09-30.
- **The deletion read is wrong if** the **09-10** or **10-15** PPI prints land on schedule *during* a
  lapse — which would say the 2025 outcome was specific to that shutdown's length, not to the release.
- **The base-effect split** — killed if the 2026-11-13 release prints the 12-month headline rate
  **below** the 12-month ex-foods/energy/trade rate, which would mean the arithmetic was backwards.
  Registered as **FT-27**, scored 2026-11-16.
- **The "last read before a live meeting" leg** — dies if the Fed moves at, or signals an intermeeting
  move around, the **10-28** meeting, or if the 12-09 SEP is dropped; the whole Leg-3 framing rests on
  12-09 being live and dot-carrying.
- **The PCE channel** — collapses if BEA's **2026-09-30** annual update does not carry the
  portfolio-management methodology change its own June SCB preview describes, which would restore the
  old map in full. Check on 09-30.
- **A hot October PPI** — headline at or above ~0.3% m/m, or core-ex-trade re-accelerating on energy
  pass-through, health care or airfares → the wholesale-pressure read is live on its own terms and the
  base-effect framing becomes the second-order story, not the first.
- **Reaction-size read** — killed if the **09-10** or **10-15** PPI produces an NDX move ≥1% or a >5bp
  front-end repricing, which would say PPI is trading as a first-tier print in this regime.
- **Corridor guard** — a calendar fact, not a market read; it stands unless the 11-06, 11-10, 11-13 or
  11-20 dates move.
