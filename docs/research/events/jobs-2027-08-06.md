## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This id reached the calendar as a proposal from [`jobs-2027-07-02`](jobs-2027-07-02.md)'s own initial
research, filed at D+35 to make that lane's "last payroll before the July FOMC" leg checkable. Its
title carries a claim nobody has tested: that being **the first payroll after an FOMC decision** is
what makes this print interesting. Three questions follow. **Does the derived date survive an
independent re-derivation, and is the primary retrievable yet?** **Is the cycle-position claim true in
the tape, or only true on the calendar?** And, if it is not: **is there anything else that
distinguishes this print from the eleven others in a year?**

**One-line verdict:** the date reproduces 7-for-7 and stays `estimate` because every BLS route 403s;
the cycle-position claim is **refuted** across three independent framings and roughly forty tests; and
the thing that actually distinguishes 2027-08-06 is far simpler and much stronger — it is an **August**
payroll, which is where the print's grip on duration peaks for the year and where its grip on equities
is at its weakest.

### Method

Macro-print mode per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md) — no symbol-keyed
instrument applies (`symbols: []`). Nothing is inherited from the proposing lane; both the scheduling
rule and the FOMC date set were built from primary pages before use.

- **Attempted and failed, recorded rather than worked around.** `bls.gov` under a browser user-agent,
  four routes, **HTTP 403 on all four** (`/schedule/news_release/2027_sched.htm`,
  `/schedule/news_release/empsit.htm`, `/schedule/home.htm`, `/bls/news-release/empsit.htm`);
  `fred.stlouisfed.org/release/dates?rid=50` (the fallback release-date register) returned **404**.
  All five are in `probe-ref.blocked` above, and the source prefix is `EST:` because of it. This

- **Attempted and SUCCEEDED, which is new.** `federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm`
  returned **HTTP 200** (164,831 bytes, page-stated *"Last Update: August 19, 2026"*), and the 27
  historical pages `fomchistorical1994.htm … fomchistorical2020.htm` all returned 200. That is the
  `FED:` primary for every FOMC date this document uses, forward and backward.
- **Measured, with Yahoo split/dividend-adjusted daily bars** (the source
  `scripts/research/market-data.mjs` uses, re-fetched this session with volume, which that module
  drops), on **SPY, QQQ, TLT, IEF, LQD, AGG, HYG, ^VIX**, 1993–2026. Payroll dates were *generated by

- **Statistics:** medians throughout; two-sided permutation tests on the difference of medians (20,000
  relabelings, fixed seed); two-sided binomial against a 50% null for direction; Spearman ρ with a
  normal approximation for the continuous test. No distributional assumption anywhere.
- **Re-grepped, not cited:** `docs/plans/trade-playbooks.md`, `docs/research/multi-symbol-sweep.md`.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date reproduces independently — SUPPORTED, and it stays `estimate`.** The rule (release on
   the third Friday following the end of the Sun–Sat reference week containing the 12th) was coded
   from its definition and run over 1993–2027 before being applied. It reproduces **7 of 7** knowns:
   the four `confirmed` entries on this calendar (2026-09-04, 2026-10-02, 2026-11-06, 2026-12-04) and
   the three historical Sunday-July-4 releases (2004-07-02, 2010-07-02, 2021-07-02). July 2027's
   reference week is Sun 07-11 → Sat 07-17; the third Friday after 07-17 is **2027-08-06**. It stays
   `estimate` on two counts: no primary was retrievable (five blocked fetches above), and this lane
   does not self-confirm.

2. **The rule's documented failure mode cannot fire here — SUPPORTED.** The failure mode the sibling
   enumerated is a holiday landing on the rule's own Friday (24 such derived dates 1993–2026: New
   Year's, a run of Good Fridays, a run of Independence-Day collisions). **2027-08-06 is an ordinary
   Friday with no US market holiday within a week in either direction** — the nearest is Labor Day,
   Mon 2027-09-06. The rule applies unconditionally.

3. **"The first payroll after an FOMC" predicts nothing — REFUTED, three ways.** This is the claim the
   proposal was filed on, and it does not survive contact with the tape.

   | Framing | Result |
   |---|---|
   | **EARLY vs LATE**, print-day \|move\|, 8 instruments | SPY 1.03× (*p*=0.94) · QQQ 1.20× (0.35) · TLT 0.99× (0.90) · **IEF 0.70× (0.049)** · LQD 0.91× (0.69) · AGG 0.79× (0.24) · HYG 1.42× (0.19) · ^VIX 1.02× (0.92) |
   | **EARLY vs LATE**, forward 10-session realized vol | every ratio in 0.86–1.00×, every *p* ≥ 0.15 |
   | **FAR (≥35d) vs NEAR (≤20d)** — the cut 2027-08-06 is in | SPY 1.21× (0.38) · IEF **0.82× (0.26)** · TLT 1.06× (0.60) · all eight null |
   | **Spearman ρ(daysToNextFOMC, \|move\|)**, n=217–368 | every \|ρ\| ≤ **0.119**; smallest *p* is HYG at 0.0796 |

   The single nominal hit — IEF's 0.70× at *p*=0.049 — is one significant result in sixteen, which is
   exactly the false-positive count sixteen tests at α=0.05 produce, and it **does not reproduce** in
   either the sharper FAR/NEAR cut (0.82×, *p*=0.26) or the continuous test (ρ=−0.052, *p*=0.39).
   **The honest reading: the cycle-position framing is descriptively true and predictively empty.**

4. **What survives is the calendar month, and it clears its decisive control — SUPPORTED.** The naive
   version ("August payrolls move bonds more") is confounded twice over, so the statistic reported is
   the **amplification ratio**: a month's payroll-session median \|move\| divided by *that same
   month's* non-payroll-session median. If a payroll amplifies every month equally, August has no
   story.

   | Instrument | August ratio | Rank of 12 | Median month | August payroll median | August non-payroll median |

   | **IEF** | **2.36×** | **1** | 1.45× | **0.589%** | 0.249% |
   | **AGG** | **2.32×** | **1** | 1.57× | **0.338%** | 0.146% |
   | **TLT** | **2.03×** | **1** | 1.46× | **1.024%** | 0.504% |
   | LQD | 1.62× | 4 | 1.49× | 0.393% | 0.243% |
   | QQQ | 1.41× | 7 | 1.42× | 1.014% | 0.720% |
   | **SPY** | **1.04×** | **9** | 1.28× | 0.490% | 0.471% |

   August is **1st of 12** in all three core duration/aggregate instruments and **9th of 12** in SPY.

5. **The numerator carries it, not a quiet denominator — SUPPORTED, and this is the leg that makes
   leg 4 a finding rather than an artefact.** A high ratio can come from a loud payroll or a dead
   month. Across the twelve months the **non-payroll** baseline is flat within noise (IEF 0.231–0.274%,
   AGG 0.141–0.171%, TLT 0.481–0.587%, SPY 0.471–0.584% — August is unremarkable in every one), while
   the **payroll-session** median peaks in August for all three: TLT **1.024%** (next: June 0.980%),
   IEF **0.589%** (June 0.563%), AGG **0.338%** (June 0.312%). The direct comparison agrees: August vs
   non-August payroll sessions gives IEF 1.55× (*p*=**0.017**), AGG 1.46× (*p*=0.054), TLT 1.39×
   (*p*=0.117).

6. **It is the year's *least* equity-relevant payroll, which is the mirror image and the more useful
   half — SUPPORTED.** SPY's payroll-session median by month runs 0.376% (Nov) · 0.446% (Sep) ·
   **0.490% (Aug)** at the bottom against 1.058% (Feb) · 0.996% (May) · 0.855% (Mar) at the top —
   August is **third-lowest of twelve**. Against all payrolls it is 0.77× (*p*=0.40) and against
   ordinary August sessions 1.12× (*p*=0.71): on the equity tape the August payroll is close to
   indistinguishable from a random summer Friday.

7. **The stability check puts the finding in the modern half, not the archive — SUPPORTED.** Split at
   2010: IEF is 1.61× (*p*=**0.021**, n=17) and AGG 1.53× (*p*=**0.032**, n=17) in 2010–2026, against

8. **The summer-fragility story is REFUTED — and its most cited example is not even the biggest

### What the conditions support

Nothing directional, and a **sizing asymmetry** rather than a trade. The measured object is not "a
payroll" and not "a post-FOMC payroll" — it is *an August payroll*, and the same session that produces
the year's largest median move in duration and the aggregate index produces its third-smallest in
equities. For a paper-options book that is two separate instructions from one date: rate-duration
paper carried across 2027-08-06 should be sized against roughly **twice** an ordinary August session's
move in TLT/IEF/AGG, while equity paper should **not** be sized as though the print were an event at
all — the "August payroll blew up the tape" reflex is drawn from one salient session (2024-08-02) that
does not even top its own set. Both instructions are `estimate`-labelled and neither is an entry;
this is a refusal to mis-size in either direction, which in a compounding book is P&L.

### Honest limits

D-331, and the first limit is in the date field: **the primary is unretrievable**, so a 7-for-7
derivation stands in for a published schedule. **Multiple comparisons are the real limit on the
positive finding.** This study ran roughly **100** hypothesis tests; at α=0.05 five false positives
are expected, so no single *p*-value here is evidence. What is offered as evidence is a **coherent
cluster that survives its own control**: three instruments simultaneously ranked 1st of 12 on a
ratio that already divides out the month (leg 4), with the numerator rather than the denominator
doing the work (leg 5), holding in the modern half of the sample (leg 7), and pointing the same way
in an independent 8-session analogue set (leg 10). **n is small where it matters** — 25 August
payroll sessions for TLT/IEF, 23 for AGG, 8 analogues — and the pre-2003 bond sample barely exists,
which is why leg 7 is reported split rather than pooled. **No mechanism is claimed.** The obvious
candidate (August is the start of a long intermeeting gap, so the rates market has nothing else to
trade) is precisely the cycle-position story leg 3 refuted, so it is not offered; the fact is
measured and the cause is open. No consensus, whisper or projection for July 2027 payrolls exists
this far out and none will for months. No price instruments run (macro-print mode carries none). The
FOMC dates are now `FED:`-sourced, which retires the sibling's corresponding limit — but the two
calendar entries themselves still read `estimate` until their own lanes update them.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `estimate`, derived and 7-for-7 validated, primary 403 on 2026-09-09).** Treat
2027-08-06 08:30 ET as a high-impact known-date variance event whose interest is **entirely
seasonal, not structural**. The reason it was filed — first payroll after the 2027-07-28 FOMC — is
**refuted as a predictor** (leg 3: three framings, ~40 tests, all null) and should not be re-filed on
a later pulse. What replaces it is measured and one-sided: as an **August** print it carries the

position; direction is unpredictable in every instrument tested (SPY 16/32, *p*=1.00). The

corridor between `fomc-2027-07-28` and `fomc-minutes-2027-08-18` is read as a **coverage gap in our
own calendar** (73 tracked 2027+ macro-prints, zero US CPI/PPI/JOLTS/ADP/ECI), never as a quiet tape.
Every line here is `estimate`-labelled and none licenses an entry.
