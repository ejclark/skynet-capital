## Initial research

### The question, plainly

What is likely to happen on 2026-11-03, how does the market react, and — the part this calendar
actually needs — is there any channel by which a US midterm election reaches a paper book holding
NVDA / MRVL / AVGO / CRWV / MSFT / GOOG / META / AAPL / AMZN and the AI-energy sleeve, beyond the
generic "divided government is good for stocks" story?

**One-line verdict:** the generic channel is real and unusable (the headline base rates are
confounded and the tape's own pricing of the day is ~1.4%), but two specific channels are neither
generic nor priced in this repo's ledgers — the **AI data-center backlash**, which is a live
campaign issue with 2026 policy actions already taken against our sleeve's thesis, and a **dated
tail on 2026-12-01** where a Georgia/Mississippi runoff can carry Senate uncertainty straight into
the funding and Fed corridor.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is certain and the outcome is not — SUPPORTED, and it is the same unusual labeling
   case the funding ledger named.** 2026-11-03 is statutory (2 U.S.C. § 7; the Bipartisan Policy
   Center's page states it plainly), so this `estimate` is *not* date doubt — it is
   date-certainty with outcome-doubt. It stays `estimate` for a mechanical reason worth recording:
   this calendar's confirmed prefixes (`IR:`/`BLS:`/`FED:`/`TSY:`/…, see `market-events-data.ts`)
   have **no entry for a statutory or election-authority source**, so there is no honest way to
   file it `confirmed` without first adding a prefix — which is a calendar-schema change, out of
   this lane's scope. Noted as a proposal, not made. A future session should not read the
   `estimate` label as uncertainty about *when*.

2. **The polling and the market both point to a split Congress, with the Senate genuinely live —
   SUPPORTED.** Silver Bulletin's generic-ballot average is **D +6.6 as of 2026-08-28** (D +7.6
   likely-voter adjusted), and it explicitly notes the number "hasn't seen much movement over the
   past month" (D +5 at the start of August). Decision Desk HQ reports a near-identical **D +6**.
   The map: **35 Senate seats up (including FL and OH specials), 23 of them Republican-held**,
   Democrats needing a **net +4** off a 53–47 chamber, with six toss-ups — **ME, MI, PA, NV, WI,
   AZ** — effectively deciding it. Prediction markets have moved to roughly **51/49 Democrat** on
   Senate control, reportedly the **first Democratic lead** on those platforms, having favored
   Democrats on the House at **56–61%** as far back as June. **Counterweight, and it is
   structural:** ten states adopted new congressional maps for this cycle (AL, CA, FL, LA, MO, NC,
   OH, TN, TX, UT), estimated to net Republicans **+6 to +10 seats** — a generic-ballot edge has
   to clear that before it becomes a chamber.

3. **The generic market base rates are confounded and this doc refuses to lean on them — MIXED,
   and the rejection is the finding.** The widely-quoted figures are a **+12.4% average S&P return
   in the 12 months after the vote across 31 midterm cycles**, gains **~95% of the time since
   1938**, and **+6.5% over the three months after vs +2.5% over the three before**. This is
   exactly the shape of statistic the [funding ledger](government-funding-deadline-2026-09-30.md)
   struck out: none of the sources controls it against **unconditional 12-month equity drift**,
   which is itself strongly positive, and "stocks rose in the year after an election" is close to
   "stocks rose." **Recorded as a rejected statistic, not a supporting one.** The defensible
   version is the *pre*-election half — average **+2.9%** in the 12 months before midterms vs
   **+8.9%** for all years, with a ~19% average intra-year drawdown — because that one is a
   comparison against a stated baseline.

4. **The market is already paying for this date, and the amount is small — SUPPORTED, and it
   bounds how big a deal election night itself is.** As of **2026-08-25**: **VIX futures 17.4
   (Sep) → 19.0 (Oct) → 19.7 (Nov)** against a spot VIX of **15.8** that day and **14.51** at our
   own 08-27 probe — a visible term-structure bump into November ("you can already see a clear
   steepening," Matthew Thompson, Little Harbor Advisors). The options market implies a **~1.4%
   S&P move for 2026-11-04**, the day after. Cboe's Mandy Xu expects "a growing number of
   election-specific trades." **The honest reading of 1.4% is that the tape expects a normal-ish
   day**, not a repricing — which is consistent with leg 3's base rates and inconsistent with
   treating election night as a shock. What the curve *does* say is that hedging demand is
   concentrated in November while spot vol sits near a 2026 low: SpotGamma's Brent Kochuba —
   "Insurance is very cheap right now." Recorded as a measurement. **The `estimate` label forbids
   date-keyed action on it.**

5. **The seasonal window is a dated, testable claim and 2026 is currently failing it — MIXED, and
   it is registered rather than asserted.** BTIG's Jonathan Krinsky (via Yahoo Finance,
   **2026-08-19**) measures midterm years since 1990 peaking around **August 18** and falling
   through **October 11**: equal-weight S&P **−6%** on average, S&P 500 **~−5%**, Nasdaq **~−7%**,
   Russell 2000 **~−8%**, against flat-to-positive in non-midterm years. The 2026 setup fit the
   warning — EW S&P **+15%** YTD, S&P **+12%**, **zero** sessions all year with >80% down-volume on
   the NYSE (typical: **21**), VIX near lows. **But the window has been running nine sessions and
   is going the other way:** RSP **219.79 → 221.45** (+0.75%), S&P **7691.76 → 7730.99** (+0.51%),
   Nasdaq **+0.96%**, Russell **−0.12%**. Registered as **FT-21** in
   [`forward-tests.md`](../forward-tests.md), score-by **2026-10-12** — n=1, an observation, never
   a promotion.

6. **The AI data-center backlash is the real channel to this book — SUPPORTED, and it is why this
   event is `high` impact despite legs 3 and 4.** This is not sentiment; it is polling plus
   enacted policy. Brookings (**2026-08-25**) records the swing: a Heatmap poll in **Sep 2025** was
   **44% support / 42% oppose**; by **Jun 2026** Reuters/Ipsos found **64% oppose** rapid
   data-center construction, **77%** worried about their electricity rates, and only **14%**
   willing to live near one. An Annenberg poll puts opposition at **61%** — **69% of Democrats and
   54% of Republicans**, i.e. bipartisan. The NRSC has reportedly called data centers a "sleeper
   issue" for the whole cycle. **The actions already taken:** New York has the first **statewide
   hyperscale moratorium**; Pennsylvania's Gov. Shapiro signed an **executive order** adding
   authorization guardrails after Republican criticism; Texas's Gov. Abbott announced **restrictions
   plus an audit** and called to end some tax incentives; Michigan's GOP Senate nominee backs a
   **one-year construction moratorium**; Michigan's Democratic candidate proposes making developers
   bear the electricity-rate increases. Ad campaigns in TX, OH, MI, WI and PA run on the power
   bill. **Mapped onto this repo:** the [AI-energy study](../ai-energy-constraint.md) is built on
   IPP scarcity rent in **PJM and ERCOT** — the two regions at the center of this fight — and

   Neither ledger currently prices a *political* siting/ratepayer risk. That gap is this doc's

8. **Control may not be known on election night, and that has a date — SUPPORTED, and it is the
