### Leg 1 — the date is real: SUPPORTED

OPEC's 41st-meeting statement (2026-06-07, item 8) reads *"Hold the 42nd OPEC and non-OPEC Ministerial Meeting on 29 November 2026."* The seven countries' 2026-11-01 release closes *"The next meeting will be held on 29 November 2026."* The 68th JMMC statement set the 69th JMMC for the same day (carried from the 10-04 ledger, not re-fetched). One search result gave "30 November"; that is the 2025 meeting (the 40th, opec.org/pr-detail/582-30-november-2025.html), not a conflict. **The 11-01 ledger's note that the 42nd ONOMM was "undated at every primary" is superseded:** the date was published in June; that session could not reach opec.org's press-release index (403), and the pr-detail page carries it.

### Leg 2 — the baselines will be argued, not necessarily settled: MIXED

The mechanism: a third-party assessment of each member's maximum sustainable production capacity (MSC), "to be used as reference for 2027 production baselines" (June statement, item 6). DeGolyer and MacNaughton assess all members except Russia, Iran and Venezuela. Reuters sources said on 2026-10-02 that the end-September deadline slipped to mid-November because the Iran war disrupted capacity projects and some members had not submitted data. CNBC (10-31) repeats mid-November and quotes Rystad's Jorge Leon: *"The much more important test comes at the Nov. 29 ministerial meeting"* and *"OPEC+ faces an extremely difficult call for 2027."* A report delivered mid-month leaves about two weeks to digest an allocation fight between 21 signatories. Nothing found says the table will be ready; nothing found says it will not. **Not established:** whether the review was delivered. This is FT-2.

### Leg 3 — January is another hold: MIXED, leaning SUPPORTED

The seven held at the September 2026 level for October, November and December (about 31.01 mb/d combined, per the 11-01 close-out). Targets: Saudi Arabia 10.5, Russia 9.9, Iraq 4.4, Kuwait 2.7, Kazakhstan 1.6, Algeria 1.0, Oman 0.8 mb/d (enerdata, 10-04 release). Members are producing well under target because of Hormuz disruption (investinglive: about 5 mb/d under prewar for the core members), Gulf exports running at 60% to 80% of normal in recent months, and CNBC says further changes are not expected before next year. The caveat is structural: January is the first month the new regime would govern, so a held number might be replaced by a baseline-derived one. This is FT-3, with a VOID path if no January level is stated.

### Leg 4 — the channel into our book: SUPPORTED as indirect only
