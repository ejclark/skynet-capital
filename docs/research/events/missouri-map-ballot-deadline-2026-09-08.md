## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Missouri's Supreme Court blocked the 2025 Republican congressional map for the 2026-11-03 general
election and sent it to a referendum. Republicans have asked the US Supreme Court to stay that
ruling. Three questions: **(1) when does this actually resolve**, **(2) which way is it likely to
go**, and **(3) is there any channel by which it reaches a paper book holding NVDA / MRVL / AVGO /
CRWV / MSFT / GOOG / META / AAPL / AMZN and the AI-energy sleeve?

**One-line verdict:** it does not resolve on **2026-09-08** — that date is a state-law ballot
deadline the federal court is expressly not bound by, and the real window runs to **09-19**; the
outcome is genuinely live rather than a foregone denial, because this Court granted the identical
relief in *Malliotakis* six months ago; and the market channel is **nil and measurably so**, which
is what keeps this a `low`-impact seat-math input rather than an event with a stance.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The 2026-09-08 date is real, is now statutorily cited, and is NOT the resolution point —
   SUPPORTED, and this is the finding that most changes what the calendar entry means.** The
   entry was filed `estimate` partly because "the 09-08 date reaches this calendar through a
   public-radio summary rather than the Missouri statute or a court order." Half of that is now
   resolved: the application cites it twice to **Mo. Rev. Stat. § 115.125.2** — "*ahead of a
   September 8 state-law deadline to alter the ballot, Mo. Rev. Stat. § 115.125.2, county election
   officials are scrambling to figure out how to comply with the order below*." But the same
   document disclaims it as a constraint on the Court: "*Although this Court is not bound by it,
   Missouri has a state-law deadline of September 8 to alter the ballot … an administrative stay by
   that time would provide much needed clarity to confused election officials.*" **09-08 is a
   convenience date the applicant would like, not a deadline the tribunal owes.** The dates the
   applicant actually asks for: "*this Court issue a decision no later than September 14, 2026*,"
   with a proposed briefing schedule of respondent's brief by **5:00 p.m. 09-07** and reply by
   **5:00 p.m. 09-08**. **Status stays `estimate` regardless** — the confirmed-prefix taxonomy
   (`IR:`/`BLS:`/`FED:`/`TSY:`/…, see `market-events-data.ts`) has no slot for a statute or a court
   filing, the same mechanical gap the
   [midterm ledger](midterm-elections-2026-11-03.md) recorded for a statutory election date and the
   [BoJ ledger](boj-decision-2026-09-18.md) for a foreign central bank. Understating the label only
   widens caution.

2. **The binding constraint is federal and lands 2026-09-19 — SUPPORTED, and it is proposed to the
   calendar in this PR.** The application: "*absent a stay, Missouri will be forced to violate the
   Uniformed and Overseas Citizens Absentee Voting Act (UOCAVA), which sets a deadline of September
   19 to start sending absentee ballots to overseas and military voters. 52 U.S.C. § 20302(a)(8)*"
   — and elsewhere, "*UOCAVA requires Missouri to send mail ballots to overseas and military voters
   by September 19 — 15 days from now*," with the State "*genuinely unsure whether it can switch its
   governing congressional map in time*." That is the date after which the question is moot for
   11-03 whichever way it goes: once ballots are printed and mailed under a map, no order restores
   the other one. **Proposed same-PR:** `missouri-uocava-ballot-mailing-2026-09-19`, `estimate` /
   `NEWS:`, `low`, `symbols: []`.

3. **The precedent cuts toward a grant, not a denial — SUPPORTED, and it inverts the naive prior.**
   The application's lead authority is *Malliotakis v. Williams*, 146 S. Ct. 809 (2026) (No.
   25A914), where this Court **stayed a New York state trial court's order** requiring a redrawn
   11th Congressional District — granted **2026-03-02**, Alito, J., concurring, Sotomayor, J.,
   joined by Kagan and Jackson, JJ., dissenting. Jurisdiction over a non-final state judgment came
   from 28 U.S.C. § 1257(a) and § 1651(a). SCOTUSblog's own read (2026-03-06, Will Baude) is that
   the order opened "a new big zone of Supreme Court discretion" over state-court election
   litigation and would likely "generate more applications." **This application is the predicted
   application.** The applicant argues its case is stronger: "*This case is far easier than
   Malliotakis*" — there, the election was four months out; here the primary has **already
   happened**. **Anyone treating "SCOTUS declines to disturb a state high court" as the base rate
   is using a prior this Court replaced six months ago.** No probability is stated here — this repo
   has no instrument that measures a court's emergency docket — but the direction of the correction
   is the finding.

4. **Purcell points the applicant's way here, which is unusual and is the crux — SUPPORTED as a
   characterization of the argument, untested as a prediction.** Purcell normally counsels *against*
   late judicial changes to election rules, which usually protects the status quo below. Here both
   directions are a change, and the status quo the voters experienced is the **disputed** map:
   Missouri ran its **August 4 primary under the 2025 HB 1 map with about 1.2 million votes cast**,
   nominees were selected in those districts, and the state court's 09-03 order moves the general
   election to the 2022 lines. The application: the order "*renders a completed federal Primary
   Election utterly pointless — disenfranchising hundreds of thousands of voters who will now be
   moved into new districts and forced to vote for candidates they had no role in selecting*," and
   "*the chaos inflicted by the order below exceeds that present in any Purcell-related case*." It
   cites *Merrill v. Milligan*, 142 S. Ct. 879, 880-81 (2022) (Kavanaugh, J., concurring) — to the
   Circuit Justice who now holds it — and *Abbott v. LULAC*, 146 S. Ct. 419, where this Court
   applied Purcell to foreclose challenges to Texas's map ten months out. **What is not tested
   here:** the respondent's brief was not due until noon 09-07 and does not exist yet, so this doc
   has read exactly one side's papers. The state court's own answer — that the Missouri
   Constitution's referendum power (art. III, § 52(a)) reaches "any act of the general assembly"
   with no carve-out for maps, and that the map was "unlawfully used during the primaries" — is
   known only through summaries. **A one-sided record is a limit, not a lean.**

5. **The seat is real and the arithmetic cannot resolve it — SUPPORTED, and it bounds how much
   this matters.** HB 1 was drawn to take Missouri's delegation from **6-2 to 7-1**, targeting the
   Kansas City district held by Rep. Emanuel Cleaver (D). One seat. The
   [midterm ledger](midterm-elections-2026-11-03.md)'s leg 2 carries a redistricting counterweight
   of "ten states adopted new maps, estimated to net Republicans **+6 to +10** seats." The sources
   this session found do not agree with that or with each other: **Cook Political Report**'s
   likeliest scenario is Republicans netting **~5** seats from redistricting; a raw enacted-map
   count has Republicans "in position to gain up to **16**" against **6** for Democrats; the House
   sits at **218-213 with four vacancies**. **A ±1-seat change is inside the disagreement between
   the estimates, not outside it** — which is the honest reason this event is `low` impact even
   though its subject matter is a House majority.

6. **There is no market channel, and that is measured rather than assumed — SUPPORTED.** The
   ruling landed 2026-09-03 (unanimous, a state supreme court reversing on a congressional map,
   national coverage). VIX across it: **15.20 (09-02) → 14.32 (09-03) → 14.53 (09-04)**. S&P:
   **7666.60 → 7747.71 → 7718.60**. VVIX **86.25 → 83.80 → 84.42**. The 09-03/09-04 sessions had
   their own named drivers in this calendar's other ledgers — ISM-services 09-03, the **+162k
   payrolls print on 09-04** ([jobs-2026-09-04](jobs-2026-09-04.md)) — which is precisely the point:
   **a redistricting ruling is not a driver the tape ranks, and cannot be separated from prints
   that are.** Add the bound the sibling ledger already established — the options market prices the
   whole of election night at **~1.4%** — and one seat inside that is unmeasurable by construction.

7. **The one non-trivial second-order channel, named and then bounded — MIXED.** If the stay is
   granted, the operative holding is not about Missouri: it is that a federal court will supervise
   state-court orders on congressional maps, extending *Malliotakis* from a one-off into a usable
   rule while a dozen-plus states litigate their own maps into 11-03. That is a live input to
   House-control odds, which the [midterm ledger](midterm-elections-2026-11-03.md) does track
   (betting aggregates vs. Silver Bulletin's model, currently disagreeing). **Why this is MIXED and
   not a finding to act on:** the transmission runs court → seat math → chamber odds → *some*
   policy the market prices, and the midterm ledger's own work says the last link is the AI
   data-center backlash, whose vehicle (H.R. 9340) cleared committee **52-0 — bipartisan**, i.e.
   largely indifferent to which party holds the chamber. **The chain has a break in it at the far
   end, and naming the break is worth more than pretending the chain holds.**
