## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** is 2027-02-01 the right date, what will the February-2027 borrowing-estimate
release contain, does the release move the tape, and does anything about *this* edition change the two
siblings' stand-aside — around 2027-02-01?

**One-line verdict:** the date holds on a rule this session rebuilt from a wider window than the
sibling used, the release-day null reproduces exactly and survives extension, and the two things that
distinguish this edition both cut toward *reading* it — it is the likeliest first debt-limit-live
edition on the calendar, and the one equity effect the sibling registered as live does not survive a
weekday-matched control.

### Method

Sourced primary research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md) (macro-print
mode), plus three first-party measurements built this session from scratch rather than inherited:

1. **Date audit, widened.** `api.fiscaldata.treasury.gov/.../auctions_query`, fetched direct
   2026-09-10 (HTTP 200), filtered to **30-Year Bond new issues** (`original_issue_date` null, i.e.
   not a reopening) maturing on the 15th of February/May/August/November. Its `announcemt_date` **is**
   the quarterly refunding announcement — **51** cycles, **2014-02 → 2026-08**.
2. **Release-day study, with an independently rebuilt FOMC set.** Yahoo **dividend-adjusted** daily
   closes via `scripts/research/market-data.mjs` → `bars()` (instrument caches busted first per the
   cache-discipline rule). Instruments **TLT · IEF · ^TNX · SPY · QQQ · ^VIX**, window 2015-01-01 →
   2026-09-10 (n=2,939 sessions). Release dates derived as announcement Wednesday **minus two days**.
   Two-sided permutation test, 200,000 iterations, against random same-size draws from the same
   session pool; placebo columns T−2 … T+2.
3. **The drift audit.** The sibling registered an equity drift on first-business-day releases against
   a control of all non-release first-business-days. This session rebuilt that control **matched on
   weekday**, and re-ran it on full SPY history (1993-01-29 → 2026-09-10, n=8,460).

FOMC dates come from the Fed's own calendars — `federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm`
for 2021–2027 and `fomchistorical<year>.htm` for 2014–2020, all fetched direct 2026-09-10, HTTP 200 —
parsed to **111** two-day meeting decisions, exactly eight per scheduled year. One source was blocked
and is recorded rather than substituted: `bipartisanpolicy.org` returned **403** on 2026-09-10, the
same Cloudflare interstitial the sibling hit; `crfb.org` returned HTTP 200 and is quoted directly.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The date holds, and February 2027 is in the class that cannot be ambiguous — SUPPORTED.** The
proposal derived 2027-02-01 from the Monday-minus-two pairing plus this calendar's already-tracked
`treasury-refunding-2027-02-03`. Both halves were re-derived here. Sorted by the weekday on which the
refunding month's 1st falls, across all **51** cycles:

| Weekday of the 1st | n | Announcement | Ambiguous? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sunday | 7 | the 4th | no |
| **Monday** | **7** | **the 3rd — 7 of 7, no exception** | **no** |
| Tuesday | 6 | the 2nd | no |
| Wednesday | 7 | the 1st | no |
| Thursday | 7 | the PRIOR month's last day | no |
| Friday | 8 | the 6th **or** the prior month's 30th | **yes — two Wednesdays in window** |
| Saturday | 9 | the 5th | no |

The window rule fits **51 of 51**: the announcement is the single Wednesday inside **[the 30th of the
prior month … the 6th of the refunding month]**. **February 2027 opens on a Monday**, so that window
(2027-01-30 → 2027-02-06) contains exactly one Wednesday — **2027-02-03** — and the estimates release
is the Monday two sessions earlier, **2027-02-01**.

**One correction to the sibling, worth a lane's attention.** The three cycles my window adds
(2014-02, 2014-05, 2014-08) break its Friday-1st **tie-break**, not its window rule. The sibling
recorded that when the 1st is a Friday, *"May has taken the later Wednesday and February/August/
November the earlier"* — 8 of 8 in its window. **2014-08 is an August that took the later**
(2014-08-06, with 2014-07-30 also in window), making that tie-break **7 of 8**. It does not touch
February 2027, which needs no tie-break; it means any lane deriving a **Friday-1st** refunding date
should treat the tie-break as a heuristic and say so.

**The entry stays `estimate`**, and the reason is unchanged and verified today:
home.treasury.gov's most-recent-refunding-documents page, re-fetched direct 2026-09-10 (HTTP 200,
73,660 bytes), publishes exactly one cycle ahead — *"(The next release is scheduled for November 2,
2026)"*, *"(The next release is scheduled for November 4 , 2026)"*, *"(The next release is scheduled
for October 16, 2026)"* — and names **no 2027 date at all**. A rule-derived date is never
self-confirmed by this lane. The canonical `treasury-borrowing-estimates-2027-02-01.json` is written

### What the conditions support

Nothing directional. No house playbook is macro-keyed (S1/S2/E1/S3/S4 + G1 are symbol/earnings-keyed).
Two disciplines travel out of this session. Inherited and confirmed a third time: **an event's
reaction function must be computed against its collision set, not its date list.** New, and the one
worth carrying to every study in this lane: **a control group must be matched on the structural
property the treatment cannot vary.** Every borrowing-estimate release is a Monday because the release
*is* the refunding Wednesday minus two sessions — so "release day" and "Monday" are the same variable,
and any control that averages across weekdays hands the release credit for the day of the week. The
sibling's drift was not a bad measurement; it was a good measurement against a control that could not
answer the question. The tell is cheap to check and worth checking by default: **before comparing a
scheduled event to "ordinary days," ask what the schedule forces to be constant.**

### Honest limits

- **The date rests on an inferred rule, and the rule has now visibly frayed once.** 51 of 51 on the
  window is a strong fit, not a schedule, and the Friday-1st tie-break went from 8-of-8 to 7-of-8 the
  moment the window widened by three cycles. February 2027 is in the unambiguous class, so this
  particular date does not depend on the frayed part — but the `estimate` label is doing real work.
- **The pre-2023 release dates are derived, not read.** The Monday-minus-two pairing is 15/15 against
  the primary press-release reads in
  [treasury-borrowing-estimates-2026-11-02](treasury-borrowing-estimates-2026-11-02.md); the 36
  earlier dates are announcement-minus-two and were not individually checked.
- **The 15:00 ET timing is still the worst signal-to-noise window on this calendar.** T+0
  close-to-close holds six and a half hours of unrelated trading before the news and one after it. The
  measurement supports "no *large* release-day effect," never "no effect." **Intraday (15:00→16:00)
  data would settle it and was not used** — unchanged from both siblings, and now three ledgers deep.
- **Leg 6 refutes an attribution, not an effect.** It shows the drift is not the Treasury release and
  not ISM; it does not establish what the 2015+ first-BD-Monday number *is*, beyond "a 54-observation
  slice of a month-turn seasonal that reverses on the long sample." Anyone wanting the real answer
  needs the seasonal literature and a longer index series, not another release.
- **Welch t=0.78 is not a proof of no difference.** With n=17 against n=33 and SPY's daily σ near
  1.1%, the test has poor power against a true effect of ~0.2pp. The honest claim is that the
  sibling's evidence does not survive its own control, not that the release provably does nothing.
- **Leg 4's timing is this session's own extrapolation** from debt-to-the-penny growth, not a CBO or
  Treasury projection. Total debt outstanding grows lumpily, includes intragovernmental holdings, and
  differs from debt subject to limit; Treasury also draws the cash balance down as a limit approaches,
  which delays the binding date. Treat "Jan–Mar 2027" as a quarter, not a date — and note the series
  moved *away* from the ceiling this week ($40.0946T on 09-04 → $40.0743T on 09-09).
- **The debt-limit consensus is partly unfetchable.** bipartisanpolicy.org returned **403** today (the
  second consecutive session; recorded in `probe-ref.blocked`), so the "reached ~May 2027 / X-date
  February 2028" framing the sibling carried is at search-snippet grade and is *not* relied on here.
  Only CRFB's fetched page is quoted.
- **Leg 5 is carried, not recomputed.** The debt-limit regime split is the sibling's; this session
  reproduced the sample it rests on but did not re-run the split.
- **The impact tier stays `low`.** Nothing in legs 2, 3 or 6 argues for raising it, and leg 4's
