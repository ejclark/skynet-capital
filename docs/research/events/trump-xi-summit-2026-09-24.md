## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** Xi Jinping is reported to be making a state visit to the White House on
Sep 24, 2026 — is the date real and primary-verifiable, what's actually on the agenda, how has the
market reacted to the two prior Trump-Xi meetings this year, and which of the names this research
already tracks (AI-semis) carry the most sensitivity to it.

**One-line verdict:** the date is heavily press-corroborated but not yet primary-confirmed, the agenda
is explicitly AI/trade/export-control-shaped (the exact risk class this research tracks for AVGO/NVDA/
MRVL/AMD), and the market's own history with this specific pairing in 2026 is a coin flip between a
muted relief rally and a global selloff — a guard-shaped conclusion, not a directional one, that
extends the flat-by-print discipline already in place for AI-semis names through the summit window.

**Conviction legs, tested:**

1. **The Sep 24 date is real and imminent.** MIXED — heavily press-corroborated, not
   primary-verified. Trump personally invited Xi to the White House for Sep 24 at a state banquet in
   Beijing in May 2026 ("tonight, it is my honor to extend an invitation to you... this Sept. 24"),
   and multiple independent outlets (Fox News, The Hill, US News, CryptoBriefing) report the visit was
   reconfirmed Aug 17, 2026 with a specific itinerary — Xi arriving late Sep 23, departing Sep 25, and
   skipping the UN General Assembly to make the trip. That level of independent, mutually-consistent
   detail (a specific itinerary, not just a date) is a stronger-than-usual press signal, but no direct
   fetch of whitehouse.gov or an official government press release was found this pass — status stays
   **estimate** per house policy until a primary source is directly read, the same treatment AVGO's
   doc gave its own IR-blocked date before the flip.

2. **AI governance and export controls are genuinely on the agenda, not just symbolic.** SUPPORTED.
   Multiple outlets frame AI dominance as the central discussion topic (Fox News: "to discuss AI"),
   alongside trade, Taiwan, and the war involving Iran; CSIS and Atlantic Council coverage of the
   broader 2026 Trump-Xi engagement track (Beijing May 2026 → this Washington visit) frames it as part
   of managing "constructive strategic stability," with a technology dialogue reportedly being
   prepared ahead of the meeting specifically on AI.

3. **This is a high-stakes, deal-shaped event, not a photo-op — and the market's reaction to the
   pairing is fat-tailed, not a clean base rate.** MIXED, and itself the load-bearing finding. Two
   prior 2026 Trump-Xi meetings, two different market outcomes: the Oct-30-2025 Kuala Lumpur/Busan
   meeting produced a muted "relief rally" (Chinese stocks held steady near a 10-year peak) with one
   concrete semis-relevant deliverable — a one-year pause on Entity List affiliate restrictions,
   described as "a clear upside catalyst for U.S. semiconductors." The May-14-2026 Beijing summit
   whipsawed instead: pre-summit optimism lifted NVDA ~2% premarket on H200-to-China approval hopes,
   but the summit "delivered a predictable script" with "nothing of real substance" on concrete
   deliverables, and markets sold off broadly afterward — CSI 300 −1.12%, KOSPI over −6%, S&P 500
   futures −1% before the NY open. n=2 is a thin sample, but it already spans the full range from
   relief rally to global selloff — there is no clean base rate here to trade either direction.

4. **AI-semis carry outsized sensitivity to this event.** SUPPORTED. Both prior 2026 meetings moved
   NVDA/semis on China-chip-access speculation even when the broader deal underwhelmed (H200 approval
   hopes both times); the AVGO pulse-check that originated this doc already flagged AVGO/NVDA/MRVL/AMD
   as the sector most exposed to the standing export-control regime. The stacking Section-301 tariff
   math sharpens this: combined Section-301 exposure on Chinese semiconductors is already near 70%
   once a reportedly-pending 7.5% "overcapacity" layer (on top of a 12.5% forced-labor-replacement
   tariff that took effect Jul 24, itself stacked on the 50% semiconductor-specific rate standing since
   the first Trump term) is formally imposed — reported as being finalized ahead of this summit
   (techtimes, checked 2026-08-28, citing the Dec-2025 USTR Section 301 final action and a March-2026
   USTR overcapacity investigation covering 16 trading partners). That 7.5% layer is *reported as
   pending*, not yet in effect — today's actual combined rate is lower than the ~70% post-implementation
   figure.

5. **The Kuala Lumpur truce's Nov-10 deadline gives this summit real teeth, not just symbolism.**
   SUPPORTED, contextual. The Oct-30-2025 Kuala Lumpur Joint Arrangement suspended the additional 24%
   retaliatory ("Liberation Day") tariff for one year, holding the rate at 10% through **Nov 9/10,
   2026** — roughly six weeks after this summit. China committed to pausing rare-earth export controls
   and addressing retaliation against U.S. semiconductor manufacturers under the same arrangement. That
   makes Sep 24 a natural staging point for a truce-extension signal (or its conspicuous absence), not
   an isolated diplomatic photo-op — the summit and the Nov-10 deadline are one connected risk window,
   not two.
