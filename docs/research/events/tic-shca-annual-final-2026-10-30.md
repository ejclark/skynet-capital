## Initial research

### The question

The tic-monthly-2026-10-16 D-0 pulse found a second "final" in Treasury's release-dates table that nothing tracked: *what is the 2026-10-30 annual survey of **U.S. holdings of foreign securities**, what will it say, and does it matter for anything we trade?* It is the outbound mirror of the foreign-holdings-of-U.S.-securities survey (SHL) that `tic-annual-survey-prelim-2027-02-26` and `tic-annual-survey-final-2027-04-30` already cover, and the first thing to settle is whether the siblings' "the final is the only durable document" frame transfers.

**Verdict:** it half-transfers. The SHCA keeps its preliminary online (the August press release and a data page), so — unlike the SHL — the preliminary-to-final revision *can* be measured, and it is tiny. The content is already known to ±0.5%. What can still surprise is the calendar.

### Method

Primary sources only, all fetched direct 2026-10-16: Treasury's release-dates table; the 2026-08-31 preliminary press release (`sb0618`) and its data page (`shcprelim.html`, 191 country rows); the 2025 preliminary and final press releases (`sb0232`, `sb0325`); the 2024 preliminary (`jy2554`) and 2022 preliminary and final releases (`jy1709`, `jy1857`); the 152-page `shca2024_report.pdf`. Rates from FRED's `DGS10` (Treasury par curve, H.15) and VIX from FRED `VIXCLS`. Instruments `earnings-cycle` / `intraday-edges` are symbol-keyed and not applicable (`symbols: []`).

### Leg 1 — the date: SUPPORTED, with a measured slip prior

- **Three Treasury primaries agree.** The release-dates table reads October `16` and `30 - final`; `sb0618` says "will be released on October 30, 2026"; and the preliminary data page says "This page and data expire on October 30, 2026." That clears the bar the sibling ledgers set (one primary plus independent corroboration), so the entry is promoted `confirmed` here — `confirmed` describes the published schedule only.
- **Mechanical check.** 2026-10-30 is a Friday and the last business day of October (the 31st is a Saturday). The finals of 2023 (Tuesday 10-31), 2024 and 2025's schedule (10-31) all sit on the last business day of the month; the 2027 table's `29 - final` is also a Friday last business day.
- **The slip.** `sb0232` (2025-08-29) scheduled the 2024 final for 2025-10-31; `sb0325` is dated **2025-11-26**. That is **+26 days**. The window coincides with the 10-01 → 11-12 lapse in appropriations, which fits the page's standing closure notice, but the cause is inferred, not stated by Treasury. The 2024 final's on-schedule release of 2024-10-31 rests on a search summary of the Treasury release, not a direct fetch.
- **Why the same cause is unlikely here.** `tic-monthly-2026-10-16`'s 10-16 row records the funding law as running through 2026-12-11 (inherited, not re-verified here), so the mechanism that cost 2025 its date is absent before 10-30. The base rate is still 1 in 3 on n=3, so the call is high-but-not-certain. Registered **FT-1**.

The 2022 final is read from the Dec-2022 column of the 2023 preliminary (the figure Treasury carries forward), not from the 2022 final's own table, which this session could not extract. Short-term debt is the noisy leg (end-2024: 393 → 380, −3.3%), but it is 2% of the total.

What the 2025 number is: **+$3,454bn (+21.8%)** on end-2024's final $15,823bn, of which equity is **+$3,151bn (91%)** — foreign equity up **26.1%** to $15,246bn against long-term debt up 7.8% and short-term debt up 11.3%. That is a valuation-led year on foreign equity prices, not a story about U.S. investors suddenly buying abroad; the 2024 final's own decomposition (equity change +0.60tn, of which valuation +0.49tn and net purchases +0.15tn) shows the same shape a year earlier. Registered **FT-2**.

### Leg 3 — the country order is stable: SUPPORTED

Top of the 2025 preliminary: Cayman Islands **3,073**, United Kingdom **2,007**, Canada **1,886**, Japan **1,483**, Ireland **1,182**, France **910**, Switzerland **803**, Netherlands **748**, Germany **685**, Taiwan **668**. The top four are **43.8%** of the total (2024 final: 45% per Treasury; 44.6% on the preliminary table).

Revisions to the 2024 top 20, preliminary → final: **largest $6bn** (Canada, 1,473 → 1,467, −0.41%), mean absolute **$1.3bn**, and the largest percentage move was Sweden at −1.1% on a $180bn base. The tightest top-10 gap this year is Germany (685) over Taiwan (668) at **$17bn** (0.09% of the total), about three times the largest revision seen — so that pair is where FT-3 is most likely to fail; the next-tightest is Switzerland over the Netherlands at $55bn (nine times), and Japan over Ireland is $301bn. Registered **FT-3**.

### Leg 4 — what the final adds: MIXED, and useful only as context

The preliminary is a country table; the final is a report. The 2024 report (152 pages) adds a decomposition of the change into net purchases, valuation and "other" (equity 2024: +0.15tn purchases, +0.49tn valuation, −0.03tn other), the net asset position against foreign holdings of U.S. securities (a downward trend, driven by U.S. equity outrunning foreign equity and by foreign purchases of U.S. bonds), asset-backed securities at about 19% of foreign debt held, 77% of foreign debt dollar-denominated, and a 37% financial-industry share. None of that moves a quote; it is context for the app's "who holds what" story and for the siblings' mirror-image survey. **Not read this session:** the 2025 final, which does not exist yet.

### Leg 5 — the tape: UNSCOREABLE, and honestly so

- **n=3, each next session confounded.** Treasury par 10-year, `DGS10`: 2023-10-31 → 11-01 **−11bp** (FOMC and refunding day); 2024-10-31 → 11-01 **+9bp** (jobs report day); 2025-11-26 → 11-28 **+2bp** (post-Thanksgiving session). Three observations, two of them on a scheduled-data day, say nothing about this release.
- **The sibling's null does not transfer by assumption.** `tic-annual-survey-final-2027-04-30` measured the SHL final at 4.32bp vs 4.27bp all sessions (n=22, p=0.95); that is a different survey, and no SHCA-final history of that length is sourced here. Treated as a prior, not a finding.
- **2026-11-02 is crowded again:** ISM manufacturing (high, confirmed), construction spending, SLOOS, and the Treasury marketable-borrowing estimates (confirmed) all land on the next session. No tape forward test is registered, because none could be scored cleanly.

### What plays the conditions support

None. `symbols: []` and `low` tier: no option structure is keyed to this release, the data is ten months old at print, the headline is public, and the release comes after the cash close. The honest deliverable is a dated date-risk, a ±0.5% bound that lets the print be recognized as non-news in one glance, and a stand-aside.

### Honest limits

- The 2022 final figure is the carried column in the following year's preliminary, not that year's own table.
- 2024's on-schedule final date rests on a search-index summary of Treasury's release, not a direct fetch; 2023's and 2025's are direct.
- n=3 survey years of revision history and n=3 release-date outcomes; both bands will tighten or widen on the first new observation.
- The funding-law dates are inherited from the tic-monthly ledger and were not re-verified.

- No SHCA-specific tape study exists in the repo; leg 5 is a statement of what could not be measured.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stand aside on every horizon.** The date is **confirmed** (Treasury primaries; the page's closure notice still applies). The content is a bounded non-event: the preliminary headline is $19,277bn and the final is expected inside ±0.5%. Estimates widen caution, never trigger action — and nothing here is an estimate for action: it is a confirmed, low-impact, symbol-free release.

Kill switches, each dated:

1. **Release slips.** The final is not on Treasury's page by 2026-10-30 23:59 ET → the date call is wrong (FT-1) and FT-2/FT-3 re-key to the actual date.
2. **Headline moves.** The final total lands outside $19,181–19,373bn → the revision regime changed (FT-2).
3. **Order moves.** Any change in the top-10 country order against the preliminary → FT-3 killed.
4. **Regime.** VIX closing above 19.46 by 2026-10-23 → re-read the corridor rather than this print.
5. **Calendar.** Treasury posting a revision notice moving the Annual `30 - final` row before 10-30 → re-check same day.
