The dominant variable at D-44 is **publication, not demand** — a 10-01 lapse delays this print roughly

### Leg 2 — a 10-01 funding lapse DELAYS this print rather than deleting it — SUPPORTED, and it is the load-bearing leg

FY2026 appropriations lapse at the end of **2026-09-30**
([`government-funding-deadline-2026-09-30`](government-funding-deadline-2026-09-30.md), `estimate`).
That doc's framing is that a lapse **deletes** jobs 10-02 and CPI 10-14 — correct, and BLS-specific.
**Census behaves differently, and the 2025 record separates them cleanly:**

| Series | 2025 lapse outcome |
|---|---|
| BLS — October 2025 CPI | **Cancelled outright**; never published |
| BLS — import/export price indexes, Oct-2025 reference month | **864 of 1,625 indexes permanently suppressed** (per [`import-export-prices-2026-11-17`](import-export-prices-2026-11-17.md)) |
| Census — Sep-2025 advance retail sales | **Delayed 40 days**, then published in full on **2025-11-25**: $733.3B, +0.2% m/m, +4.3% y/y |
| Census — Oct-2025 advance retail sales | Delayed 2025-11-14 → **2025-12-16** |

The mechanism explains the split rather than merely describing it. CPI collects prices *in the
reference month*, by field observation; a month not collected is a month that does not exist. MARTS is
a **retrospective survey of retailers' own books** — the respondent still has September's sales ledger
in December, so the estimate is recoverable late. That is why every Census month came back and the CPI
month did not.

**The delay is long, and Census's own schedule proves it.** The 2026 release schedule fetched today
shows the recovery path in its own dates: January-2026 data → **March 6**, February → **April 1**,
March → **April 21**, April → **May 14** — the first release back on a normal ~one-month lag. So the
2025 disruption took roughly six months to clear, not six weeks. Applied to 2026: a lapse beginning
10-01 plausibly pushes this print past **2026-10-28**, and degrades the cadence of every subsequent
retail-sales entry this calendar carries into 2027.

**The branch is real but shrinking on a dated schedule.** Both chambers have passed a CR — Senate
amendment to H.R. 6500 (to Dec 11) **90–6 on 2026-08-08**; House H.R. 9770 (to Dec 4) **220–205 on
2026-07-21** — and the entire remaining gap is a one-week difference in expiry date, not a policy
fight. The House returned **2026-08-31** and a vote is expected in the first week of September.
Nothing is signed; that is the honest state.

### Leg 3 — this print is the scorecard on 09-16, not an independent read — SUPPORTED

[`retail-sales-2026-09-16`](retail-sales-2026-09-16.md)'s 2026-09-01 stance quantifies ~**+0.6–0.7pp**
of purely mechanical lift on the August headline off a −0.6% July base: ~+0.27pp from record pump
prices and ~+0.39pp from the un-doing of July's −2.2% nonstore drag. Neither is demand. **September is
the first month carrying neither**, for two independently verifiable reasons:

- **The Prime-Day base effect is spent.** Amazon's press center states Prime Day 2026 ran **June
  23–26** — a *primary* confirmation of the sibling doc's press-sourced "moved to June" leg. July was
  depressed by the pull-forward and August bounces back against it; September sits outside both sides
  of that comparison entirely.
- **The gasoline anomaly does not repeat, and may partly reverse.** August 2026 was the most expensive
  August at the pump on record (~$4.07 monthly average, AAA). The national average on **2026-09-01 is
  $4.0656** — flat to that average. Because the series is seasonally adjusted, what matters is not
  whether September prices fall but whether they fall by **more or less than the seasonal norm** the
  adjustment already embeds: post-Labor-Day demand decline, the mid-September winter-blend switchover,
  and October refinery maintenance. EIA's own 2026 outlook has retail gasoline down ~6% (~20¢/gal) on
  the year. Starting September flat at a record level with Brent still ~$89–90 on Hormuz risk means the
  normal decline is likely *blunted* — a small positive contribution at most, an order of magnitude
  below August's anomaly, and plausibly negative if the seasonal path reasserts.

**And the same report revises August.** The advance report revises the immediately preceding month —
verified in the July report's own text, which carries "June 2026 (revised) $768.1 billion, 0.2%*" and
states "The May 2026 to June 2026 percent change was unrevised." So 10-15 adjudicates the
price-versus-demand question twice: once in the September number, once in what August's headline
becomes on revision.

### Leg 4 — the July base carries confidence intervals the coverage ignored — SUPPORTED (new to this doc)

Quoted from `marts_current.pdf` (decompressed locally, 2026-09-01): July advance sales were **$763.6
billion, down 0.6 percent (± 0.4 percent)** from June, **up 5.0 percent (± 0.5 percent)** from July
2025, "adjusted for seasonal variation and holiday and trading-day differences, **but not for price
changes**." The report's own chart carries a footnoted asterisk on any figure whose "90 percent
confidence interval includes zero. There is insufficient statistical evidence to conclude that the
actual change is different from zero" — and in July that asterisk sits on **ex-auto (−0.3%\*)** and
**ex-auto-and-gas (−0.2%\*)**, and on **June's +0.2%\*** headline. Only the −0.6% headline itself
cleared.

The read-through is a discipline rule for 10-15, not a forecast: **a sub-CI "miss" or "beat" in the
narrower measures is not statistically distinguishable from zero**, and press coverage routinely
reports them as if it were. Read the asterisk before reading the reaction.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `confirmed`, Census primary re-fetched 2026-09-01).** Treat 2026-10-15 08:30 ET as a
high-impact known-date variance event with an unusual property: **its largest single uncertainty at
D-44 is whether it publishes.** No directional macro position, no new rate-sensitive paper entries
into the 10-15 session, and no new AAPL/AMZN consumer-adjacent exposure sized around the print. Any
open position is sized as though all three branches are live — a hot print, a soft print, and a lapse
that moves the print past the 10-28 FOMC. The base case (**estimate-labeled**: structural reasoning,
no street consensus, base month unprinted) is that a CR is signed, this prints on schedule, and it
reads as the first September in this window free of the two mechanical distortions that inflate its
predecessor — making it the cleaner demand read of the pair, and the one whose control group and
August revision jointly adjudicate the sibling doc's price-not-demand thesis. The asymmetric risk is
not a number: it is **the lapse branch**, in which the 10-28 FOMC — no SEP, no forward guidance, no
October payrolls, no October CPI — takes its last hard consumer reading from a print expected to carry
~+0.6–0.7pp of non-demand lift, with no scheduled correction before it votes.

**Kill switches:**

- **A CR signed before 2026-09-30** retires the entire existence branch — the doc's dominant variable
  at D-44 — and drops this to an ordinary high-impact macro print. That is the *good* kill and the
  most likely one; re-read the whole decision header if it fires.
- **A negative August control group on 2026-09-16** kills the scorecard framing at its source: it
  would mean demand fell far enough to swamp the nonstore normalization the control group includes,
  making 10-15 a demand read rather than an adjudication of mechanical lift.
- **Census cancelling rather than delaying the September reference month** would break leg 2's
  survey-versus-field-collection mechanism and mean this calendar should treat Census exactly like
  BLS. Registered as **FT-45** in [`forward-tests.md`](../forward-tests.md).
- **The 09-16 FOMC resolving the rate question decisively in either direction** drains this print of
  the variance the corridor framing rests on — the same kill the sibling doc carries, inherited.
- **Energy re-escalation carrying pump prices materially higher through September** would put the
  gasoline distortion back into *this* print rather than leaving it behind in August, and leg 3's
  "neither distortion" claim would need re-argument rather than patching.
- **An Amazon primary announcing fall Prime-event dates inside late September** (rather than the
  historically-observed first half of October) would move that nonstore distortion into this print's
  reference month; no such primary exists today.
