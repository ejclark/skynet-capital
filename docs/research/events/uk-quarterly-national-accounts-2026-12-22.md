## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This event reached the calendar as **one proposal and no canonical file**:
`proposals/uk-quarterly-national-accounts-2026-12-22.from-uk-quarterly-national-accounts-2026-09-30.json`,
filed 2026-09-09 by the Q2 QNA lane during its own initial research. It was read in full before anything
here was written, and the canonical
`src/domain/market-events/uk-quarterly-national-accounts-2026-12-22.json` is written in this PR. The
proposal is unusually specific about why it exists, and it is not "for completeness":

> *"the December edition is the ONLY quarter of the four that does NOT fall on a calendar quarter-end,
> which makes it the natural control for any claim that a QNA release day is eventful … proposes this
> entry so a future pulse can test the same identification out of sample."*

So this document has one job the proposal handed it and one it found for itself. The handed job: **is the
December edition's measured quiet a fact about the release, or a fact about Christmas?** The found job:
**what is actually on this morning** — because 2026-12-22 turns out not to be the solo release day the
proposal's framing implies.

**One-line verdict:** the quiet is **the release's own** — a pre-Christmas control window is not quiet at
all, so the seasonality objection is *refuted* rather than survived — but this particular December is the
**rare co-print case**, a triple ONS stack whose three historical analogues are the noisiest December
editions on record, and that is the caution the tier carries forward.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md), plus a
first-party event study against this repo's own market-data layer (`scripts/research/market-data.mjs`,
split/dividend-adjusted daily bars). Primaries fetched direct 2026-09-10, **all HTTP 200, no blocked
fetches this session**: the ONS release page for **GDP quarterly national accounts July to September
2026** and its `/data` JSON endpoint; the `/data` endpoints for **Blue Book 2026**, **Public sector

December band" hides.** **SUPPORTED.**
`ons.gov.uk/releases/gdpquarterlynationalaccountsukjulytoseptember2026` reads *"GDP quarterly national
accounts, UK: July to September 2026"*, *"Release date: 22 December 2026 7:00am"*, *"This release is not

`"cancelled": false`. Cadence, scraped across four pages of the ONS's own past-release listing (40

| Regime | Years | Rule | Dates |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-2020 | 2016–2019 | the **last Friday before Christmas** | 23 Dec 2016 (Fri), 22 Dec 2017 (Fri), 21 Dec 2018 (Fri), 20 Dec 2019 (Fri) |
| Modern | 2020–2025 | **22 December**, next working day if the 22nd is a weekend | 22 Dec 2020 (Tue), 22 Dec 2021 (Wed), 22 Dec 2022 (Thu), 22 Dec 2023 (Fri), **23** Dec 2024 (the 22nd was a Sunday), 22 Dec 2025 (Mon) |

publisher's own page is doing the work. The `estimate` label is the taxonomy gap and the no-self-confirm

### Honest limits

**First, the co-print finding rests on three observations.** n = 3, t = 1.92 on the only series where it
points strongly, and ^FTSE points the *other* way on n = 2. It is carried in the calls as a caution
because ignoring the subset that matches 2026 would be cherry-picking, not because three days establish
anything. 2026-12-22 makes four.

**Second, every market number here is a daily close-to-close move.** IGLT.L stops trading at 16:30
London, so a 07:00 release's signature is diluted by a full session of everything else, and IGLT.L is an
**all-maturity** gilt index that under-weights the long end where UK fiscal stress shows. `GBPUSD=X`
daily-close timing is not dependable and no weight is placed on its ratios.
