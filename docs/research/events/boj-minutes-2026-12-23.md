### The question, plainly

The sibling [`boj-minutes-2027-03-24`](boj-minutes-2027-03-24.md) already asked and answered the

This event has two questions of its own, and one of them was handed to it explicitly. The

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The pair rule the proposing ledger asked for is real, and it is 84/84 — SUPPORTED, measured,
   and this is the leg the event was filed to produce.** Both BoJ year-archive families were parsed
   today (85 Minutes, 86 Summaries) and joined on meeting date. For every consecutive meeting pair
   (N−1, N) where both documents exist, the gap from the Minutes of N−1 to the Summary of N:

   | Gap (days) | 2 | 3 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 10 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | Count | 3 | 26 | **45** | 5 | 3 | 1 | 1 |

   **n=84, median 5, mean 4.50, range 2-10, and every single gap is positive** — the Summary of
   meeting N has *never once* preceded the Minutes of meeting N−1 in eleven years. The modal gap is
   exactly **5 days (45/84)**, which is this corridor's own value (**12-23 → 12-28**). The mechanism
   falls straight out of the two lag laws the sibling and the SoO ledger measured separately —
   Minutes chain ~5 days after the *next* MPM, Summaries ~10 days after *their own* — but nobody had
   put them together, and the consequence is worth stating plainly: **the BoJ always publishes the
   older, five-times-longer record before the newer digest.** The SoO ledger's guess is upgraded
   from "suggests" to "84/84, no exceptions."

3. **The release window shifted in 2019, and the cause is Japan's holiday calendar rather than
   anything the BoJ did — SUPPORTED, and it is measured off the tape rather than asserted.** The
   table in leg 2 has an obvious break: **2016-2018 all published Dec 26; 2019-2025 all published

   | Dec 23 | 2014 | 2015 | 2016 | 2017 | 2018 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
