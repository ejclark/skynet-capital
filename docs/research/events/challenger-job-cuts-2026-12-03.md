## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** what will Challenger, Gray & Christmas's November 2026 job-cut report
show, how does the tape treat this release, and what does its AI-cited line say about the house
conviction that employers have moved off AI as the stated reason?

**One-line verdict:** a `low`-impact count the market has been shown to ignore in a normal data
week, worth a doc because it is the only tracked series that prices AI-cited cuts; the call is
stand aside, read the AI share, size nothing.

   last three reports (Sep 3, Oct 1, Nov 5) were each the Thursday before BLS payrolls, and the
   October PDF header reads "THURSDAY, NOVEMBER 5, 2026". Payrolls are `confirmed` for Friday

   Last year's November report came out Thursday 2025-12-04, a week before the delayed payrolls,

2. **The 2026 level is low and the y/y comparison is nearly mechanical — SUPPORTED.** October:
   **43,994**, up 2% from September's 43,281 and **down 71%** from October 2025's 153,074, the lowest
   October since 2023. YTD through October: **617,189**, **−44%** vs 1,099,500 (Table 1). The

   Jun 45,849 · Jul 33,429 · Aug 52,881 · Sep 43,281 · Oct 43,994. June–October averaged

   column), so any print under that is a y/y decline; the only months of 2026 that exceeded their

3. **Base case ~35–60k, anchored on the trailing five months — SUPPORTED as a range, thin as a
   point.** No street consensus exists (the September close-out found none; none found now). The
   range is June–October's band (33–53k) widened for November's seasonal pattern in the table:
   2025 saw 71,321 (Verizon's >13,000 plus UPS and others, per press), and the report's own
