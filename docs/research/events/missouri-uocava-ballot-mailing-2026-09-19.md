### Conviction legs, tested

1. **2026-09-19 is exactly what the statute produces — SUPPORTED, and this upgrades the sibling's
   second-hand read to a direct one.** 52 U.S.C. § 20302(a)(8), verbatim: "*transmit a validly
   requested absentee ballot to an absent uniformed services voter or overseas voter — (A) except as
   provided in subsection (g), in the case in which the request is received at least 45 days before
   an election for Federal office, not later than 45 days before the election.*" The arithmetic was
   computed rather than trusted: **2026-11-03 minus 45 days = 2026-09-19**. The sibling's proposed
   date survives its own primary-source check. **Status stays `estimate` regardless** — the
   confirmed-prefix taxonomy (`IR:`/`BLS:`/`FED:`/`TSY:`/…) has no slot for a statute, the same
   mechanical gap the [09-08 ledger](missouri-map-ballot-deadline-2026-09-08.md), the
   [midterm ledger](midterm-elections-2026-11-03.md) and the [BoJ ledger](boj-decision-2026-09-18.md)
   each recorded, and this lane may not self-confirm an event it discovered in-sweep. Understating
   the label only widens caution.

2. **The verb is "transmit," not "mail" — SUPPORTED, and it lowers the operational bar the brief
   implied.** The application characterised the duty as "*start sending absentee ballots*" and
   "*send mail ballots*." The statute says **transmit**, and the Ohio agreement confirms the
   practice: ballots go "*either electronically or by mail, according to the UOCAVA voter's
   choice.*" Electronic transmission counts. The picture of physical ballots being printed and
   irretrievably posted — the image doing the work in the sibling's mootness claim — is not what
   the statute requires.

3. **The deadline is expressly waivable, and the waiver is a live, exercised mechanism — SUPPORTED,
   and it is the single largest correction to this event's premise.** (a)(8) is subject on its face
   to "*except as provided in subsection (g)*." Subsection (g)'s enumerated undue-hardship grounds
   include "*The State has suffered a delay in generating ballots due to a legal contest*" — **the
   precise posture Missouri is in**. The chief state election official applies to the Presidential
   designee (the **Secretary of Defense**, delegated to DoD/FVAP), and where the hardship is a legal
   contest the request is submitted "*as soon as practicable*," with approval or denial due **not
   later than 5 business days** after submission. The State must supply a comprehensive plan showing
   voters still have "*time to receive, mark, and submit their ballots in time.*"

4. **But the base rate on that waiver, on this exact ground, is a DENIAL — SUPPORTED, and it is
   why leg 3 is a correction rather than an escape route.** From the Ohio MOA verbatim: Ohio's
   congressional and legislative districts were established 2022-02-24 and 2022-03-02, "*much closer
   to the May 3, 2022, primary election than expected*," with the congressional plans "*subject to
   pending litigation*." "*On February 26, 2022, the State through Secretary LaRose requested from
   the Presidential Designee for UOCAVA, the Secretary of Defense, a hardship exemption … In its
   waiver request, Ohio claimed an undue hardship due to a legal contest.*" "*On March 4, 2022, the
   Department of Defense … denied the State's application for a waiver.*" Request to denial =
   **6 calendar days = 5 business days**, the statutory clock run to its limit. **A state in
   Missouri's position that asks for this waiver should not expect to get it.**

5. **Missing the date is not self-executing, and it does not moot an election — SUPPORTED, and this
   is what refutes the "terminal date" framing.** Ohio's remedy after the denial was not compliance
   and not catastrophe: the MOA capped transmission at "*as soon as the ballot is finalized, and no
   later than April 5, 2022*" — the 45-day date for a 2022-05-03 primary was **2022-03-19**, so the
   agreed transmission ran **17 days past the statutory deadline**. Consideration flowed the other
   way: express delivery both directions at state expense, and a receipt window extended "*through
   the twentieth day after the day of the election*" (ballots executed and sent by close of polls
   05-03, received by **05-23**). In exchange "*the United States agrees to forgo litigation under
   UOCAVA as to the May 3, 2022, primary election.*" **Enforcement is discretionary and adversarial,
   not automatic**: "*The United States Attorney General is authorized to enforce the provisions of
   UOCAVA, 52 U.S.C. 20307*," in federal district court. Nothing in the statute makes 09-19 a
   jurisdictional cliff, and no provision converts a missed transmission into a settled map.

6. **The MOA anticipates Missouri's scenario almost by name — SUPPORTED, and it is the most
   useful single sentence found this session.** Term 2: should officials be unable to transmit by
   the agreed date, "*or the State … determines it is premature to do so, due to a pending
   candidate-related protest, an order of the Ohio Supreme Court or other court in relevant
   litigation, or any other reason,*" the Secretary notifies the United States and "*the parties
   shall meet and confer to discuss appropriate modification of this Agreement or other necessary
   remedial measures.*" **A state supreme court order colliding with the 45-day rule has a named,
   negotiated answer, and that answer is "meet and confer," not "the map is now locked."**

7. **The mootness premise is unsupported rather than disproved — MIXED, and stated as MIXED
   deliberately.** No authority was found this session holding that transmitted UOCAVA ballots fix
   the operative map beyond judicial reach, and legs 5–6 cut against it. A search summary of the
   **Arizona CD-8 2018** matter points the same way — the state missed the 45-day transmission for
   the 2018-02-27 special primary, and Maricopa was permitted to transmit for the April general
   "*notwithstanding the possibility that the results … may be subsequently re-canvassed and
   re-certified*," i.e. transmission proceeded with the underlying result still unsettled.
   **What is not established:** that a court *would* order re-transmission under a restored map in
   Missouri, which is a practical question no source here answers. The honest statement is that the
   sibling asserted mootness without support and this session found support running the other way —
   **not** that mootness is refuted.

8. **The respondent's side is now partially readable, and it inverts the applicant's Purcell
   argument — MIXED, and only search-summary sourced.** The sibling ledger's leg 4 recorded the
   applicant's claim that Purcell runs *toward* a stay because the August 4 primary already ran
   under the disputed map, and flagged the one-sided record as its central limit. **Rick Hasen**
   (Election Law Blog, ~2026-09-04) argues the inverse: applying Purcell to mid-decade redistricting
   "*would encourage gamesmanship*" by legislatures that time a new map to run out the clock on
   judicial review, handing mapmakers "*a free bite at the apple.*" On that reading Missouri's late
   primary under HB 1 is the gamesmanship, not the reliance interest. **This is a search summary of
   a page that Cloudflare-403'd on direct fetch** — the argument is recorded, its exact wording is
   not verified, and no probability attaches to either side.

9. **There is no market channel, and it is measured rather than assumed — SUPPORTED, and the
