What should a US-equity paper book do with the October UK consumer-confidence barometer, and what is worth knowing before it prints? **Verdict:** nothing is tradable (**no market channel**), the interesting fact is structural (the survey is in the field now and carries the policy week), and the only directional lean is a low-confidence "the climb stops" resting on one named channel, inflation.

### Method

Read the sibling ledger [`uk-consumer-confidence-2026-09-25`](uk-consumer-confidence-2026-09-25.md) and both proposals naming this id in full; re-read the September 2026 NIQ release (primary, fetched direct today) and the NIQ landing page; pulled the 2023, 2024 and 2025 October releases from NIQ primaries for fieldwork windows and September→October changes; secondary press (labelled) for the Budget backdrop; VIX from a public chart endpoint. ### Leg 2 — September's −13 was an inflation story, not a Budget story — **SUPPORTED**

### Leg 4 — price and rate pressure is real but offset by spending — **MIXED**

For: UK CPI printed **3.1%** y/y on 09-16 (from 2.9%), the BoE held at 3.75% (6–3) and replaced its annual QT review with a multi-year unwind plan (30y gilts −11–12bp on the day), and FOMC hiked in the same week — all recorded in the sibling's 09-22 row. Against: UK retail sales rose **+2.4% y/y** in August and the savings index is at +27, so households have a cushion. NIQ's own wording ties the headline to inflation, which is the one channel with a named, dated driver.

### Leg 5 — the Budget channel: speculation is live, the mechanism is not shown — **MIXED**

City AM (secondary, dated 2026-09-24) reports the Chancellor, John Healey, weighing a smaller fiscal buffer than the OBR's March £23.6bn to soften tax rises, with a bank levy, capital-gains and a lower "mansion tax" threshold among options reported. The October window therefore sits at peak speculation — the configuration of 2024. But 2026's September window sat deeper into the run-up than 2025's and showed no trace of it, so the channel is not leaned on. If it bites it should show first in *general economy, next 12 months* (**−22**).
