## Initial research

### The question
Does the 2026-11-30 INIO lock-up expiry put enough new supply on the market to be worth a position, a hedge, or a stand-aside, and on what date does it actually fire?

**Verdict: the date is firm, the potential supply is huge but gated, and the price premise is absent — stand aside.** The lock-up is one clean 180-day period with no early-release trigger, so unlike CBRS (staged) and XE (shortened by a blackout) there is no date risk beyond a discretionary waiver. The size is the one thing that stands out against the lockup study's sample (the 3.5–7.6x unlock-size bucket fell most of the three), but the study itself calls size "no dial", and the holder's Rule 144 cap plus a stock below its IPO price both argue against an immediate dump.

### Leg 1 — the date: SUPPORTED
The 424B4 says holders "restrict... transfer common shares for 180 days from the date of this prospectus" (dated 2026-06-03), and the eligible-for-sale table says "beginning 181 days after the date of this prospectus... all remaining shares will become eligible". 180 days after 06-03 is 2026-11-30 (a Monday); day 181 is Tuesday 2026-12-01, the lockup study's day 0. The clause names no earnings, price or blackout trigger; the only early path is that Goldman Sachs, J.P. Morgan and Morgan Stanley "may, in their sole discretion... release all or some portion... at any time". The Q3 10-Q (filed 2026-10-27) repeats the 180-day text with the same 2026-06-03 anchor and shows no amendment. EDGAR lists no 8-K since the IPO other than the two earnings 8-Ks (2026-07-28, 2026-10-27). A secondary search result also reads Dec 1 (an aggregator, unverified).

### Leg 2 — supply: MIXED (huge eligible stock, gated by Rule 144)
750.0M shares outstanding. The IPO sold 103.5M shares, the full 90M plus the 13.5M over-allotment (10-Q: "including the exercise in full"), all by the selling shareholder, so the Principal Shareholder, AI Alpine (Luxembourg) S.à r.l., holds **646.5M (86.2%)** and the free float is 103.5M (13.8%, ~$1.69B at $16.30). Unlock size is 6.2x the float, the study's "3.5–7.6x" bucket. In volume terms that is **~102 days** of the 20-day average (6.3M shares) or ~124 days of the 60-day average (5.2M). But eligible is not saleable: as an affiliate the holder may sell in any three months only the greater of 1% of shares (7.5M) or the four-week average weekly volume (~31.5M, my arithmetic from the 20-day average), about **5 days of volume or 4.9% of its stake**. Larger sales need a registered offering under its registration rights (demand, short-form and piggyback). The company has filed no S-1 or S-3 for a resale as of 2026-10-31 and is not S-3 eligible until about June 2027 (12 months reporting; my reading of the form's rule), so a deal would surface as an S-1 weeks ahead. The holder is a vehicle owned by Advent International and ADIA (the prospectus says co-owned by funds managed by Advent and ADIA; a secondary source puts the split at about 54/45, unverified), so a sale needs two sponsors to agree on price. Options vested at the lock-up date do not become eligible then (prospectus risk factor).

The sponsor sold 103.5M shares at $27 on 06-05 and now sits 40% under that mark; a sponsor selling a second block below its own IPO price is less likely than one selling above it, but the low is a reason to wait, not a promise. The stock's own business is strong: Q3 (reported 2026-10-27) revenue $1.17B (+57%), adjusted EBITDA $245M (+70%), order backlog $8.0B (+142%), full-year revenue guidance raised to $3.9–4.0B and adjusted EBITDA to $780–800M.
