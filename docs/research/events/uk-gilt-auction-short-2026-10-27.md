## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The Budget ledger ([`uk-autumn-budget-2026-10-28`](uk-autumn-budget-2026-10-28.md)) found the one gilt supply auction left before the Budget and filed it here. The question: **does this auction tell anyone anything — about gilt demand, about the Budget, about the US — and what would a bad one look like?** One-line verdict: **a calibrated, low-information event; watch the size notice and the cover/tail against a five-print band, and trade nothing.**

### Method

Primary sources only for the facts: the DMO's calendar notice (28 Aug 2026), its consultation agenda (17 Aug) and record (25 Aug), and its per-auction result notices (24 Jun · 16 Jul · 4 Aug · 18 Aug · 10 Sep · 22 Sep · 13 Oct · 14 Oct), fetched direct 2026-10-16 as PDFs and decoded locally from their text layer. DMO's auction-results and calendar web pages returned a bot-challenge page (HTTP 200, no content) to plain fetchers and are recorded in `blocked`, not worked around. The gilt-ETF check uses IGLT.L daily closes (Yahoo chart API, 251 sessions to 2026-10-16). Two web-search summaries mislabelled coupons (e.g. "4⅝%"); every figure below was re-read in the DMO notices themselves.

### 1. The date and the move — SUPPORTED, with the stock and size still open

The 28 Aug notice lists **Tuesday 27 October, 10.00am**, details announced **Tuesday 20 October** (3:30pm column), and no 28 October slot. The 17 Aug consultation agenda carries the footnote verbatim: *"This auction was previously scheduled for Wednesday 28 October 2026. Due to the timing of the Budget on 28 October 2026 the auction has been moved to Tuesday 27 October 2026."* Typed **short conventional gilt**. Stock and size are in neither notice. The consultation record (25 Aug) shows the market asked for two re-openings of 4% 2032 and one of 4% 2033; the 14 Oct auction was a re-opening of the new 4% 2030 and 4% 2030 is scheduled again 10 Dec. So 27 Oct is **most likely a 4% 2032 or 2033 re-opening** — an inference, not a notice (`FT-…-1`).

Two medium auctions in the window (4% 2036: 07-16 3.13x/0.1bp; 08-18 3.65x/0.1bp) sit inside the same range. **Five prints bound a range; they do not make a distribution.** The hard facts: no short auction in the sample covered below 3.0x or tailed past 0.4bp, and the weakest of the five (09-22, 3.07x/0.4bp) came right after the size rose to £4.75bn — the closest the sample comes to a miss.

### 3. Yields up, demand not — SUPPORTED, and what it is worth

The same stock cleared **+15.8bp** higher in 34 days (4% 2030: 4.789% on 09-10 → 4.947% on 10-14) and 4% 2032 **+23.2bp** in 49 days (4.615% → 4.847%). Over the same stretch the size rose 18% (£4.25bn → £5.0bn) and total bids reached **£17.07bn on 10-14**, the highest of the five. Higher yields attracted bids; this is how a clearing price is supposed to work, not evidence of stress. It also means a *level* argument ("yields are high, so the auction fails") has no support in this sample.

### 4. Does an auction day move the gilt market? — REFUTED as a premise

IGLT.L (all-maturity gilt ETF; daily σ **0.430%**, 251 sessions): mean absolute daily move **0.315%** overall and **0.323%** on the seven 2026 conventional-auction days — **1.03x**, n=7, no separation. The 09-10 launch day was the largest (-0.71%) and it printed 3.24x with a 0.3bp tail, i.e. a normal result. Two sessions in the year closed at or below -1.30% (the Budget ledger's tail trigger); neither was an auction day in this sample. Limit: the ETF is all-maturity and closes at 16:30 London, so a short-gilt move at 10:00 is diluted; a short-end series would be sharper.

### 5. Is it a leading read on the Budget? — MIXED, leaning no

The Budget ledger framed this auction as the earliest hint that the gilt-to-Treasury channel (R² 0.308) is opening. Two honest reservations. **The fiscal worry sits at the long end**, where the DMO's own 10-13 tender of 4% Treasury Gilt 2063 cleared at 5.804% on 3.98x, and a five-year-and-under gilt is the least fiscal-credibility-sensitive auction on the calendar. **And it prints before the news it is meant to lead**: the Chancellor speaks the next morning, so a clean result says the market was calm *before* the Budget, which every Budget-eve is. The first long-end read afterwards is the **11-10** long conventional auction (est, proposed with this PR), after the BoE's 11-05 decision.

**Stance (2026-10-16, D-11; date `estimate`, stock and size unannounced).** **Stand aside, permanently.** Nothing on this calendar is keyed to the DMO or to the 10-27 gilt session. **Kill switches.**

2. **The band breaks** — 10-27 cover **below 3.00x** or tail **at or above 0.5bp** (`FT-…-2`). The result goes to the Budget ledger as information, not to a position.

4. **The auction market changes character** — a short-gilt tail **≥ 1.0bp** at any DMO short auction before **2026-12-10**.
