## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **This is the print that scores the one before it.** Its sibling
[`uk-cpi-2026-12-16`](uk-cpi-2026-12-16.md) says the November headline **rises** on duty timing — the
2026 Budget moved from 26 November to 28 October, so the alcohol-and-tobacco uprating lands in the
November index instead of December — and that a reader who calls that reacceleration has it backwards.
**This print is where that claim gets adjudicated, and the arithmetic says it hands the rise straight
back.** Three legs, all from ONS series fetched today. **The base is hard on its own terms:** all-items
December 2025 m/m was **+0.430%** (D7BT 139.5 → 140.1), the **third-highest of thirteen Decembers**
against a 13-year mean of **+0.281%** — so for this annual rate to *rise*, December 2026 has to beat a
bar only **two of the other twelve** Decembers cleared. **The duty signature splits 3-for-3 in both
directions:** division 02 (D7BV) printed **negative** m/m every December the uprating had already been
taken in November (2018 **−1.067%**, 2021 **−0.816%**, 2024 **−0.200%**) and **positive** every December
it landed there instead (2017 **+0.564%**, 2023 **+1.352%**, 2025 **+1.025%**). Weighted, that unwind
is **−0.064pp**, which nets to **+0.006pp** against the sibling's **+0.070pp** November push — the round
trip a pure timing shift must produce. Add food (**−0.041pp**) and a little energy drift (**+0.010pp**)
and the attributable move is **−0.06 to −0.13pp**. **And the placement is the inverse of the sibling's.**
The BoE's own dates page (fetched) puts the **February Monetary Policy Report round on 4 February 2027**
— so this print lands **fifteen days ahead of a forecast round**, not inside a voting morning, and it is
the **only** CPI print between the December and February announcements. **None of it is ours to trade** —
`symbols: []`, and this calendar's tracked universe is eight US mega-caps. Date is **estimate** on a
taxonomy gap, not on the evidence; it widens caution and licenses nothing.

**December 2025 is the third-highest of thirteen**, 0.149pp above the mean. This is the exact mirror of

**The threshold that follows is the cleanest statement in this document.** For the December 2026 annual

The annual rate itself already shows the round trip, in the tape rather than in arithmetic (D7G7,

December index that year, lifted the annual rate 0.2pp, and was gone the month after.

**Provenance, stated rather than blurred.** Every m/m figure is ONS D7BV, fetched today. The Budget

six rows are from the general record and were **not** verified from a primary this session. **The claim
rests on the verified rows and survives on them**: three late-October Budgets, three negative Decembers;
one late-November Budget, one +1.025% December.

**The size.** Taking the mean of the three late-October-Budget Decembers (**−0.694%**) as the central
case against the **+1.025%** base, the swing is **−1.719pp** on a 2026 weight of **37.1650/1000**
(CHZS) = **−0.064pp** on the annual rate. The 2024 analogue alone gives −0.046pp; the 2018 analogue
gives −0.078pp. Call it **−0.046 to −0.078pp**.

**And here is the check that validates both ledgers at once.** The sibling computed the November push
from the same mechanism at **+0.070pp**. The two sum to **+0.006pp**. A duty *timing* shift moves which
month carries a one-off level increase; by the second print, both index levels have carried exactly one
uprating in their trailing twelve months, so the annual-rate effect **must** net to zero. It does, to
within six thousandths of a percentage point, from two independently computed terms. Neither ledger was
fitted to the other.

**One thing the table also settles, for the print after this one.** Every January in ten years printed
**+1.3% to +2.7%** in division 02, regardless of Budget timing — the annual alcohol-duty uprating and
New Year repricing, which are calendar-fixed rather than Budget-fixed. That lands in the **January**
index, published 2027-02-17, and is one of the four notches Leg 5 counts.

### Leg 4 — "Only division 02 moves." **REFUTED. Food adds a second, independently-measured give-back.**

| 2025 | +0.278% | +0.840% | +0.562pp |
| **mean** | | | **+0.456pp** |

2026's own run rate, February–July, is **+0.012%/month** — food m/m has printed −0.139, 0.000, +0.347,
+0.069, −0.138, −0.208, 0.000 across the year. Run rate plus mean seasonal gives an implied December

standard deviation of about **0.43pp** across those ten years, so a one-sigma band puts the implied

**−0.088pp** to **+0.007pp**. The central estimate is negative; the sign is not guaranteed at one sigma.
It is included because it is measured rather than assumed, not because it is confident.

### Leg 5 — "Energy or a tax notch could offset it." **REFUTED, and the reason generalises into the sharpest thing about this print.**

**December is structurally the quietest month in the UK energy series.** D7DT (electricity) printed
**0.000% m/m in December in every year from 2014 to 2023**, then +0.106% (2024) and +0.052% (2025).
D7DU (gas) printed **0.000% in every December from 2014 to 2024**, then −0.067% (2025). That is not
luck: the Ofgem cap steps on 1 January, 1 April, 1 July and 1 October, and the 2026 tape shows those

The only live term is the fixed-tariff rolling-cohort drift the sibling ledgers document (+0.43%/month
electricity, +0.51–0.58%/month gas through May–June 2026), against a near-zero Dec-2025. Weighted at
20.6065 and 10.3002 per 1000, that is about **+0.010pp** — a small *offset* to the give-back, and the
one term in this document that points the other way.

**And the electricity VAT zero-rating is neutral here, for the same reason it was neutral for the
sibling.** SI 2026/987's −0.098pp entered the level in October 2026, so it sits on **both** sides of

### Leg 6 — "It is just another monthly print." **REFUTED. It is the only CPI the February forecast round gets.**

The Bank of England's upcoming-MPC-dates page, fetched today, is titled *"Monetary Policy Committee
dates for 2026 and 2027"* and lists under the heading **"2027 provisional dates"**, verbatim:
*"Thursday 4 February — February MPC Summary and minutes and **February Monetary Policy Report**."*

Two things follow, and together they invert the sibling's placement finding:

1. **This print is the only CPI release between the two announcements.** The December 2026 committee
   announces 17 December; the February committee announces 4 February. CPI publishes 16 December
   (November data), **20 January** (December data), and 17 February (January data). Exactly one falls in
   between, and it is this one.
2. **It lands fifteen days ahead, into a forecast round rather than a voting morning.** By the BoE's
   published convention — announcement one day after the meeting ends, established in the sibling ledger
   from the July 2026 minutes' verbatim forward notice — a 4 February announcement follows a meeting
   **ending 3 February**, so the print arrives **fourteen days** before the vote is taken. Its sibling
   arrives at 07:00 **on** the morning the December meeting ends. Same series, opposite placement.

The consequence is a difference in *kind*, not degree. A datum landing on the voting morning can only be
cited; a datum landing two weeks before a **Monetary Policy Report** enters a published forecast. And
the specific question it answers is the sibling's: whether November's higher headline was duty timing
(this ledger's claim, and the sibling's) or something in the underlying. If the December rate falls back
as computed here, the attribution is confirmed with the MPR round two weeks away; if it does not, the
sibling's Leg 6 is wrong and so is this document.

### Honest limits

- **The 4 February 2027 date is the BoE's own word "provisional."** Unlike the ONS entries, whose
  `estimate` label is a pure taxonomy artifact, this one is a real publisher-stated uncertainty. The
  meeting-end date of 3 February is an additional inference from a convention, not a publication.
- **The duty rise is an assumption about a Budget that has not happened.** Both this ledger and its
  sibling depend on the 28 October 2026 Budget uprating alcohol and tobacco duty. If it defers or
  freezes them, the November push never happens, there is nothing to give back, and Leg 3's entire term
  goes to zero in both documents at once.
- **Three prints between here and there do not exist yet.** October (2026-11-18), November (2026-12-16)
  and the level either produces are all forward. **The call registered here is the direction of the
  change from November's print to this one**, never a level — deliberately, for the same reason the
  sibling gave.
- **The food term is one-sigma-ambiguous in sign** (Leg 4). It is measured, not confident, and the
  stance would survive its removal: duty alone is −0.046 to −0.078pp against a +0.010pp energy offset.
- **The seasonal-excess method is a decomposition, not a model.** It assumes December's excess over a
  year's own run rate is stable, which the ±0.43pp spread says is only roughly true. It is used because
  it is auditable from the fetched series, not because it forecasts well.
