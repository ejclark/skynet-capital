## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** does the 7-year's reputation as the coupon block's weakest-demand tenor
still hold in the 2026 cycle, and — landing after the Sep-16 FOMC, after Sep-18 OpEx, and the day
after the Sep-23 5-year that closes the coupon calendar — does its result change how the tape or our
tracked long-duration names should sit?

**One-line verdict:** the reputation is real but not currently the sharpest edge in the belly — the
**5-year**, not the 7-year, is 2026's weakest print — and this auction is a second-order signal that
closes the Sep coupon block, reads the post-Fed belly landscape, and inherits the 5-year's tone the
day before. No symbols; monitoring, not entries.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance: stand aside / watch-only.** No new position is initiated or implied by this auction. Treat
it as the closing read of the September coupon block — a conditioning input on the belly/rate side of
the long-duration-tech duration-sensitivity picture, read *after* the Sep-16 FOMC, Sep-18 OpEx, and
the fresher Aug-27 and Sep-23 prints that precede it. The 5-year (not the 7-year) is 2026's weakest
belly tenor; the 7-year is soft-not-disastrous.

**Refined 2026-09-02 (D22) — same stance, but the thesis's two legs traded places.** Nothing turns:
stand-aside and watch-only both hold, and this event still licenses no trade. Two things moved, in
opposite directions. (1) **The demand leg got weaker as a worry.** Both August belly auctions — the
data this doc's last two rows were explicitly waiting on — printed at-or-better than average:
Aug-27 7Y stopped at **4.512%** on a **0.0bp tail**, bid-to-cover **2.50x**, indirect **60.8%**;
Aug-26 5Y drew **2.37x** on a **+0.2bp tail**, indirect **61.5%** (both figures primary-sourced in
the [`Aug-27 7Y`](treasury-7y-note-2026-08-27.md) and [`Aug-26 5Y`](treasury-5y-note-2026-08-26.md)
close-outs, from the TreasuryDirect result PDF and the Fiscal Data API respectively). The "indirect
below ~57%" signal condition is therefore **stood down**, and leg 3's "5-year is 2026's weakest
tenor" claim is now the *stale* half of this doc — the 5Y's own July weakness did not extend into
August. (2) **The rate-level leg got much stronger.** Warsh's 2026-08-28 Jackson Hole keynote and
the 09-01 Hormuz escalation repriced the whole belly: 7Y constant maturity **4.52% → 4.66%**, 5Y
**4.38% → 4.55%**, 2Y **4.20% → 4.39%** between 08-27 and 09-01 (Treasury's own daily par yield
curve + Fed H.15), taking the 7Y ~**15bp through** where the August 7Y actually cleared, with
September hike odds at **~65–68%** against the ~26% this doc's own 08-26 row recorded. Leg 4's
sequencing framing survives but its content inverts: Sep-24 reads the belly after a Fed the market
expects to have *hiked*, not held. The receipt is the D22 row.

**Refined 2026-09-09 (D15) — the founding premise is refuted, and the leg that replaced it
half-retired.** Stance does not turn; stand-aside and watch-only both hold and this event still
licenses no trade. Three things moved.

(1) **The demand thesis is now affirmatively refuted, not merely "stood down."** D22 stood down one
signal condition on two prints. Today's pulse pulled the **full trailing-12 distribution** from the
Fiscal Data primary and resolved the denominator question initial research left open (*"re-verify
bidder shares against fiscaldata.treasury.gov before citing exact figures"*): bidder shares are
computed on **competitive accepted** — indirect + direct + dealer — **not** on `total_accepted`,
which includes SOMA add-ons and understates every share by ~7pp. On the correct denominator the
secondary tracker this doc cited (helious.io) reconciles **exactly** (Jun-25 57.6%, Jul-28 70.1%,
Aug-27 60.8%), and the twelve 2025-09→2026-08 auctions read: **bid-to-cover 2.40–2.52** (median
2.495), **indirect 56.4–78.4%** (median ~60.9%), **dealer take-down 9.3–13.1%**, all at **$44B**.
Dealers have never once been forced past 13.1%. Leg 2's "soft-not-disastrous" and leg 3's "the 5Y
is the weak tenor" were both built on two press-cited prints; against twelve, **neither the 7Y nor
the 5Y shows a weak-demand series at all** — the 5Y's own 12 run 2.28–2.41 bid-to-cover on
59.2–74.9% indirect, a tight band around a lower mean, which is a *structurally* lower cover ratio
for a larger auction, not softness. The "weakest tenor" framing was a press characterization this
doc inherited and never tested against a distribution. It is now tested, and it fails.

(2) **The rate-level leg survives on price but loses its Fed.** The concession is intact and
extended — 7Y constant maturity **4.66% (09-01) → 4.63 (09-03) → 4.65 (09-04) → 4.68% (09-08)**,
~**17bp through** August's 4.512% stop (Treasury daily par yield curve, fetched today) — and it
*held through* both a cycle-record payroll surprise and a 21-point round trip in hike odds, which
says the repricing is fully in the price. But what it prices against changed: September hike odds
ran **70.2% (09-02) → 49.4% (09-03, Waller) → 58% (09-04, payrolls) → 58% (09-07) → ~54–56%
(09-08)** against the **~65–68%** this doc's D22 row recorded. D22's framing — "a Fed the market
expects to have already tightened" — is no longer accurate; Sep-24 reads a belly after a **coin
flip** the FOMC resolves on 09-16, and the deciding input is **CPI on 09-11**, which Waller named
explicitly on 09-03. The Cleveland Fed's 09-08 re-run has August core at **+0.20% m/m / 2.38% y/y**.

(3) **The oil channel is real but mistimed, and D22's own amendment needs the correction.** Brent
ran **$89.70 (08-27) → $99.50 (09-09)**, +10.9% to a cycle high, *through* an OPEC+ quota hold
(09-06, primary) and *through* Iran/Oman reporting a Hormuz shipping deal "days away." D22 amended
the standing read to make Hormuz a belly driver via oil → inflation → Fed path; that is
directionally right and it is why the belly held its concession. But the same Cleveland nowcast
assigns the surge to **September's** CPI, published **2026-10-14** — after the 09-16 FOMC and after
this auction. So the oil leg is a **Q4** belly story that this print will not see, and it is the
second thing cutting against the hawkish urgency D22 imported. The receipt for all three is the
D15 row.

**Kill switches (what would change this stance):**
- A markedly weak 7Y print (bid-to-cover well under ~2.40, tail beyond ~1.5bp, indirect materially
  below the ~57% floor of the last two prints) **landing into a soft rate tape / hawkish-FOMC
  aftermath** — escalates from "context" to a compounding risk-off factor worth naming in the
  long-duration-tech stance, not just this doc.
- A clean 7Y print (bid-to-cover back near/above the ~2.50 recent band, tail near zero) removes the
  belly-of-curve concern for this cycle and would be noted at the next pulse.
- If the Aug-27 7-year (the nearer print) breaks decisively in either direction, the belly read shifts
  and should be logged at the next pulse.
- **The concession is not enough (added 2026-09-02)** — a markedly weak 09-24 print landing *despite*
  a belly already ~15bp cheaper than August's stop would say price is not the binding constraint on
  belly demand, which is a different and more serious finding than the demand-softness story this
  doc was originally built on. Its mirror: a clean print on that concession retires the demand
  question for this cycle outright.
- **Supply, not demand, becomes the story (added 2026-09-02)** — the **09-17** announcement raising
  the 7Y above its recent ~$44B would be the first size change of the year, and would land on a
  belly that has already repriced 15–19bp. Size is knowable a week before the auction; nothing else
  about the print is.
- **Recalibrated 2026-09-09** — the "bid-to-cover well under ~2.40" threshold the two switches above
  inherit sits on the trailing-12 **floor**, so as written it fires only on a two-year record. Read
  it as tiered instead: **< 2.43** = worse than 11 of 12; **< 2.40** = worse than all 20 since Jan
  2025; **dealer take-down > 13.1%** = above every one of the last 12, and the cleaner tell. The
  switches themselves are unchanged in substance — only the bar is stated honestly.
- **Supply base rate, established 2026-09-09** — the "supply, not demand" switch above called a
  09-17 size increase "the first size change of the year." The primary says worse for that branch:
  it would be the first in **20 months** (every 7Y auction $44B since 2025-01-28, Fiscal Data), and
  it would have to break the 2026-08-05 refunding statement's standing commitment. The switch keeps
  its place as a falsifier; its prior is much lower than D22 implied. Registered as
  `FT-treasury-7y-note-2026-09-24-1`, scoring **2026-09-17**.
- **A calendar gap, logged not filed (2026-09-09)** — Treasury's **5Y-to-7Y** liquidity-support
  buyback bucket, the one that literally spans this tenor, ran **2026-05-21** ($0.30B accepted /
  $4B cap) and **2026-08-25** ($1.19B accepted from $8.40B offered), a roughly quarterly cadence
  putting the next in the late-November window. The calendar tracks 7Y-10Y, 10Y-20Y, 20Y-30Y,
  2Y-3Y, 1Mo-2Y and TIPS buybacks and carries **no 5Y-7Y or 3Y-5Y entry at all**. No forward
  schedule is retrievable from the feeds this lane can reach — Fiscal Data's `buybacks_operations`
  dataset carries no rows past today, and two TreasuryDirect buyback paths tried today both 404 —

  undated finding is never filed as an `estimate` event.
- No forward test was registered before 2026-09-09 — a Treasury auction *result* is not a
  directional equity prediction of the kind that ledger scores. The one now registered is about the
  **announcement**, not the result: a size, knowable from a primary on a known date, with a
