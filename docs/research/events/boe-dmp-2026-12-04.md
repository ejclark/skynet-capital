## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Does the 4 December Decision Maker Panel tell us anything that changes what we do? **No — structurally, as for its two siblings.** The useful question is narrower: *what will the MPC read in it thirteen days before a second-hike vote, and what number would make that reading unusual?*

**Verdict (estimate-dated):** a reading assignment, not a trade. The headline cells are three-month averages over a rotating third of the panel and move 0.1pp at a time. October showed the cells that *can* move are the one-year CPI expectation and the price-raising-intent share, which are not headline cells.

### Conviction legs, tested

**Leg 1 — The date is real: SUPPORTED.** Two Bank pages agree: the November page ("published on 4 December 2026") and the October release's own next-release line. Still `estimate` on the no-self-confirm limit.

**Leg 2 — It is the last firm-expectations read before the vote: SUPPORTED.** 4 December precedes 12-16/12-17 by 12–13 days. The Bank has published the schedule one release ahead and broke its monthly cadence once in 2026 (July data 24 July, then 4 September), so a later date change is possible but none is signalled.

**Leg 3 — The headline cannot surprise: SUPPORTED.** Own-price expectations ran 3.9 → 3.8 → 3.7 → 3.8% across the last four releases, 3y CPI expectations 2.8% for four, wage expectations 3.4% for four. Moving a three-month-average headline 0.3pp needs the one new month to swing ~0.9pp.

**Leg 4 — The cells that can move are not the registered headline: MIXED.** October's close-out found price-raising intent +11pp to **68%** and one-year CPI expectations +0.2pp to **3.3%** under flat headlines. Since then UK October CPI printed **3.6%** (up from 3.5% in September; fuel the largest upward contributor, record diesel, per ONS as read in that close-out). That leans November's one-year CPI expectation level-to-up, so FT-3 takes that cell.

**Leg 5 — A surprise matters more now than in October: MIXED.** The committee hiked 8–1 on 11-05 and Bailey and Lombardelli both leave a further rise open (press-relayed, per the 12-17 ledger). A hawkish break (3y ≥3.0%, own-price ≥4.1%) is the survey's first real evidence for a second hike; a dovish one would not move the strip much because that vote rests on services CPI and energy.
