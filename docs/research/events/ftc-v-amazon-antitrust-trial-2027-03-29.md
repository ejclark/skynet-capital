## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **A trial *start* is not a decision, and the tape says so.** The closest comparable —
the US v. Google search trial opening on **2023-09-12** — moved GOOGL **−1.15%** against QQQ
**−1.11%**: an excess of **−0.04%**, indistinguishable from nothing. What actually repriced Google
was the **remedy** day (**2025-09-03**, GOOGL **+9.14%** vs QQQ +0.79% = **+8.35% excess**, ~$234B
of market cap). This case cannot produce a remedy anywhere near 2027-03-29, because on
**2024-09-30** Judge Chun granted the FTC's motion to **bifurcate**: liability is tried first,
remedies in a separate later proceeding. Google's own liability→remedy gap was **~13 months**
(2024-08-05 → 2025-09-02), so even a fast path here puts an AMZN remedy in **2028–2029** and
appeals past that. Layer on a date that has already reset **twice** (Oct 2026 → 2027-02-09 →
2027-03-29), is `estimate`/press-sourced, and sits **211 days** out, and there is nothing to do on
any horizon. **Stand aside on the date; the real thing to watch is undated** — Chun's pending
ruling on Amazon's summary-judgment and Daubert motions, which can narrow the case months before
anyone walks into a courtroom. Two honest counterweights kept on the record: the **stake** is not
small (the challenged conduct sits on ~**$46.8B** of Q2-2026 third-party seller services plus
~**$17.2B**/quarter of advertising), and the base rate is thin but one-directional — the FTC lost
Meta outright (**2025-11-18**) and the DOJ won Google on liability yet got **behavioral-only**
relief, so recent big-tech §2 litigation has produced **zero** structural remedies.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The [AMZN Oct-print ledger](amzn-2026-10-29-print.md)'s 2026-08-30 adjacency sweep found this trial
and filed it as this calendar's first dated checkpoint for Amazon's largest structural legal
overhang. So: **is the date real, does a monopolization trial *opening* actually reprice the
defendant, and is there anything between now and 2027-03-29 that this book should be positioned
for?**

**One-line verdict.** The date is real but soft and press-sourced; a trial opening is a measured
non-event (**−0.04%** excess in the closest comparable); and bifurcation pushes every decision that
*has* historically repriced a defendant into **2028–2029** — so this is a watch item with an
undated near-term catalyst (the pending summary-judgment ruling), not a position.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md) (sector
kind). Primary docket access was attempted first and **failed** — CourtListener's docket page

### Legs tested

1. **"The 2027-03-29 date is real."** **SUPPORTED, and it stays `estimate`.** MLex ("US
   FTC-Amazon antitrust trial pushed back to March 2027," published 2025-12-15) reports a
   scheduling order signed by Judge Chun: **bench trial 2027-03-29**, **trial briefs 2027-03-22**,
   **pretrial conference 2027-03-15**, with the slip attributed to the late-2025 government
   shutdown's effect on case schedules. Two independent reasons it cannot be promoted. First,
   **no primary document was fetched** — every route to the docket refused this session. Second,
   and decisively, **promotion is mechanically blocked even with a perfect source**:
   `market-events-data.ts`'s prefix table has **no `confirmed` prefix for a federal court**
   (`IR:`/`CAL:`/`BLS:`/`FED:`/`PJM:`/`SEC:`/`TSY:`/`OCC:`/`BEA:`/`CENSUS:`/`ISM:`/`CB:` — none
   fits a district-court docket entry). Same schema gap the
   [Georgia PSC ledger](georgia-psc-data-center-cost-shift-2026-12-31.md) hit for a state utility
   commission and the [Georgia runoff ledger](georgia-senate-runoff-2026-12-01.md) hit for a state
   election authority — **third instance**, noted as a proposal, not made.
2. **"The date is firm."** **REFUTED.** Three settings on the record: **October 2026** (set 2024),
   → **2027-02-09** (amended order), → **2027-03-29**. That is a soft date by revealed behavior,
   and it is the correct reading of the entry's `estimate` status independent of sourcing. A
   fourth reset is the base case a later session should be least surprised by.
3. **"A monopolization trial *opening* reprices the defendant."** **REFUTED — measured.** The
   cleanest comparable is the US v. Google search trial, opening **2023-09-12**: GOOGL **−1.15%**
   against QQQ **−1.11%** — **excess −0.04%**, noise. The second comparable is dirtier: FTC v. Meta
   opened **2025-04-14** with META **−2.22%** vs QQQ **+0.68%** (**−2.90% excess**) — but that
   session sits inside the April-2025 tariff week, where QQQ ran +1.84% / +0.68% / +0.11% / −3.02%
   on consecutive days. **Confound named, not resolved:** one clean reading of ~0% and one dirty
   reading of −2.9% is not enough to claim trial openings are *always* nothing; it is enough to
   refuse to pay for one 211 days early.
4. **"The decision days are where the money is."** **SUPPORTED — measured, and the effect is
   large.** Google's **remedies** ruling: **2025-09-03**, GOOGL **+9.14%** vs QQQ **+0.79%** =
   **+8.35% excess**, press-quantified at ~**$234B** of market cap added (AAPL +$130B the same
   day), on Judge Mehta declining to force a Chrome divestiture. The liability ruling
   (**2024-08-05**, GOOGL −4.45% vs QQQ −2.98% = **−1.47% excess**) is a much weaker data point —
   it landed on the yen-carry crash session, and the excess is small enough that the day's macro
   plausibly owns most of it. The shape holds: **the remedy, not the verdict and not the calendar,
   is what the market pays for.**
5. **"A decision can land near 2027-03-29."** **REFUTED — the load-bearing structural fact.** On
   **2024-09-30**, ruling on Amazon's motion to dismiss, Chun also granted the plaintiffs' motion
   to **bifurcate**: the court will hold **separate proceedings on liability and remedies**
   (TechPolicy.Press's case tracker; the FTC's own filing is captioned "Order on D's MTD & Pls'
   Mtn to Bifurcate"). So the March 2027 proceeding decides **liability only** — no injunction, no
   structural relief, nothing with a P&L. Applying Google's own clock (liability **2024-08-05** →
   remedies **2025-09-02** → final judgment **2025-12-05**, ~**16 months** start to finish) to a
   trial that *begins* in March 2027 puts an AMZN liability finding in late 2027 at the earliest, a
   remedy in **2028–2029**, and appellate exhaustion well past that. The `medium` impact tier the
   entry was filed with is correct, and this leg is why.
6. **"The exposure is small, which is why this isn't a trade."** **REFUTED as framed — and this
   correction matters.** The challenged conduct (anti-discounting measures, conditioning Prime
   eligibility on Amazon's own fulfilment, ad load displacing organic results, seller fees) sits
   directly on Amazon's highest-margin revenue: **third-party seller services ~$46.8B in Q2 2026**
   alone (vs $40.3B in Q2 2025), and **advertising ~$17.2B in Q1 2026** (+24% y/y, ~$68.6B for
   FY2025). Those figures are aggregator-sourced (MarketplacePulse / trade press) and used for
   *scale only*, not for any calculation. The reason to stand aside is **timing and probability**,
   never size — saying otherwise would be the kind of flourish
   [`CLAUDE.md`](../../../CLAUDE.md)'s domain-accuracy rule forbids.
7. **"The base rate favors Amazon."** **SUPPORTED, weakly — a prior on the tail, not a forecast.**
   Two recent big-tech §2 outcomes, both terminal: the FTC **lost Meta outright** on
   **2025-11-18** (Judge Boasberg, after a six-week bench trial begun 2025-04-14, held the FTC
   failed to prove a current personal-social-networking monopoly given TikTok/YouTube competition;
   FTC noticed an appeal in January 2026), and the DOJ **won Google on liability** but obtained
   **behavioral-only** remedies — data sharing and a ban on exclusive preload contracts, no Chrome
   divestiture. **Zero structural relief in two.** n=2 is a prior, not a probability, and the
   Amazon case differs materially (conduct-based §2 + FTC Act §5(a) rather than retrospective
   merger challenge). What it *does* justify is refusing to carry a breakup scenario as the
   modal case.
8. **"An outright win is worth a lot to the defendant."** **REFUTED — measured, and the most
   useful number here.** Meta won its case *completely* on **2025-11-18** and closed **−0.72%**
   against QQQ **−1.22%**: **+0.50% excess**. Total vindication in a case seeking divestiture of
   Instagram and WhatsApp bought **half a percent**, because the market had already priced the FTC
   as likely to lose. Read together with leg 4, the asymmetry is specific: the market pays big for
   a **surprise** (Google escaping a divestiture it was widely expected to suffer) and pays
   ~nothing for a **confirmed** expectation. Any future AMZN thesis here has to argue about
   *positioning*, not about the merits.
9. **"AMZN's own history with this case shows a repricing channel."** **MIXED — and it decays.**
   The complaint filing itself was the largest move: **2023-09-26**, AMZN **−4.03%** vs QQQ
   **−1.50%** = **−2.53% excess**. Every subsequent case event faded toward noise — the MTD ruling
   (**2024-09-30 / 10-01**: −0.87% vs QQQ +0.27%, then −0.64% vs −1.39%, i.e. roughly −1.1% and
   *positive* excess on the unseal day) and the **$2.5B ROSCA settlement** (**2025-09-25**, AMZN
   −0.94% vs QQQ −0.43% = **−0.51% excess**) both landed inside ordinary noise. A $2.5B cash
   settlement moving AMZN half a percent is the clearest available calibration of how little this
   book's largest name cares about this litigation on any given day. *Data caveat:* the Yahoo
   series returned an identical 125.98 close for 2023-09-27 and 09-28, an obvious stale-print
   artifact — the 09-26 reaction day is used, the two days after it are not.
10. **"The Prime/ROSCA case is the same case."** **REFUTED — a live conflation trap.** Searches for
    "FTC v. Amazon summary judgment" repeatedly surface **FTC v. Amazon.com (ROSCA)**,
    No. **2:23-cv-0932-JHC** — the Prime enrolment/cancellation "dark patterns" suit, same judge,
    different case, **jury** trial, which Amazon partly lost at summary judgment days before trial
    and then settled for **$2.5B on 2025-09-25**. The monopolization case is
    No. **2:23-cv-01495-JHC**, a **bench** trial. Recorded explicitly so a later session does not
    import a ROSCA docket event into this ledger.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (estimate — no date-keyed action licensed).** **Watch-only, no position, and no impact
tier change.** Base case: the summary-judgment and Daubert rulings narrow the case somewhat at an
undated moment; the trial opens on or near 2027-03-29 (with a fourth slip a live possibility) and
passes with **no measurable AMZN excess return**; a liability ruling lands months later; and the
only day with genuine repricing potential — a **remedies** decision — arrives in **2028–2029**, at
which point the Google template says the move is large and its **sign depends on positioning, not
on the merits**. The value of tracking this event is not the 2027 date. It is that AMZN's largest
structural legal overhang now has a docket number, a bifurcation structure, a measured reaction
function, and a named near-term catalyst that no calendar carries.

**Kill switches.**

- **The "trial start is a non-event" read dies** if AMZN's close-to-close excess vs QQQ on the
  first trial session exceeds **±2%** — registered as **FT-23** and scored at close-out.
- **The "no decision before 2029" read dies** if the court **un-bifurcates**, consolidates remedies
  into the liability trial, or sets a remedies-phase date — any of those makes 2027-03-29 the
  opening of a proceeding that can actually produce relief, and warrants re-tiering the event above
  `medium`.
- **The whole event goes quiet** if the FTC and Amazon settle or the case is disposed of on summary
  judgment. Precedent exists on both sides of that: the ROSCA case settled for **$2.5B** on
  **2025-09-25**, while Ferguson's FTC has publicly pressed for faster, not softer, monopolization
  enforcement. If it happens, close this entry out rather than re-dating it.
- **The tracked date is wrong** the moment a fourth scheduling order publishes a different date —
  re-date the entry, do not re-research around it (**FT-24** scores whether the date holds).
- **The sourcing upgrades** if any session fetches the actual scheduling order — though promotion to
  `confirmed` still needs a federal-court prefix that `market-events-data.ts` does not define, which
  is out of this lane's scope and is noted, not made.
