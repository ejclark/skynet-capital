## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The House votes today on H.R. 9340, the bill that would push data centers to pay the full
incremental cost of the grid built to serve them. Is there any channel by which that vote reaches a
paper book holding NVDA / MRVL / AVGO / CRWV / MSFT / GOOG / META / AAPL / AMZN and the AI-energy
sleeve — and is the IPP drawdown of the last week that channel showing up in prices?

**One-line verdict:** no, on three independent counts, any one of which alone would be enough —
the bill is procedurally inert, its *sign* is the opposite of the intuitive read, and the tape's
move does not clear the sleeve's own noise floor once beta and rates are taken out.

### Leg 3 — "the bill is bearish the AI/data-center complex" → **REFUTED.** The sign is backwards.

What the bill actually does: it amends **PURPA §111(d)** to require state regulatory authorities and
non-regulated utilities to **consider establishing** a "large-load standard" — that rates charged to
non-residential customers with **100 MW+** peak demand recover "the full, incremental cost of any
generation, transmission, or distribution upgrade" built to serve them, plus financial assurances.

Three things follow, and all of them cut against the bearish read:

- **"Consider" is the whole operative verb.** Ari Peskoe (Harvard Law) calls the provision
  **"largely symbolic"**: there is no enforcement mechanism, Congress cannot compel state compliance
  under current Supreme Court jurisprudence, and states may "reject the ratemaking standards or
  adopt them in a way that deviates from what Congress may have intended." No appropriation, no
  federal preemption, no dated obligation on anyone.
- **The hyperscalers are *for* it.** Microsoft calls it an "important step to help ensure American
  families are protected"; Google backs specialized large-load tariffs. The reported logic is
  political cover — retail power bills rise regardless, public attitude toward data centers is
  souring fast, and the developers would rather be able to point at a federal standard.
- **The one objection on the record is a scope complaint.** The **Data Center Coalition** (Josh
  Levi, 2026-07-22) said it supported the *original* text and objects that E&C's rework "narrow[ed]
  its scope to single out the data center industry, leaving everyday Americans vulnerable to the
  costs associated with large load growth from other industries." That is an argument for a *wider*
  bill, not a protest against paying.

There is also no statutory path in sight. Husted's Senate companion (**S. 5028**) exists, but Senate
ENR has not prioritized data-center energy legislation and Chair Mike Lee has it only "coming up in
some of our conversations about permitting reform." A House-passed messaging bill with no Senate
vehicle changes no rate case, no capacity auction, and no IPP's contracted revenue.

**The cumulative move is not significant** — −1.61σ over four sessions is an ordinary week for a

- **The whole utility complex fell, not just the IPPs.** **XLU −3.48%** since 09-09; AEP −1.16%,

  that hits beneficiary and target identically is not a policy channel.

  NRG −28.75% YTD on two straight integration misses, CEG −19.4% YTD; the sleeve has been
  compressing on softening ERCOT forward curves since well before H.R. 9340 was introduced

**No attribution.** The 09-14 outlier is recorded as an unexplained single-session move, not as this
bill being priced.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stand aside on the vote, and treat the vote's outcome as a record rather than a signal** —
`estimate`-dated, `symbols: []`, no house playbook with a policy trigger, and a federal "shall
consider" that reaches no tracked name's cash flow. **This stance would be killed by** the IPP
basket's beta-adjusted excess on the **2026-09-15** session closing outside **±4.00pp**, which
would say the channel is being priced and that "informational, not directional" needs rewriting
rather than adjusting.

**Do not carry the AI-backlash trade off this bill in either direction** — it is backed by Microsoft
and Google and objected to by the Data Center Coalition *for being too narrow*. **This would be
killed by** a hyperscaler or the Data Center Coalition publicly opposing final passage on cost
grounds on or before **2026-09-16**.

**Treat the statutory path as closed for this quarter.** **This would be killed by** the Senate
scheduling S. 5028 or H.R. 9340 for markup or floor time on a dated notice before **2026-10-15**.
