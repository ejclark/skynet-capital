### Legs tested

1. **"There is a real PSC proceeding on data-center cost allocation."** **SUPPORTED, and upgraded.**
   `psc.ga.gov`'s docket page returns **Docket 57171 — "RTP Revenue Credit and Allocation
   Methodology," industry Electric, status Open, opened 2026-06-02**. The commission calendar lists
   three hearings on it: **2026-09-01, 2026-10-15 and 2026-11-12, each 9:35 a.m.** The calendar
   entry's own `source` string says no docket number was captured and PSC filings were not fetched
   — both are now false, and the entry is updated in this PR.
2. **"Findings are due 2026-12-31."** **MIXED — stays `estimate`.** Multiple independent outlets
   state it plainly (WABE; The Current, 2026-07-08; Environment+Energy Leader; GovTech, 2026-07-10)
   and one adds the procedural spine — staff data requests issued **2026-07-09**, initial responses
   due **2026-08-10**, review targeted for completion by year-end. But no PSC scheduling order
   stating 12-31 was fetched: the docket page listed **no document filings**, so the deadline rests
   entirely on press. Separately, promotion is blocked *mechanically* even with a perfect source —
   `market-events-data.ts` has **no `confirmed` prefix for a state utility commission** (same gap
   the runoff ledger hit for a state election authority). Noted as a proposal, not made.
3. **"The PSC voted on 2026-07-08."** **MIXED — likely off by one day.** Several outlets date the
   unanimous vote **2026-07-07**; Rough Draft Atlanta's account is internally inconsistent, saying
   "Tuesday, July 8, 2026" when **July 7 is the Tuesday** (July 8 is a Wednesday) — and Tuesday is
   the PSC's Administrative Session day. Publication datelines cluster on 07-08, consistent with
   next-day coverage of a 07-07 session. Not load-bearing for anything (the tracked date is 12-31),
   but the entry's `source` prose is reworded to stop asserting a date the evidence undercuts.
4. **"~$1B is at stake, so this is a big number."** **REFUTED as framed.** The figure is
   intervenor-side testimony that roughly **$1B in fuel-related costs** (firm pipeline
   transportation, hedging, related) is borne by residential/small-business classes rather than
   Real-Time-Pricing customers, with PSC staff estimating the current structure could raise other
   customers' fuel cost **~5–11% per month by 2028** (Commissioner Hubbard separately cites ~$118M
   of firm gas transportation at issue in 2027). Every one of those is a **reallocation between
   rate classes**, not a new cost and not a change in Georgia Power's revenue requirement — base
   rates are frozen through at least 2028 (PSC order, 2025-07-01), and the freeze explicitly does
   not cover pass-through fuel, which is exactly the bucket in dispute. Utility-level P&L impact:
   approximately none. The 5–11% is a *staff estimate in a filing*, not an adjudicated finding —
   the hearings are where it gets tested.
5. **"It reaches the hyperscalers this book holds."** **REFUTED.** Even taking the full $1B and
   assigning all of it to data centers — the maximal reading nobody has argued for — it is spread
   across Georgia Power's large-load class in one state, against combined MSFT/GOOG/META/AMZN
   capital spending running in the hundreds of billions annually. There is no plausible arithmetic
   in which this is an earnings event for any name held here. The transmission channel is
   **precedent**: Georgia is a template state (the PSC certified **9,985 MW** of new generation on
   **2025-12-19**, ~80% expected to serve data centers), and a finding that RTP customers must
   carry firm-transport and hedging costs would be cited in NY/PA/TX/OH/VA proceedings. Real, slow,
   and undated — which is why it is a watch item and not a position.
6. **"Who rules on this is itself in play."** **SUPPORTED — the load-bearing structural fact.**
   Two of five PSC seats are on the **2026-11-03** ballot: **District 3, Peter Hubbard (D,
   incumbent) vs Fitz Johnson (R)** — a rematch of the 2025 special election Hubbard won — and
   **District 5, Shelia Edwards (D) vs Josh Tolbert (R)**. The commission is **3R–2D**; two D wins
   make it **3D–2R**, and Hubbard is the commissioner who pushed this investigation hardest.
   Winners of the 2025 special elections took office in **January 2026**, so 2026 winners seat in
   **January 2027**. Consequence: **the 12-31 findings arrive at the outgoing commission; any
   remedy is written by the incoming one.** That is why the "This quarter" call is watch, not act
   — the enforceable output of this docket most likely exists in 2027, outside every horizon here.
7. **"A Libertarian on the District 5 ballot could force a 2026-12-01 PSC runoff."** **REFUTED —
   and the stale source is worth naming.** Ballotpedia lists **Thomas Blooming (L)** in District 5;
   that listing reflects *qualifying* (March 2026), not certification — The Current's own March and
   May coverage says Blooming would appear "provided he collects enough signatures." He did not:
   the Libertarian Party of Georgia needed **72,679** signatures, submitted a few hundred, and
   **2026 is the first year since 1986 that only Republicans and Democrats appear on Georgia's
   statewide ballot** (AJC/WABE, 2026-07-10/11; ballot-access.org, 2026-07-10). District 5 is a
   two-way, somebody clears 50%, and there is **no Georgia runoff branch** — independently
   confirming the [runoff ledger](georgia-senate-runoff-2026-12-01.md)'s conclusion by a second
   route. The mechanism is worth recording for its irony: the LP lost automatic access on 2024 vote
   share partly **because the PSC races were pulled from the 2024 ballot during Voting Rights Act
   litigation** — the same office now deciding this docket.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (estimate — no date-keyed action licensed).** Watch-only, no position, no play proposed,
and explicitly **no impact tier upgrade** despite better sourcing: the sourcing improved, the
economics did not. Base case — the docket runs its three hearings, produces a findings report at or
near year-end that recommends some reallocation of firm-transport/hedging costs to RTP customers,
and the commission seated in **January 2027** decides what, if anything, becomes an order. Nothing
in that chain reaches a name this book holds; the reason to keep tracking it is that Georgia is the
first state where the data-center cost-shift argument has a docket, a hearing calendar and a
regulator election in the same quarter — the precedent channel the
[midterm ledger](midterm-elections-2026-11-03.md) flagged, made concrete.

**Kill switches.**

- **The "no earnings channel" read dies** if any PSC filing or Georgia Power response proposes a
  *specific* RTP surcharge large enough to change large-load siting economics in Georgia — watch
  the **09-01** and **10-15** hearings for a number attached to a date.
- **The "report, not an order" read dies** if the PSC issues an actual order on or before
  **2026-12-31** rather than accepting findings — that would put an enforceable precedent in place
  a full year earlier than this stance assumes, and warrants re-tiering the event.
- **The date leg upgrades** the moment a PSC scheduling order stating 12-31 is fetched — though
  promotion to `confirmed` also needs a schema change (`market-events-data.ts` has no state-utility-
  commission prefix), which is out of this lane's scope and is noted, not made.
- **The 12-31 date is wrong** if a fetched order names a different completion date; re-date the
  entry rather than re-researching around it.
- **The whole event goes quiet** if the docket is consolidated into the next fuel-cost-recovery
  case or the 2025 rate-freeze stipulation is reopened — either would move the venue and make this
  entry track the wrong proceeding.
