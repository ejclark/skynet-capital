## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** what will the November 2026 PPI show, and how will the market react — given
that it prints at 08:30 ET on Dec 15, *after* the Dec 8–9 FOMC and the Dec 10 CPI, and eight days
before the Dec 23 PCE?

**One-line verdict:** a medium-impact wholesale print whose September sibling's framing does not
transfer — it cannot set up a decision already made, so its only live transmission channels are the
**12-23 core PCE nowcast** and the **January-2027 rate path**, both slow; the honest house answer is
guard-shaped, and the two things actually worth watching between now and then are a funding lapse
(which can delete the print) and the base arithmetic (which makes the 12-month rate sticky-to-rising).

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md)
(macro-print mode: no price instruments exist for it). Primary sources fetched **direct** with a
UA header this session — bls.gov's PPI release schedule, its December-2026 monthly schedule, its
July-2026 PPI news release (Table A in full), its 2025/2026 lapse revised-dates page, and
federalreserve.gov's FOMC calendar. All four BLS pages 403 to a plain fetcher; that is a tooling
artifact, not a source problem, and it is why the sibling [`cpi-2026-12-10`](cpi-2026-12-10.md) pulse
had to fall back to the OMB PFEI schedule on 08-31. Press used only for reaction narration and for
market pricing, each claim dated. Regime context inherited from the sibling ledgers, re-checked.

### Leg 2 — the role inverts versus the September print. SUPPORTED (structural, confirmed dates).

[`ppi-2026-09-10`](ppi-2026-09-10.md) is a *setup* print: PPI 9/10 → CPI 9/11, so the wholesale read
pre-loads CPI-day positioning and the tradeable hazard is the 24-hour whipsaw. December reverses the
order — jobs **12-04**, FOMC + dot plot **12-09**, CPI **12-10**, then this print **12-15**. Nothing it
says can move a decision taken six days earlier, and the FED calendar (fetched today) puts the next
FOMC at **January 26–27, 2027** — 43 days after this print, with no scheduled meeting in between. So
the September doc's "morning-before-CPI tell" framing does not transfer and should not be copied into
this one. What survives is narrower: PPI 12-15 is a *scoring* print for the corridor's verdict and an
*input* to the two things that come after it.

### Leg 3 — the live channel is PCE, not CPI. SUPPORTED (mechanism), unquantified.

Several PPI lines are transcribed into the PCE deflator rather than merely correlating with it —
health-care services (physician and hospital fees), financial services including **portfolio
management**, and airfares. That gives this print a direct read on the **12-23 Personal Income and
Outlays** release eight days later. The July cycle is the clean worked example: PPI's core-ex-trade
+0.4% was led by portfolio management **+6.5%** (BLS primary, 08-13), and the 08-26 July-data core PCE
came in at **3.3%** with headline PCE **3.7%**, 0.1pp *above* consensus — a hot headline surprise
(CNBC, 08-26; scored in the sibling [`pce-2026-08-26`](pce-2026-08-26.md) and re-cited in the
[`cpi-2026-12-10`](cpi-2026-12-10.md) 08-31 row). This channel is why the print is worth tracking at
all in a policy vacuum, and it is a *methodological* mapping — not a quantified forecast of the 12-23
number.

### Leg 4 — wholesale runs hot against consumer, and the spike has already round-tripped. SUPPORTED (BLS Table A, fetched today).

12-month final demand, 2026: Jan **3.1** · Feb **3.4** · Mar **4.3** · Apr **5.7** · May **5.9** · Jun
**5.5** · Jul **4.7**. Ex-foods/energy/trade services: **3.5 · 3.5 · 3.6 · 4.4 · 5.0 · 5.0 · 4.7**.
The hump is the oil shock passing through the goods leg and unwinding — monthly final-demand energy ran
**+10.5% (Mar), +7.2% (Apr), +8.2% (May), −6.5% (Jun), −3.1% (Jul)**, with July's headline held to
0.0% by gasoline **−5.7%**. Two readings matter. First, wholesale inflation at **4.7%** sits ~130bp
above CPI headline (3.4%) and ~140bp above core PCE (3.3%) — either margin compression at the
producer level or a consumer-price pipeline not yet delivered; this doc does not claim to know which.
Second, the *core* measure peaked at 5.0% and has given back only 0.3pp, so the disinflation on the
headline is overwhelmingly an energy artifact rather than a broad cooling.

### Leg 5 — the base arithmetic makes the 12-month rate sticky-to-rising. MIXED (estimate; an approximation).

The four 2025 months that roll out of the 12-month window between the July-data print and the
November-data print are Aug **−0.2**, Sep **+0.6**, Oct **+0.1**, Nov **+0.4** — a cumulative base of
only **+0.9%**. Mechanically, holding 4.7% requires the incoming Aug–Nov 2026 months to average about
**+0.22%/mo**; anything faster raises the annual rate into this print. July's core-ex-trade pace was
**+0.4%**. On the core measure the 2025 base is heavier (+0.4, +0.2, +0.5, +0.2 = **+1.3%**, ~0.33%/mo
to hold), so the two measures can diverge. **The honest caveat, stated because it matters:** Table A's
monthlies are seasonally adjusted and the 12-month rates are unadjusted, so this is an arithmetic
approximation of direction, never a forecast of a level. It also aligns with an independent read the
September doc recorded on 08-26 — prediction markets then put **56.5%** on August y/y PPI printing
above **5.1%**, i.e. re-accelerating.

### Leg 6 — the reaction function, and why it should be *smaller* here. MIXED (estimate).

This cycle's only clean PPI precedent is 08-13: a soft headline (0.0% vs +0.2% est., core +0.2% vs
+0.3%) was bought — S&P 500 to a record close ~+0.65%, Nasdaq ~+0.8%, yields down, September hike odds
pared below 40% (CNBC/NBC/CME, 08-13). PPI trades through the same yield channel as CPI with less
force. Three things argue the *December* print's same-day reaction should be smaller still, and one
argues the opposite. Smaller: (a) it lands after the decision, with no meeting for 43 days; (b) CPI
prints five days earlier and will already have set the narrative; (c) the chair pre-committed on 08-28
to reading "trends rather than isolated data points" and explicitly discounted better-than-expected
prints, which flattens the dovish-relief channel the 08-13 reaction ran on. Larger: (d) it is the
last macro input before **December triple witching on 12-18**, so a surprise repricing arrives into
stacked expiries and index-rebalance flow. Net expectation (**estimate**): a muted print-day tape with
the risk displaced into opex-week mechanics and into the 12-23 PCE — not a standalone volatility event.

### Leg 7 — production risk, and PPI carries the worst precedent on this calendar. SUPPORTED (BLS primary).

BLS's own revised-dates page ("Revised news release dates following the 2025 and 2026 lapses in
appropriations", fetched today) is unambiguous about what a lapse does to *this* release. In the
2025 lapse, the **September 2025 PPI moved from Thursday Oct 16 to Tuesday Nov 25, 2025 — a 40-day

milder for the price releases (January-2026 CPI Feb 11 → Feb 13; Employment Situation Feb 6 → Feb 11),
and PPI does not appear on that table at all. The sibling [`pce-2026-11-25`](pce-2026-11-25.md) entry
records the harder 2025 outcome on the consumer side — the full October 2025 CPI was never produced.
FY2026 funds lapse **2026-09-30**; the chambers remain unreconciled (House CR to **Dec 4**, 220-205 on
07-23; Senate Collins/Murray CR to **Dec 11**, 90-6 on 08-08 — re-checked today, still unreconciled,
House back from recess in early September). Both candidate CR dates sit *inside* this print's corridor.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date confirmed, BLS primary, two independent views 2026-08-31).** No position and no
directional lean on this print at any horizon. The deployable content is the **corridor guard**: no
new unhedged overnights held across **2026-12-04 → 2026-12-18**, and entries deferred past the 12-15
open per E1 — wider than the sibling CPI doc's Dec 4–11 window because *this* print's corridor runs
through import/export prices 12-17 to triple witching 12-18. Working read (**estimate**): the print's
same-day tape should be muted — it follows the decision into a 43-day policy vacuum — while its real
content routes to the **12-23 core PCE** through PPI's PCE-feeding lines, and its real risk in the
next 30 days is not the number at all but whether a **09-30 appropriations lapse** delays or deletes
the reference month. Base-case direction (**estimate**, trend + base arithmetic, no consensus exists):
the 12-month rate stays elevated in the **4.4–5.4%** area rather than continuing to fall, because the
2025 base rolling out is unusually light and the July disinflation was almost entirely energy.

**Kill switches:**

- **Date/source** — a BLS reschedule, or the two schedule views disagreeing on a later fetch, kills
  the header date. Re-fetch with a UA header each pulse; a 403 is a tooling failure, never evidence.
- **Date/production** — an appropriations lapse from **2026-09-30** threatens the reference month
  itself, not just the release day; the measured precedent for *this* release is a **40-day** slip
  (Sep-2025 data, Oct 16 → Nov 25, 2025). Retired by a CR or full-year bills enacted before 09-30.
- **The policy-vacuum framing** — dies if the Fed signals an intermeeting move, adds a meeting, or the
  Jan 26–27 date moves; the whole "muted print, slow channels" read rests on nothing being scheduled.
- **The PCE-nowcast channel** — weakened if the 09-25 or 10-29 PCE prints diverge from what the prior
  month's PPI components implied; killed if BEA changes the PPI-sourced inputs (note the **09-30**
  annual update already restates core PCE by a sourced −10 to −30bp).
- **The base-arithmetic read** — killed by the **09-10 / 10-15 / 11-13** prints running m/m below
  ~0.2% on average, which would drag the 12-month rate down through this print despite the light base.
- **Reaction-size read** — killed if the 09-10 or 10-15 PPI produces an NDX move ≥1% or a >5bp
  front-end repricing, which would say PPI is trading as a first-tier print in this regime.
- **Corridor guard** — a calendar fact, not a market read; it stands unless the 12-04, 12-09, 12-10 or
  12-18 dates move.
