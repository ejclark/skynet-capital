## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This event exists because the [midterm ledger](midterm-elections-2026-11-03.md) found, on
2026-08-29, that Senate control might not be known on election night — Georgia and Mississippi both
require a majority, and their runoff is 2026-12-01, three days before the House CR's expiry and
eight before the December FOMC. So: **is that branch actually live, how likely is it to fire, and
does it reach a paper book holding NVDA / MRVL / AVGO / CRWV / MSFT / GOOG / META / AAPL / AMZN and
the AI-energy sleeve?**

**One-line verdict:** the Georgia branch was already dead when the event was proposed — the
Libertarian ballot-access failure in July removed the exact mechanism that produced the 2021 and

but is Solid R and therefore cannot supply the "control unknown" premise the corridor was built on;
the corridor should be downgraded, and the live December Georgia story for this book is a **PSC
data-center cost-shift docket due 12-31**, not the runoff.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is right, and it is now primary-sourced — SUPPORTED, and this is a citation upgrade
   over the entry as proposed.** The State of Georgia's own calendar publishes an event titled
   **"Election Day - General Election Runoff"** on **Tuesday, December 1, 2026**, with polls
   **7 a.m.–7 p.m.** and the rule stated plainly: *"In Georgia, if no candidate receives a majority
   of the vote (50 percent plus one) in the General Election, the top two finishers will advance to
   a runoff."* The statutory mechanic agrees — **O.C.G.A. § 21-2-501** requires a majority and sets
   the runoff on the **twenty-eighth day** after the general "unless such runoff date is postponed by
   court order," and it reaches **federal** offices, not just state ones. 2026-11-03 + 28 days =
   **2026-12-01**. **The entry stays `estimate` for two reasons that a later session should not
   conflate:** the event is *conditional* (it may simply never occur), and separately this calendar
   has **no confirmed source prefix for a state election authority** (see `market-events-data.ts`),
   so promotion would need a schema change out of this lane's scope — the same mechanical gap the
   [midterm ledger](midterm-elections-2026-11-03.md) recorded for a statutory date. Noted as a
   proposal, not made.

2. **The Georgia branch is near-dead on ballot mechanics, and this is the finding — SUPPORTED.**
   Georgia runoffs in Senate races have not been produced by close two-way results; they have been
   produced by a **third name skimming just enough**. In 2020 the Libertarian took **2.3%** and in
   2022 **2.1%**, and both times the majors landed under 50% and the race went to a runoff. **That
   mechanism is absent in 2026.** The Libertarian Party of Georgia **lost automatic statewide ballot
   access** on its 2024 vote share and had to petition back on; the requirement is roughly
   **72,000 signatures**, and the party turned in **a few hundred** at the **2026-07-07** deadline
   before conceding the drive. The **AJC (2026-07-11)** headline states the consequence directly —
   *"Libertarians won't be on the ballot in Georgia, meaning likely no runoffs"* — and the body:
   *"It means there likely won't be statewide runoff races in Georgia this year,"* naming both the
   Senate race (Ossoff vs Collins) and the governor's race. **A two-name ballot cannot hold both
   candidates under a majority** except in the write-in tail of leg 4. The proposing doc did not
   have this fact; it is the single largest correction this document makes.

3. **The Georgia branch is also not close on the numbers — SUPPORTED, an independent second reason.**
   **Fox News** (field **2026-06-23→06-27**, n=1,002 registered voters, ±3) had **Ossoff 56 /
   Collins 43** — a 13-point lead that already sums to 99%, leaving no room for a sub-50% leader
   even before the ballot-access fact. The tighter August read, **InsiderAdvantage 2026-08-18**, has
   **Ossoff 50 / Collins 43**, i.e. the leader already *at* the threshold with the remaining 7% to
   allocate in a two-way. The Republican nominee question the parent ledger left open is now
   resolved: **Mike Collins** won the **2026-06-16** primary runoff with **55.5%** after a Trump
   endorsement, having failed to clear a majority against Derek Dooley and Buddy Carter on
   **2026-05-19**. **No probability is put on the November outcome here** — the point is narrower and
   safer: two independent conditions (no third name, a non-trivial gap) each independently suppress
   the runoff.

4. **The only surviving Georgia path is a certified write-in plus a razor margin — MIXED, and it is
   the dated falsifier this doc hangs the call on.** Georgia allows declared write-ins, and the
   Libertarians said they may run as write-ins; write-in votes are votes cast and *do* sit in the
   majority denominator. The mechanics have hard dates: **O.C.G.A. § 21-2-133** requires notice of
   intent published and filed **no later than the Tuesday after the first Monday in September** —
   **2026-09-08** for this cycle — with the published-notice affidavit due within five days
   (**2026-09-14**). **But the tail needs a conjunction**, not a single event: a certified write-in
   *and* a Georgia margin thin enough that a fraction of a percent decides majority status. Declared
   write-ins in Georgia statewide races have historically drawn a fraction of what a ballot-line
   Libertarian draws. Graded MIXED rather than REFUTED because the September deadline has not passed
   yet and is cheaply checkable — which is exactly why it is the "This week" falsifier.

5. **Mississippi is the branch that survives, and the proposing doc had the two states backwards —
   SUPPORTED, and it is this doc's second correction.** The parent ledger called Georgia "the live
   one" and treated Mississippi as an afterthought. The ballots say the reverse. Mississippi's
   November ballot carries **three** names — **Cindy Hyde-Smith (R)**, **Scott Colom (D)** and
   **Ty Pinkins (I)** — and the only public poll found, **Data for Progress (field 2026-08-05→08-15)**,
   reads **Hyde-Smith 44 / Colom 38 / Pinkins 7 / undecided 11**: *the leader is below the majority
   threshold with an independent holding 7%*, which is precisely the arithmetic that forces runoffs.
   Mississippi's runoff is also **2026-12-01**; the state moved its runoff interval from three weeks
   to **four** in **SB2144 (2024 regular session)**, which is what makes the two states' runoff dates
   coincide. Primary results (**2026-03-10**): Hyde-Smith **80.8%**, Colom **72.9%**.

6. **…but Mississippi cannot carry the market consequence the corridor was built on — SUPPORTED,
   and this is why the downgrade holds even though leg 5 keeps a branch alive.** All three
   handicappers rate the seat out of reach: **Cook Political Report Solid R (2026-08-20)**,
   **Sabato's Crystal Ball Safe R (2026-08-19)**, **Inside Elections Solid R (2026-04-23)**. For a
   Mississippi runoff to hold *Senate control* open through December, three things must all be true
   at once: the runoff must fire, the rest of the map must land exactly one seat apart, and a
   **Solid-R** seat must be the tipping seat. That is a conjunction of three conditions, one of
   which every rating service prices as near-zero — not a base case. **The honest statement is:
   a Mississippi runoff is plausible; a Mississippi runoff that keeps the chamber unknown is not.**

7. **So the corridor premise gets downgraded across the December ledgers — SUPPORTED as reasoning,

8. **Georgia's live December story for this book is a ratepayer docket, not the runoff — SUPPORTED,

proposed to the calendar in this PR. Confidence on the *election* is unstated by design; a political
forecast is not something this repo has an instrument for.
