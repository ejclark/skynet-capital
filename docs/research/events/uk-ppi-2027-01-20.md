**And the July 2026 report shows why the Bank does not need it.** It runs its own oil pass-through

Bank measures producer-cost pass-through **on CPI components directly**, which is better instrumented
for its purpose than a 50-day-old goods index.

**The sharp finding is the asymmetry, and it is this event's alone.** The one feature that makes this
release different from every other UK PPI print — the quarterly **services** producer price index — is
cited **zero times in 200 pages of MPR**, in an institution whose stated focus is services inflation.
Goods output prices at least get an aside. The services half gets nothing.

### Leg 3 — "The flat base ended at the December print." **REFUTED as a regime; it was a one-month event, and this print's base is friendly again.**

The 12-16 lane's central arithmetic: Aug/Sep/Oct 2025 input m/m ran **0.0 / 0.0 / −0.1%**, then

|---|---|---|

Two things follow.

**First, the hostile base lasts one month.** December 2025 ran **−0.2%**, so for this print
Δ(annual rate, 2027-01-20 − 2026-12-16) = (Dec-2026 m/m) − (−0.2%) = **(Dec-2026 m/m) + 0.2pp**. At the
~9.2% effective beta the 12-16 lane measured from the publisher's own product-group detail, the annual
rate **falls** below the December print only if first-week-December Brent in sterling drops **more than
2.2%** on first-week-November. On any flatter path it **rises**. This is the exact mirror of the
sibling's call, and it is not a disagreement with it — the sibling is right about its own print and
wrong only if read as a trend.

**Second, the sibling's own base has already moved 0.2pp.** Its stall call is built on Nov-2025
**+0.3%**; the next bulletin published **+0.5%**. That is 0.2pp against a forward test whose threshold
is 0.5pp (**FT-uk-ppi-2026-12-16-2**). Recorded here, on the record; that ledger's rows are
append-only and are not edited from this lane.

### Leg 5 — "The SPPI acceleration resolves here." **REFUTED — the base quarter is exactly normal, and the record quarter does not roll out until July 2027.**

This is the arithmetic that is genuinely this event's rather than any sibling's. The full quarterly
path, from three bulletins fetched today (March 2026, June 2026, December 2025), with the revisions
visible:

| Quarter | q/q | Annual, as first published | Annual, latest vintage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 2025 | +0.6% | 2.3% | — |
| Q2 2025 | +0.8% | 1.9% | — |
| Q3 2025 | +0.6% | 1.9% | 2.0% |
| **Q4 2025** | **+0.8%** (was +0.7%) | 2.9% | **2.8%** |
| Q1 2026 | +0.8% | 3.0% | **3.2%** |
| **Q2 2026** | **+1.8%** | **4.3%** | — |

An annual SPPI rate is the sum of four consecutive q/q changes, so:

**annual(Q4 2026) = annual(Q3 2026) + q/q(Q4 2026) − q/q(Q4 2025) = annual(Q3 2026) + q4 − 0.8pp.**

**The base quarter is exactly the norm.** The five non-spike quarters run **0.6 / 0.8 / 0.6 / 0.8 /
0.8**, mean **+0.72%**. Q4 2025's +0.8% is the top of that ordinary range and nothing like the Q2 2026
outlier. So the base **does not bind**: on any ordinary quarter the annual rate lands within
**−0.2pp to 0.0pp** of the Q3 2026 rate.

**And the record quarter is still inside the window.** The +1.8% Q2 2026 quarter — which the publisher
attributes to transportation and storage at *"the highest… since records began"* on shipping and fuel
— enters the annual rates for Q2 2026, Q3 2026, **Q4 2026** and Q1 2027, and drops out only at the
**Quarter 2 2027** edition (the "June 2027 including services, April to June 2027" release, ~July
2027). **A reader waiting on this print to tell them whether the services acceleration was real gets
no answer.** It is a level, not a test.

**Base case.** Taking the [`uk-ppi-2026-10-21`](uk-ppi-2026-10-21.md) lane's Q3 2026 call of ~4.3%
(band 3.8–4.8, itself unprinted), an ordinary Q4 gives **4.1–4.3%**. Widen for the live fuel-and-
shipping shock, whose 2026 precedent is exactly the +1.8% quarter above: **3.9–5.0%, centred ~4.3%** —
**flat, with the war as the only asymmetry.** Registered as **FT-uk-ppi-2027-01-20-3**.
