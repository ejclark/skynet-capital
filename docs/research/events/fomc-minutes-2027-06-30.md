## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The minutes of the June 8–9, 2027 FOMC publish at 14:00 ET on **2027-06-30** — the last trading
session of Q2 2027, one session after a Conference Board confidence print, and (if the Select Sector
test fires) the close at which a forced index trade clears. Three sibling ledgers
([10-07](fomc-minutes-2026-10-07.md), [11-18](fomc-minutes-2026-11-18.md),
[12-30](fomc-minutes-2026-12-30.md)) have already said *read it, do not trade it*, each from a
handful of observations. So there are two questions worth a session, and neither is "is the channel
weak?": **does the family's refusal survive a twenty-year sample rather than n=3, and is there
anything about a quarter-end slot that changes it?**

**One-line verdict:** the refusal survives and gets *stronger*, but the family's stated reason does
not — at **n=160** a minutes day is statistically an **ordinary** session, not a quiet one, which
means the correct rule is "don't trade it *and* don't assume it's safe." The quarter-end slot is a
genuine first (0 of 160 precedents) and it is a **high-volume** venue, which makes 2027-06-30 the
strongest available test of the null rather than the weakest. Date `estimate`; no directional call.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date derives from a re-fetched primary and a rule measured over twenty years, not five.**
   SUPPORTED. `fomccalendars.htm` (HTTP 200, 164,826 bytes today) states the convention verbatim —
   *"The minutes of regularly scheduled meetings are released three weeks after the date of the
   policy decision"* — and its 2027 panel reads *"January 26-27 | March 16-17\* | April 27-28 | **June
   8-9\*** | July 27-28 | September 14-15\* | October 26-27 | December 7-8\*"*. 2027-06-09 + 21 days is
   **Wednesday 2027-06-30** (weekday verified, not assumed). The proposal that seeded this entry
   measured the rule on that page's 45 published release dates; this session extended it to **160
   release dates 2007–2026** by parsing all fourteen `fomchistorical<year>.htm` pages, every one of
   which is a trading session, and the same rule with the same class of holiday exception holds
   throughout. **Status stays `estimate`** — the same page states *"Each meeting date is tentative
   until confirmed at the meeting immediately preceding it"*, so the trigger is the **2027-04-27/28**
   meeting, not a better fetch. `newsevents/2027-june.htm` and `/2027-july.htm` both **404** today
   (the month pages carrying the 2:00 p.m. time do not exist yet) and are recorded in `probe-ref`.

2. **The family's channel claim is right in its conclusion and wrong in its evidence — corrected
   here.** MIXED, and this is the headline. The siblings argued from three observations (−0.14%,
   −0.28%, +0.21%) all inside ±0.3%. Across **all 160** releases 2007–2026:

   | Measure | Minutes days (n=160) | All sessions 2007–2026 (n=4,950) |
   |---|---|---|
   | Mean \|S&P close-to-close\| | **0.776%** | 0.798% |
   | Median \|move\| | **0.468%** | 0.523% |
   | Share within ±0.3% | **33.1%** | 33.4% |
   | Share ≥1.5% | **12.5%** (20 days) | 14.1% |
   | Mean VIX change | **−0.295 pt** | — |

   Bootstrap: **P(a random 160-session mean ≤ the minutes mean) = 0.40**. So minutes days are
   *marginally* below baseline and nowhere near distinguishable from it. Restricting to 2021–2026
   reproduces it exactly (0.739% vs 0.746%, n=46). **The ±0.3% framing is the artefact:** the twenty
   minutes days that moved ≥1.5% include **2008-11-19 −6.12%**, **2008-10-07 −5.74%**, **2020-04-08
   +3.41%** and **2025-04-09 +9.52%** — none minutes-driven, all of them real risk borne on a day a
   reader of the sibling ledgers would have filed as inert. The honest statement is **"a minutes day
   is an ordinary session"**, which refuses the trade *and* refuses the safety. *Confound, named and
   load-bearing:* this is a base rate, not a mechanism — a minutes day inherits whatever else the
   tape is doing, which is precisely why the ≥1.5% tail exists and why the null is the right read.

3. **2027-06-30 would be the first minutes release ever to land on a quarter's final session, and
   the first June-meeting minutes to publish inside June.** SUPPORTED. Of the 160 releases, **zero**
   fall on a quarter's last trading day. The closest approaches in twenty years are **2025-12-30**
   (one session *before* year-end — the very release [12-30](fomc-minutes-2026-12-30.md) leans on)
   and **2020-07-01** (one session *after* quarter-end). All **20** June-meeting minutes 2007–2026
   published in July: the four earliest are **07-01, 07-03, 07-05, 07-05**, the latest 2007-07-19.
   The cause is mechanical and was already measured next door — [`fomc-2027-06-09`](fomc-2027-06-09.md)
   found every 2027 FOMC sits one day earlier than its 2026 counterpart **except June, which sits
   eight days earlier**, making 2027-06-09 the earliest June FOMC in a 21-year record. That eight-day
   shift is the entire reason these minutes leave July.

4. **The 12-30 sibling's venue argument is backwards, and correcting it makes this release the
   stronger test.** REFUTED, as stated there. That ledger called the dead week *"the most
   amplification-friendly venue a minutes release ever gets"* and treated a null there as the
   strongest possible refusal. Measured against each session's own trailing-60-day median volume on
   `^GSPC`: the dead week (Dec 28–31) runs **0.676×**, quarter-end final sessions run **0.993×**,
   **June** quarter-end runs **1.149×**, against a **1.027×** all-session mean. Thin tape widens
   spreads; it does not manufacture participation. The venue where flow could actually amplify a
   release is the **quarter-end close**, and that is exactly the slot 2027-06-30 occupies — with a
   conditional forced index trade in it (leg 6). So this release is the first real test of the null,
   not a repeat of a weak one, and that is what **FT-fomc-minutes-2027-06-30-1** registers.

5. **A sibling's quarter-end regime does not reproduce at index level over twenty years — stated,
   not buried.** MIXED. [`consumer-confidence-2027-06-29`](consumer-confidence-2027-06-29.md)
   measured that the last four sessions of a quarter-end month run narrower, 6 of 9 names, with
   p-values from 0.0011 to 0.0295 (n=96 vs 1,167). Re-running that construction on `^GSPC` over
   **2007–2026**: quarter-end last-4 **0.775%**, ordinary month-end last-4 **0.777%**, non-month-end
   **0.803%** — quarter-end and ordinary month-end are *identical*, which is the separation that
   sibling's finding turns on. On the shorter **2021-09→** window it does appear (0.685% vs 0.767%,
   n=84). Two honest readings, and I cannot pick between them from here: the effect may be
   name-level and index-diluted, or it may be window-dependent. What this ledger takes from it is
   only the weaker, uncontested part — **the June quarter-end session's own baseline is 0.702%**
   (n=20) against a **0.739%** June baseline — and that is the number FT-1 is scored against. The
   sibling's claim is not contradicted at its own construction; it is **not reproduced at index
   scale**, and a session reading either document should know that.

   [`consumer-confidence-2027-06-29`](consumer-confidence-2027-06-29.md). Re-fetched from SEC EDGAR

   2027-06-29** — so the test is the CB print's own session and the trade, if triggered, clears at

7. **The proposal's pairing claim is true of this calendar and not structurally novel.** MIXED. The
   proposing note called 2027-06-29→06-30 *"the first time in the consumer-confidence chain that a
   tracked event sits one day after a CB edition"* — true of what this repo tracks. Structurally it

### Honest limits

The channel measurement is a **base rate, not a mechanism** (leg 2) — no attribution work
distinguishes a minutes-driven move from a coincident one, which is exactly why the tail exists.
Release dates come from the Fed's own pages, but the 2027 month page that would carry the release
*time* 404s, so 14:00 ET is convention, not sourced for this release. Leg 5 leaves a real
disagreement with a sibling unresolved and says so. Leg 6's base case rests on the March sibling's
reasoning about a reset, and its breach evidence is ETF holdings weights, which are floors on the
index weights the test applies to. Leg 8 refuses a date rather than guessing one, which means this
corridor may be missing the single largest volume session of 2027. And the whole document rests on a
meeting — **2027-06-08/09** — that is tentative by the Board's own rule and nine meetings away.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `estimate`):** **read it, do not trade it — and do not treat it as a safe day
either.** The family's refusal is upheld and its evidence replaced: across all **160** minutes
releases 2007–2026 a minutes day is statistically an **ordinary** session (0.776% vs 0.798%,
p=0.40; 12.5% moved ≥1.5% against a 14.1% baseline), so the correct posture is neutral in **both**
directions rather than "quiet." What distinguishes this release is structural, not tradable:
**2027-06-30 would be the first FOMC minutes release in twenty years to publish on a quarter's final
session** and the first June-meeting minutes to publish inside June, caused entirely by the June 2027
meeting sitting eight days earlier than its 2026 counterpart. That slot is a **1.149×**-volume venue
carrying a conditional forced index trade — the inverse of the thin-tape venue
[12-30](fomc-minutes-2026-12-30.md) leaned on, and therefore the strongest test of the null this
family has had. No directional call, no size; `estimate` widens caution and licenses nothing.

**Kill switches:**

- **Relevance / date kill:** the June 2027 FOMC moving off **06-08/09**, or federalreserve.gov
  publishing a minutes date other than 2027-06-30. The release reverts to July, legs 3, 4 and 6 die
  together, and this becomes a routine technical. Registered **FT-2**, score by **2027-05-03** (after
  the 2027-04-27/28 ratifying meeting).
- **Venue kill:** 2027-06-30's `^GSPC` close-to-close |move| exceeding the **0.702%** June
  quarter-end baseline *with the minutes as the session's named driver*, or the release moving
  September-2027 hike/cut odds **≥10pp** on the day. Legs 2 and 4 fail together and the quarter-end
  close is a repricing venue after all. Registered **FT-1**, score by **2027-07-01**.
- **Flow kill:** the Select Sector secondary reweight **triggering** on the 2027-06-29 test. The base
  case in leg 6 fails and the 06-30 close carries a forced trade the minutes have to be separated
  from. Registered **FT-3**, score by **2027-07-01**.
- **Measurement kill:** a defensible attribution study showing minutes releases *do* move the tape
  once coincident macro is controlled for. Leg 2 is a base rate and would not survive it; the
  conclusion might, but it would need re-arguing rather than patching.
- **Corridor kill:** FTSE Russell publishing a 2027 reconstitution calendar dating the June recon
  inside 2027-06-25→07-05. Leg 8's refusal converts to a proposal and this corridor gains the year's
  largest volume session.
