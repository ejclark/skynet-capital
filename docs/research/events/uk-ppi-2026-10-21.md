## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Two statistics publish in this one 07:00 slot, and they are opposite objects.** The goods
half is *already finished*: ONS's PPI methodology says firms price **"transactions that occur on or
near to the first of each month"**, so the September index was priced around **1 September 2026** — a
date now in the past. First-week Brent in sterling ran **+12.0%** in August and **+16.3%** in
September against a **flat 2025 base**, which at crude's **6.9%** input weight puts the September
input rate near **7.0%**, against 4.9% in July. That is not a forecast of a price; it is arithmetic on
a price already on the tape, which makes this the cleanest possible test of the model its goods
sibling built — **with no forward price risk left in it**. The services half is the reverse: the
quarterly **SPPI** has no market proxy, prints three weeks after its reference quarter, and is the one
number in this release nobody can nowcast. There the call is **against extrapolation** — SPPI has run
2.0 → 2.9 → 3.0 → **4.3%** across four quarters, but Q2's jump was one non-repeating group
(transportation and storage **+7.3% q/q**, which the publisher itself calls *"the highest… since
records began in Quarter 4… 2008"*, on shipping and fuel), and quarterly-average Brent in sterling
then **fell 7.8%** into Q3. Base case: the acceleration **stalls near 4.3%**. Put together, UK producer
inflation is an **imported energy event, not a broad regime** — and **none of it is ours to trade**
(`symbols: []`). One correction to the calendar while here: **2026-10-21 is a three-release ONS minute,
not two** — CPI, PPI *and* public sector finances, all verified at 7:00am from their own release pages
today. Date is **estimate** on a taxonomy gap, not on the evidence.

### Leg 3 — "The goods half is a forecast." **REFUTED. It is already determined.**

The ONS *Producer Price Indices QMI* states that firms report prices for **"transactions that occur
on or near to the first of each month"** (quoted in the goods sibling from the same document). The
September 2026 index therefore priced around **1 September 2026** and publishes **21 October 2026** —
a **50-day** lag on a one-day snapshot. **That day has passed.**

Recomputed here from Yahoo `BZ=F` × `GBPUSD=X` (2y daily, forward-filled FX onto Brent trading days),
averaging only the first seven calendar days of each month — the window the QMI describes:

| Month | Brent wk1, GBP, m/m | Brent full-month, GBP, m/m | ONS crude group m/m |
|---|---|---|---|
| June 2026 | −9.8% | −17.8% | **−20.9%** |
| July 2026 | **−23.6%** | −0.5% | **−18.0%** |
| August 2026 | **+12.0%** | +3.0% | *publishes 2026-09-16* |
| **September 2026** | **+16.3%** *(n=4 trading days)* | +10.1% *(MTD)* | ***publishes 2026-10-21 — this print*** |

These reproduce the sibling's figures to the decimal from an independent pull, which is the point of
recomputing them. Crude petroleum, natural gas and metal ores carry **6.9%** of the input basket and
explained ~76% of June's and ~73% of July's index move.

The arithmetic, with a flat 2025 base (input m/m **0.0%** in August 2025 and **0.0%** in September
2025, from the sibling's Table 1 extract, and September 2025's input annual rate was **0.8%**):

- **August 2026:** +12.0% × 6.9% = **+0.83pp** → input ≈ **+0.9% m/m** → **~5.8% y/y**
- **September 2026:** +16.3% × 6.9% = **+1.12pp** → ≈ **+1.2% m/m** → **~7.0% y/y**

**Base case for the goods half: input 6.0–8.0% y/y, centred ~7.0%, against 4.9% in July.** The band is
as wide as the sibling's despite carrying no price risk, because the remaining uncertainty moved
rather than shrank: two ONS monthly clears and the QMI's own hedge (the oil and energy first estimate
is partly **the suppliers' own estimate** for the rest of the month) are unmeasured. What is gone is
the part the sibling could not remove — *what Brent does next*. That is why this print, not
2026-11-18, is the honest first full test of the model (**FT-uk-ppi-2026-10-21-2**).

### Leg 4 — "SPPI is the same kind of object as the goods index." **REFUTED on every axis that matters.**

From the ONS *Services Producer Price Inflation QMI* and the bulletins, all fetched today:

| | Goods PPI | Services PPI (SPPI) |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | monthly | **quarterly** |
| Pricing point | one day, "on or near" the 1st | across the quarter |
| Lag to publication | ~48–50 days | **~3 weeks** from quarter end |
| Market proxy | Brent in sterling explains ~¾ of the monthly move | **none known** |
| Designation | official statistics, not accredited | **official statistics, not accredited** |
| Revisions | latest 12 months | **"the last two quarters are provisional and the last five quarters are subject to revisions"** |
| Coverage | the manufacturing input/output baskets | **"a partial, best estimate… limited coverage of the services sector because of data collection challenges"** — nine product groups, CPA E37→S |

The consequence is the one that changes this ledger's shape. The goods sibling's central stand-aside
argument — *"a print that is three-quarters a lagged function of a price on the screen contains almost
no information the tape does not already hold"* — is **correct for the goods half and does not
transfer to the services half.** SPPI is a genuinely un-nowcastable number: no observable proxy, a
short lag, and a survey whose weighted response rate for Q2 2026 was **77.1%**, down from **83.0%** a
year earlier. It contains information. It is simply information about a sector this book has no
instrument in — which is a **different reason** to stand aside, and the honest one to state.

### Leg 5 — "The SPPI acceleration is a trend to extrapolate." **REFUTED. It is one non-repeating group, and its input has reversed.**

The series, each figure quoted from the bulletin that published it:

| Quarter | SPPI y/y | SPPI q/q | Source bulletin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q3 2025 | **2.1%**, later revised to **2.0%** | **0.9%**, later revised to **0.7%** | Sep 2025 incl. services (22 Oct 2025), revised in Dec 2025 incl. services |
| Q4 2025 | **2.9%** | **0.7%** | Dec 2025 incl. services (21 Jan 2026) |
| Q1 2026 | **3.0%** | **0.8%** | Mar 2026 incl. services (22 Apr 2026) |
| Q2 2026 | **4.3%** | **1.8%** | Jun 2026 incl. services (22 Jul 2026) |

Read naively that is four consecutive rises and a doubling, and extrapolating the last step puts Q3
above 5.5%. Two facts kill that read.

**Fact one: the Q2 step was one group, at a record.** From the June 2026 bulletin, verbatim, on
transportation and storage services:

> "On a quarterly basis, prices for this product group rose by **7.3%** in Quarter 2 2026, up from an
> increase of 0.7% in Quarter 1. This is **the highest the quarterly rate has been for this product
> group since records began in Quarter 4 (Oct to Dec) 2008**."

and on its cause:

> "The annual price rise in Quarter 2 was mainly caused by increases in prices for 'other
> transportation support services' because of **increased shipping rates and fuel charges resulting
> from the conflict in the Middle East**."

The headline ran **+1.8% q/q** against a **0.7–0.8%** run rate in the other three quarters — an excess
of roughly **1.0pp**. Attributing that excess to a group moving 7.3% against a 0.7% norm implies a
weight near **15%** for transportation and storage. That is a *derived* number, not a published one
(SPPI product-group weights were not found), and it is the softest input to this leg — but the
direction does not depend on it: a group printing its highest quarterly rate in **eighteen years** is
by construction not a run rate.

**Fact two: its input reversed.** Quarterly-average Brent in sterling, computed here: Q2 2026
(Apr/May/Jun full-month GBP averages 76.11 / 77.18 / 63.44) → mean **72.24**; Q3 2026 (Jul/Aug/Sep-MTD
63.13 / 65.03 / 71.58) → mean **66.58**. That is **−7.8% q/q**. The fuel-charge half of the group's
record quarter faces a *falling* cost base through Q3, even though spot has since rebounded to
**$101.04** — because SPPI averages the quarter rather than snapshotting a day, the June–July collapse
sits inside Q3 and drags it.

**The arithmetic.** The identity is exact: y/y(Q3 26) = y/y(Q2 26) + q/q(Q3 26) − q/q(Q3 25), i.e.
**4.3 + X − 0.7 = 3.6 + X**.

- X at the non-Q2 run rate (**0.7–0.8%**) → **4.3–4.4%**
- X with transport reverting to zero rather than to trend → ~**0.6%** → **4.2%**
- X with transport actually falling on cheaper quarterly fuel → ~**0.4%** → **4.0%**
- X with residual April carry (the National Living Wage rose **4.1%** to £12.71 on 1 April 2026, a
  level step in labour-intensive services that does not repeat in Q3) → up to **1.2%** → **4.8%**

**Base case for the services half: SPPI 3.8–4.8% y/y, centred ~4.3% — flat on Q2, the acceleration
stalls** (**FT-uk-ppi-2026-10-21-1**), with the mechanism itself testable separately in the transport
group's own q/q (**FT-uk-ppi-2026-10-21-3**).

**The divergence is the finding.** Goods input re-accelerating ~2.1pp (4.9 → ~7.0%) while services go
sideways (4.3 → ~4.3%) is not "UK producer inflation is rising". It is an imported energy and shipping
shock arriving in the goods basket and *not* broadening into domestically-produced business services —
the same conclusion the goods sibling reached about pass-through, reached here from the other side of
the release.

### Leg 6 — "The 2025 outage was a scheduling failure." **REFUTED — it was a methodology error, and its fingerprints are still on this print's base.**

The goods sibling recorded the outage as *"Publications from February to August 2025 were cancelled"*.
The **Producer prices development plan (October 2025)**, fetched today, names the cause: publication
was **"suspended in March 2025"** following **a chain-linking methodology error**, with data resuming
22 October 2025 after **"a comprehensive review, correction, and quality assurance"** — and the
correction **applied retrospectively to data since 2014**. The December 2025 bulletin confirms the
retrospective scope. On the services side the June 2026 bulletin's own note is narrower than the goods
one: **"Publications for Quarter 1 (Jan to Mar) and Quarter 2 (Apr to June) 2025 were cancelled."**

Three consequences for the 10-21 print specifically:

1. **The Q3 2025 base quarter exists** — it was published 22 October 2025, in the resumption bulletin.
   The annual rate on 10-21 is computable against a real, contemporaneously-published base, which is
   *not* true of every recent goods print.
2. **The base was revised after the fact** — 2.1% → **2.0%**, one quarter later. Every band in this
   ledger is stated against the revised figure, and a further revision to it moves the answer without
   anything happening in the economy.
3. **There is a live sample discontinuity.** The development plan's timetable has CPA-division sample
   updates *"completed by February 2026"*, affecting **"approximately 70% of the sample by weight"**,
   and the March 2026 bulletin notes SPPI estimates *"published with updated weights in line with the
   annual chain-linking methodology"* effective from Q4 2025. The Q3 2026 annual rate therefore spans
   a reweighting. Direction unknown; it is a reason for the band, not a lean.

The series remains **"official statistics"** while ONS addresses the OSR *Spotlight on Quality*
requirements ahead of reaccreditation.

### Honest limits

- **The 15% transport weight is derived, not published.** SPPI product-group weights were not found on
  the publisher's site today. The weight is backed out of the headline residual, and it is the softest
  number in Leg 5. The leg does not depend on it — an 18-year record quarterly rate is not a run rate
  at any weight — but the *size* of the expected give-back does.
- **One clean year of SPPI history.** Q1 and Q2 2025 were cancelled, so there is exactly one prior Q3
  observation (2025: +0.7% q/q revised) and no way to test whether Q2 is structurally the reset
  quarter. The April-reset story is a *hypothesis consistent with one year*, not a measured seasonal.
- **September's first-week Brent is four trading days.** 1–7 September 2026 contains four Brent closes
  (n=4); the +16.3% is the thinnest number in the goods arithmetic, and it is unchanged from the
  sibling's because it is the same window.
- **The crude group is not pure crude.** "Crude petroleum and natural gas; metal ores" bundles gas and
  metal ores; applying a Brent move to the whole 6.9% probably overstates oil sensitivity slightly.
- **Shipping rates were not measured.** The publisher names them alongside fuel as the driver of the
  transport group's record quarter. No freight index was pulled today, so Leg 5 quantifies one of the
  two named causes and assumes nothing about the other. If container/bulk rates rose through Q3 while
  fuel fell, the stall call weakens — this is the honest gap in it and it is a kill switch below.
- **No consensus to be surprised against.** No published Reuters/Bloomberg consensus for the September
  2026 UK PPI or Q3 SPPI was found today; the base cases are this ledger's arithmetic.
- **The 4.9% July input rate is provisional**, as is Q2's 4.3% SPPI figure — both are inside their
  publisher's revision windows, and both are the anchors of the arithmetic above.
- **The taxonomy gap, now at ten entries.** ONS (three times over), HM Treasury, Ofgem, MOF Japan,
  S&P Global, NYSE/SIFMA, legislation.gov.uk and HMRC have all been filed `estimate` purely because
  `market-events-data.ts` has no confirmed prefix for a credible non-US primary. Flagged for the
  calendar's owner, not self-resolved — the schema is not this lane's to widen.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date: estimate on a taxonomy gap; both underlying releases are on the publisher's calendar).**
**Stand aside at every horizon** — but for two different reasons, and saying which is the point of this
ledger. The **goods half** carries no information the tape does not already hold: its pricing day has
passed and it is arithmetic on Brent in sterling. The **services half** carries real information that
nobody can nowcast — and this book has no instrument in the sector it describes. Four commitments,
none of them a trade:

1. **The goods half is finished.** The September index priced around **1 September 2026**. First-week
   Brent in GBP: **+12.0%** (Aug), **+16.3%** (Sep), against a flat 2025 base. At crude's 6.9% weight,
   input inflation prints **6.0–8.0% y/y**, centred **~7.0%**, against 4.9% in July. This is the
   sibling's model with the forward price risk removed, which makes 10-21 its first honest test.
2. **The services acceleration stalls.** SPPI **3.8–4.8% y/y**, centred **~4.3%**, flat on Q2 — because
   Q2's +1.8% q/q was transportation and storage at **+7.3%**, *"the highest… since records began in
   Quarter 4… 2008"* on fuel and shipping, and quarterly-average Brent in sterling then fell **7.8%**.
3. **The two together are a divergence, not a regime.** Goods input up ~2.1pp while services go
   sideways is an imported energy and shipping shock that is **not broadening into domestically
   produced business services**.
4. **02:00 ET on 2026-10-21 has three suspects.** CPI, PPI and public sector finances all publish at
   07:00 London — verified from three release pages today. This is the only such minute on the
   calendar; the sibling's two-release rule is one release short here.

**Kill switches** — any one of these and the stance is rebuilt, not adjusted:

- **A tracked name moves >2%** on a session between 2026-09-09 and 2026-10-21 attributable to UK
  producer-price data → the no-channel premise is false.
- **ONS moves or cancels the 2026-10-21 release**, or suspends publication again as it did in March
  2025 → the date comes off the board and the quality flag becomes the story.
- **The 2026-09-16 August print shows an input annual rate at or below 4.9%** → the first-week-Brent
  transmission is not doing what the 6.9% weight implies, and Leg 3's whole goods case is rebuilt
  before this print even lands.
- **The 2026-10-21 SPPI prints at 5.0% or above** → the Q2 step was a trend and not a record-setting
  one-group event; Leg 5 is rebuilt (**FT-…-1**).
- **A freight-rate index shows Q3 2026 container or bulk rates rising materially against Q2** → the
  unmeasured half of the transport group's driver is still pushing, and the stall call loses the leg
  that Leg 5 explicitly did not quantify.
- **ONS publishes SPPI product-group weights that put transportation and storage far from ~15%** →
  the size of the expected give-back is wrong even if its direction holds.
- **A published consensus appears that differs from 6.0–8.0% (goods) or 3.8–4.8% (services)** → the
  base cases stop being the only estimates on the table and get tested against it rather than asserted.
