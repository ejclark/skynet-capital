### The question, plainly

This event existed only as `proposals/fhfa-hpi-2027-04-27.from-fhfa-hpi-2027-11-30.json`, one of five 2027
rows the November sibling filed in a single PR to close out FHFA's own posted 2027 table. That proposal is

Re-derived. And the instruction to control before believing turns out to bite the proposal's own framing:
**if day one is the thing that makes this date special, it has to survive a control on the blackout it sits
inside.** So the question is three-layered: is the derivation right, is day one a regime in its own right or
just blackout membership wearing a hat, and does the FHFA print contribute anything to either?

**One-line verdict:** the derivation is exactly right, day one is **not** a regime once you control for the
blackout, and the print contributes **nothing** measurable to a day-one session — so the date's only
distinctive feature is one the Fed owns and this calendar already tracks under three other ids.

### Leg 2 — **2027-04-27 is FOMC day one, and day one is the MODAL case for a monthly edition** · **SUPPORTED**, and it deflates the proposal's framing

The Board's 2027 panel reads `April | 27-28`, with **no** SEP asterisk. Applying the Board's stated rule —
the blackout opens at 12:00 a.m. ET on the second Saturday preceding the meeting and ends at 11:59 p.m. ET
the day after it — gives **2027-04-17 → 2027-04-29**. Two independent checks, both exact: this repo already
carries `fomc-blackout-start-2027-04-17`, titled *"FOMC communications blackout begins (through
2027-04-29)"*, decoded by a sibling from the Board's own blackout-calendar PDF; and `fomc-2027-04-28`,
titled *"FOMC decision (meeting Apr 27-28, no SEP)"*. The derivation here reaches both from the posted panel
alone.

**So the proposal is right — and that is where the interesting part starts.** Parsing all **56** posted
meeting pairs and classifying every post-policy release day reproduces the sibling chain's month table and
adds the column it never needed:

| Month of the last Tuesday | in a blackout | **is** FOMC day one | edition type |
|---|---|---|---|
| **January** | **7 of 7** | **7 of 7** | monthly |
| **April** | **7 of 7** | **4 of 7 — 2021, 2024, 2026, 2027** | monthly |
| **July** | **7 of 7** | **7 of 7** | monthly |
| **October** | **7 of 7** | 4 of 7 | monthly |
| November | 1 of 7 | 0 of 7 | quarterly |
| the other seven months | 0 of 7 | 0 of 7 | mixed |

**The structural fact that follows, and it is the mirror image of the November sibling's.** Day-one last
Tuesdays fall only in **January, April, July and October**. Quarterly editions fall only in **February, May,
August and November**. The two sets are **disjoint**, so **no quarterly edition can ever be an FOMC day
one** — and, checked directly against the archive, **17 of 68** post-policy releases were day one and **all
seventeen are monthly**:

> 2021-01-26 · 2021-04-27 · 2021-07-27 · 2022-01-25 · 2022-07-26 · 2023-01-31 · 2023-07-25 · 2023-10-31 ·
> 2024-01-30 · 2024-04-30 · 2024-07-30 · 2025-01-28 · 2025-07-29 · 2025-10-28 · 2026-01-27 · 2026-04-28 ·
> 2026-07-28

**This reframes the date.** The `2027-11-30` ledger could say *"the only quarterly edition in the panel
inside a blackout"* and mean it as rarity. Here the same arithmetic runs the other way: **17 of 45 monthly
editions (38%) are FOMC day one**, so 2027-04-27 is an ordinary member of a large class, not a special case.
**23 of 68** releases sit in a blackout, and **0 of 68** fall on an FOMC day two — the day-two impossibility
the `2026-10-27` ledger recorded holds.

of the `2027-11-30` sibling's rarity finding, and the reason that ledger's framing does not transfer.

November sibling measured (**best p=0.1149**) — so no sibling's control is importable here, and neither is a
