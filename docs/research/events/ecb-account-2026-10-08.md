## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This calendar tracks two ECB decisions and one ECB account. The account was added on 2026-09-05 by
the [`ecb-decision-2026-09-10`](ecb-decision-2026-09-10.md) adjacency sweep, on the reasoning that
"the vote is not published, so this is the only structured evidence on how close the Governing
Council was." That reasoning deserves a test rather than an inheritance. The question is therefore
two-part: **is an ECB account ever a market event — measurably, not by assertion — and if not, is
the qualitative read it offers worth a calendar slot at all?**

**One-line verdict:** the account is **measured to be an ordinary day** in both euro benchmarks and
in EUR/USD across 13 consecutive editions, the "how close was it" premise is **half-right and needs
narrowing** (the account carries no vote and no names, so it yields an adjective rather than a
count), and the slot earns its keep only as an **attribution warning** on a day already contaminated
by the FOMC minutes 18 hours earlier.

**Method:** sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md), plus a
**measured** leg run this session. ECB primaries fetched direct 2026-09-05: the monetary-policy
accounts index (the "typically four weeks" rule and "Next release: 8 October 2026"), the 2026 and
2025 accounts indexes (13 publication dates and the meeting each covers), the account of the
**4–5 February 2026** meeting read in full for structure and attribution convention, and the
quiet-period explainer. Price work used `scripts/research/market-data.mjs` `bars()` with the
instrument cache busted first (`rm -rf node_modules/.cache/earnings-cycle
node_modules/.cache/intraday-edges`, 2026-09-05): Yahoo split/dividend-adjusted daily bars for
`^STOXX50E`, `^GDAXI`, `EURUSD=X`, `^GSPC`, `^VIX`, `FEZ` and `NVDA`. No symbol-keyed instrument was
run — `symbols: []`, no issuer, and `earnings-cycle.mjs`/`intraday-edges.mjs` have no macro mode.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **An ECB account is not a market event — 13 editions, three instruments, one answer.**
   SUPPORTED, and it is the load-bearing leg. Publication dates taken from the ECB's own 2025 and
   2026 accounts indexes (fetched today): **2025-02-27, 04-03, 05-22, 07-03, 08-28, 10-09, 11-27**
   and **2026-01-22, 03-05, 04-16, 05-28, 07-09, 08-27**. Close-to-close absolute move on those days
   against the all-days 2025–26 baseline:

   | Instrument | Baseline mean \|ret\| | Account days (n=13) | Ratio |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Euro Stoxx 50 (`^STOXX50E`) | 0.75% (n=417) | **0.87%** | 1.15× |
   | DAX (`^GDAXI`) | 0.81% (n=424) | **0.78%** | 0.97× |
   | EUR/USD (`EURUSD=X`) | 0.33% (n=433) | **0.36%** | 1.10× |

   One instrument is fractionally above baseline, one fractionally below, one flat. **1 of 13**
   account days reached 2× baseline in any instrument, and leg 2 attributes it elsewhere. There is
   no reading of this table in which the account is a scheduled volatility event.

2. **The one big account day was a global shock, and the most recent one was an NVDA earnings gap
   — both times the account was the coincidence.** SUPPORTED, and this is the practical lesson.
   **2025-04-03** (account of the 5–6 March 2025 meeting) is the sample's only 2× day: STOXX50E
   **−3.59%**, DAX **−3.01%**. Same session, `^GSPC` **−4.84%** and `^VIX` **+39.6% to 30.02**; the
   next session was worse still (SPX **−5.97%**, VIX **45.31**, STOXX50E **−4.60%**). That is the
   tape after the **2025-04-02** US reciprocal-tariff announcement, and it has nothing to do with
   what the ECB published. The near-tape case is subtler and closer to home: **2026-08-27**, the
   most recent account, produced the **largest euro-minus-US divergence of the whole 2026 sample**
   — STOXX50E **−0.71%** against SPX **+0.72%**, a **−1.43pp** residual at the **90th percentile** of
   2026's residual distribution. The US side of that gap is **NVDA +8.74%** on its post-earnings
   session (the same session `FT-AVGO`'s sympathy clause turns on), not anything the account said.
   **An account day that looks like an event has, in both observed instances, been something else.**

3. **The four-week rule is real, its slip is always exactly one week, and it slips ~31% of the
   time.** SUPPORTED, primary + arithmetic on the ECB's own two index pages. Measured across the same
   13 editions, publication minus meeting Day 2: **9 landed at exactly 4 weeks**
   (2025-02-27, 04-03, 07-03, 10-09, 11-27; 2026-03-05, 04-16, 05-28, 07-09) and **4 at exactly 5
   weeks** (2025-05-22, 2025-08-28, 2026-01-22, 2026-08-27). **Zero landed at any other interval.**
   So the ECB's "typically" quantifies to a one-week-slip risk with no other dispersion — which is
   exactly the shape a dated calendar entry can carry honestly. For this event both readings agree
   with the file: 2026-09-10 + 28d = **2026-10-08**, and the accounts index *states* "Next release: 8
   October 2026" independently of the arithmetic — a stated date is stronger evidence than a rule,
   which is why the alternative registered in **FT-ecb-account-2026-10-08-2** is the +7d slip to
   **2026-10-15** rather than an open range. Corroborating trace, weak but consistent: investing.com's
   calendar for this release carries stale rows at **2026-01-15** and **2026-08-20** — the four-week
   dates for the two 2026 editions that actually slipped.

4. **The account publishes no vote and names no member — the "how close was it" premise needs
   narrowing.** SUPPORTED, primary. The **5 March 2026** account (of the 4–5 February meeting) read
   in full today: two sections — *Review of financial, economic and monetary developments and policy
   options* (financial market developments · the global environment and euro-area developments ·
   monetary policy considerations and policy options) and *Governing Council's discussion and
   monetary policy decisions*. **Attribution is uniformly collective**: "Members broadly agreed with
   the assessment", "It was argued that", "It was pointed out that", "Several concerns were raised",
   "A question was raised regarding". No tally, no names, no dissent register. This is a real
   correction to the calendar note that motivated the entry: the account **is** the only public
   record of the argument, but it converts a disagreement into an **adjective** — "some members", "a
   few members", "a number of members" — and a reader who turns that into a vote count has invented
   precision the document does not contain. The qualitative read is still the entry's best use; it
   is simply worth less than a Fed-style dissent line, which is why `low` is the right tier.

5. **It is not even the last word before 10-29 — 13 days of live ECB speech follow it.**
   SUPPORTED, primary. The ECB's quiet-period explainer (fetched today) states the rule verbatim:
   Governing Council members and their alternates "avoid making comments … in the **seven days before
   a scheduled meeting**", with interviews and conference speeches restricted. The next monetary-policy

   leaving **13 days** in which any Governing Council speaker can say something newer, on the record,
   about the same question. A four-week-old anonymised minute is the *oldest* evidence available in
   that window, not the freshest. **That window's start date is proposed to `market-events.ts` in this
   PR** as `ecb-quiet-period-start-2026-10-21` (`estimate`), the ECB analogue of the
   `fomc-blackout-start-*` entries this calendar already carries.

### Honest limits

The measurement is **daily close-to-close** and cannot see an intraday reaction that fires at 13:30
CEST and fades by the close; a 5-minute study of euro benchmarks is not available through this
repo's instruments, so leg 1 is a claim about *daily* significance only. **n is small** — 13 account
days against a 400-plus-day baseline — and no significance test is run; the honest statement is that
the account-day mean sits inside the ordinary distribution, not that a difference has been excluded.
A companion cut is recorded rather than buried because it **refuted the intuitive story**: on the
2026-only sample, ECB *decision* days ran **1.30% / 1.26% / 0.36%** against a **0.77% / 0.81% /
0.29%** baseline (~1.6× for both equity indices), which looked like a clean "decisions are events,
accounts are not" contrast — and it **did not survive** extending to 2025+2026, where decision days
come in at **1.08× / 0.99× / 1.17×**. So the correct finding is narrower than the one first
available: at daily resolution neither an ECB decision day nor an account day is reliably a
bigger-than-average day for these instruments, which strengthens the stand-aside rather than
weakening it. The **publication clock (~13:30 Frankfurt)** is **not** stated on the ECB primary — it
is inferred from investing.com's calendar rendering the 2026 releases at 12:30 UTC in winter and
11:30 UTC in summer, which is self-consistently 13:30 local across the DST boundary, and 2026-10-08
falls before the **2026-10-25** EU DST end so 13:30 CEST = 07:30 ET; leg 7's ordering claim is sound
on *sequence* (Fed minutes the prior evening, account mid-euro-session, both before the US open) and
merely conventional on the exact minute — the same weakness the sibling ledger recorded for the
presser times. Leg 6 is a **conditional on an event that has not occurred**, and every market-pricing
figure it borrows is a press-cited snapshot from the sibling ledger (98.9% on 09-01, near-100% on
09-04, the 91%/78% poll surveyed 08-31→09-03) — no futures curve was fetched there or here.
`^STOXX50E` and `^GDAXI` bars are Yahoo index series, unadjusted for dividends by construction, and
the 2026-08-27 residual leg pairs a euro cash close against a US close **six and a half hours
later**, so it measures a session pair rather than a synchronous window. Finally, leg 8 is a null
claim, and the honest failure mode of a null is an uninstrumented channel — which is why the "Today"
kill switch is written as a measurable price test on named tickers rather than as a restatement.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `estimate`):** **stand aside, and downgrade the expectation the calendar entry was
filed with.** The 2026-10-08 account is measured — not asserted — to be an ordinary trading day:
**1.15×** baseline for the Euro Stoxx 50, **0.97×** for the DAX and **1.10×** for EUR/USD across the
13 accounts published in 2025–26, with the sample's only 2× day attributable to the April-2025
tariff shock and its largest euro-vs-US divergence attributable to an NVDA earnings gap. This book
holds nothing with a euro-rates channel and none is proposed. Two analytical commitments follow.
First, the entry's stated purpose is **narrowed**: the account is the only public record of the
09-10 argument, but it publishes **no vote and no names**, so it yields an adjective, not a count,
and it is followed by **13 days of unrestricted Governing Council speech** before the ~10-21 quiet
period — it is the oldest evidence in the pre-10-29 window, not the best. Second, it inherits an
**attribution discipline** in the mirror of the sibling ledger's: `fomc-minutes-2026-10-07` publishes
after the euro close the previous evening, so the 10-08 euro session opens on the Fed, and no 10-08
move may be credited to the account until the minutes have been ruled out. Estimates widen caution
and license nothing.

**Kill switches:**

- **Channel kill (the one that would rebuild this doc):** a tracked name (NVDA/AVGO/MRVL/CRWV)
  moving **>2%** in the **07:00–09:30 ET** window on **2026-10-08** with the tape attributing it to
  the account. Leg 8's "no price channel" claim would be false. Score by **2026-10-09**.
- **Event kill (registered):** the **2026-10-08** Euro Stoxx 50 close-to-close move exceeding
  **1.50%** (2× the measured 0.75% baseline) **and** EUR/USD exceeding **0.66%** (2× 0.33%). Leg 1's
  13-edition measurement would have missed a regime in which accounts do move markets. Registered as
  **FT-ecb-account-2026-10-08-1**, score by **2026-10-09**.
- **Date kill (registered):** the account publishing on **2026-10-15** rather than 2026-10-08 — the
  +7d slip that hit 4 of the last 13 editions. The header date breaks and the corridor changes (it
  would land beside `cpi-2026-10-14` and `ppi-2026-10-15`). Registered as
  **FT-ecb-account-2026-10-08-2**, score by **2026-10-09**.
- **Purpose kill:** the **2026-09-10** presser delivering explicit path guidance, closing the ~50bp
  terminal gap. The account's one qualitative use disappears and this entry becomes a formality the
  next pulse should retire in a line. Score by **2026-09-11**.
- **Staleness kill (the other direction):** a Governing Council member giving dated, on-the-record
  path guidance between **2026-10-08 and 2026-10-21**. Leg 5's "live speech beats the record" claim
  would be *confirmed*, and the entry's value shrinks further rather than growing. Re-check at the
  pulse after 10-08.
- **Structure kill:** the account of the 09-10 meeting publishing a **vote count or named
  attribution** — a break from 13 editions of collective phrasing. Leg 4 dies and the entry deserves
  a tier upgrade. Score by **2026-10-09**.

Two forward tests registered in [`forward-tests.md`](../forward-tests.md) —
**FT-ecb-account-2026-10-08-1** (the null-event test, whose asymmetry is stated at registration: 12
