## At a glance

**TL;DR.** Read it, never trade it — and this pass found the reason on the UN's own pages rather
than in the wire copy. The vote (**estimate**, 09-17) renews a **monitoring body**, not a sanction:
snapback nullified resolution 2231's sunset clauses in September 2025, so the measures themselves
have **no expiry** and a veto cannot lift one of them. What expires is the Panel of Experts, and
`main.un.org` says so verbatim — *"The Panel comprises up to eight experts. Its current mandate ends
on 26 September 2026."* Two further primary reads say the body being renewed is **not currently
functioning**: the 1737 Committee page states *"The Chair of the Committee has not yet been elected
by the Security Council"* — eleven months after snapback — and the Panel's own Reports page lists
**four reports, the newest S/2015/401 of 2 June 2015, and nothing since**, even though its mandate
requires a final report *"no later than 30 days prior to the termination of its mandate"* (due
~2026-08-27; not published as of 2026-09-04). So the base case is that a Russia and/or China veto
kills a panel that has not filed a report in eleven years — the exact **DPRK 1718 precedent** (Russia
veto 2024-03-28; panel dead 2024-04-30; sanctions survived; monitoring moved outside the UN).
The market case is stronger still: when the far larger event — the whole snapback — landed in
September 2025, Iran's exports hit their **2025 high** (~63.8m bbl, ~2.13 mb/d, **87.35% to China**).
And the arithmetic kills the trade outright: Brent's trailing-60-session **median daily move is
2.01%** on the live Hormuz conflict, so any vote-day signal is smaller than the noise already there.
Meanwhile equity vol sits at a three-month **low** (VIX 14.03) and defense at a three-month **low**
(ITA 225.33, −9.9% in 21 sessions) while crude is near a three-month **high** — the equity tape is
not pricing Iran at all. `symbols: []`; nothing to hold.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The 09-26 expiry is now UN-primary; the 09-17 vote date is not, and the `estimate` label is
   correct on both the fact and the schema.** SUPPORTED, with the label unchanged.
   `main.un.org/securitycouncil/en/sanctions/1737/panel-of-experts/work-and-mandate` states verbatim:
   *"The Panel comprises up to eight experts. Its current mandate ends on 26 September 2026."* That
   upgrades the **expiry** from wire-reported to primary. The **vote date** did not upgrade: the UN's
   own account of the September programme (`press.un.org`, 2026-09-01) confirms the Council will
   address *"renewal of the Panel of Experts established pursuant to resolution 1929 (2010)"* and
   quotes President Jérôme Bonnafont that *"the heart of [Council] discussions this month will be the
   nuclear issue"*, alongside *"freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz"* — but names **no
   date**. `un.org`'s September programme-of-work PDF path returns **404**, and
   `securitycouncilreport.org` serves a Cloudflare JavaScript challenge to this lane on both the
   monthly-forecast and What's-in-Blue paths. So 09-17 rests where it did: Reuters, diplomats,
   unnamed. Two independent reasons the label could not flip even on a better read: `CONFIRMED_PREFIX`
   in `scripts/event-scan-validation.mjs` is `IR|CAL|BLS|FED|PJM|SEC|TSY|OCC|BEA|CENSUS|ISM|CB|UMICH`
   with **no slot for a UN or diplomatic primary** — the **eleventh** ledger to bank that gap, after
   the ten already carrying it — and the lane's own no-self-confirm limit applies regardless.

2. **The vote renews a referee, not a rule — a veto lifts no sanction.** SUPPORTED, and this is the
   load-bearing leg. Snapback, completed 2025-09-27 after the E3 triggered it on 2025-08-28,
   reactivated resolutions **1696, 1737, 1747, 1803, 1835 and 1929** and **nullified resolution 2231's
   sunset clauses** — the measures therefore have no expiry to arrive at. What *does* expire is the
   Panel's mandate, and the 1737 Committee itself is a standing subsidiary organ, not term-limited.
   Read the measures the UN page lists — proliferation-related embargo, arms restrictions, travel ban,
   assets freeze — and none of them lapses on 09-26 under any outcome of this vote. **A lapse is a
   loss of eyes, not of teeth.** Anyone reading a veto as sanctions relief, or a renewal as
   tightening, has the mechanism backwards.

3. **The body being renewed appears not to have functioned since 2015.** SUPPORTED on the records,
   stated as inference on the conclusion — and it is the finding that reframes the event. Three
   primary observations, all read direct 2026-09-04: (a) the 1737 Committee page states *"The Chair of
   the Committee has not yet been elected by the Security Council"* — eleven months after the
   Committee was restored; (b) the Panel's **Reports** page lists exactly four documents —
   **S/2015/401 (2 June 2015)**, S/2014/394, S/2013/331, S/2012/395 — and nothing after; (c) the
   Panel's mandate requires *"an interim report on its work no later than 90 days after the Panel's
   appointment, and a final report to the Council no later than 30 days prior to the termination of
   its mandate."* Against a 09-26 expiry, that final report was due **~2026-08-27**; it does not
   exist on the UN's page as of 2026-09-04, and neither does any interim report from a post-snapback
   appointment. **Honest limit:** absence from a UN webpage is evidence of non-publication, not proof
   of non-appointment — a page can lag. But three independent absences pointing the same way, plus an
   unelected chair, make "the Panel has not been reconstituted in any working sense" the strong read.
   Registered as a checkable test rather than asserted: `FT-unsc-iran-panel-mandate-vote-2026-09-17-2`.

4. **The base case is failure, and it has a clean precedent with a scored outcome.** SUPPORTED.
   Russia and China stated jointly in **March 2026** that the Council *"cannot proceed to re-establish
   the 1737 Committee or its Panel of Experts"* — a position about the panel's legal existence, which
   no drafting compromise reaches. The Council has already failed twice on this file inside twelve
   months: the resolution to continue sanctions relief failed, and Russia and China's own six-month
   extension draft failed **2025-09-26**, with sanctions returning the next day. The precedent that
   prices the outcome is **DPRK**: Russia vetoed renewal of the 1718 Committee's Panel of Experts on
   **2024-03-28** (China abstaining, breaking 14 years of unanimous adoption), the panel's mandate
   ended **2024-04-30**, the DPRK sanctions themselves survived untouched, and monitoring migrated
   outside the UN to a coalition body (the MSMT, launched October 2024). Expect the same three-step
   here: lapse, sanctions unaffected, monitoring reconstituted outside the Council.

5. **No price channel — and the reason is measured, not asserted.** SUPPORTED. Three separate
   readings kill the trade. **(a) The precedent event moved nothing in the right direction.** UN
   snapback — the whole architecture, not one panel — landed in September 2025, and Iran's exports
   that month hit their **2025 high**: ~63.8m bbl, averaging **2,127,714 b/d**, **87.35% to China**,
   at a 5–10% discount to Brent. The binding enforcement is US secondary sanctions and naval presence,
   not UN monitoring. **(b) The signal is smaller than the ambient noise.** Brent's trailing-60-session
   daily |move| has a **median of 2.01%** and a **p75 of 3.89%** (2y: 1.32% / 2.45%) — the live Hormuz
   conflict has roughly **1.5×'d** crude's own volatility, so a vote-day effect is not separable from
   a Tuesday. **(c) The equity wrapper is not transmitting any of it.** XLE's trailing-60 distribution
   (median **0.88%**, p75 **1.40%**) is indistinguishable from its 2y (0.83% / 1.44%), VIX sits at
   **14.03** — its three-month **low** — and ITA at **225.33** is at its three-month **low**, −9.9%
   over 21 sessions, while CL (**90.74**, +17.4%/21d), BZ (**95.49**, +15.8%) and USO (**140.99**,
   +18.6%) sit near three-month highs. The entire Iran premium is in the barrel; none of it is in the
   equity book this calendar trades.

6. **Attribution on 09-17 is already impossible, by construction.** SUPPORTED. Nine tracked events sit
   within ±5 days: Houston and Gastech (09-14), the 20Y auction (09-15), **FOMC, retail sales and
   import/export prices (09-16)**, the 10Y TIPS auction (**09-17, the same day**), quarterly opex
   (09-18) and the 2Y auction (09-22). The vote lands the session after a hike-modal FOMC decision and
   the session before triple witching. Note also the calendar-entry observation that motivated this
   research and survives it: **09-17 and 09-26 are different dates**, and only the second is primary.
   A slipped vote still meets a hard deadline — one that falls on a **Saturday**, during UNGA leaders'
   week, with the nearest sessions being Friday 09-25 (durable goods, UMich final) and Monday 09-28.

### What the conditions support

Nothing to buy or sell. Two things to *do*: (1) watch the **manner** of the vote — vetoed on the
record, withdrawn before a vote, or adopted — as this calendar's only dated read on whether a
US/E3–Russia/China channel on Iran exists, which is the same channel any Hormuz off-ramp needs; and
(2) keep the **transit count** as the tracked variable in this theatre, because it is the one with a
measured price relationship. The vote is a free observation attached to an expensive conflict.

### Honest limits

- No primary UN document naming **09-17** was obtained; the date is wire-reported and the entry stays `estimate`/`NEWS:`.
- Leg 3's conclusion is inference from three absences on UN web pages, not from a statement that the Panel was never appointed. It is registered as a test, not banked as a fact.
- The DPRK analog is **one** precedent. It is structurally close (same instrument, same vetoing state, same "sanctions survive" mechanics) but n=1.
- Iranian export figures are secondary (FDD, tanker-tracking derived) and vary by counter.
- Hormuz transit counts quoted in press coverage are point observations, not an official series.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (the event is `estimate`-dated; nothing here is date-keyed action).** Stand aside on the
09-17 vote and on the 09-26 expiry alike. No position, no hedge, no sizing — `symbols: []` and the
mechanism carries no channel: post-snapback UN measures have no sunset, so neither outcome moves a
sanction, and the enforcement that binds Iranian barrels is American and naval, not multilateral.
Treat the vote as an **observation**, graded on its manner rather than its result, and treat Hormuz
transit counts as the variable that actually prices this theatre.

**Kill switches — any one of these ends the stance and earns its own ledger row:**

- **A Panel of Experts report for the 1737 regime dated after 2015 appears on the Council record before 2026-09-17.** The "eleven years dark" read dies, the lapse forfeits something real, and leg 3 is withdrawn.
- **The renewal is adopted on or before 2026-09-26.** Russia and China's stated legal position gave way, which is a materially better read on the channel than this stance assumes.
- **A crude move >5% within 5 sessions of the vote that is separable** from strikes, transit counts, OPEC+ and the FOMC. Separability is the bar; an unseparable move is not evidence.
- **VIX ≥ 20 or ITA reclaiming its 21-session high before 2026-09-26** — the equity tape starting to price the Iran theatre would break leg 5(c), which is the reason no hedge is held.
- **Hormuz commodity transits falling below ~5/day on a sustained basis** — an escalation this stance does not model, and the one development that would make a UN-track headline worth reading twice.
