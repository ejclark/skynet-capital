## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The proposal that created this event stated its own thesis in one line: *"The proposer's whole
information content is a single number — the 10-Year TIPS new-issue size — and this is the session
it becomes public, seven days before the auction."* It then supplied a base-rate argument for a
step-up: *"the 10-Year series has never held one size for four consecutive new issues … each of the
three prior runs that reached three was ended by a step-up at the fourth, 3 of 3."*

Both claims are testable against primary data, and this session's question is exactly that: **is
2027-01-14 where the number becomes public, and is the step-up counter-case real?**

**One-line verdict:** neither survives. The number becomes public on **2026-11-04**, in writing,
71 days earlier — and the "never four in a row" claim is an artifact of a short window that hides a
nine-long run and a six-long run. The event is a **read-back with one live unknown in the bills**.

**4. The proposal's step-up counter-case is a window artifact — REFUTED, and the correction cuts
two ways.** Pulling the TIPS record back to **1997** rather than 2018 (268 rows across two

### What the conditions support

Nothing directional — the standard answer for a `symbols: []`, `estimate`-dated rates event. What
the conditions support is a **reading correction, and it is the deliverable**. The proposal filed
this event as a reveal; it is a read-back. A calendar that treats 2027-01-14 as the day the 2027
TIPS size becomes knowable will be **71 days late**, and the date it should have been watching —
**2026-11-04** — is already tracked. Concretely: any rates ledger in this corridor reasoning from
*"the January TIPS size is unknown"* should carry the qualifier *"until 2026-11-04."*

Second, the two base-rate corrections in leg 4 are worth more than this event's own numbers. This
calendar has been quoting a TIPS window that starts in 2018 or 2023 and calling the results
structural. Back to 1997 the same series shows a nine-long freeze, a six-long freeze, a **downward**
step, and one dated instance of a size change expressing at a reopening. Those are regime
observations. The distinction matters because the sibling ledgers use the short-window version as a
**kill switch**, and a kill switch built on a window is a kill switch that fires on regime change
rather than on the thing it names.

Third, the honest unknown here is the **bill magnitude**, and January is where it lives. That is a
cheap, dated, already-scheduled read — and unlike the 11-12 sibling, there is no early tell for it.

treasurydirect's `upcoming` feed does not reach 2027. **The run analysis in leg 4 mixes issuance
