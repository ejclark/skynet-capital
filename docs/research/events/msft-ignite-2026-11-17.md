## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Microsoft Ignite 2026 runs Nov 17–20 at Moscone Center, San Francisco (date **estimate**), three
weeks after MSFT's estimated FY27-Q1 print. Does it give MSFT a tradable edge distinct from that
print — and if not, can this ledger say so with a measurement rather than an argument?

### One-line verdict

No edge, and the refusal is measured rather than asserted: Ignite week sits inside a post-print
window where MSFT lags its benchmark two-thirds of the time (n=15), MSFT trailed QQQ in each of the
last three Ignite weeks (n=3, descriptive only), the keynote session itself is indistinguishable
from noise, and the 2026 week is contaminated by four tracked events — so watch-only, no position,
at every horizon (**estimate**).

### Method

Both repo instruments re-run fresh **2026-09-06** with the cache busted first per

### Legs tested

**1. "Ignite 2026 is Nov 17–20 at Moscone Center, San Francisco" — SUPPORTED; status stays
`estimate`.** The venue's own event page (moscone.com/events/microsoft-ignite-2026) and
registration-open coverage both give Nov 17–20 at Moscone, and this session adds two corroborating
details. First, an **optional pre-day on Monday 2026-11-16**, matching Ignite 2025's structure
(pre-day 11/17, main conference 11/18–21). Second, **2026-11-17 is a Tuesday**, and the Ignite
keynote has landed on the Tuesday in each recent edition (2024: Tue 11/19, Chicago; 2025: Tue 11/18,
San Francisco) — so the calendar's point-date is the likely keynote day, not merely day one.
Against that: **microsoft.com's own properties still yield no date text.**
`ignite.microsoft.com/en-US/home` re-fetched today rendered only "Your home for Microsoft Ignite,"
and the Microsoft partner-blog registration post rendered "San Francisco" with no dates. No `IR:`
primary, therefore **no flip** — the calendar entry's `notes` gain the pre-day and keynote-day
detail in this PR, its `status` does not.

**2. The earnings instrument's blind spot is closed — and it answers the sibling ledger's open
question. This is the session's headline finding.** [`msft-2026-10-27-print`](msft-2026-10-27-print.md)
has carried an explicit "instrument integrity caveat" since its initial research: the study's newest
print was **2026-04-29**, so every modern-era cell excluded the **2026-07-29** print — the largest
observation in the sample — and the S3 reaction-day fade's post-2024 red streak could not be checked
against it. Today's run ends at **2026-07-29 (88 prints, up from 87)** and the modern cell moves
**n=14 → n=15**. The fade cell moves with it: 2023-2026 win **13% (2/15)**, mean **−0.85%**, median
−1.07%, against 7% (1/14) / −1.13% before. Arithmetic alone implies the added observation was green;
it was verified directly from daily bars rather than inferred from a rounded win rate — **2026-07-30
opened 437.90** (a **+12.13%** gap off the 390.54 close) **and closed 451.10, +3.01% open→close**.
Green. That is verbatim the condition the print ledger's own kill switch named: *"if it was green,

**Scoring it is that ledger's job, not this one's** — its rows are append-only and belong to the lane
that owns them; this doc supplies the dated receipt and routes it.

### What the conditions support

Nothing directional, at any horizon, in either direction. The deployable set is the universal guards
— and they belong to the **print**, not to this event: S2 (any paper MSFT exposure flat through the
estimate-widened Oct 27–29 window) and E1 (execution hygiene). Ignite's own tradable output is a
*watch*: whether the keynote extends or punctures whatever narrative the first dollar-denominated
Azure line sets three weeks earlier.

### Honest limits

The date is an **estimate** with no `IR:` primary. The Ignite-week study is hand-built from daily
bars, not a repo instrument, at **n=3**, with 2023's window approximate and every window overlapping
November opex — it is descriptive, and it would be dishonest to call three observations a reaction
function. The D+11/D+21 mapping assumes a 10/27 print that is itself an estimate; an IR confirm on
10/28 shifts Ignite one session within the window and changes nothing material. The post-print cell
measures **drift**, not a conference — nothing here isolates an Ignite effect from the drift it sits
inside, and that cuts against a hypothetical short as much as against a long. Keynote content is
unknowable pre-event. And Ignite 2026 is the first edition where the market will have a **published
quarterly Azure dollar line** (the 2026-09-02 8-K's two-segment recast) to react against — no
history exists for how that changes the conference's read-through, so the n=3 prior is drawn from a
disclosure regime that no longer applies.
