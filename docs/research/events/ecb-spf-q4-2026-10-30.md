entry was filed as the forecasters' read *after* the 10-29 ECB decision. The ECB's own Q3 report says SPF rounds are

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The September ECB ledger registered one durable claim (FT-ecb-decision-2026-09-10-2): that the economists beat the futures strip on where euro rates stop. The SPF is the only dated primary on this calendar that re-measures the forecaster side of that gap. So: **what will the Q4 round say, is it an event for this book, and what did the calendar entry get wrong?**

**One-line verdict:** it is a free scoreboard, not an event — expect the professionals to have lifted their peak from July's 2.50% to 2.75%, and expect it to be fielded before the decision it was filed to follow.

**Method:** the ECB's SPF index page and the Q3 and Q2 2026 reports fetched direct (HTTP 200) today, 2026-10-16. Market and poll figures are press-cited (RTE re-publishing Reuters, fetched through a summarising tool, so numbers are secondary); Morningstar returned HTTP 403. Prices from Yahoo daily closes. No instrument scripts: `symbols: []`, and the earnings tools have no macro mode.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The Q4 round is fielded before 10-29, so it cannot read the decision.** SUPPORTED, ECB primary. The Q3 report (24 July) states the rounds "conducted ... in January and July 2026" for Q1 and Q3; the Q3 text speaks of "the survey's later cut-off" capturing the mid-June de-escalation. The Q4 field window is not printed on the pages fetched, so "early October" is inferred from that quarterly rhythm and the 10-30 publication, and is not itself sourced. This corrects the calendar entry's "after they have seen whether October delivered".

2. **July's baseline is far from today's world.** SUPPORTED, primary vs live prices. Q3 SPF (24 July): HICP **2.7 / 2.2 / 2.0** (2026/27/28), longer-term (2031) **2.0%** with ~65% of respondents at exactly 2.0%; core **2.4 / 2.2 / 2.1**; GDP **0.6 / 1.2 / 1.3**; wages **3.2 / 3.0 / 2.8**; oil assumed ~**$80** in H2 2026, EUR/USD ~**1.16**. Since then: ECB staff projections (09-10) at HICP **3.0 / 2.5 / 2.1**, September flash HICP **3.8%**, Brent **$109.69** (10-16 Yahoo close), EUR/USD **1.1244**. The oil gap alone is ~$30 per barrel.

3. **The deposit-rate distribution was already at the economists' camp, and has moved with the facts.** SUPPORTED, primary plus press. July: modal DFR **2.25%** for Q3 (61% of respondents), **2.50%** for Q4 2026 and Q1 2027, peak "around 2.50% at the turn of 2026-27", then drifting to 2.00%. The ECB hiked to 2.50% on 09-10. Reuters (10-05→10-08, 73 economists): **70** hold on 10-29, **64** hike in December, peak **2.75% (40 of 70)** or **3.00% (24 of 70)**; "market pricing: nearly three hikes by end-2027". A month earlier more than 90% expected no further hike. So the economists' own view has moved toward the strip — FT-ecb-decision-2026-09-10-2's gap is narrower than when registered, not closed.

4. **The release is not a price event.** MIXED, n=5 only. The ECB page lists only five releases (25 Jul 2025, 31 Oct 2025, 6 Feb 2026, 4 May 2026, 24 Jul 2026). Absolute daily moves on those days against the trailing-two-year mean: EUR/USD **0.25% vs 0.33%** (0.78×, three dates with data), Euro Stoxx 50 **1.02% vs 0.74%** (1.38×), DAX **0.91% vs 0.78%** (1.16×). Two of the five (05-04, 07-24) carried the largest equity moves and fell in a war-driven tape, so the sample cannot separate the SPF from the news around it. Indistinguishable from baseline is the honest read; the release time is not sourced.

5. **The 10-30 morning is crowded.** SUPPORTED, own calendar (`event-scan --on-date`): BoJ decision, ECI (confirmed, high), Chicago PMI, Tokyo CPI, BoE DMP, UK Blue/Pink Book and the Russell month-end recap all land the same date. Any tape read must clear them first.
