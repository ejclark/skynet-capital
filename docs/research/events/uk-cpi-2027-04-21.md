### The question, plainly

This entry arrived by **proposal, twice**, and the two proposals point at different things. Both were

- `proposals/uk-cpi-2027-04-21.from-boe-decision-2027-04-29.json` — filed by a **central-bank** lane.
  Its claim: on the April 2026 analogue this print lands on the exact day the Monetary Policy

  path by a single session or just outside it."*

Being the baseline endpoint of somebody else's measurement is not, by itself, research. So the
question this lane owns is: **is this endpoint actually quiet — and are the two claims attached to it
true as stated?**

**One-line verdict:** the endpoint is quiet, **measurably and asymmetrically so** (March electricity
m/m has moved 0.30% at most in eight years, against April's median 8.79%); the Easter grade inherited
from the sibling is **wrong at the item level and right at the headline**, and the difference is a
regression with r = 0.856; and the MPR claim has **the right dates around the wrong concept** — the
15-day window conditions market prices, not data.

### Leg 4 — "The Easter effect here is MIXED in direction and cannot be netted out." **REFUTED at the item level, SUPPORTED at the headline — and the two halves are the finding.**

Easter Sunday 2027 is **28 March** and 2026's was **5 April** (computus). The sibling lane graded the
direction **MIXED**, reasoning that *"the two nearest analogues disagree in sign (Easter 27 Mar 2016
gave −14.3%; Easter 31 Mar 2024 gave +6.5%)"* — both April readings — and concluded the effect
*"cannot be netted out"*.

**Those two years do not disagree.** They are four days apart on a strong, signed relationship, and
what moves between them is not the *size* of the Easter effect but **which month it lands in**.
Regress the **April-minus-March** month-on-month swing in **D7EH** (air fares) on Easter Sunday's
distance from 1 March, 2001–2026:

| | slope | r | R² | residual sd | n |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Air fares (D7EH)** | **+1.543pp / day** | **0.856** | **0.732** | 8.13pp | 26 |
| Headline (D7BT) | +0.022pp / day | 0.500 | 0.250 | **0.33pp** | 26 |

A later Easter shifts the spike out of March and into April at about **1.5pp of air-fare index per
day**. Fitted for 2027's Easter at day 28: **April minus March = −13.1pp** — the spike is in
**March**, in this print.

**The raw split says the same thing without a model.** March-Easter years vs April-Easter years,
1997–2026:

| | March m/m | April m/m |
|---|---|---|
| Easter in **March** (n=7) | mean **+7.09%**, median +5.41% | mean **−3.42%**, median −4.70% |
| Easter in **April** (n=23) | mean +1.23%, median +1.35% | mean **+10.64%**, median +7.34% |

**And the sub-sample that actually matches 2027 is 3 for 3 in both months.** Years with Easter on or
before 28 March (2027's is the 28th):

| Year | Easter | March air fares | April air fares |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2005 | 27 Mar | **+5.41%** | −7.14% |
| 2008 | 23 Mar | **+8.51%** | −4.70% |
| 2016 | 27 Mar | **+22.93%** | −14.29% |

The three late-March years (2002, 2013, 2024 — all Easter on the **31st**) are the ambiguous ones,
and **2024 is the single year whose spike landed in April instead** (March +0.07%, April +6.49%).
That is exactly what the slope predicts, and 2027's Easter is three days earlier than 2024's.

**Now the honest half, which sustains the sibling's conclusion by a different route.** Run the same
regression on the headline and it collapses: **R² 0.250**, residual **sd 0.33pp**, fitted
April-minus-March for a 28-March Easter **−0.06pp**. So at the headline the Easter effect is
**−0.06pp against a VAT step of +0.098pp** — the same order of magnitude, the **opposite sign**, and
partially cancelling — while the residual sd is **3.4× the VAT step**. Sized at the 2026 air-fares
weight (CJXW = 5.2374/1000), the March excess implied by the matching sub-sample is **+0.021 to
+0.113pp** of headline, i.e. as much as a full rounding unit at the top and nothing at the bottom.

**The correct grade is therefore split, not MIXED:** at the item level the Easter confound is
**modelable and signed** (and this print is where it lands); at the headline it is **not readable**,
which is the third independent reason — after the sibling's Legs 2 and 3 — that the headline is the
wrong venue. Registered as **FT-uk-cpi-2027-04-21-2**.

| July 2026 | 30 Jul 2026 | 15 UK working days to **20 July 2026** | 10 days |

And the March 2026 CPI was indeed released **22 April 2026** — ONS's own page reads "Released: 22
April 2026 7:00am" — the same day the April 2026 window closed. Applying the 8–10 day band to the
2027-04-29 MPR puts its close at **2027-04-19 to 2027-04-21**.

**But the 15-working-day window is not a data cut-off, and the proposal's "inside the published
forecast's market path" reads it as one.** The Bank's own words put it on market prices: the April
2026 Report's Chart 1.15 footnote reads *"The April 2026 curves are estimated based on the 15 UK
working days to 22 April 2026"* — market-implied policy-rate and instantaneous-forward curves — and
Section 3 conditions energy *"on market futures curves in the 15 UK working days to 22 April 2026"*.
The Report uses a **different** phrase for statistical inputs; in the same document, a chart omits an
April Citi/YouGov reading *"released after the data cut-off for incorporation in the charts"*.

**So the question the proposal poses does not arise.** Whether the MPC has *seen* this print is not
in doubt — eight days is comfortably inside any forecast round. What the window actually decides is
much smaller and much sharper: whether the **market's reaction** to this print (gilt and SONIA
repricing on Wednesday 2027-04-21) enters the conditioning average at all, and at what weight.

- **8-day analogue** → window closes 2027-04-21 → the reaction is the **final day of fifteen**, a
  **1/15 = 6.7%** weight in the average.
- **10-day analogue** → window closes 2027-04-19 → the reaction is **entirely outside** the path.

That is the whole content of the claim, and it is worth about one sentence in the April 2027 MPR's
own footnote — which is what settles it, and which is what
**FT-boe-decision-2027-04-29-1** should be scored against. The observation is recorded here for that
lane; its fragment is append-only and not this lane's to edit.

### Leg 8 — "The March index cleanly contains the zero rate and the April index cleanly does not." **NOT ESTABLISHED — a named open uncertainty, and the most likely way the two-endpoint framing breaks.**

SI 2026/987's operative words, read direct today, bind the relief to *"supplies **made** in the
period beginning with 1st October 2026 and ending with 31st March 2027"*. Domestic electricity is a
**continuous** supply, and a continuous supply's VAT tax point is normally the earlier of the invoice
being issued or payment being received — not the month of consumption. A bill issued in April for
March consumption can therefore sit on either side of the boundary depending on a supplier's billing
cycle.

Whether that smears the step across the March and April **indices** depends entirely on what ONS
collects: a published tariff unit rate (which would step cleanly on 1 April) or something closer to a
modelled bill (which would not). The ONS methodology page that would answer it was not located from
two guessed URLs, both 404 — so this is recorded as an **uncertainty, not a finding**, and it is not
carried into any call above.

**What resolves it, dated:** ONS's own April 2027 bulletin commentary on 2027-05-19, or the CPI
technical manual once its correct URL is found — which is a free task for the next pulse, not a
blocker. Note that it cuts *against* this ledger's own anchor claim if it turns out to be real, which
is why it is stated rather than buried: a smeared step would put some VAT movement into the **March**
index, and **FT-uk-cpi-2027-04-21-1** is the test that would catch it.

### Honest limits

- **Data vintage.** Every MM23 series fetched today ends at **May 2026**, three months behind this
  session's date. Nothing above depends on the June–August 2026 readings, but the electricity and gas
  history is one cap quarter shorter than it will be at the next pulse, and the October 2026 zero-rate
  entry itself is not yet observable in any series.
- **The Easter regression is a reduced form.** It regresses the April-minus-March air-fare swing on
  the Easter date and on nothing else; oil, capacity, and the 2020–21 pandemic years are all in the
  sample untreated. **R² = 0.732 is the whole fit, not an Easter-only attribution**, and the residual
  sd of 8.13pp is large in absolute terms.
