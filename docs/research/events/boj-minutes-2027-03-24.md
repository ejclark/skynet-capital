## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This calendar now tracks four central-bank meeting accounts, and three have ledgers that all refuse.
The [ECB account](ecb-account-2026-10-08.md) refuses on lag (~4 weeks, 1.15× a Euro Stoxx session);
[FOMC minutes](fomc-minutes-2026-12-30.md) refuse on lag (3 weeks, n=3 inside ±0.3%); and the
[BoJ Summary of Opinions](boj-summary-of-opinions-2027-03-29.md) refuses *despite* being the fastest
of the three — median 10-day lag across all 86 editions, and still 0.84× an ordinary S&P session.
This entry was proposed by that SoO ledger's own adjacency sweep, which named it "the richer of the
two BoJ documents" in the late-March corridor and left the claim untested.

So the question is not "is a meeting account tradable" — three siblings answered no. It is the two
this venue actually raises: **how slow is the BoJ's Minutes, given the same bank owns the fastest
account of the three majors — and is the SoO ledger's ranking of the two documents right?**

**One-line verdict: the BoJ Minutes are the slowest meeting account of the three majors (median 52
days vs the Fed's measured 21 and the ECB's stated ~28) and, uniquely among them, are published
*after the next meeting has already decided* — 84 times out of 84 — which makes them structurally
obsolete on arrival; they are measurably a non-event (Nikkei median 0.84×, S&P median 1.03× / 0.90×
trimmed, yen retention 4%) and the SoO ledger's "attributed" ranking is half wrong.** Stance: stand
aside.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The BoJ Minutes are the slowest meeting account of the three majors — SUPPORTED, measured, and
   it is the exact mirror of the sibling's finding.** All 85 Minutes the Bank published 2016-03-18
   through 2026-08-05 were parsed today from its own year archives. Publication minus the meeting's
   final day:

   | Range | **28-74** | 7-21 | **19-21** | — |

   The BoJ and FOMC columns are this session's own parses; the ECB figure is its stated convention,
   inherited from the [ecb-account ledger](ecb-account-2026-10-08.md) and **not** re-derived. **The
   same institution publishes the fastest account of the three majors and the slowest** — its
   Minutes lag is **5.2×** its own Summary of Opinions. The 2027 case is ordinary for its cohort:
   **Jan 22 → Mar 24 is 61 days**.

2. **The lag range is fully explained by meeting spacing, not by variable drafting — SUPPORTED, and
   it is what makes leg 3 predictive.** The 28-74 spread looks like noise and is not. The **28-day
   minimum** is the 2020-06-19 minutes of the *unscheduled* **2020-05-22** emergency MPM, whose next
   meeting was only 3.5 weeks later. The **68-74 day maxima** (2017-09-26, 2020-09-24, 2021-09-28,
   2022-09-28) are all **July** meetings: the BoJ holds **no August MPM** — meeting-month counts
   parsed today are Jan 11, Mar 11, Apr 10, Jun 11, Jul 10, Sep 10, Oct 9, Dec 10, **Aug 0** — so a
   July meeting's record waits for September.

3. **The publication date is a function of the *next* meeting, and it is 84/84 — SUPPORTED,
   measured, and no ledger on this calendar carries it.** Pairing each release with the first MPM
   that followed its own meeting:

   | Days after the NEXT MPM's close | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 9 | 10 | 11 | 13 | 28 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | Count | 14 | 1 | **37** | 22 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 1 |

   **n=84, median 5, and every single one is positive** — the BoJ has never published a set of
   minutes before the next meeting concluded. **74/84 (88%) land in the 3-6 day window.** The ten
   exceptions have one shared cause: **nine are early-May releases** (2016-05-09, 2018-05-07,
   2019-05-08, 2021-05-06, 2022-05-09, 2023-05-08, 2026-05-07 and neighbours), i.e. the
   post-April-MPM slot pushed out by Japan's **Golden Week** holidays; the tenth is the 2020-06-19
   COVID-emergency case from leg 2. Against this, the **Fed is 0/43** — its minutes have never
   published after the next meeting, because its stated 21-day convention sits well inside a median
   **42-day** inter-meeting gap (both figures parsed from the Fed's own calendar today).

   **The obvious mechanism is statutory approval at the subsequent meeting, and this session could
   not source it.** boj.or.jp's law pages returned **404** and japaneselawtranslation.go.jp returned
   **403** to this runner, so no text of the Bank of Japan Act is quoted here. The 84/84 regularity
