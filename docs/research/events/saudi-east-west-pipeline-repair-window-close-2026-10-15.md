## At a glance

worth carrying forward is that nobody has priced it.** Saudi Arabia shut the 1,200km East-West
pipeline after drones hit it in two areas, near Riyadh and near Medina; the line normally moves

### Leg 1 — "2026-10-15 is a real milestone" → **REFUTED**

Nothing is scheduled for this date. It is arithmetic: five weeks past a shutdown, applied to the far
end of a range two unnamed officials gave the AP. Riyadh "has not yet disclosed … a restart
timetable" (aljazeera.com 09-14), no Saudi primary names any date, and the shutdown's own date is
disputed — this calendar's [`opec-plus-meeting-2026-10-04`](opec-plus-meeting-2026-10-04.md) ledger
and this event's originating proposal both say the drones hit **09-10**, while aljazeera.com's 09-12
explainer and Reuters (via irishtimes.com 09-15) both say **Friday 09-11**. Five weeks from 09-10 is
10-15; from 09-11 it is 10-16. The date therefore carries **±1 day of anchor error on top of the
range's four-week width**, and it stays `estimate` on that ground alone, before the no-self-confirm
limit is even reached.

What the date *does* carry is the framing the originating proposal wrote down and this research
affirms: it is the line past which a still-shut pipeline stops reading as a transient repair and
starts reading as a structural supply premium. That is a useful threshold for a reader. It is not a
tradeable moment.

mistake the label for a consensus.

### Leg 3 — "Saudi precedent says fast" → **MIXED, and the cited precedent is the wrong one**

This is the finding worth the session. The East-West line is a **repeat target with a documented
fast-restore record in 2026** — and both 2026 episodes were materially milder than this one:

- **April 2026.** The line, plus the Manifa and Khurais fields, took damage in the US-Israel war on
  Iran. The pipeline's loss was a **700k bpd reduction** — a degradation, *not* a shutdown; the line
  kept running. The Energy Ministry announced restoration on **2026-04-12**: "This quick recovery
  reflects the high operational resilience and crisis management efficiency of Saudi Aramco."
  (aljazeera.com/economy/2026/4/12, fetched direct.)
- **March 2026.** Aramco's CEO said the line would hit full capacity "in the next couple of days"
  (spglobal.com, slug `031026` = 2026-03-10; the article body returned **403** and is recorded in
  `probe-ref.blocked` — the date is read from the URL, the quote from the search index).

This outage is a different class: a **full precautionary shutdown of 4-5 mb/d** with two separated
impact points and an undisclosed station count. The matching-severity analogue is **2019 Abqaiq**,
and it is the cautionary case:

- 2019-09-14: missiles and drones disrupted **5.7 mb/d** — over half of Saudi production, ~5% of
  global supply. Khurais resumed inside 24 hours; Abqaiq did not.
- 2019-09-17: Aramco reported Abqaiq at 2 mb/d and said it **expected full output capacity restored
  by the end of September**.
- Actual: full 12 mb/d capacity **did not return until late November** — Nasser's revised promise,
  with October output guided to ~9.9 mb/d.

That is an **~8-week slip in officials' own guidance, in this exact setting**. It does not predict
this outage's duration; it prices the *estimates*. Every number in Leg 2 is sourced to officials or
to people briefed by them, so all four should be read as the optimistic edge of their own
distribution — which is the reason a 10-15 still-shut state deserves a calendar row at all rather
than being dismissed as the tail.

### Leg 4 — "Saudi Arabia's incentive forces an early partial restart" → **SUPPORTED**

With Hormuz blockaded, this line is not a bypass any more — it is the **only** export route. Saudi
buyers and traders told Reuters the Kingdom "could begin to exhaust oil available for export within
days unless it restores operations on the East-West pipeline" (via irishtimes.com 09-15). Energy
Aspects' Bronze "expects that Saudi Arabia will be able to resume some flows through the pipeline
while making repairs." And the milestone is *low*: the only traded market resolves on a Saudi
government statement that the line "is presently operating," with **partial or reduced capacity
qualifying** (third-party attributions, future-tense and conditional statements explicitly do not —
so Wright's 09-14 comment, being both third-party and future-tense, does **not** count).

So the base case is an announced partial restart well inside the window. Legs 3 and 4 are not in
conflict: **an early partial restart and a late full restoration are the same story**, and only the
first is what this event's nearest instrument measures.

### Leg 5 — "the premium clears when the line reopens" → **REFUTED**

The supply shock is broader than the pipeline, and this week's tape is the evidence. Brent is live
**109.32** (+3.44%, day range 105.11-109.45, four-month high, 52-week high 126.10); WTI
~**102.92** (+1.51%). That is crude *rising* on the same day the US Energy Secretary said the line
would be back soon and the restart book sat at 42% — i.e. the market is not trading the pipeline's
repair, it is trading the region. What else is on the tape as of 09-15:

- **Fresh Houthi attacks 09-14** on the Khamis Mushait military airbase — hangars, radar, runways,
  ammunition depots — in retaliation for Saudi strikes in Yemen (Reuters via irishtimes.com).
- **Gulf Arab states postponed planned discussions with Iran** after the attacks.
- **Hormuz commodity-vessel transits under 10 a day over the weekend**, against a 10-day average of
  14 — a route that normally carries about a fifth of global oil.
- **>10 mb/d of Gulf production shut in** (IEA OMR, 09-11, as recorded in
  [`opec-plus-meeting-2026-10-04`](opec-plus-meeting-2026-10-04.md); the IEA report itself returned
  403 to this lane and is in that ledger's blocked list).

A restart headline removes one leg of a multi-leg disruption. Treat it as a pipeline event first and
a supply event only to the extent a route is open.

### Honest limits

- **The Polymarket read is a prediction market on an announcement**, not on physical throughput; its
