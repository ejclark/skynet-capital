## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Stand aside on the date; the exposure is duration, and it is already live in the rates
ledgers.** Saudi Arabia shut the East-West pipeline — 1,200km, 7 mb/d nameplate, the one route that
bypasses a Strait of Hormuz closed since March — on **2026-09-11**, after drones hit pumping
stations in the Riyadh and Medina regions on 09-10. That removed **4-5 mb/d**, ~4% of global supply,
and Brent went **$97.92 (09-08) → $109.07 (09-15 live), +11.4%**. This entry's date is **estimate**

HSBC's modelled deficit window ends "mid-October" — so a line still shut on 10-15 has falsified
every published estimate except Goldman's eight-week tail (11-05). Two things make this untradeable
here and one makes it worth watching. Untradeable: `symbols: []`, and **no house playbook covers

### Leg 1 — is 2026-10-15 a real boundary, or an artefact of one wire story? — **SUPPORTED, with a correction to what it bounds**

Four independent estimates now exist, and they do not say the same thing:

- **AP** (two regional officials briefed on the matter, 09-14): "three to five weeks," including at a
  major pumping facility; one added "the line may work partially while teams carry out the repairs."
  → restored **10-01 to 10-15**.
- **Reuters** (sources, 09-13): one said "five to six weeks"; another said it "could be fixed sooner
  and could resume pumping partially." → **10-15 to 10-22**, or earlier and partial.
- **HSBC** (09-15): models the line "mostly offline for three to five weeks" and names the deficit
  window explicitly as **"mid-September to mid-October."**
- **Goldman Sachs** (09-15, via BNN Bloomberg): the published spread runs **"very soon" to eight
  weeks** → out to **~11-05**.

The proposal filed this date as the outer bound of one range. That is right but incomplete: **10-15
is where the AP's range expires and Reuters' begins**, and it is also the literal end of HSBC's
modelled window. That coincidence is the date's whole content. **A line still shut on 2026-10-15 has
falsified every published estimate except Goldman's eight-week tail** — which is a genuine regime
statement (transient repair → structural supply premium), and it is the reading the proposal's note
already reached for. Nothing is *scheduled* on the day.

### Leg 2 — does the April 2026 precedent set the base rate? — **REFUTED as a base rate; the two events are not the same shape**

In April 2026 a strike hours after an Iran-war ceasefire damaged **one of the pipeline's 11 pumping
stations**, cutting throughput by **700,000 bpd**; Saudi Arabia announced full **7 mb/d** capacity
restored on **2026-04-12**, and the energy ministry called it evidence of "the high operational
resilience and crisis management efficiency of Saudi Aramco" (gCaptain / The National, 04-12).

Three differences make that a poor prior for this outage: the April event damaged **one** station
and this one hit **two regions** (Riyadh **and** Medina) including "a major pumping facility"; April
degraded throughput while this one produced a **full precautionary shutdown**; and April caused no
reported injuries. **A recorded conflict, unresolved:** Al Jazeera's 09-12 explainer says the April
repairs were done "just three days later," while the 04-12 restoration reporting dates the strike to
roughly 04-05 — about a week. Both are secondary; neither is adopted as the April duration, and the
spread does not change the conclusion, which is that the April analogue is being cited far more
confidently in the press than its differences support.

### Leg 4 — will a restart headline close the premium? — **MIXED, and the asymmetry is the finding**

Polymarket's resolution text, read direct: the market resolves YES when "the government of Saudi
Arabia announces that the East-West oil pipeline is operational or no longer shut down," and
"qualifying announcements must indicate present operation (**including partial or reduced
capacity**)." An AP official said the line **may work partially during repairs**. So the most likely
sequence is: a partial-operation statement resolves the book and produces the "pipeline restarts"
headline, while 4-5 mb/d of capacity is still not flowing.

The physical picture behind the headline does not clear on partial. Hormuz has been **closed since
March** and transit runs **6-9 mb/d against ~22 mb/d pre-war** (Reuters 09-13); the IEA's 09-11 OMR
put strait transit at 7.6 and bypass at 5.5 mb/d. HSBC (09-15) models a temporary **~6 mb/d global
deficit** across the disruption window and calls it "the largest implied deficit since the start of
the conflict," against **500 mb+ of inventory reductions since February**. EIA's May STEO had
already cut 2027 OPEC spare capacity to **2.5 mb/d** from 3.8 — there is no swing barrel for a 4-5
mb/d hole. **A restart returns a route, not barrels.**

### Leg 5 — does the 10-15 corridor transmit the shock into our prints? — **MIXED; the timing is the trap**

`fomc-blackout-start-2026-10-17` filling the rest. That is the densest inflation-pass-through
corridor of the quarter, and it will be read as if it carries the outage.

It largely does not. **CPI 10-14, PPI 10-15 and import/export prices 10-16 all measure the September
reference month**, which contains only ~20 days of the outage — and retail pump prices lag crude by
roughly two weeks, so the $98→$109 move lands mostly *outside* September's energy component. **The
pass-through print is October's, released ~11-12.** The corridor's real significance for this event
is the other one: **FOMC blackout starts 10-17**, so 10-16 is the Committee's last chance to
characterise energy before the **10-28** decision the `jobs-2026-10-02` ledger now prices as a live
second-hike vote (~56% conditional).

### Leg 6 — is there an official timeline at all? — **REFUTED; there is none**
