## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** what will the 2026-10-14 Beige Book likely say, does it move anything when
it lands the same session as a high-impact CPI print, and does it change how we sit in the tracked
names (NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL AMZN) into the Oct 27–28 FOMC?

**One-line verdict:** it is the last thing the Fed itself publishes before the **10-17** blackout and
the only Fed input immune to Warsh's quieter-Fed posture — but it is stale by nine days at
publication and second-billed to CPI on its own release day, so the honest call is *stand aside, and

4. **The forecasting value is real and it is in the District reports, not the national summary —
   SUPPORTED, and this is the genuinely new leg.** Two Federal Reserve Bank publications, read from
   their own pages **2026-08-31**, reach the same place by different methods. **Boston Fed, Current
   Policy Perspectives, Mary A. Burke & Nathaniel R. Nelson, 2025-11-06, "The Beige Book's Value for
   Forecasting Recessions":** incorporating Beige Book sentiment indexes "may improve the performance
   of established recession-forecasting models that use financial indicators… especially for
   shorter-term forecasts," but the value is weaker at longer horizons — and, decisively for
   procedure, "the Beige Book information that matters most for helping to forecast recessions comes
   from the **individual District reports rather than from the national summary**." They also find
   the content overlaps the term spread, the excess bond premium and the S&P 500 "yet it is not
   wholly redundant." **Cleveland Fed, Economic Commentary EC 2024-08, Filippou/Garciga/Mitchell/
   Nguyen, 2024-04-16:** NLP sentiment indices built from every edition since 1970 show District
   sentiment is "extremely heterogeneous," national sentiment "is not always the simple aggregation
   of District-level sentiment," and District heterogeneity forecasts US recessions "over and above
   the information contained in national economic sentiment." **These do not contradict the 2005
   market-reaction finding — they sit on a different axis.** High forecasting content, low tradable-
   event content: exactly the shape that justifies *read it, don't trade it*, and it retires the
   09-02 procedure of diffing two national-summary counts as the whole job.
