## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** what will the 2026-10-14 Beige Book likely say, does it move anything when
it lands the same session as a high-impact CPI print, and does it change how we sit in the tracked
names (NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL AMZN) into the Oct 27–28 FOMC?

**One-line verdict:** it is the last thing the Fed itself publishes before the **10-17** blackout and
the only Fed input immune to Warsh's quieter-Fed posture — but it is stale by nine days at
publication and second-billed to CPI on its own release day, so the honest call is *stand aside, and
change how it is read rather than whether it is traded*.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md)
(macro-print mode — no price instruments; `symbols: []`). Primary sources over aggregators: the
Board's October-2026 calendar page and its Beige Book schedule page for date/time, the Board's own
July-2026 and October-2025 national summaries for the baseline and the cutoff convention, and two
Federal Reserve Bank research publications read from their own pages (both 403 to plain fetch and
were retrieved with browser headers — recorded, not hidden). The 2005 market-reaction study is
carried forward from the [`09-02` sibling ledger](beige-book-2026-09-02.md), which read it from the
source PDF; it was not re-derived here. All fetches dated **2026-08-31**.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date and time are right, and the entry is promoted to `confirmed` — SUPPORTED.** Two
   independent federalreserve.gov pages agree, both fetched **2026-08-31**: the **October 2026
   calendar** lists "Beige Book" at **2:00 p.m. on October 14**, and the Board's **Beige Book
   schedule** page lists the 2026 editions as **Jan 14 · Mar 4 · Apr 15 · Jun 3 · Jul 15 · Sep 2 ·
   Oct 14 · Nov 25**. Oct 14 is also exactly the two-weeks-before-FOMC convention against the
   confirmed **Oct 27–28** meeting (the Cleveland Fed commentary in leg 4 states that cadence
   verbatim: "released about two weeks before each FOMC meeting"). Promotion is taken here and the
   source prefix is `FED:`. **Why this differs from the 09-02 sibling, which declined to promote on
   the same two pages:** the no-self-confirm limit binds the *sweep that proposes* an entry, not the
   event's own initial-research session holding the primary itself — the convention two sessions
   settled on **2026-08-31** (`fomc-blackout-start-2026-10-17` and `import-export-prices-2026-10-16`
   both promoted this way, the former recording the reasoning explicitly). The 09-02 entry predates
   that convention and is another event's row; it is not edited from here.

2. **It is second billing on its own release day — SUPPORTED, and this is the load-bearing
   anti-trade leg.** `cpi-2026-10-14` is `confirmed` (BLS primary), `high` impact, and prints
   **08:30 ET the same morning**. The [CPI ledger](cpi-2026-10-14.md) already establishes that the
   market's reaction function to that release is core-keyed and asymmetric, and that this corridor is
   two prints deep (PPI **10-15** follows). A qualitative 14:00 ET report with no consensus number,
   arriving into a session already repriced by a quantitative inflation print at 08:30, has no
   independent channel to move anything through. This is a **stronger** version of the September
   argument: on 09-02 the Beige Book merely competed with a busy *week*; on 10-14 it is outranked
   inside the same *session*.

3. **The nine-day collection cutoff is now measured, not asserted — SUPPORTED, and it closes the
   09-02 ledger's open limit.** That sibling recorded "the collection-cutoff date is asserted from
   convention, not verified" after the Board PDF would not parse and a District mirror returned 403.
   Two Board summary pages settle it: the **July 2026** edition was "prepared by the Federal Reserve
   Bank of Chicago based on information collected on or before **July 6, 2026**" (published 07-15 —
   **9 days**), and the **October 2025** edition "was prepared at the Federal Reserve Bank of San
   Francisco based on information collected on or before **October 6, 2025**" (published 10-15 —
   **9 days**). Applied to this edition, books close on or about **2026-10-05**. Two consequences,
   both concrete: (a) the report **can** carry anecdotes reflecting ISM 10-01, jobs **10-02**, and
   the first ~4 days of any funding lapse; (b) it **cannot** carry one word about CPI 10-14, PPI
   10-15, or the 10-17 blackout. As a side effect the same arithmetic dates the **09-02** edition's
   cutoff to ~**2026-08-24**, which is *before* Warsh's 08-28 keynote — confirming that sibling's
   caveat with evidence rather than convention.

4. **The forecasting value is real and it is in the District reports, not the national summary —
   SUPPORTED, and this is the genuinely new leg.** Two Federal Reserve Bank publications, read from
   their own pages **2026-08-31**, reach the same place by different methods. **Boston Fed, Current
   Policy Perspectives, Mary A. Burke & Nathaniel R. Nelson, 2025-11-06, "The Beige Book's Value for
   Forecasting Recessions":** incorporating Beige Book sentiment indexes "may improve the performance
   of established recession-forecasting models that use financial indicators… especially for
   shorter-term forecasts," but the value is weaker at longer horizons — and, decisively for
   procedure, "the Beige Book information that matters most for helping to forecast recessions comes
   from the **individual District reports rather than from the national summary**." They also find
   the content overlaps the term spread, the excess bond premium and the S&P 500 "yet it is not
   wholly redundant." **Cleveland Fed, Economic Commentary EC 2024-08, Filippou/Garciga/Mitchell/
   Nguyen, 2024-04-16:** NLP sentiment indices built from every edition since 1970 show District
   sentiment is "extremely heterogeneous," national sentiment "is not always the simple aggregation
   of District-level sentiment," and District heterogeneity forecasts US recessions "over and above
   the information contained in national economic sentiment." **These do not contradict the 2005
   market-reaction finding — they sit on a different axis.** High forecasting content, low tradable-
   event content: exactly the shape that justifies *read it, don't trade it*, and it retires the
   09-02 procedure of diffing two national-summary counts as the whole job.

5. **The measured market-reaction evidence stays weak — SUPPORTED (carried forward, not
   re-derived).** Zavodny & Ginther, *"Does the Beige Book Move Financial Markets?"*, **Southern
   Economic Journal 2005, 72(1), 138–151**, read from the source PDF by the 09-02 session
   (2026-08-30): a more positive growth tone lifts intermediate and long rates, **but not after
   controlling for other macroeconomic indicators**; markets treat it as a summary indicator of
   growth, not of near-term policy. The control condition is what binds on 10-14, where the other
   macroeconomic indicator is on the same tape six hours earlier. The honest caveat is unchanged and
   is repeated rather than buried: the study is one paper, its sample predates the forward-guidance
   era, and it is being applied to a Fed that communicates differently than any in its window.

6. **It is the only pre-blackout Fed input immune to the quieter-Fed posture — SUPPORTED.** The
   blackout runs from 12:00 a.m. ET Saturday **2026-10-17** through **2026-10-29**
   ([`fomc-blackout-start-2026-10-17`](fomc-blackout-start-2026-10-17.md), `confirmed` from the
   Board's blackout PDF). The October calendar fetched this session lists **no governor or president
   speeches at all** — the Fed-sourced inputs before the gate are **FOMC minutes 10-07** (`estimate`)
   and **this report 10-14**. Both are *publications*, not speech, which matters under a chair who
   argued on 08-28 for "a quieter Fed" and that forward guidance has overstayed its welcome: Warsh
   can quiet the speech channel at will, but the Beige Book is an institutional publication on a
   published schedule. That is the strongest structural argument for tracking this event at all — and
   it is also the ceiling on it, because it is the *last* word, not a *new* one.

7. **The lapse branch upgrades this event rather than deleting it — SUPPORTED by 2025 precedent, and
   calibrated down.** `government-funding-deadline-2026-09-30` is `estimate` with a base case of
   *averted*: both chambers have passed a CR and the whole remaining gap is a one-week expiry date
   (House to Dec 4, Senate to Dec 11), nothing signed. In the lapse branch, the asymmetry is
   documented rather than assumed: the Fed is self-funded and **published the October 2025 Beige Book
   on schedule on 2025-10-15, in the middle of the Oct 1 – Nov 12 2025 shutdown**, while BLS deleted
   the October 2025 CPI outright. So a 10-01 lapse leaves CPI 10-14 dead and this report alive, as
   close to the only fresh federal evidence the 10-28 FOMC sees — a meeting that also carries **no
   SEP** and, since 08-28, **no forward guidance**. **The calibration, and it cuts against
   over-expecting:** the 2025 edition's cutoff was five days into that shutdown and its national
   summary yielded exactly one line — "One District report highlighted the downside risk to growth
   from a prolonged government shutdown." With a ~10-05 cutoff four days into a hypothetical lapse,
   expect thin lapse content, not a shutdown-impact study.

8. **The July baseline still diffs cleanly, and 09-02 will reset it — SUPPORTED.** From the Board's
   **July 15, 2026** national summary (re-fetched 2026-08-31): activity increased at a **slight to
   moderate pace in eleven of twelve Districts**, one reporting no change; labor **five Districts**
   with modest/moderate/solid gains and **seven** with little-to-no change; prices **nine moderate,
   two robust, one slight**, with cost pressure from energy, transportation, raw materials and
   tariffs, and margin compression where input costs outran selling prices. These are the numbers to
   diff — but the **09-02 edition lands in two days** and becomes the live baseline for this one, so
   the first pulse on this doc should carry the 09-02 counts forward rather than re-reading July.

9. **Our exposure is second-order and unchanged — SUPPORTED.** The entry carries `symbols: []`;
   transmission is Oct-28 odds → long-duration equity (NVDA, AVGO, MRVL, CRWV, MSFT, GOOG, META,

### What the conditions support

**No trade, no structure, and one procedure change.** There is no house macro playbook (S1/S2/E1/S3/
S4 + G1 are all symbol-keyed), no consensus number, no whisper and no implied move. What this session
actually buys is *how to read the release*: open the **twelve District reports** and look at
dispersion, since that is where two Fed-system studies locate the forecasting content, and treat the
national summary's two counts as a headline check rather than the job.

### Honest limits

- **No consensus, no whisper, no implied move.** Structural to a qualitative report — the reason this
  cannot be traded as an event, not a gap in the research.
- **The market-reaction leg is one old study, carried forward.** Zavodny & Ginther was not re-read
  this session; it is cited with its source and the 09-02 session's read date. Its sample predates
  the forward-guidance era it is now applied past the far side of.
- **The two Fed studies measure forecasting, not tradability.** Neither says a Beige Book release
  moves prices; using them to justify *reading* the report is in-scope, using them to justify a
  position would not be.
- **The cutoff for THIS edition is inferred, not published.** Two editions at exactly nine days is a
  strong convention, not a rule the Board states — ~2026-10-05 is arithmetic, and the actual line
  will be readable only in the release itself.
- **Hike-odds pricing is 2026-08-28 vintage and unreconciled across feeds.** No fresher usable figure
  was obtainable this session; it is recorded as a spread and should be re-derived, not trusted.
- **Two research pages needed browser headers.** bostonfed.org and clevelandfed.org both returned
  **403** to plain fetch and were retrieved with a browser User-Agent — recorded as method, since a
  future session hitting a 403 should retry rather than conclude the source is gone.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stand aside. Read the 12 District reports as evidence into 10-28; take no position sized to the
release.** Date is now `confirmed` (two federalreserve.gov primaries, 2026-08-31) — which removes a
caution-widener but licenses nothing, since the reasons not to trade this are structural, not
date-related. Concretely: no new risk opened for the 14:00 ET release, no adjustment to any tracked
name on its content alone, and the 10-14 session is treated as a **CPI** session with a Beige Book in
it. The July counts (prices 9/2/1, labor 5/7) are logged for diffing, to be superseded by the 09-02
edition's counts at the next pulse.

**Kill switches:**

- **The 2026-09-02 edition moving the tape in its own 14:00–15:00 ET hour with the release as the
  named driver** — falsifies leg 5 for this regime and forces the reaction function to be rebuilt
  from scratch rather than patched. This is the cheapest test available and it resolves in two days.
- **No CR signed by 2026-09-30** — the lapse branch opens: CPI 10-14 dies, this report survives, and
  its weight into 10-28 rises enough that the `medium` impact tier gets re-checked at the next pulse.
  Kills "second billing," not the stand-aside.
- **Four or more Districts describing "robust" or "strong" price growth on 2026-10-14** (vs two in
  July) — district-side corroboration of the inflation-breadth case, landing inside a live hike
  debate three days before the blackout; re-read the Oct-28 odds channel.
- **Three or fewer Districts reporting employment gains on 2026-10-14** (vs five in July), or
  explicit layoff language — the district-side contradiction of "quite stable … consistent with full
  employment," with no jobs print left before 10-28 to adjudicate it.
- **A measurable tape move in the 2026-10-14 14:00–15:00 ET hour attributable to this release rather
  than the morning's CPI** — falsifies leg 2. Stated with its own difficulty: on a CPI day the two
  are hard to disentangle, so the test is the 14:00 hour specifically, not the session.
- **The 10-14 release slipping or the time changing** — the entry is now `confirmed`; a schedule
  change is a calendar correction, not a market signal.

**No forward test registered.** The stance takes no position sized to any branch, so there is nothing
to score in [`forward-tests.md`](../forward-tests.md); the kill switches above carry the
falsification, each with its date.
