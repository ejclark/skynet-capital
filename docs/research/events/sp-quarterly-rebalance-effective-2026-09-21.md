## Initial research

### The question

This id reached the calendar as a single proposal from the [`opex-2026-09-18`](opex-2026-09-18.md) pulse-check
adjacency sweep, filed 2026-09-08. That proposal made three claims worth testing: that the effective date is
owner-published, that the trade prints into the **2026-09-18** quad-witching close, and — stated in capitals
as a caveat against itself — that **"WHAT THIS PROPOSAL DOES NOT ESTABLISH"** is the size of the resulting
market-on-close imbalance, because the share-count and float deltas live in client-distributed files.

Two sibling ledgers had already answered part of this and left a specific hole. [`sp-rebalance-proforma-2026-09-04`](sp-rebalance-proforma-2026-09-04.md)
declared the folklore trade dead on Greenwood & Sammon's *The Disappearing Index Effect* — S&P 500 inclusion
abnormal returns of 7.4% in the 1990s falling to **~1.0% in 2010–2020, indistinguishable from zero**. And
[`russell-quarterly-ipo-review-effective-2026-09-21`](russell-quarterly-ipo-review-effective-2026-09-21.md),
which lands on the *same session* for the *other* index family, closed with an explicit admission: its nulls
were measured with an ETF proxy calibrated as **near-blind**, so they "establish nothing at all about
single-name behaviour."

**So: is the index effect actually dead, or was it only ever measured where it died? And can this session
answer the opex ledger's size question by a route that does not need the client files?**

**One-line verdict:** the effect is dead in the S&P 500 and alive at 12-of-12 one tier down, the sibling's
single-name hole is the whole finding, and the size question has a tape answer — the quarterly third Friday
runs +0.30× SPY median volume over an ordinary opex Friday at p=0.072%.

1. **The effective date is owner-published and the proposal's reading is exact — SUPPORTED.** The release
   says *"effective prior to the open of trading on Monday, September 21, 2026, to coincide with the quarterly
   rebalance"*, and stamps all 34 rows *"Sept 21, 2026"*. "Effective prior to the open of Monday" is how an
   index owner words a trade that prints at the **preceding close**, 2026-09-18 — the same geometry FTSE
   Russell states outright for its own leg (*"after the close on the third Friday of the month"*), which the
   [Russell twin](russell-quarterly-ipo-review-effective-2026-09-21.md) established from the owner's standing
   rule. What remains `estimate` is the **prefix**, not the fact.

2. **The three S&P 500 deletions are DEMOTIONS, not expulsions — the proposal missed this, and it changes
   how the closing auction should be read.** Each of TAP, TTD and BLDR appears **twice** in the owner's own
   table:

   | Name | Row 1 | Row 2 | Net |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Molson Coors (TAP) | S&P 500 **Deletion** | S&P SmallCap 600 **Addition** | down two tiers, still indexed |
   | The Trade Desk (TTD) | S&P 500 **Deletion** | S&P SmallCap 600 **Addition** | down two tiers, still indexed |
   | Builders FirstSource (BLDR) | S&P 500 **Deletion** | S&P SmallCap 600 **Addition** | down two tiers, still indexed |
   | Everpure (P) | S&P 500 **Addition** | S&P MidCap 400 **Deletion** | up one tier |
   | Illumina (ILMN) | S&P 500 **Addition** | S&P MidCap 400 **Deletion** | up one tier |

   Ten of the release's moves are migrations of this kind (CPRI and SAM 400→600; CORT and EAT 600→400). Only
   Bloom Energy enters the S&P Composite 1500 from outside it, and only seven names — NABL, SHEN, MATW, VRRM,
   FBRT, AMSF, CCOI — leave it entirely. **Consequence:** the 09-18 market-on-close imbalance in a migrating
   name is the *net* of an S&P 500 tracker selling and an S&P 600 tracker buying, not the gross of one side.
   This is also the mechanical reason the literature's deletion effect is small — most deletions were never
   expulsions.

   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | **S&P 600 additions** (vs IJR) | 5 | **+6.47%** | +5.3% | **5 / 5** | **3.00×** |
   | **S&P 600 clean-exit deletions** (vs IJR) | 7 | **−2.97%** | −3.0% | **7 / 7** | **2.55×** |

4. **The "disappearing index effect" is confirmed exactly where it was measured and refuted one tier down —
   SUPPORTED, and it reconciles this ledger with its sibling.** The [announcement sibling](sp-rebalance-proforma-2026-09-04.md)

   one benchmark method. **The effect did not disappear; the literature measured the tier where it had
   already decayed.** The mechanism that predicts this is not tracking-asset scale — index flow is far larger
   in dollars for the S&P 500 — but flow **relative to a name's float and daily turnover**: the same dollar of
   tracker demand is a rounding error against a $30B constituent and a multiple of daily volume against a
