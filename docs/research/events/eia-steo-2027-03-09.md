## Initial research

**The question.** `eia-steo-2027-02-09` did not propose this date for calendar completeness. It proposed
it as a **control**: that session measured that a *newly printed* out-year moves about half as much as
its near year while a *mature* one moves about two-thirds as much, called it a tendency at n=2 per
cohort, and named 2027-03-09 — the first edition whose "previous forecast" column is itself a revised
forecast — as the reading that would either confirm the tendency or expose February as merely quiet.
So: **does the stickiness finding survive its control, and what does the first ordinary revision
actually do?**

**One-line verdict:** the control **passes** on the ratio and **qualifies the headline** — a first-print
out-year really is stickier, but "sticky" lasts **one edition**, and March is the edition that moves it,
by 2–3× February and second-largest of the whole series in both years measured.

(macro-print mode; the house instruments are equity/earnings-keyed and cover no commodity or
