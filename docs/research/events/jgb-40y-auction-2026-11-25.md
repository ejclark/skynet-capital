## At a glance

**TL;DR.** Japan sells its 40-year bond on **2026-11-25**, an anticipated ¥300bn reopening of the 3.8%
2066 — MOF has **not announced the size** and will not until roughly 11-18. **Nothing here is ours to
trade.** This entry exists to close out the research program it was created to run, and the answer is
that the program cannot be run. The [10-20 ledger](jgb-20y-auction-2026-10-20.md) proposed the 40-year
as the **clean control** for the BoJ's bucket taper — 89.6% of its float sits in the untapered "over
25 years" bucket — with **cover** as the outcome, since this single-price tenor prints no tail. Both
halves fail on measurement. **Cover cannot be the outcome**: it is a mechanical function of auction
size whose sign is *opposite* at the two tenors being compared (cover on ln(size), 40-year **−1.080,
t = −7.39**; 20-year **+0.749, t = +7.72**), and the cover DiD comes back **−0.023, t = −0.10** with a
minimum detectable effect of **0.62** cover points. **And the control is not a control**: over exactly
the comparison window MOF cut the 40-year **−44.6%** against the 20-year's **−18.8%**, while bid volume
follows size at an elasticity of **0.75 (se 0.07)**. The tenor picked as untreated took the largest
dose of the *other* policy. The one size-free metric — the clearing concession — does jump (**−2.28bp →
+1.65bp, t = +5.08**, DiD **−4.05bp, t = −3.01**, sign *opposite* to the mechanism) but does **not**
survive: the 40-year yield rose 1.94% → 3.84% across the same 16 auctions, and level-adjusting
collapses it to **+0.67bp, t = +0.77**. What *is* usable: the 95-auction concession calibration of
**−0.25bp is stale** — the ¥300–500bn era runs **+1.65bp** — and two more plausible stories die here
(**November seasonality**, neighbour-controlled +0.089, t = +0.56; **the Japanese holiday week**,
−0.168, t = −1.07). Date and size both **estimate**; they widen caution and license nothing.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This entry was created on 2026-09-09 by the [`jgb-20y-auction-2026-10-20`](jgb-20y-auction-2026-10-20.md)
adjacency sweep with an unusually specific job written into its proposal. That ledger had just refuted
the [30-year ledger](jgb-30y-auction-2026-10-08.md)'s prediction that the BoJ's **−55% cut to its
10–25-year purchase bucket** would show up first in 20-year auction demand — difference-in-differences
tail gap **−0.41bp, t = −0.77**, sign reversed — and found the reason: *the bucket is a
remaining-maturity band, not a tenor*, so its "control" (the 30-year) had **65.5%** of its own float
sitting inside the tapered bucket. The proposal's fix was to run the same test against the one tenor
that is nearly clean:

> Testing the BoJ bucket mechanism needs an untapered control… The 40-year is the only tenor that is
> nearly clean: ¥42.6trn of its ¥47.5trn, 89.6%, sits in the untapered "more than 25 years" bucket.
> The cost is that the 40-year is a SINGLE-price auction and prints no tail… so the control series is
> the cover ratio alone.

So the question is again methodological before it is a market question:

**does the 40-year actually work as the untapered control — and if the cover ratio is the only series
available, can that series carry a difference-in-differences at all?**

**One-line verdict:** **no, on both counts, and the second is the fatal one.** Cover is a mechanical
transform of auction size whose sign flips between the control and treated tenors, and MOF cut the
40-year's size nearly two and a half times as hard as the 20-year's over exactly the test window —
so the tenor chosen as untreated received the larger dose of the confounding policy. The program is
**retired**, not refuted: there is no verdict here to reach, and a session that runs it anyway will
report a null it manufactured.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The proposal's bucket premise is SUPPORTED, and reproducing it surfaces a refinement it missed.**
Summing MOF's workbook by remaining maturity at **2026-11-25** — competitive allotments *plus*
non-price-competitive I and II, which is what makes two independent parses agree:

| Tenor | Outstanding | ≤10y | 10–25y (tapered) | >25y (untapered) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 40-year | ¥47.5trn | 0.0% | 10.4% | **89.6%** |
| 30-year | ¥171.0trn | 6.0% | **65.5%** | 28.5% |
| 20-year | ¥264.8trn | 51.7% | 48.3% | 0.0% |

Identical at 2026-10-20 — no bond crosses a boundary in those 36 days — so this **reproduces the 10-20
ledger exactly** (¥244.8trn tapered bucket, 52.2% 20-year / 45.8% 30-year / 2.0% 40-year) rather than
adding a fresh reading. Two pipelines, same numbers.

The refinement is in the **other** direction, and the 10-20 ledger did not state it. Read the untapered
bucket by composition instead of by tenor:

| Bucket | Total | 20-year paper | 30-year paper | 40-year paper |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10–25y (tapered −55%) | ¥244.8trn | 52.2% | 45.8% | 2.0% |
| **>25y (untapered)** | **¥91.4trn** | 0.0% | **53.4%** | **46.6%** |

**The BoJ's untapered bucket is majority 30-year paper, not 40-year paper.** The 40-year is a clean
control *tenor* — 89.6% of its own float is untreated — but "40-year vs 30-year" is a much weaker
treatment contrast than the labels suggest, because seasoned 30-year bonds supply the plurality of the
tapered bucket **and** the majority of the untapered one. The only sharp contrast available is
40-year against 20-year, and leg 3 shows why even that one cannot be measured.

**2. Cover cannot be the outcome variable — REFUTED as a design, and this is the load-bearing finding.**
The proposal accepted the loss of the tail and named cover as the replacement series. Cover is
`bids ÷ accepted`, so it is a ratio in which MOF sets the denominator. Regressing cover on ln(offering
size) over each tenor's full history:

| Tenor | n | slope on ln(size) | t | implied by a halving of size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 40-year | 95 | **−1.080** | **−7.39** | cover **+0.75** |
| 30-year | 225 | +0.151 | +0.90 | cover −0.11 |
| 20-year | 343 | **+0.749** | **+7.72** | cover **−0.52** |

**The sign is opposite at the control and the treated tenor.** At the 40-year, cutting size mechanically
lifts cover; at the 20-year, size and cover move together (bigger auctions have historically been
scheduled into stronger demand regimes). A difference-in-differences whose outcome variable responds to
a *third* policy with opposite signs in the two arms is not measuring the treatment.

Run anyway, on the windows the 10-20 ledger defined from the BoJ's own monthly operation files
(**A** 2024-01 → 2025-03, both buckets flat; **B** 2025-04 → 2026-09, 10–25y ¥450.9bn → ¥200.8bn/mo,
25y+ flat at ¥150.4bn):

| Tenor | A: n · size · cover | B: n · size · cover | Δ cover | t |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 40-year (control) | 8 · ¥700bn · 2.441 | 8 · ¥388bn · 2.545 | +0.104 | +0.76 |
| 30-year | 15 · ¥900bn · 3.364 | 16 · ¥694bn · 3.399 | +0.035 | +0.27 |
| 20-year (treated) | 15 · ¥1,000bn · 3.393 | 16 · ¥812bn · 3.473 | +0.081 | +0.46 |

**DiD (20-year − 40-year) = −0.023, t = −0.10**, minimum detectable effect at 80% power **0.622** cover
points. **DiD (30-year − 40-year) = −0.069, t = −0.37**, MDE **0.521**. The MDE is five to six times
larger than any effect anyone has proposed. Registered as `FT-jgb-40y-auction-2026-11-25-1`.

**3. The control took a bigger dose of the confounding policy than the treated arm — REFUTED as a
control, and this is why leg 2 cannot be rescued by waiting for more auctions.** Across the same A→B
windows:

| Tenor | mean size A | mean size B | change | competitive bids A → B | change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **40-year (the "control")** | ¥700bn | ¥388bn | **−44.6%** | ¥1,697bn → ¥969bn | **−42.9%** (t = −9.19) |
| 30-year | ¥900bn | ¥694bn | −22.9% | ¥2,332bn → ¥1,760bn | −24.5% (t = −7.76) |
| 20-year (treated) | ¥1,000bn | ¥812bn | −18.8% | ¥2,612bn → ¥2,088bn | −20.1% (t = −5.70) |

Bid volume tracks offering size almost one-for-one in every tenor. Estimated directly, ln–ln since
2019: elasticity **0.75 (se 0.07)** at the 40-year, **0.69 (se 0.09)** at the 30-year, **0.77 (se
0.10)** at the 20-year — all significantly below 1 (t = −3.57, −3.37, −2.27) and nowhere near 0.

So the ln(bids) DiD comes back **+40.1% (t = +4.64)** against the 20-year and **+32.2% (t = +3.93)**
against the 30-year — highly significant, and **it is not a demand result at all**: it is MOF's
issuance schedule, arriving with a t-statistic. The tenor selected as untreated is the one MOF cut
hardest. **A control more heavily treated by the confounder than the treated arm identifies nothing**,
at any sample size.

**4. The one size-free metric moves, significantly, in the wrong direction — and then dies under
control. MIXED, and honest about which half wins.** The clearing concession — highest accepted yield
minus MOF's same-session par yield at the matching tenor — is a price, not a quantity, so it is not
mechanically pinned to issuance size. On the same windows:

| Tenor | A concession | B concession | Δ | t |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 40-year (control) | **−2.28bp** (n=8) | **+1.65bp** (n=8) | **+3.93bp** | **+5.08** |
| 30-year | +4.23bp (n=15) | +6.30bp (n=16) | +2.07bp | +1.15 |
| 20-year (treated) | +1.61bp (n=15) | +1.49bp (n=16) | −0.13bp | −0.11 |

**DiD (20-year − 40-year) = −4.05bp, t = −3.01** (MDE 3.77bp); (30-year − 40-year) = −1.86bp, t = −0.95.
Read naively this is a significant result whose **sign is the reverse of the bucket mechanism**: the
tenor the BoJ kept buying is the one whose price concession blew out, while the tapered 20-year did not
move at all.

It does not survive the obvious control. Across those 16 auctions the 40-year par yield rose
monotonically **1.943% (2024-01-25) → 3.842% (2026-07-22)**, so window and yield level are collinear by
construction. Regressing concession on the level *inside* the window and re-running:

- **level-adjusted A → B: +0.67bp, t = +0.77.** The jump is gone.
- On the full-sample slope instead it survives at +3.75bp (t = +4.85) — but the full sample shows
  essentially no level relationship at all (corr **+0.075**, t = +0.73), so that specification is the
  one doing the assuming, not the one doing the controlling.

At **n = 8 + 8** with the level rising monotonically through the window, this design cannot separate
"BoJ bucket" from "yields doubled." **No claim is made in either direction.** What the leg leaves
behind is a calibration, not a mechanism — see leg 5.

The date is also independently reproduced from a *second* MOF primary. Japanese market holidays fall
out of the par-yield business-day series as weekdays with no reading, and November behaves exactly as
the fixed-date statute predicts — Nov 23 in 2015-2017 and 2020-2023, the substitute Monday in 2014
(11-24) and 2025 (11-24) when Nov 23 was a Sunday, and **nothing** in 2018 and 2024 when it was a
Saturday. 2026-11-23 is a Monday.

### Honest limits

- **"Retired" is a statement about the design, not about the BoJ.** Nothing here says the bucket taper
  has no effect on super-long demand. It says the 40-year cannot serve as the control and cover cannot
  serve as the outcome, so *this* test cannot answer the question. The mechanism remains open.
- **n = 8 + 8 at the control tenor.** The 40-year prints six auctions a year. Every window-based
  statement about it rests on single-digit samples, and the leg-4 level control is fitting one
  parameter to sixteen points in which level and time are collinear. Neither the finding nor its
  refutation is strong; the honest reading of leg 4 is *undetermined*.
- **MOF's par curve is a fitted spline, and the concession metric divides by it.** The 25Y point
  printing above the 30Y — 63 of the 83 such sessions in history are since 2026-05-27 — is exactly the
  region where a spline is least trustworthy. A concession measured against a mis-fitted 40Y point is a
  fitting artifact. The 10-20 ledger flagged this for the 25Y and it applies here with more force.
- **The elasticity in leg 3 is not causal.** MOF sets size in response to conditions, so size and
  demand are jointly determined; 0.75 is a descriptive elasticity, not a supply-curve slope.
- **The bucket table has a stale denominator.** The workbook was last saved 2026-08-13, so the
  outstanding figures miss any 2026-08 and 2026-09 issuance. The effect on shares is second-order at
  ¥244.8trn, but the numbers are not current to today.
- **The BoJ purchase series is taken from the 10-20 sibling ledger, not re-fetched.** Its ¥450.9bn →
  ¥200.8bn/mo (10–25y) and flat ¥150.4bn (25y+) come from 44 BoJ monthly operation files that ledger
  read on 2026-09-09. This session used its window definitions and did not independently re-derive
  them; the failure mode is that both ledgers inherit the same reading error.
- **The holiday-week test cannot see 2026 itself.** Japanese holidays are derived from gaps in a
  par-yield series that ends 2026-08-31, so 2026-11-23 is established from the JPX page and from the
  statute's fixed date, not from the series. The derivation is validated on 2013–2025.
- **The auction result does not exist**, neither does its size, and the workbook stops at 2026-07-22.
  Everything here is prior.
- **USD/JPY and VIX are Yahoo, i.e. secondary**, and the 09-09 USD/JPY reading is intraday.
- **One cited path 404'd** (`data/jgbcm.csv`) and is recorded in `probe-ref.blocked`. The substitute is
  a MOF path serving the identical file, so no prefix downgrade applies — but a future session should
  cite `jgbs/reference/interest_rate/jgbcm.csv`, not the old path.
- **The date and the size are both `estimate`.** MOF is a primary and the date is not in doubt; the
  label reflects an unannounced size plus a gap in `market-events-data.ts`'s prefix taxonomy, which has
  no slot for a non-US sovereign debt office. Estimates widen caution and license nothing.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (2026-09-09, D-77; date and size both `estimate`).** **Stand aside, permanently.** No entry,
exit or hedge is keyed to the 2026-11-25 auction or to the 11-25 session. The entry is kept for three
diagnostic jobs, in descending order of value:

1. **It closes a research program.** The 40-year was proposed as the BoJ taper's clean control with
   cover as the outcome. Cover is a size transform whose sign is opposite at control and treated tenor
   (−1.080 vs +0.749 on ln(size)), and MOF cut the control's size **−44.6%** against the treated arm's
   **−18.8%** across the very window being compared. **A future session should not run this test.** If
   the question is to be answered, it needs a size-free outcome measured across a regime where yields
   are not simultaneously doubling — which is not the window we are in.
2. **It corrects a live calibration.** The 09-29 sibling's **−0.25bp** mean concession spans the ZIRP
   era. For a ¥300–500bn auction use **+1.65bp** (n=8, 2025-05-28 → 2026-07-22), and expect the two
   live conditions — smallest-ever size, 25s30s at the 1.0th percentile — to push the same way.
3. **It banks two more killed stories.** November super-long strength is regime clustering
   (neighbour-controlled +0.089, t = +0.56), and the four-session Japanese holiday week does not move
   cover (−0.168, t = −1.07) — even though 11-23 is a *total* Tokyo blackout including JGB futures.

Three reading rules attach to the day itself:

- **Read cover as an issuance statistic.** On ¥300bn, expect bids of ¥750–1,050bn and a cover of
  2.5–3.3x almost regardless of demand. Restate any cover against the yen it absorbs before comparing
  it to 2024.
- **There is still no tail.** Single-price (Dutch); MOF prints `-` in both average-price columns and
  the workbook's 40年債 sheet has 11 columns to the 20-year's 16. Any reported 40-year "tail" is
  fabricated.
- **Do not run the spillover test on this date.** Six tracked US events land on 2026-11-25, two of
  them `high`, plus the US 7-year note auction. The sequencing is clean and the identification is not.

**Kill switches.**

1. **The no-price-channel premise breaks** — a tracked name (NVDA/AVGO/MRVL/CRWV) moves **>2%** on a
   session between **2026-09-09** and **2026-11-25** that the tape attributes to a JGB or yen headline.
   This document is rebuilt, not patched.
2. **The design-retirement breaks** — MOF announces a 2026-11-25 size **at or above ¥540bn** (within
   10% of the 30-year's ¥600bn). The issuance-cut confound that disqualifies the 40-year as a control
   would be unwinding and the test would become worth re-running
   (`FT-jgb-40y-auction-2026-11-25-1`).
3. **The calibration update breaks** — the 2026-11-25 result clears **at or below** MOF's same-session
   par 40-year, i.e. a negative concession. The pre-2025 mean would still describe the current regime
   (`FT-jgb-40y-auction-2026-11-25-2`).
4. **The size-pinned-bids reading breaks** — competitive bids land **outside ¥750–1,050bn** on an
   announced ¥300bn. Bid volume would carry information the elasticity says it does not
   (`FT-jgb-40y-auction-2026-11-25-3`).
5. **The holiday-week null breaks** — 11-25 covers **below 2.5x** *and* the 30-year on 11-10 and the
   20-year on 11-18, in ordinary weeks, both cover at or above their own medians. The four-session week
   would be the one thing distinguishing them.
6. **MOF changes the terms or the cadence** — a size other than **¥300bn**, a date other than
   **11-25**, a **new issue 20** rather than the fourth reopening of issue 19, or terms published
   before **2026-11-11** (off the "about one week prior" note). MOF's calendar allows changes "in light
   of changes in circumstances."
7. **JPX reopens 11-23** — the derivatives holiday-trading page republishing 2026-11-23 as `Open`. The
   BCP blackout, and the proposal filed on it, would be wrong.
