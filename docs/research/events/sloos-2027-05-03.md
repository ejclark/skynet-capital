**Signals & conditions** — the buy/sell/hold triggers:

- **Never** — treat a SLOOS release as an entry or exit trigger. n=67, KRE excess +0.109% (p=0.496); 65 of 67 releases sit at or below KRE's own p90, and the two that don't were a vaccine announcement and a bank seizure.
- **Never test a fixed-weekday event against an all-session pool.** 63 of 67 SLOOS releases are Mondays; the all-session control manufactures a p=0.008 volatility "finding" that is p=0.126 weekday-matched.
- **The 2027-05-03 session's mild upward equity tilt is the calendar, not a release.** First-trading-day-of-month Mondays run SPY **+0.275%** (p=0.042, n=77) with no release attached; three releases share that day and none of them can claim it.

- **Escalate the read (not the size) if** — the NDFI series continues, because bank lending *to* private-credit intermediaries is the one SLOOS channel that touches a debt-financed name this book already tracks (**CRWV**). Still not a position.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

SLOOS is expected at 14:00 ET on **2027-05-03**, the Monday after the April 27–28 FOMC and the same
session as ISM Manufacturing (10:00 ET) and the Treasury borrowing estimates (15:00 ET). It reached
this calendar yesterday's-work-ago: the
[`treasury-borrowing-estimates-2027-05-03`](treasury-borrowing-estimates-2027-05-03.md) initial
research proposed it on **2026-09-09** as *"the tightest collision on that session."* Its only
sibling ledger, [`sloos-2026-11-02`](sloos-2026-11-02.md), proved the release does not move
regional banks — on **n=3**, one calendar year, one regime, and closed with the honest note that
reconciling its own data conflict was *"the first job of the next pulse."*

So three questions, in order of how much they could change the call: **is the date right and is the
rule behind it right; does the null survive a sample twenty times larger; and is there anything
about *this* edition that the sibling's framing misses?**

**One-line verdict:** the date is right and the rule behind it needed one repair; the null does not
merely survive the extension, it survives a control the sibling never applied and would have failed
without; and the one thing genuinely new is that SLOOS started surveying the private-credit funding
chain in 2026, which is the channel the sibling ruled out.

### Method

Sourced primary research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md) (macro-print
mode), plus three first-party measurements built this session. Every federalreserve.gov fetch
returned HTTP 200 to plain `curl` on **2026-09-09**; nothing was blocked.

1. **Release-date audit.** Every SLOOS edition page on federalreserve.gov — `/data/sloos/sloos-YYYYMM.htm`
   for 2018→2026, `/data/sloos/YYYYMM/default.htm` for 2017, `/boarddocs/SnLoanSurvey/YYYYMM/default.htm`
   back to 1997 — fetched and parsed for its `Last Update` field, giving **121** dated releases.
   Cross-validated **13 for 13** against the Fed's own `newsevents/YYYY-month.htm` calendars, which
   list SLOOS with its 2:00 p.m. slot from 2023-05 onward (earlier calendars do not carry the release
   at all). FOMC meeting end-dates from `monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm` (2021–2027) and
   `fomchistorical<year>.htm` (2010–2020).
2. **Release-day study.** Yahoo dividend-adjusted daily closes via
   `scripts/research/market-data.mjs` → `bars()`, instrument caches busted first per the
   cache-discipline rule. Instruments **KRE · KBE · XLF · SPY · QQQ · ^VIX · TLT**, window
   2010-01-01 → 2026-09-09 (4,196 sessions). Event set: the **67** modern-regime releases.
   Significance by two-sided permutation test, 200,000 iterations, against random same-size draws
   from a stated pool — and the choice of pool is the whole point of leg 3.
3. **Collection-window derivation.** Each 2018→2026 edition page publishes its own *"respondent banks
   received the survey on X, and responses were due by Y"* footnote — **32** editions, giving a
   measured sent-lag and due-lag distribution rather than the sibling's single observation.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The date is right, and the rule behind it needed one repair — SUPPORTED.** The proposal derived
2027-05-03 from *"the Monday after the FOMC"*, citing the four 2026 editions. Measured against **67**
releases from 2010-02-01 to 2026-08-03, that rule is right **64 times** and the three misses are all
the *same* mechanism:

| Release | Derived Monday | Actual | Cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oct-2012 edition | 2012-10-29 | **2012-10-31** (Wed) | Hurricane Sandy — no NYSE session on 10-29 or 10-30 (verified in the price data: SPY jumps 10-26 → 10-31) |
| Oct-2018 edition | 2018-11-12 | **2018-11-13** (Tue) | Veterans Day observed (11-11 fell on a Sunday) |
| Oct-2024 edition | 2024-11-11 | **2024-11-12** (Tue) | Veterans Day |

The repaired rule: **the Monday five sessions after the FOMC's final day, shifted to the next
business day when that Monday is a federal holiday or the market is shut.** Veterans Day is the trap
worth naming, because the NYSE *trades* that day while the Board of Governors is closed — a lane
checking only the exchange calendar would derive the wrong date and see nothing wrong. **2027-05-03
is an ordinary Monday** (2027-05-01 is a Saturday; there is no federal holiday in early May), so the
repaired rule and the naive one agree, and the derivation lands on the same date the proposal did.
Applied forward, the whole 2027 series is **02-01, 05-03, 08-02, 11-01** — the first two of which are
proposed in this PR.

**The entry stays `estimate`.** federalreserve.gov publishes no 2027 newsevents page (`2027-may.htm`
and `2027-february.htm` both returned **HTTP 404** on 2026-09-09), so neither the date nor the 14:00
ET slot has a primary; the FOMC calendar it derives from carries the Fed's own caveat that *"each
meeting date is tentative until confirmed at the meeting immediately preceding it"*; and this lane
does not self-confirm what it derived. The canonical `sloos-2027-05-03.json` is written in this PR
from the one proposal that named it, which becomes inert.

**2. The null does not merely survive a 22× larger sample — it hardens — SUPPORTED.** Close-to-close,
2010-01-01 → 2026-08-03, 200k-iteration permutation against the all-session pool:

| Instrument | SLOOS days (n=67) | p | All-session mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| KRE excess vs SPY | 32/67 up, **+0.109%** | **0.496** | −0.005% |
| KBE excess vs SPY | 31/67 up, +0.162% | 0.262 | −0.007% |
| XLF excess vs SPY | 34/67 up, +0.085% | 0.321 | −0.005% |
| TLT | 31/67 up, −0.098% | 0.395 | +0.014% |

Robustness, same pull: dropping **2020-11-09** takes KRE to **−0.104%** (p=0.522); dropping
**2023-05-08** as well gives **−0.074%** (p=0.649). The 2015→ subset is the only one that leans
positive (**+0.308%**, p=0.111, n=47) and it does not clear anything. Distributionally, KRE's
|excess vs SPY| since 2010 has a median of **0.668%** and a p90 of **1.993%** (n=4,196), and
**exactly 2 of 67** release days exceed that p90 — **2020-11-09 (+14.17%)**, the Pfizer-vaccine
value-rotation Monday, and **2023-05-08 (−2.03%)**, the Monday after the First Republic seizure. Both
are days on which the tape would have moved regional banks whether or not a survey had published.
T+1 is empty too (KRE excess −0.141%, p=0.380). The sibling's three-observation refusal was right.

**3. The trap, and the leg the sibling could not have caught: 63 of 67 releases are Mondays —
SUPPORTED, and this is the session's transferable finding.** Run against the all-session pool, SLOOS
days look like a genuine volatility event:

| Pool | ^VIX mean | p | n |
|---|---|---|---|
| SLOOS days vs **all sessions** | **+2.973%** | **0.008** | 67 |
| **All Mondays** vs all sessions | +1.878% | 0.000 | 785 |
| Mondays that are **not** SLOOS days | +1.756% | 0.000 | 722 |
| SLOOS Mondays vs the **Monday** pool | +3.268% | **0.126** | 63 |

The VIX Monday effect — weekend calendar decay realised at Monday's open — is old, large and
nothing to do with bank credit. Against the right pool the SLOOS "effect" is not distinguishable.
The same correction applies to KRE: **+0.090%, p=0.607** against the Monday pool. **The rule this
buys the calendar: an event that lands on a fixed weekday must be tested against that weekday's
pool.** Almost every macro release this book tracks has a fixed weekday — SLOOS and the Treasury
borrowing estimates are Mondays, jobs is a Friday, FOMC is a Wednesday — so an all-session control
is the wrong control for most of the calendar, and it errs in the direction of manufacturing
findings.

**4. The same correction bears on the borrowing-estimates ledger's one open question — SUPPORTED,
and it is reported, not acted on.** [`treasury-borrowing-estimates-2027-05-03`](treasury-borrowing-estimates-2027-05-03.md)
leg 7 found an equity drift on its own release Mondays (SPY **+0.478%**, p=0.079, n=17
first-business-day releases) against a control of **120 non-release first-business-days averaging
+0.092%**, named ISM Manufacturing the rival owner, and registered it as **FT-…-4** with a kill
switch of *"SPY's close-to-close return on 2027-05-03 exceeds +0.092%."* That control is not
weekday-matched. Measured this session over the same 2010→ window:

| Subset | SPY mean | p | n |
|---|---|---|---|
| All first trading days of a month | +0.147% | 0.124 | 201 |
| First trading days that are **Mondays** | **+0.275%** | **0.042** | 77 |
| …excluding SLOOS days | +0.235% | 0.125 | 57 |
| SLOOS days that are first trading days | +0.386% | 0.113 | 20 |
| …vs the **first-biz-Monday** pool | +0.386% | **0.317** | 20 |

QQQ is the same shape (+0.333% p=0.045 on first-biz-Mondays; the SLOOS subset +0.532% dissolves to
p=0.239 against it). So the drift on that session class is substantially **"first trading day of the
month, and it's a Monday"** — a fourth candidate owner nobody had named, alongside ISM, the Treasury
release and SLOOS itself. **2027-05-03 is a first-trading-day Monday carrying all three releases.**
This does not score the sibling's forward test — that is its lane's file and its lane's call, rows
are append-only, and one file per owner — but it is the number its next pulse should weigh, and it
argues the drift is *less* attributable, not more.

**5. The collection window closes about 2027-04-02, derived from 32 editions rather than one —
SUPPORTED.** Every 2018→2026 edition publishes its own sent and due dates. The due-lag is remarkably
tight: **27–33 days before release, median 31, n=32**. The sent-lag is bimodal by season — **42 days
for the April, July and October editions** (18 of 32 at exactly 42) and 46–54 for the January
edition, which straddles the holidays. Sharper still, every April edition's questionnaire has gone
out on the **Monday after the March FOMC** (2018-03-26, 2019-03-25, 2020-03-23, 2021-03-22,
2022-03-28, 2023-03-27, 2024-03-25, 2025-03-31, 2026-03-23 — all Mondays). Applied to a **2027-05-03**
release with a **2027-03-16/17** FOMC: **sent ≈ 2027-03-22, responses due ≈ 2027-04-02.** Two
consequences pulling opposite ways, the same pair the sibling named but now with a measured window
rather than a single-edition extrapolation: the survey captures bank views formed in the **~two weeks
after the 2027-03-17 decision**, making it the first structured read on that decision's credit
transmission; and it is **~31 days stale** relative to the **2027-04-28** meeting whose Monday it
follows. The edition that reads the April *and* June decisions is **2027-08-02**, proposed here.

**6. SLOOS started surveying the private-credit funding chain, and that is the sibling's one
reversed conclusion — MIXED, and it is the reason to keep reading.** `sloos-2026-11-02` leg 6 named
**CRWV** as the nearest thematic link and then ruled it out: its financing *"runs through private
credit and asset-backed markets, **not** the surveyed bank channel."* That was true of the editions
available when it was written. It is no longer the whole picture. Searching all **219** edition pages
fetched this session for nondepository-financial-institution content returns exactly three hits:
**July 2016** (a single new question on the *level* of standards for NDFI loans), then — after a
nine-year gap — **April 2026** (a full special-question set on *changes* in NDFI lending standards
and demand over the past year) and **July 2026** (a second set, on *levels* relative to the range
since 2011, reporting standards *"at the tighter ends of their historical ranges"* for every queried
type, explicitly including **private equity funds** and **business credit intermediaries**). Two
consecutive editions is a series, not a one-off. What this does **not** do is make SLOOS tradable or
make CRWV a position: it is a quarterly, four-week-stale, qualitative read on the *upstream* of a
funding chain, `symbols: []` stays, and leg 2 says the release day is empty regardless. What it does
is change the *reason to read it* — from a general credit-standards curiosity to the one available
structured read on bank credit to private-credit intermediaries. Whether it recurs is registered as
a forward test rather than assumed.

**7. What the last editions actually say — SUPPORTED, and the direction is flat on a tight level.**
The July 2026 edition (released 2026-08-03, the freshest available today) reports **basically
unchanged C&I standards** for firms of all sizes with **stronger C&I demand** from large and
middle-market firms; **easier CRE standards** with basically unchanged demand; mixed household
standards with **weaker RRE demand**; **tighter credit-card standards**; unchanged auto and other
consumer standards with weaker auto demand. Its special questions put the *level* of standards *"at
the tighter end of the range"* since 2005 **for all loan categories except C&I**, where standards are
*"generally easier than their midpoints"* — while being **easier than the July 2025 survey** for
every category except consumer loans. Flat changes on top of a historically tight level is the single
most misread thing about this release, and the distinction is what the May-2027 edition should be
read against.

**8. Its signal is buried by construction, and the corridor is now known rather than thin —
SUPPORTED.** Within five days of 2027-05-03 this calendar tracks **fomc-2027-04-28** (`high`),
**boj-decision-2027-04-28**, **russell-style-month-end-capping-effective-2027-04-30**,
**tic-annual-survey-final-2027-04-30** (confirmed), **treasury-borrowing-estimates-2027-05-03** and
**treasury-refunding-2027-05-05**, plus three sibling-owned proposals — **boe-decision-2027-04-29**,
**ism-manufacturing-2027-05-03** (10:00 ET) and **jobs-2027-05-07**. A `low`-impact survey that
publishes at 14:00 ET between a 10:00 ET ISM print and a 15:00 ET Treasury release, five days after
an FOMC decision and four days before payrolls, has no window in which to be the story. Unlike
2026-11-02 there is no election the next day — but that only means the confounds are all
macro-calendar rather than political, which is worse for attribution, not better.

### What plays the conditions support (date is an `estimate`)

None directional. No house playbook is macro-keyed (S1/S2/E1/S3/S4 + G1 are symbol- and
earnings-keyed). A reading assignment and two disciplines:

- **Calendar the 14:00 ET release on 2027-05-03 as reading, not as an event.** Three things to
  extract, listed in the signals above; none is sized.
- **Test a fixed-weekday event against its own weekday's pool.** This is the leg-3 finding, and it is
  the more valuable of the two because it is not about SLOOS at all — it is a control-set rule for
  every Monday, Friday and Wednesday release on this calendar.
- **A rival owner can be a weekday.** Leg 4's answer to an open ownership question was not another
  release; it was the day of the week and the turn of the month.

Explicitly **not** claimed: that SLOOS is informationally empty. Legs 5, 6 and 7 are the opposite
claim, and the sibling's citation of the Fed's own 2026-02-23 FEDS Note (SLOOS expectation shocks
predict later standards changes) is carried unchanged. Unpriced and uninformative are different
words.

### Honest limits

- **The release dates are `Last Update` fields, not a published release calendar.** They validate
  **13 for 13** against the Fed's own newsevents calendars, which is why the series is trusted — but
  that overlap is only 2023-05 → 2026-08. Two dates in the wider set are ambiguous by exactly one
  day and are carried as-is: **2021-08-03** (a Tuesday where the rule says Monday 08-02, with no
  holiday to explain it — most likely a next-day page revision) and **2018-11-13**, which the
  Veterans Day mechanism explains but which could equally be a revision of a 11-12 release. Both
  land inside the same trading week; neither changes any result above.
- **The 201804 page's `Last Update` is a June-2019 revision**, so that edition's release date
  (2018-05-07) is derived from the rule rather than read. One of 67.
- **Daily bars cannot see a 14:00 ET release.** T+0 close-to-close contains six hours of unrelated
  trading before the survey publishes and two after it. The measurement supports *"no large
  release-day effect,"* never *"no effect"* — and unlike the borrowing-estimates sibling, which has
  the same limit, this one has an extra confound: **ISM at 10:00 ET on the very date in question.**
  Intraday (14:00→16:00) data would settle it and was not used.
- **n=67 is not decisive, it is just much better than 3.** With KRE's daily |excess| median at 0.668%,
  67 observations have decent power against a mean effect near ±0.4% and poor power below that.
- **The leg-4 first-biz-Monday result is one of several subsets I looked at**, and p=0.042 does not
  survive a serious multiple-comparison correction across the ~30 tests in this session. It is
  registered as a forward test precisely because it is sub-threshold, not because it is believed.
- **Leg 6 rests on a text search of the pages I could fetch.** 219 edition pages were parsed, but the
  pre-2018 boarddocs pages are summaries and their special-question detail is thinner than the modern
  ones; an earlier NDFI question could be present in a table I did not read. The claim I stand behind
  is the narrow one: **two consecutive 2026 editions carried NDFI special questions**, and no other
  edition since 2018 did.
- **The date is an `estimate` and this ledger keeps it there** even though the derivation is now
  audited on 67 observations, because the Fed's 2027 meeting dates are themselves tentative and the
  release calendar for 2027 does not exist.
- **The impact tier stays `low`** and nothing in legs 2, 3 or 8 argues for raising it.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (`estimate`-dated — and an estimate widens caution, it never licenses an entry):** **read
it, do not trade it.** The channel is measurably absent at **n=67** and stays absent under the
control the sibling never applied: KRE's excess vs SPY on modern-regime SLOOS days is **+0.109%**
(p=0.496) against all sessions and **+0.090%** (p=0.607) against Mondays; only **2 of 67** releases
exceed KRE's own p90 and both have named non-SLOOS causes. The apparent VIX reaction
(**+2.973%, p=0.008**) is the **Monday effect** — 63 of 67 releases are Mondays, all Mondays run
**+1.878%**, and weekday-matched the release effect is **p=0.126**. What earns the slot is timing and
one content change: the response window closes ≈ **2027-04-02**, making this the first structured read
on the **2027-03-17** decision's credit transmission and ~31 days stale versus the **04-28** meeting
whose Monday it follows; and the **NDFI special questions** carried by the April- and July-2026
editions are the first time SLOOS has surveyed the bank credit that funds private credit — the
channel [`sloos-2026-11-02`](sloos-2026-11-02.md) ruled out for **CRWV**. `symbols: []` stays, no
directional call, no size. **Attribute no 2027-05-03 move to this release:** ISM Manufacturing
prints at 10:00 ET and the Treasury borrowing estimates at 15:00 ET on the same Monday, the FOMC
decided 04-28, refunding lands 05-05 and payrolls 05-07 — and the session's own mild upward tilt is
best explained by it being the **first trading day of a month that is also a Monday** (SPY +0.275%,
p=0.042, n=77), which is not a release at all.

**Kill switches:**

- **Date kill:** federalreserve.gov publishing a 2027 SLOOS date other than **2027-05-03** once a
  2027 newsevents page exists, or announcing one of the *"one or two additional surveys"* it
  sometimes runs. Leg 1's 64-of-67 rule would have failed its first genuinely out-of-sample test and
  every rule-derived date on this calendar gets re-derived rather than patched. Re-check by
  **2027-02-01**; registered as **FT-sloos-2027-05-03-1**.
- **Channel kill (deliberately NOT registered as a forward test):** KRE's close-to-close excess vs
  SPY on **2027-05-03** landing outside **±1.99%** (the measured p90) with session wraps naming
  SLOOS. Leg 2 would die. It stays a kill switch rather than an FT for the same reason the sibling
  gave and more so: three releases and a month-turn share the session, and a test that cannot
  attribute its own result is not a test.
- **Control kill:** the Monday VIX effect failing to reproduce out of sample — all Mondays from
  **2026-09-09 → 2027-05-03** averaging **below +0.307%** (the all-session mean). Leg 3's correction
  would be the artifact rather than the finding it corrected, and the weekday-pool rule this session
