## At a glance

**TL;DR.** Germany sells **EUR 2.5bn of 30-year Bund on 2026-10-14**, three hours before a
**confirmed** US CPI print. **Stand aside — the same measured null as its September sibling, plus a
second hypothesis killed before anyone could build on it.** The obvious story — that a big sovereign
auction cheapens the long end into the date — was tested and is a **composition artifact**: pooled
across **213** German 30Y auction dates since 2003 the Bund 30Y rises **+1.14bp** over T-5→T-1
(t = 1.93), but that is entirely the *small* auctions (**+1.26bp**, t = 2.01, n = 176); on the
**exact shape 10-14 is** — two lines, EUR 2.5bn, n = **23** — it is **−1.17bp (t = −0.49)**, the
opposite sign and nothing. The spillover null replicates independently (US 30Y CMT **+0.01bp**,
t = 0.01). What *is* new and real is **supply**: the Q4 calendar carries only **two** 30Y dates and
**no December one at all**, which puts 2026 full-year 30Y auction supply at **EUR 33.25bn vs EUR
37.0bn in 2025 — the first down year since 2022**, and partially fires the September ledger's
supply kill switch. Date **estimate**; it widens caution and licenses nothing.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This id reached the calendar as a proposal — `proposals/bund-30y-auction-2026-10-14.from-bund-30y-auction-2026-09-16.json`,
filed on 2026-09-09 by its sibling's own initial research, which read it as "the direct next
observation" for that ledger's forward tests. It was read first; this PR writes the canonical file.

So the honest question is not "will the auction go well," and it is not even the September
question again. That ledger established the null. The question here is: **the September entry
justified itself as an attribution instrument on FOMC day — does the same justification survive
when the collision is a confirmed CPI print three hours later instead of a Fed statement 8.5 hours
later, and is there anything about this auction the September study did not already answer?**

**One-line verdict:** the attribution job is **weaker** here and the expectation job is
**stronger** — CPI at 08:30 ET will own the session in a way the September study showed the FOMC
does not, while the allotment band is far better grounded (n = 23 identical-shape auctions, not a
retention average); and the two genuinely new findings are that **the pre-auction concession
everyone would assume does not exist for this auction's shape**, and that **Germany's 30-year
supply stops growing in 2026**.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The date, size and structure — SUPPORTED by two independent Finanzagentur primaries, and the
terms are LESS fixed than September's were.** The Q4 2026 calendar carries two lines dated
**14.10.2026**: `Bund 30 Y (R)` at **EUR 1.5bn** and `Bund 30 Y (R)` at **EUR 1.0bn**, with ISIN,
coupon and maturity all shown as `-`. The June-2026 H2 outlook workbook, published months earlier
and never touched since, carries the same date as `Bund | 30 years | Reopenings (multi-ISIN) |
2500`. Two primaries, one number.

The contrast with the sibling is the point. On **09-16** the calendar names DE000BU2D012 (2.90%,
15.08.2056) for EUR 1.5bn and leaves only the EUR 1.0bn line open — **60% of the volume fixed at
D-7**. On **10-14**, **neither** line is named at D-35. The auction-process page explains why:
*"For the multi-ISIN auctions in the 15- and 30-year maturity segment, the benchmark bonds to be
reopened are not finalised until the quarterly calendars are updated. The second bond … [is] only
announced in a press notice in the week before the auction."* Four bank business days before
Wednesday 14.10.2026 — counting back Oct 13, 12, 9, 8, with no German bank holiday in the way
(Unity Day, 3 October, falls on a Saturday in 2026) — is **Thursday 2026-10-08**. The rule is
observable, not merely stated: the 09-09 reopening was announced 09-03, invited 09-08, allocated
09-09, and the press index's newest entry is still September 08.

**One mechanic worth naming, because it bounds the size claim.** In H2 2026 the "second bond" of a
15/20/30-year multi-ISIN twice landed in the **15-year** bucket, not the 30-year one: 2026-08-12
allotted 30Y DE0001102614 (EUR 1.5bn offered) beside 15Y DE0001102598 (EUR 1.0bn), and 2026-08-26
allotted 30Y DE0001102432 (EUR 1.0bn) beside 15Y DE000BU2F009 (EUR 1.0bn). The June outlook labels
exactly those second lines generically, *"15/20/30 years"*. The Q4 calendar does **not** — it tags
both 10-14 lines `Bund 30 Y`, and it tags the 04.11.2026 pair `Bund 15 Y` and the 23.09.2026 flexible
line `Bund 15 Y`. So the segment tag is the debt office's own commitment and it is discriminating:
**EUR 2.5bn of 30-year duration is what the calendar commits to on 10-14.** Only the ISINs are open.

**2. There is no measurable Bund-auction → US-long-end spillover — REFUTED as a tradeable signal,
and now independently replicated.** Same construction as the September study (every 30-year German
auction date 2003-01-01 → 2026-08-26 with published bids, multi-ISIN lines aggregated to one
date-level bid-to-offer = total bids ÷ total offered, matched to the same-date yield change,
because bidding closes 05:30 ET and the same US session is the first that could react):

| Split (median bid-to-offer = 1.32) | n | US 30Y same-date | Bund 30Y same-date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soft (below median) | 102 / 106 | **−0.53bp** | **−0.06bp** |
| Firm (at or above) | 102 / 107 | **−0.54bp** | **−0.41bp** |
| Difference | — | **+0.01bp** (SE 0.69, **t = 0.01**) | **+0.35bp** (SE 0.54, **t = 0.66**) |

| Split | n | US 30Y same-date | Bund 30Y same-date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bids **<** offer | 47 | — | — |
| Difference vs covered | — | **+0.25bp** (SE 0.85, **t = 0.30**) | **+1.04bp** (SE 0.65, **t = 1.60**) |

Against daily standard deviations of **5.47bp** (US 30Y) and **4.64bp** (Bund 30Y). Identical
verdict, arrived at independently: the only measure that gets near a signal is a genuinely
undersubscribed auction moving **Germany's own** 30-year by about **1bp**, at t = 1.60 — sub-noise
and invisible across the Atlantic.

**One methodological refinement the September ledger did not state.** The US 30Y CMT series has a
**1,455-day hole, 2002-02-15 → 2006-02-09** — the 30-year bond's suspension — and **9 of the 213**
auction dates fall inside it. They are dropped, not interpolated across the gap; interpolating them
is what turns this leg into a spurious t = 1.13. The US column above therefore runs on **204**
dates. Registered as `FT-bund-30y-auction-2026-10-14-1`.

**3. There is no pre-auction supply concession on this auction's shape — REFUTED, and this is the
leg that did not exist before.** The intuitive story is that a EUR 2.5bn 30-year auction cheapens
the long end into the date and the concession reverses after. It has never been tested here.
Measured on the Bundesbank 30Y in event time (trading days around each of the 213 dates):

| Window | Pooled (n = 213) | Single-line / < EUR 2.0bn (n = 176) | Multi-ISIN ≥ EUR 2.0bn (n = 37) | **Exactly the 10-14 shape** (2 lines, EUR 2.5bn, n = 23) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T-5 → T-1 | **+1.14bp** (t = 1.93) | **+1.26bp** (t = 2.01) | +0.54bp (t = 0.33) | **−1.17bp** (t = −0.49) |
| T-1 → T0 | −0.23bp (t = −0.88) | −0.06bp (t = −0.20) | −1.08bp (t = −1.51) | −1.61bp (t = −1.62) |
| T1 → T5 | +0.23bp (t = 0.40) | −0.12bp (t = −0.20) | +1.89bp (t = 1.44) | +2.17bp (t = 1.29) |

The all-trading-day baseline for the same 4-day horizon is **−0.10bp** (SE 0.11, sd 9.05,
n = 6,627), so the pooled +1.14bp is a real deviation from drift. **It is also a composition
artifact.** It lives entirely in the small and single-line auctions; on the big multi-ISIN dates it
is a third the size and t = 0.33, and on the **exact shape 10-14 is** it flips sign. That sample's
own dispersion says the same thing: T-5→T-1 has sd **11.38bp** with a range of **−25 to +21bp** and
**3 of 23** at or above +10bp against **6 of 23** at or below −10bp. There is no concession here to
trade, to hedge, or to read into a 10-07 → 10-13 Bund selloff.

Eight windows were tested. The one nominally significant pooled result (t = 1.93) does not survive
any multiple-comparison correction, and it vanishes on the subsample that matters — which is the
honest reason to register this as a null rather than as a discovery.
Registered as `FT-bund-30y-auction-2026-10-14-3`.

**4. The allotment band is the number that carries information, and it can be much tighter than
September's — SUPPORTED.** September derived its band from a retention *average* (18.6% of EUR
2.5bn ⇒ ~EUR 2.04bn, band 1.85–2.20bn). There is a better estimator available: the workbook holds
**23** German 30Y auction dates that offered **exactly EUR 2.5bn across exactly two lines** —
10 in 2023, 11 in 2025, 2 in 2026, spanning 2023-01-18 → 2026-03-18. Their allotments:

| Statistic | EUR m |
|---|---|
| Mean | **2,001** |
| Median | 1,986 |
| Standard deviation | 105 |
| Min / Max | 1,888 (2025-04-16) / 2,396 (2025-06-18) |
| Inside **1,850–2,100** | **21 of 23 (91%)** |

Retention on that shape averages **20.0%** of offered. But the regime has drifted, and in one
direction: on this shape 2023 allotted a mean of 2,016 (retention 19.4%, bid-to-offer 1.56), 2025
allotted 2,004 (19.8%, 2.06), and **2026 allotted 1,908 (23.7%, 1.38)** — 2026-02-11 at 1,909.9 and
2026-03-18 at 1,905.6. Across all 30Y shapes, 2026 retention runs **22.0%** for the year and
**21.5%** over the last twelve auction dates, the highest since 2019. So the honest October band is
**EUR 1,850–2,100m**, midpoint ~1,950 — one hundred million *tighter at the top* than September's,
and stated with its 91% historical hit rate rather than as a point estimate.
Registered as `FT-bund-30y-auction-2026-10-14-2`.

**5. Germany's 30-year supply stops growing in 2026 — MIXED, and it partially fires the September
ledger's own kill switch.** That ledger's leg 5 framed Germany as Japan's mirror image: 30Y auction
supply roughly tripling while Japan cut its super-long 30–40%. Its kill switch 6 was *"Germany
announces a cut to 30-year issuance in its Q4 2026 or 2027 planning."* The Q4 calendar is now
published, and it carries **exactly two 30-year dates — 14.10 and 11.11, EUR 2.5bn each — and no
December date at all.**

| 30Y gross, EUR bn | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Auction process (Auc + M-A) | 13.1 | **11.4** | 32.5 | 27.5 | **37.0** | **33.25** (25.75 realised + 2.5 on 09-16 + 5.0 in Q4) |
| Including syndications | 24.6 | 23.4 | 44.0 | 41.0 | 47.5 | **40.75** |

That is **−10.1%** year on year on auctions and **−14.2%** including syndications — **the first
year-on-year decline since 2022**. Q4 alone: **5.0bn planned** against Q4 actuals of 7.0 (2025),
4.5 (2024), 5.7 (2023), 1.2 (2022).

**MIXED, not SUPPORTED, and the distinction is the whole point.** This is read off a published
calendar, not off an announced policy. The Finanzagentur has published no statement reducing
30-year issuance, the 2027 plan does not exist yet, and EUR 33.25bn is still **2.9x** the 2022
trough. The correct amendment to the sibling's framing is therefore *the tripling plateaued*, not
*Germany is cutting.* Registered as `FT-bund-30y-auction-2026-10-14-4`.

**6. The CPI collision is a genuinely worse attribution setup than September's FOMC collision —
SUPPORTED on structure, UNMEASURED on magnitude.** September's leg 3 established, and this session
re-verified on a broader FOMC set, that a Fed decision day does not enlarge the long end's *daily*
move: across **215** decision days 2003→2026, **26 of the 213** auction dates (12.2%) were FOMC
days, and on them the US 30Y's mean absolute move was **3.12bp** versus **3.88bp** on the other 180
— replicating the sibling's 3.17 / 3.87 almost exactly. All sessions: 4.18bp on FOMC days vs 4.03bp
otherwise, a ratio of **1.04x**.

CPI is not the same animal, and the sequencing makes it worse rather than better. The auction
result publishes at **05:30 ET**; [`cpi-2026-10-14`](cpi-2026-10-14.md) prints at **08:30 ET** and
is **confirmed**, high-impact, with a consensus number attached; the Beige Book follows at 14:00 ET
but its own ledger records that its collection cutoff is ~9 days earlier, so it cannot contain a
word about that morning's print. A 10-14 daily long-end move is CPI's by construction.

**And the magnitude half of this leg is honestly unmeasured.** The CPI-day base rate — does a CPI
session enlarge the 30Y's daily move the way an FOMC day demonstrably does not? — needs the BLS
historical release-schedule archive, which returned **403** to this session's fetcher. It is
recorded in `probe-ref.blocked` and routed to the next pulse, not guessed at. What can be said
without it is that **2026 is an unusually quiet long-end year in both currencies** (mean daily |Δ|:
US 30Y **2.90bp** YTD vs 4.03bp full sample; Bund 30Y **2.55bp** vs 3.37bp), so the prior that any
single session's long-end move is small has strengthened, not weakened.

**7. This auction is not part of a synchronised long-end cluster — REFUTED, and it is the second
place October differs from September.** The sibling's framing was three sovereigns selling long
duration inside 48 hours (JGB 20Y 09-15, Bund 30Y 09-16, on FOMC day). October does not look like
that. The cluster is **2026-10-08**, six days *before* this auction:
[`treasury-30y-bond-2026-10-08`](treasury-30y-bond-2026-10-08.md),
[`jgb-30y-auction-2026-10-08`](jgb-30y-auction-2026-10-08.md),
[`treasury-buyback-20y30y-2026-10-08`](treasury-buyback-20y30y-2026-10-08.md) and
[`ecb-account-2026-10-08`](ecb-account-2026-10-08.md) all land that session — which is also, by
the four-business-day rule, the day this auction's own ISINs get named. By 10-14 the US and
Japanese super-long supply is four sessions old and the US bond market has been shut for a day in
between ([`sifma-bond-market-closure-2026-10-12`](sifma-bond-market-closure-2026-10-12.md),
Columbus Day). Whatever "synchronised global long end" means, it does not describe 2026-10-14.

**8. The concession is real, the level is a 15-year high, and the transatlantic convergence has
stalled within 2026 — SUPPORTED (Bundesbank + US Treasury, both primary).** Bund 30Y closed

### Honest limits

- **Daily readings, not intraday.** Both yield series are one reading per day, so every leg
  measures the *net* session. On 10-14 an 08:30 ET CPI print plainly moves the tape; nothing here
  contradicts that, and the ledger does not claim CPI days are calm — it claims the auction is not
  what moves them.
- **The CPI-day base rate is unmeasured, not assumed.** `bls.gov` returned 403 to this session's
  fetcher on both schedule pages even with browser headers, and both FRED/ALFRED hosts failed to
  connect. Recorded in `probe-ref.blocked`; the next pulse should attempt it with a rendering
  fetcher, which [`cpi-2026-10-14`](cpi-2026-10-14.md) records as reaching bls.gov successfully.
- **The US 30Y CMT has a 1,455-day hole** (2002-02-15 → 2006-02-09), dropping 9 of 213 auction
  dates from every US-side statistic. Handling it either way lands the same verdict; interpolating
  across it does not.
- **Eight event windows were tested in leg 3.** The single nominally significant pooled result does
  not survive a multiple-comparison correction. It is reported because it is *interesting and
  false*, not because it is a finding.
- **The T-5→T-1 window for this auction contains a US bond-market holiday** (10-12), so any US-side
  concession measure around 10-14 spans four US sessions, not five. The Bund-side window is
  unaffected.
- **Both ISINs are unpublished.** Every allotment statement is a prior drawn from 23 same-shape
  dates; the identities land 2026-10-08.
- **Retention is a policy variable, not just a demand read.** The Finanzagentur chooses it and can
  use it to absorb weak demand or bank paper for market-making. A high retention print is ambiguous
  by construction, which is why the band — not a point forecast — is the registered form.
- **Bid-to-offer is not a US-style tail.** A multi-price auction has no single stop; coverage and
  retention are what exist. No cross-sovereign comparison of "softness" is attempted.
- **The supply plateau is a calendar reading, not an announced cut.** No Finanzagentur statement
  reducing 30-year issuance was found, and the 2027 plan does not exist yet.
- **VIX is Yahoo, i.e. secondary**, and 15.72 is the 2026-09-08 close (21-session mean 15.12, range
  14.25–16.34).
- **The date is `estimate`.** The Finanzagentur is the German sovereign debt office and two of its
  own primaries agree on the date and size; the label reflects a gap in `market-events-data.ts`'s
  prefix taxonomy, the same limit [`bund-30y-auction-2026-09-16`](bund-30y-auction-2026-09-16.md),

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (2026-09-09, D-35; date `estimate`).** **Stand aside, permanently.** No entry, exit or
hedge is keyed to the 2026-10-14 auction or to the 10-14 session. The entry is kept for two jobs,
and their weights have moved since the September sibling:

- **Expectation (the stronger job here).** The auction publishes at 05:30 ET with a *computable*
  allotment band — **EUR 1,850–2,100m**, hit by 21 of 23 identical-shape auctions — three hours
  before a confirmed CPI print nobody can compute. It is the one checkable number on the day.
- **Attribution (the weaker job here).** September could say a long-end move was not automatically
  the Fed's, because FOMC days demonstrably do not enlarge the 30Y's daily move (re-verified:
  3.12bp vs 3.88bp across 26 collisions). No such claim is available for CPI — the base rate is
  blocked, not known — so this ledger states only the sequencing and refuses to imply more.

Four reading rules attach to the day, and the third is new:

- **"Uncovered" is the format, not the news.** 22.1% of German 30Y auction dates since 2003 took
  bids below the offer. Judge the **allotment**.
- **Do not credit the auction for the session.** CPI at 08:30 ET owns 10-14 by construction; the
  auction is three hours older by the time the print lands.
- **Do not read a 10-07 → 10-13 Bund selloff as auction concession.** On this shape T-5→T-1 is
  **−1.17bp (t = −0.49)**, sd 11.38 — no concession exists to attribute a move to.
- **Do not call the supply plateau a cut.** Two dates in Q4 and no December date is a calendar, not
  a policy announcement.

**Kill switches.**

1. **The no-price-channel premise breaks** — a tracked name (NVDA/AVGO/MRVL/CRWV) moves **>2%** on
   a session between **2026-09-09** and **2026-10-14** that the tape attributes to a Bund or
   euro-rates headline. This document is rebuilt, not patched.
2. **The spillover null breaks** — the **2026-10-14** auction prints bid-to-offer **< 1.0** *and*
   the US 30Y CMT rises **≥ 11bp** the same date (2 sigma on the 5.47bp daily sd). Confounded by
   CPI by construction, so it reopens the test rather than settling it
   (`FT-bund-30y-auction-2026-10-14-1`).
3. **The retention reading breaks** — the 10-14 auction allots **outside EUR 1,850–2,100m** of the
   EUR 2.5bn offered, or the Federal government **rejects all bids**
   (`FT-bund-30y-auction-2026-10-14-2`).
4. **The terms are not completed on schedule** — no Finanzagentur press notice naming **both**
   10-14 ISINs by **2026-10-09**. The four-bank-business-day rule is load-bearing for the whole
   "terms are knowable at D-4" framing.
5. **The concession null breaks** — the Bundesbank 30Y cheapens **≥ +21bp** from the 2026-10-07
   close to the 2026-10-13 close (above the entire observed range of the 23 same-shape dates)
   (`FT-bund-30y-auction-2026-10-14-3`).
6. **The supply plateau reverses** — a **December 2026** 30Y auction date appears on the
   Finanzagentur calendar, or Q4 2026 30Y auction supply exceeds **EUR 5.0bn**
   (`FT-bund-30y-auction-2026-10-14-4`).
7. **The size commitment breaks** — the 10-08 announcement names a **15- or 20-year** bond for
   either 10-14 line, i.e. the calendar's `Bund 30 Y` segment tag turns out not to be binding.
   Leg 1's "EUR 2.5bn of 30-year duration" claim would be wrong and every allotment number here
   would be measuring a different auction.

Four predictions carry score-by dates and are registered in
[`forward-tests/bund-30y-auction-2026-10-14.md`](../forward-tests/bund-30y-auction-2026-10-14.md).
