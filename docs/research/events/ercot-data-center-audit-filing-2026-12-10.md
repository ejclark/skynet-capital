## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The [EIA STEO ledger](eia-steo-2026-09-09.md) discovered this date on 2026-09-02 while measuring
the only dated, quantified **downward** revision to AI-driven load growth on this calendar — the
August STEO cutting Texas 2027 electricity-load growth from **14% to 6%** in one month after
Abbott's pause. It filed 12-10 as "the date that actually gates the Texas data-center pause." So:
**is 2026-12-10 a real date, what precisely lands on it, and does the thing that lands actually
gate the pause?**

**One-line verdict.** The date is a genuine, on-the-record ERCOT goal and is now far
better-specified than when it was filed — but it is a **necessary-not-sufficient** step whose
sufficient condition (the Batch Zero study) has already lost its own deadline, against a directive
that sets none and a milestone chain that is **0 for 2**; nothing about it reaches a held name, and
its real value to this book is a dated collision with the PJM auction leg, not a position.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md)
(sector kind — no price or options instrument exists for it, and `symbols: []` leaves nothing to
run). Primaries attempted first: **gov.texas.gov reached** (the directive itself), **ercot.com
market notices reached** (M-A080326-03 read verbatim); **PUCT was not reached** — both
`interchange.puc.texas.gov` and `puc.texas.gov` failed TLS certificate validation. Everything else
is triangulated across trade press and law-firm alerts. All fetches/searches 2026-09-02; closes are
2026-09-01, the latest session available.

### Legs tested

1. **"2026-12-10 is a real target date."** **SUPPORTED as a goal, and materially upgraded in
   specificity.** Utility Dive (2026-08-20) carries Chad Seely, ERCOT SVP of regulatory policy and
   general counsel, verbatim at that day's PUCT open meeting: *"Our goal is to head toward a
   December 10 filing,"* with delivery planned about a week before the PUCT's December open
   meeting. The entry filed that quote with nothing behind it; this session adds the **content** —
   two named documents, the **Batch Zero Eligibility Verification Report** and the **Community
   Impact Review Report** — the **proceeding** (PUCT **Docket 59220**, alongside the good-cause
   exception filing **59142** the [08-20 ledger](texas-puct-audit-2026-08-20.md) already scored),
   the **process** (RFIs issued through interconnecting utilities end-Aug into Sep, further rounds
   Oct and Nov, cure windows between), and the **venue** (a **2026-12-17** PUCT open meeting
   presentation). Scope: ~**300** Batch Zero projects at ≥75 MW for eligibility verification, and a
   community-impact review reaching **25 MW and above** including crypto-mining sites.
2. **"The 12-10 filing is the checkpoint at which the queue reopens or stays shut"** — the
   calendar entry's own framing. **REFUTED as framed; the correct reading is
   necessary-not-sufficient.** Both halves are documented and they do not agree with the entry.
   *Necessary:* the audit must complete before any data center advances — Abbott's directive
   (gov.texas.gov, fetched primary, 2026-08-03): *"The PUCT and ERCOT must complete this audit
   before any data center project moves forward,"* and *"Any project that fails to comply ... will
   be denied connection to the Texas grid."* Seely to the Texas Tribune (2026-08-14): the
   verification is *"moving that verification process now to the front of the line before we begin
   the interconnection study process."* *Not sufficient:* the study that follows — the one that
   actually classifies and energizes loads — is the gate on connection, and Seely said plainly at
   the same 08-20 meeting: *"We will not have the study done by April 9, 2027. I think what we're
   saying is, we're still working on what that new timeline might be."* A necessary step clearing
   in December, followed by a study with **no date**, does not reopen a queue in 2026. This is the
   same shape the [Georgia ledger](georgia-psc-data-center-cost-shift-2026-12-31.md) found and
   named: **findings are a report; the remedy is a separate act by a separate body, later.**
3. **"The 12-10 goal is likely to be met."** **MIXED, leaning slip — and the base rate is
   measurable.** Under this audit, ERCOT has set two dated milestones and missed both, each on a
   primary of its own: **M-A080326-01** (2026-08-03) announcing it would not meet the **08-07**
   Batch Zero classification deadline, and **M-A080326-03** — read verbatim this session, dated
   **2026-08-31**, subject *"Delayed Issuance of Batch Zero Conditional Classifications"* — saying
   *"ERCOT requires additional time to complete its data validation and due diligence processes"*
   and pushing to "later this week," against a date the PUCT had itself **ordered on 08-20**. RTO
   Insider ran it on **2026-09-01** as a missed deadline. Two independent structural facts point
   the same way: the directive carries **no timeline** (Gibson Dunn, 2026-08-14: *"The letter does
   not provide any timeline on which the audit must be conducted"*), and ERCOT's own estimate for
   the verification process is *"several months, but less than nine months"* from August — a window
   whose far edge is **2027-04**, wider than a December finish. **Stated limit: n=2 is a thin base
   rate**, both misses are days-to-weeks rather than months, and neither is scored as a forecast
   here — it is why the stance treats 12-10 as soft, not a probability.
4. **"It reaches a name this book holds."** **REFUTED for revenue; MIXED for narrative.**
   `symbols: []` is correct and stays. No source found ties CRWV — the book's only ERCOT-adjacent
   name — to a Batch Zero project, and its channel remains siting and narrative rather than P&L,
   exactly as the entry's own notes state. The narrative channel is real but points **both ways**,
   which is why it is not a trade. Bernstein (2026-08-04) argued the audit *raises* the value of
   already-approved Texas capacity — *"If MWs are scarce, it makes sense to earn more per MW"* —
   rating CIFR, CLSK, CORZ, WULF, RIOT and IREN Outperform (MARA Market-perform), noting miners
   contracted ~**8 GW** across 20+ deals worth >**$160B** over two years. Needham read the same
   tape as compression, with AI-data-center equities down near **50%** from 52-week highs on
   political pressure. **Neither read is visible in the tape over the last week** (08-27→09-01
   closes): CORZ **17.62 → 16.07**, IREN **40.53 → 36.82**, WULF **16.49 → 14.65**, CRWV **86.80 →
   81.85**, VST **139.81 → 138.08**, NRG **116.14 → 109.51**, XLU **43.18 → 42.56** — but VIX went
   **14.51 → 16.34** over the same span, so that is a risk-off week, **not** evidence against the
   scarcity-rent thesis. Recorded as an observation, explicitly not an attribution.
5. **"Both legs of VST's Tier-2 entry condition resolve in the same week."** **SUPPORTED — the
   structural finding worth keeping.** [`ai-energy-constraint.md`](../ai-energy-constraint.md)
   gates VST on **both** "the Dec-15-2026 auction passing with credible cap-sunset signals **and**
   Texas audit resolving." Those two legs sit **five days apart**: this filing (12-10) and
   [pjm-capacity-auction-2026-12](pjm-capacity-auction-2026-12.md) (12-15). The ±5-day corridor
   around 12-10 holds six tracked events — **fomc-2026-12-09**, **cpi-2026-12-10** (same morning),
   **cr-expiry-2026-12-11** and **government-funding-deadline-2026-12-11**, **ppi-2026-12-15**,
   **pjm-capacity-auction-2026-12**. Consequence, stated now rather than in December: a
   power-complex move that week is **unattributable** to either leg without a same-day document, so
   the entry condition should be evaluated on **filings, not on price**. That is a planning output,
   not a play.
6. **"The pause is a Texas story."** **MIXED — worth one line, no more.** The audit's stated
   grounds are compliance with a PUCT survey on water and power usage under the General
   Appropriations Act, plus community impact, incentives and ownership — the same backlash channel
   [midterm-elections-2026-11-03](midterm-elections-2026-11-03.md) flagged and the
   [Georgia docket](georgia-psc-data-center-cost-shift-2026-12-31.md) instantiated. Texas is the
   larger instance by an order of magnitude (**~1,800 requests, ~474 GW, ~90% data centers, >5×**
   the **91,089 MW** all-time peak set **2026-07-22**), which is why the STEO could price it in one
   edition. The precedent risk is real, slow and undated — a watch item, never a position.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (estimate — no date-keyed action licensed).** Watch-only, no position, no play proposed,

leg does on 12-15. The reason to keep tracking it is that this is the largest instance anywhere of
the AI-load-growth premise being cut by policy rather than by demand, and the STEO prices it
monthly.

**Kill switches.**

- **The "no 2026 reopening" read dies** if ERCOT or PUCT publishes a Batch Zero study schedule with
  a **2026 or Q1-2027** completion date before 12-10, or if the **12-17** open meeting produces a
  PUCT **order** lifting the pause rather than accepting a report. Either would mean the pause is
  resolving a full quarter earlier than this stance assumes — re-tier and reassess immediately.

  fetched ERCOT notice or PUCT filing says so. Score it honestly against the 0-for-2 base rate this

  project denied or withdrawn on audit grounds. That converts a siting story into an earnings one.
- **The date leg upgrades** the moment a PUCT order or ERCOT market notice stating **12-10** is
  fetched — though `status` would still need a source-prefix decision, since this calendar has no

  [Georgia ledger](georgia-psc-data-center-cost-shift-2026-12-31.md) hit). Noted, not made.
- **The date is wrong, not merely unconfirmed,** if ERCOT states a different target — re-date the

- **The whole event goes quiet** if the audit is folded into the Batch Zero study itself or into a
