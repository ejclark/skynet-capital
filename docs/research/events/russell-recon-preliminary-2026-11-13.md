1. **The date is right, and the FAQ states the rule that generates it — SUPPORTED.** Both proposals rest
   on Appendix F's *"Four weeks prior to implementation"* plus the page's November-13 sentence. This
   session adds a third, independent form of the rule, from the FAQ: *"Periodic Review Announcement:
   Additions, deletions, and indicative shares in issue and free float data are published in advance of the
   June and December review implementations. For the June review, this information is published on the
   fifth Friday prior to the effective date, and for the December review, on the fourth Friday prior to the
   effective date."* Counting Fridays back from the 2026-12-11 implementation — 12-04, 11-27, 11-20,
   **11-13** — the fourth is this event's date; the identical count on June 2026 (implementation 06-26)
   returns **05-22**, which is the date the owner's own 2026 schedule press release publishes. And the
   February 2026 parallel-run paper's Exhibit 1 gives `13-Nov-26` a fifth time. **One discrepancy is
   recorded rather than smoothed:** the FAQ says *"prior to the effective date"* while elsewhere naming the
   Review Effective Date as **2026-12-14**; counted from 12-14 the rule returns 11-20, not 11-13. The rule
   only reproduces the published dates if "effective date" means the **implementation Friday** — which is
   how Appendix F words the same rule ("prior to implementation"). The two documents are not saying the
   same thing, and a reader of only the FAQ would be one week off.

2. **The December scope question is not open — the owner published its own simulated December leg, and no
   ledger in this chain had read it — SUPPORTED, and this is the session's central finding.** FTSE Russell
   ran a full parallel test of the semi-annual process in November 2025 and published the results in
   February 2026. Exhibit 7, verbatim:

   | Index | Jun-2025 adds | deletes | turnover (A) | Nov-2025 sim adds | deletes | turnover (B) | A+B |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | Russell 1000 | 38 | 25 | 2.00% | 32 | 16 | **0.73%** | 2.73% |
   | Russell 2000 | 235 | 173 | 10.43% | **141** | **119** | **5.37%** | 15.80% |
   | Russell 3000 | 229 | 154 | 2.14% | 133 | 95 | 0.75% | 2.89% |
   | R1000 Growth | 71 | 78 | 7.59% | 21 | 8 | 0.47% | 8.06% |
   | R1000 Value | 56 | 48 | 8.46% | 24 | 15 | 1.02% | 9.48% |

   The paper's own summary: *"Index turnover in the November 2025 parallel run was well below June 2025
   levels, with the Russell 1000 and Russell 3000 under 1% compared to roughly 2% in June 2025."* **The
   scope asymmetry is now sized, and the size is the finding.** As a fraction of the matching June figure,
   simulated December turnover runs **0.37×** (R1000) and **0.51×** (R2000) for the core indexes but
   **0.06× / 0.12×** for the R1000 growth/value pair and **0.28× / 0.22×** for the R2000 pair. That is the
   rules made arithmetic: the migration leg lands close to half of June, and the style leg — which
   December re-scores only for new entrants and size migrants — very nearly does not land at all. The
   `russell-reconstitution-2026-12-11` ledger stated this asymmetry as *"directionally certain and
   quantitatively unsized"*; it is sized here, from the owner's own numbers, in the direction it predicted.
