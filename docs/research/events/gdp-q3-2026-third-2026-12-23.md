2. **The lapse framing on this event's own calendar note is REFUTED — and this is the load-bearing
   finding of the pass (primary).** The `notes` field asserted that "BEA deletes rather than delays a
   GDP vintage in a lapse — so unlike the 11-25 second estimate, this release sits on the far side of

   schedule-update posts from the 2025 lapse, both fetched direct today, say something materially

   estimate of corporate profits"** and **"Jan. 22: Updated estimate (replaces the typical third

   State and Personal Income by State, 3rd Quarter 2025 … released on Jan. 23, 2026."* Read the
   mechanism, not the headline: BEA deletes a vintage **when a later vintage can absorb it** — an
   advance estimate caught in a lapse is worthless by the time staff return, because better source
   data has arrived. A *third* estimate is terminal; nothing downstream can absorb it, and its
   industry / profits / state payload exists nowhere else. So in 2025 the exact payload bundle this

   claim: 12-23's funding exposure is **a moved date, not a hole in the record**. The calendar
   `notes` are amended in this PR.

3. **The revision itself is BEA's smallest published step — SUPPORTED (primary), and it is why this
   event is `medium`.** BEA's *Comparisons of Revisions to Real GDP* (updated November 2025;
   comparisons "based on the period from 1999 through 2024 and reflect the results of the 2025 Annual
   Update") gives, for real GDP quarterly percent changes: advance→second **0.1 / 0.5 / 0.4**
   (average / average without regard to sign / sd), advance→third **0.1 / 0.6 / 0.5**, **second→third
   0.0 / 0.3 / 0.4**, advance→latest **0.2 / 1.2 / 1.0**. The step this event *is* — second to third —
   is unbiased at 0.0 and the smallest of the four by mean absolute revision, and its 0.3pp is half
   the 0.6pp the whole advance→third journey covers: **the second estimate does most of the work, and
   this vintage cleans up.** The same document adds that the direction of change between advance and
   latest matches **97 percent** of the time, and that estimates correctly place growth above, near or
   below trend **92 percent** of the time. The operational read is a ceiling on how much 12-23 can
   matter, and it is what makes `impact: medium` correct rather than conservative.
