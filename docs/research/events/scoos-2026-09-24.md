## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The Senior Credit Officer Opinion Survey on Dealer Financing Terms publishes at 14:00 ET on
**2026-09-24**. It was proposed to this calendar on 2026-09-01 by the
[`beige-book-2026-09-02`](beige-book-2026-09-02.md) pulse check, off the Board's September calendar,
filed `low` and `estimate` with the framing that it is "the Fed's own quarterly measurement of what
Warsh's 2026-08-28 Jackson Hole keynote asserted from market prices." Does that framing survive
contact with the primaries — and does a qualitative 20-dealer survey carry anything for a book long
nine AI-infra / mega-cap names?

### One-line verdict

The date is confirmed and the tier is correctly `low`, but the proposing note is wrong in two
checkable ways — its **baseline is one edition stale**, and its **"scorecard on Warsh" framing is off
by about four days** — and the corrected version is *more* interesting, because SCOOS turns out to
measure the one leg of financial conditions Warsh's own evidence base leaves out.

### Leg 2 — THE first correction: the baseline is one edition stale, and the current one is less benign. SUPPORTED (Fed primaries, both editions read in full).

The calendar note says "The March 2026 edition found dealers' overall use of financial leverage and
financing terms mostly unchanged, with dealers expecting to increase securities-financing capacity —
that is the baseline to diff." The March description is accurate. It is also **not the latest
edition**: `data/scoos.htm` lists **June 2026** (released 06-25, conducted **May 12–26**, covering
March→May), and the September print will diff against *that*. The two differ in ways that matter:

| Core-question line | March 2026 | **June 2026 (the real baseline)** |
|---|---|---|
| Price/nonprice terms, all counterparties | Largely unchanged | Largely unchanged |
| Hedge funds pushing for better terms | **One-fifth** reported increased intensity | **About one-third** |
| Mark & collateral disputes, counterparties | "Basically unchanged, on net, for all counterparty types" | **"Roughly one-fifth of respondents, on net, reported that the volume, duration, and persistence of mark and collateral disputes increased over the past three months for nearly all counterparty types"** |
| CCP practices affecting bilateral terms | "A small fraction" reported some influence | **One-half** reported at least a small degree of effect |
| Clients' use of financial leverage | Basically unchanged | Basically unchanged |
| Demand for equity funding | More than one-half reported an increase | About two-fifths |

And the special questions moved from a benign forward-looking topic (March: expected 2026 funding
demand and capacity — dealers broadly expecting to *increase* capacity) to a credit-quality one. June's
special questions covered **warehouse financing for CLOs**, and the private-credit half is the single
least benign paragraph SCOOS has published in the recent run:

> "One-half of respondents indicated that they were currently active in the provision of warehouse
> financing facilities for private credit CLOs. On net, about one-third of these respondents indicated
> their provision of this financing increased over the past 12 months. **A net fraction of one-fifth
> reported having tightened the terms on such financing. Among those who reported tightened terms, the
> reasons most commonly cited as important were a deterioration in the credit quality of underlying
> collateral and increased uncertainty about collateral valuation.**"

Read against Leg 3: that is dealers pulling back on the private-credit leverage chain *in May 2026*,
while corporate and leveraged-loan spreads sat "near the low ends of their historical ranges." The
aggregate "unchanged" headline and the component tell disagree, and the component is the one with
information in it.

### Leg 3 — THE second correction: this is the leg Warsh's argument omits, not a scorecard on it. SUPPORTED (speech text, fetched direct).

The proposing note's quote is **verbatim correct** — verified in `speech/warsh20260828a.htm` this
session. What the note misses is the sentence *before* it. The full passage:

> "…the Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey on Bank Lending Practices, banks tell us that standards for
> commercial and industrial loans are on the easier end of their historical range. That helps explain
> the growth we've seen this year in those loans. Credit and loan markets are showing few signs of
> policy restraint. Certain sectors—like housing and agriculture—are showing strains. But, on balance,
> **I would be hard pressed to describe broad financial conditions as restrictive.**"

Plus, separately: "Credit spreads on corporate bonds and leveraged loans are near the low ends of their
historical ranges, and issuance volumes in these markets have been quite strong this year."

So Warsh's evidence base is **SLOOS (banks) + market prices**. He does not cite SCOOS anywhere in the
keynote. SCOOS is the Fed's measurement of the **third** conduit — dealer financing, which the Board's
own series description calls "important conduits for leverage in the financial system" — and it is the
one leg his non-restrictive claim rests on by omission rather than by evidence. That is a materially
better reason to carry this row than "scorecard," and it survives the tier being `low`: the value is in
the *read*, and the read is a check on the chair's premise, not a trade.

1. **It is not a scorecard.** It measures the same March→August period Warsh was describing, and closes

   conduit he did not cite — not a verdict on how markets took the speech.

   is **~30 days stale**, and the first SCOOS that can see any of it is the December edition.
3. **The next edition is the interesting one.** A December 2026 SCOOS conducted ~Nov 10–23 would be the
   first read on whether a September hike (if it happened) transmitted into dealer financing terms —
   and, on Leg 7's precedent, it publishes regardless of the funding branch.

The 2022 edition closed **Aug 29**, so this is a strong pattern rather than a certainty; the prediction
is registered as **FT-45** and settled mechanically by footnote 1 of the 09-24 release.

### Leg 7 — the lapse-immunity claim now has a dated receipt. SUPPORTED (dates, primary-anchored).

### Leg 8 — the AI channel: SCOOS has touched this book exactly once, and it is not on this quarter's ballot. MIXED.

The one time SCOOS spoke directly to a portfolio of AI-infra names was **December 2025**, whose special
questions asked dealers about equity financing and synthetic exposure provided to hedge funds,
including a dedicated block on **AI-focused stocks** (defined in the report's own footnote 4 as
companies deriving significant revenue from AI products, leading core AI development, or making
substantial proprietary AI research investments):

- **Two-thirds** of dealers reported hedge-fund demand for AI-focused equity exposure had **increased
  relative to other sectors** over the prior two years.
- Price **and** nonprice terms offered on that exposure **remained basically unchanged** relative to
  other sectors — dealers were financing AI concentration without charging for it.
- Separately, **one-third** reported the notional of **synthetic** equity exposure to hedge funds had
  risen relative to traditional prime brokerage, and **two-thirds** said they offer better price terms
  where internalization potential is high.

That is the closest thing to a leverage-in-our-own-names read this calendar has. Graded **MIXED**
rather than supported as a live signal for two reasons: it is a **December 2025** observation now nine
months old, and special questions **rotate** — Dec-2025 was AI equity financing, Mar-2026 was 2026
funding expectations, Jun-2026 was CLO warehouses. The September topic is unknowable until 14:00 on
09-24, so this is a "what to hope for when the report lands," not a basis for positioning.

- **The June-2026 private-credit finding is a net-fraction of ~10 active dealers** (one-half of a 20-firm panel), stated qualitatively with no magnitudes. "A net fraction of one-fifth" is roughly two firms. Directional, not measurable.
