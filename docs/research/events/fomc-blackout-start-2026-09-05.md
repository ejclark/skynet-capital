## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The Fed's communications blackout for the Sep 15–16 FOMC starts 2026-09-05. It is not a print — it
is a gate on *who may speak*. Given that a single Fed speech just repriced September hike odds by
~20 points in one session, what actually changes for the tape when the gate falls, and what should
the book do about it?

**One-line verdict:** the blackout is a **structural change in how information arrives, not an event
that prints** — it removes the scheduled Fed-speak channel for 12 days spanning two long-end
auctions, PPI and CPI, which concentrates all remaining odds-repricing into two channels you cannot
schedule around (the data itself, and an unscheduled press leak). The supported response is a
*timing* discipline and a vol posture, never a direction. Date **confirmed**.

**Method:** sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md) —
federalreserve.gov primary documents fetched and parsed directly today, press-reported FedWatch odds
cited with as-of dates, and the published academic work on the pre-FOMC window as the prior. No
price instruments run: this is a macro gate with no issuer and `symbols: []`.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date derives mechanically from a Fed primary source — flipped `estimate` → `confirmed`.**
   SUPPORTED. federalreserve.gov's "2025–2027 FOMC Trading and External Communications Blackout
   Calendar" PDF was fetched and its text layer decompressed directly on 2026-08-30 (the proposing
   pulse recorded that it "did not parse on fetch" — that gap is what kept this an estimate, and it
   is now closed). Its footnote quotes the policy verbatim: *"The blackout period will begin at
   12:00 a.m. Eastern Time the second Saturday before a meeting and end at 11:59 p.m. Eastern Time
   the day after a meeting. For example, if the Committee meeting starts on a Tuesday, the blackout
   period will begin at the start of the Saturday that falls ten days earlier, and if the meeting
   ends on a Wednesday, the blackout period will end at the end of Thursday."* The Sep 15–16 meeting
   is **confirmed** (FED: FOMC calendar, per [`fomc-2026-09-16.md`](fomc-2026-09-16.md)); Sep 15 is a
   Tuesday and Sep 16 a Wednesday, so the source's own worked example applies without judgment:
   ten days earlier is **Saturday 2026-09-05**, and the end is the end of **Thursday 2026-09-17**.
   No arithmetic of mine is load-bearing that the source did not already perform.

2. **The last legal Fed voice is 2026-09-04 — jobs day — and by the Board's own calendar there may
   be no Board voice at all.** SUPPORTED, with a stated caveat. The gate falls at 12:00 a.m. ET on
   Saturday 9/5, so Friday 9/4 is fully outside it: an official *could* react to the jobs print on
   the day. But federalreserve.gov's September 2026 Board calendar, fetched today, lists nothing for
   2026-09-01 → 09-04 except statistical releases (G.5, H.15, CP, H.4.1, H.8) and the **Beige Book on
   9/2** — no governor speech, no Board event. On today's schedule, Warsh's 2026-08-28 Jackson Hole
   keynote is on track to be the **last Board-level Fed voice before the decision**. *Caveat:* Board
   speeches are routinely added on short notice and regional-president appearances live on district
   sites, not the Board calendar — this is a snapshot, not a guarantee, and it is the first thing the
   next pulse should re-check.

3. **This blackout is unusually load-bearing because the speech channel just proved what it is
   worth.** SUPPORTED. On 2026-08-28 CME FedWatch went from ~35% hike the prior day to ~55.7–60%
   hike intraday on Warsh's keynote — the single biggest one-day repricing of this hike-watch cycle
   (CNBC "September Fed decision is now a coin flip", TheStreet, Kalshi, all 2026-08-28). A cut
   remains priced at 0%. In a cycle where one speech is worth ~20 points of hike odds, closing the
   speech channel for 12 days is a genuine change to the information supply, not a technicality —
   which is the whole reason this entry exists on the calendar at all.

4. **The blackout does not close the channel; it reroutes it into one unscheduled article.**
   SUPPORTED, one canonical precedent. The WSJ's Nick Timiraos is the widely-documented conduit when
   the Committee needs to move expectations mid-blackout. The canonical case: in June 2022, with the
   Fed over a week into a blackout and consensus at 50bp, a Timiraos piece flagged that a
   0.75-percentage-point move was likely to be considered — and the Fed then hiked 75bp. The risk
   shape inside 9/5 → 9/16 is therefore **gap risk on no schedule**, which is precisely the exposure a
   short-volatility position is worst equipped to hold. *Honest weight:* this is one famous case plus
   a broadly-held market belief, not a measured base rate — I did not measure how often a mid-blackout
   press story actually moves odds.

5. **The academic prior says the pre-FOMC window is low-realized-vol with a mild positive drift —
   and that prior is weaker than usual here.** MIXED. Lucca & Moench (NY Fed Staff Report / NBER)
   document the pre-FOMC announcement drift: **+33.2bp** average S&P 500 excess return in the 24
   hours before scheduled announcements, significant at the 1% level, with realized volatility *and*
   liquidity both lower in that window — consistent with no new FOMC information arriving during the
   purdah. Later work finds the effect has decayed and is state-dependent (stronger in high-vol
   states, associated with VIX declines). Three reasons not to lean on it: (a) it is estimated on the
   **24 hours** before the announcement, not a 12-day blackout; (b) it is averaged across meetings
   whose direction was rarely two-sided at ~57/43; (c) this particular window contains two top-tier
   inflation prints whose surprises will be repriced with no official interpretation to damp them.
   The mechanism the literature identifies (no new FOMC information) is exactly the mechanism that
   *fails* when the non-FOMC information flow is this heavy.

6. **The corridor is what makes it matter, not the blackout by itself.** SUPPORTED. Tracked events

   **30Y bond 9/10** (confirmed) plus a **10y/20y buyback 9/10** (estimate), **PPI 9/10** (confirmed),
   **CPI 9/11** (confirmed), **20Y bond 9/15** (confirmed), **retail sales 9/16** (confirmed), and the
   **FOMC decision 9/16** itself. Two long-end auctions and a CPI print into a Fed that cannot
   comment is the first time this cycle a weak-auction-plus-hot-CPI sequence would have no official
   damping mechanism available to it. The `estimate`-labelled items in that list widen caution only —
   they license nothing.

7. **Our exposure is inherited, not direct.** SUPPORTED. `symbols: []` — the blackout has no issuer.
   Exposure runs entirely through the tiering [`fomc-2026-09-16.md`](fomc-2026-09-16.md) already

### What plays the conditions support (date confirmed)

None directional. Two behavioral rules, both already implied by the sibling FOMC stance and made
*specific* here by the gate's start time:

- **A deadline, not a trade.** Any position that depends on a Fed voice clarifying the hold-vs-hike
  branch has until the **2026-09-04** close. After that the only inputs are the data and a leak.
- **No short volatility across 2026-09-05 → 09-16.** The window carries unscheduled headline gap-risk
  with no scheduled speaker to bleed it off. VIX at 14.35 is the one regime where cheap convexity is
  cheap *for a reason* rather than a gift — which is the same observation the FOMC doc's "buy
  protection, not direction" call rests on, arriving at it from the supply side of information rather
  than the price side.

date itself: I read the PDF's **rule text and worked example**, not the shaded cells of its 2026

mine. Two independent press summaries also state 2026-09-05 → 09-17; they are corroboration, not the
basis. FedWatch figures are press-cited snapshots that drift daily, checked 2026-08-30.

## Stance & kill switches

trade**. Concretely: treat the **2026-09-04** close as the deadline for anything that needed Fed
commentary, and treat **2026-09-05 → 09-16** as a no-short-vol window. No directional call and no
size, in either direction. The blackout does not change the hold-vs-hike branch; it changes who is
permitted to change it — and this cycle that distinction has a measured price (~20 points of hike
odds for one speech, 2026-08-28).
