## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **The proposal that created this entry said "announced." As of yesterday it is law, and that
changes what the entry is for.** SI 2026/987 — The Value Added Tax (Supplies of Domestic Electricity)
Order 2026 — was **made 7 September** and **laid 8 September 2026**, coming into force **1 October**,
zero-rating domestic electricity in Great Britain until **31 March 2027**. It needs **no further
parliamentary act**: none of VATA 1994 s.97(4)'s made-affirmative limbs is engaged, so it falls to
s.97(5) and is in force unless the Commons annuls it. The **−0.10pp** the government claimed
reproduces **exactly** from ONS's own weights — CPI electricity weight **20.6065/1000** × the
**4.7619%** (5/105) price fall = **0.098pp**. So the interesting question is not *whether* but
*what it means*, and there are three answers. **One: nothing happens on 1 October.** An effective
date produces no print; the first observable is the ONS October CPI bulletin, **18 November 2026** —
*after* the 5 November MPC votes. **Two: the one genuine uncertainty is pass-through, and HMRC itself
refuses to assert it** ("subject to the degree to which these businesses pass the VAT savings on to
their customers") — **Ofgem's cap settles it for capped tariffs**, because the October cap is set
VAT-inclusive and already embeds the removal. **Three, and this is where this lane parts company with
the sibling ledger: the cut is a look-through event by construction** — a six-month notch whose
sunset is written *inside* the Order — so it is **weaker** evidence for a dovish Bank Rate path than
its headline size suggests. **None of it is ours to trade.** `symbols: []`; the calendar tracks eight
US mega-caps and no UK listing, utility, gilt or sterling instrument. Date is **estimate** on a
taxonomy gap, not on the evidence; it widens caution and licenses nothing.

### The question, plainly

This entry arrived by **proposal**, not by discovery: the [`uk-cpi-2026-09-16`](uk-cpi-2026-09-16.md)
lane found it on 2026-09-09 while testing whether the August print reached this book, and filed

the 21 July gov.uk announcement and Ofgem's 26 August cap release. That proposal was read in full

placement finding survives intact.

So the question is not "is a UK VAT cut interesting". It is: **is a pre-announced, quantified,
mechanical change to a foreign CPI series anything other than a number already in everyone's
spreadsheet — and does any part of it reach a US-only, equity-and-earnings-keyed book?**

**One-line verdict:** the size is genuinely mechanical and reproduces to the decimal from ONS's own
weights, so it carries no information as a *number* — but three things about it were **not** in the
proposal and are not in the commentary: it stopped being an announcement and **became law on
7 September**, under a procedure that makes it **easy to grant and hard to withdraw early**; the one
leg that could still fail is **pass-through, which HMRC explicitly refuses to vouch for** and only
Ofgem's cap secures; and because the sunset is written *into the instrument*, the cut is a
**look-through notch** rather than a disinflation signal — which makes it weaker support for a dovish
rate path than the sibling ledger's quarter-horizon call implies.

### Leg 1 — "The date is announced, not legislated." **REFUTED, and this is the headline.**

The proposal, correctly for 2026-09-09 morning, treated this as a policy intention. It is not. **The
Value Added Tax (Supplies of Domestic Electricity) Order 2026, SI 2026/987** carries, verbatim from
legislation.gov.uk:

- **Made:** 7th September 2026
- **Laid before the House of Commons:** 8th September 2026
- **Coming into force:** 1st October 2026
- **Enabling powers:** sections **29A(3), 30(4) and 96(9)** of the Value Added Tax Act 1994
- **Effect:** "supplies made in the period beginning with **1st October 2026** and ending with
  **31st March 2027**"

Its four articles are *Citation, commencement and effect* · *Modifications of the Value Added Tax Act
1994* · *Zero-rating of supplies of domestic electricity: England and Wales and Scotland* ·
*Consequential modifications to Schedule 7A*. HMRC's TIIN, published the following day, states the
mechanism plainly: the Order **inserts a new Group 24 into Schedule 8** ("Domestic Electricity:
England and Wales and Scotland") and **narrows Schedule 7A** so the 5% reduced rate applies only in
Northern Ireland — "maintaining the existing descriptions of when those supplies qualify for relief."

The event is therefore as dated as this calendar gets. The `estimate` label is a **schema gap**, not a
doubt: the confirmed-prefix taxonomy in `market-events-data.ts` (IR:/CAL:/BLS:/FED:/PJM:/SEC:/TSY:/
OCC:/BEA:/CENSUS:/ISM:/CB:/UMICH:/FHFA:) has no slot for a UK statutory instrument.

### Leg 2 — "It could still need a Commons vote before it bites." **REFUTED, from the statute itself.**

This was the one leg worth real legal reading, because a required approval vote inside 28 days of
7 September would have been a dated risk sitting right on the effective date. It is not there.

VATA 1994 **s.97(3)** imposes the made-affirmative procedure — "unless it is approved by that House
before the expiration of a period of 28 days beginning with the date on which it was made, it shall
cease to have effect" — but only on the orders **s.97(4)** lists. The relevant limb, s.97(4)(c),
catches an order making provision:

- (i) "for **increasing** the rate of VAT" — the Order cuts it;
- (ii) "for excluding any VAT from credit under section 25" — not engaged;
- (iia) "for varying Schedule 7A so as to cause VAT to be charged on a supply at the rate in force
  under section 2 **instead of** that in force under section 29A" — i.e. moving a supply from the
  **reduced** rate to the **standard** rate. The Order moves GB electricity from the reduced rate to
  the **zero** rate, not the standard rate;
- (iii) "for varying Schedule 8 or 9 so as to **abolish** the zero-rating of a supply" — the Order
  *creates* a zero-rated group.

None applies, so **s.97(5)** governs: the instrument "shall be subject to **annulment** in pursuance
of a resolution of the House of Commons." It is in force on 1 October unless the Commons prays against
it and annuls it.

**The structural asymmetry that falls out of this, and it is not in any commentary read this session:
the cut was easy to grant and would be hard to withdraw early.** An order abolishing the new Group 24
before 31 March 2027 *would* be caught by s.97(4)(c)(iii) and would need an affirmative Commons vote
inside 28 days. The **lapse** on 1 April 2027 needs nothing at all — it is written into article 1. So
the ratchet runs one way: cheap to start, cheap to end on schedule, expensive to end early.

### Leg 3 — "−0.10pp CPI / −0.14pp RPI." **CPI SUPPORTED to the decimal; RPI MIXED.**

Removing a 5% VAT rate from a VAT-inclusive consumer price cuts that price by **5/105 = 4.7619%**.
Against ONS's own published 2026 weights (series fetched as CSV today):

| Index | 2026 electricity weight (per 1000) | × 4.7619% | Government's stated figure |
|---|---|---|---|
| CPI (CJXA) | **20.6065** | **0.098pp** | "around 0.10 percentage points" ✅ |
| RPI (CZXL) | **27** | **0.129pp** | "0.14 percentage points" ⚠️ |

The CPI number is **exactly** reproducible — a rare case where a government inflation claim can be
audited from published data in one multiplication. The RPI number is **not**: reaching 0.14pp would
require a weight near **29.4** per 1000 against the 27 ONS publishes. The 0.011pp gap is small and may
reflect RPI's different elementary aggregation or a costing done on a different weight vintage; this
lane could **not close it from published weights** and records it as an honest limit rather than
guessing. Nothing in this book turns on RPI, so the gap is noted, not pursued.

**Two refinements the round number hides.** First, the relief is **GB-only** — Northern Ireland stays
on the 5% reduced rate under the Windsor Framework, and the Order narrows Schedule 7A to say so —
while the CPI weight is UK-wide. Even at a generous 5% NI share of UK household electricity spend the
shave is **0.005pp**, comfortably below CPI's published **0.1pp** precision. Second, and more
practically: at **0.098pp** against a series published to one decimal, **the effect may or may not be
visible in the headline at all**, depending on where the unrounded number sits. Any forward test keyed
to seeing 0.1pp move in the headline would be untestable by construction — which is why the tests
registered below key on the **electricity index**, not the headline.

### Leg 4 — "It will show up in bills, so it will show up in CPI." **MIXED — and HMRC agrees it is mixed.**

This is the only leg with real doubt in it, and the source that ought to be most confident is the one
that hedges. HMRC's own TIIN, on households: they "will benefit from lower prices charged by domestic
electricity providers, **subject to the degree to which these businesses pass the VAT savings on to
their customers**." A tax authority declining to assert pass-through in its own impact note is a
genuine flag, not boilerplate — the note is otherwise specific (≈100 affected suppliers, "negligible"
administrative impact, named contact, ministerial declaration).

**Ofgem's cap is what closes it, for capped tariffs.** The 26 August 2026 press release announcing the
1 October–31 December cap states the removal "is reflected in this update", that "the VAT reduction
means electricity bills will remain broadly stable" while gas rises **8%**, that the overall cap rises
**4%** to **£1,723**, that households without gas see "less than 1%", and that without the intervention
the figure "would have been around £45 higher". The cap is a **VAT-inclusive maximum**: a supplier
cannot decline to pass through what the cap has already netted out. Pass-through is therefore
mechanical where the cap binds, and a supplier-behaviour question only on fixed tariffs outside it.

That asymmetry is exactly what makes this testable, and it is the subject of
**FT-uk-electricity-vat-zero-rate-2026-10-01-1**: watch the **electricity index** in the 2026-11-18
bulletin, which should rise materially less than gas, rather than the headline, which rounds.

### Leg 6 — "A −0.1pp cut is a dovish argument for the MPC." **MIXED — and here this lane parts company with its proposer.**

The sibling [`uk-cpi-2026-09-16`](uk-cpi-2026-09-16.md) ledger's quarter-horizon call is "take the
under on the Q4 peak — the government has already pre-announced a −0.10pp cut the strip is not
pricing", against a strip implying a higher Bank Rate by December (Bank Rate **3.75%**, 09-08). The
*arithmetic* underneath that is right and is not disputed here. The **policy inference** is where this
lane would put it more carefully, on a fact that only became available yesterday:

**the sunset is inside the instrument.** Article 1 ends the zero rate on 31 March 2027 with no further
act required, and HMRC's TIIN budgets suppliers for updating systems "again to change the rate from
0% to 5% **when the temporary zero rate comes to an end on 31 March 2027**." So the CPI path gets a
−0.1pp notch from October 2026 and a **+0.1pp notch back** from April 2027. A committee forecasting to
a two-year horizon looks through both. The November MPR *will* embed the cut — it is legislated and
public well before the 5 November meeting — but embedding a self-reversing tax notch is not the same
as observing disinflation, and it is not a reason to vote differently.

**Where the dovish reading does have teeth:** on **realised** headline CPI in the Q4-2026 window, which
the July MPR put at a peak near 3.2%, a −0.098pp shave is real and could round a print down. The
distinction that matters — and that this entry exists to hold — is between *the printed number in
November* (genuinely lower) and *the policy signal* (unchanged). **They are being conflated, and this
lane's stance is that the second reading is wrong.**

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date: estimate on a taxonomy gap; the underlying instrument is in force).** **Stand aside at
every horizon — there is no instrument in this book with a channel to a British consumption-tax
change,** and none is claimed. The entry's operational value is threefold and none of it is a trade:

1. **A date to rule out.** 2026-10-01 is an effective date that publishes nothing. Any move that day is
   the US funding deadline, PCE, ISM, the BoJ Tankan or OPEC+, not this.
2. **An attribution rule for 2026-11-18.** A UK October CPI print ~0.1pp softer than a naive model is a
   **tax change**. It is not disinflation, and it is not a dovish surprise.
3. **A correction to the dovish reading.** Because the sunset sits inside SI 2026/987, the cut is a
   self-reversing notch the MPC looks through in both directions. It lowers the *printed* Q4-2026
   number; it is materially weaker evidence about the *rate path* than its size suggests.

**Kill switches** — any one of these and the stance is rebuilt, not adjusted:

- **A tracked name moves >2%** on a session between 2026-09-09 and 2026-10-01 attributable to UK
  electricity VAT policy → the "no price channel" premise is false.
- **SI 2026/987 is annulled**, or a prayer motion against it is tabled before 2026-10-01 → the date and
  the −0.098pp both come off the board.
- **The ONS October 2026 bulletin (2026-11-18) shows the CPI electricity index up ≥2.0% m/m** →
  pass-through failed, HMRC's hedge was the right call and Ofgem's cap did not bind as assumed
  (**FT-uk-electricity-vat-zero-rate-2026-10-01-1**).
- **The 2026-10-28 Budget extends the zero rate beyond 2027-03-31** → the notch becomes a level shift,
  Leg 6's look-through argument weakens, and the dovish reading gets real support
  (**FT-uk-electricity-vat-zero-rate-2026-10-01-2**).
- **An ONS or OBR publication restates the CPI effect outside 0.08–0.12pp** → Leg 3's arithmetic, and
  with it the claim that this number carries no information, is wrong.
