## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This calendar tracks **eight** ECB entries and, before this one, **zero** Eurostat inflation
releases — which is why the euro area's August HICP flash, published 2026-09-01, did not reach the
[`cpi-2026-09-11`](cpi-2026-09-11.md) ledger until that ledger's D-3 adjacency sweep a week later,
and this event was proposed there. So the question is not "what will the number be" (it was
published on 09-01) but: **what, if anything, does a complete release add that the flash did not —
and is any of it worth a session, a slot, or a position?**

**One-line verdict:** three things are knowable and none is a trade — the **headline is already
public and has survived four consecutive complete releases unrevised**, the release lands **after
both policy meetings it could inform**, and the one genuinely new artefact (the contribution table)
is **precomputable today to ~±0.05pp**, which is registered below as a forward test rather than
claimed as insight.

**Method:** sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md).
Eurostat primaries fetched direct 2026-09-08: the August flash (2-01092026-ap), the July complete
(2-19082026-ap), the June complete (2-17072026-ap), the May complete (2-17062026-ap) and the HICP
information-on-data page. The ECB statistical release calendar for HICP fetched direct the same day.
The April flash/complete pair (2-30042026-ap, 2-20052026-ap) is search-relayed rather than fetched,

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The headline is already public, and the complete release has not moved one in four consecutive
   months.** SUPPORTED, primary, and it sets everything below. The August flash (2-01092026-ap,
   published 2026-09-01, re-fetched today) reads verbatim: *"Euro area annual inflation is expected
   to be 3.3% in August 2026, up from 2.9% in July"*, with **energy 14.3%** (from 10.3%),
   **services 3.0%** (from 3.3%), **non-energy industrial goods 1.2%** (from 0.9%), **food, alcohol
   & tobacco 1.2%** (stable), **core ex-energy-and-food 2.4%** and **m/m +0.4%**. Against that, the
   flash→complete record this session assembled from the releases themselves:

   | Reference month | Flash (release, date) | Complete (release, date) | Moved? |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | April 2026 | 3.0% (2-30042026-ap, 30 Apr) | 3.0% (2-20052026-ap, 20 May) | no *(search-relayed pair)* |
   | May 2026 | 3.2% (2-02062026-ap, 2 Jun) | 3.2% (2-17062026-ap, 17 Jun) | no |
   | June 2026 | 2.8% (2-01072026-ap, 1 Jul) | 2.8% (2-17072026-ap, 17 Jul) | no |
   | July 2026 | 2.9% (2-31072026-ap, 31 Jul) | 2.9% (2-19082026-ap, 19 Aug) | no |

   Eurostat's own **HICP information-on-data** page (fetched today) goes further than "rarely
   revised": a flash-vs-final difference is *not treated as a formal revision at all* — the two are
   distinct publication events, and the HICP's actual revision policy runs through Article 7(5) of
   Regulation 2016/792. Registered as **FT-eurostat-hicp-final-2026-09-17-1** in the bounded form
   the evidence supports (**within ±0.1pp**), not as "exactly 3.3%".

2. **The date carries a second primary, and the clock is the load-bearing detail: 12:00 CET =
   06:00 ET.** SUPPORTED. The Eurostat flash names the date (*"The next release with full data for
   August 2026 is scheduled for 17 September 2026"*) but no time. The **ECB statistical release
   calendar** for HICP (fetched today) supplies both: *"17/09/2026 12:00 CET — Euro area seasonally
   adjusted HICP (Dataset: HICP) Reference period: Aug-2026"*, with the next cycle at **02/10/2026
   15:00 CET** (September flash) and **16/10/2026 12:00 CET** (September complete). Two consequences
   this book cares about: the release clears **before the US cash open**, so even a hypothetical
   reaction would be an overnight-futures event rather than a session one; and the flash-then-
   complete cadence is a *scheduled* pair, not a correction mechanism.

3. **It lands after both policy meetings it could inform — by construction it cannot move either.**
   SUPPORTED from this calendar's own entries. [`ecb-decision-2026-09-10`](ecb-decision-2026-09-10.md)
   is **seven days before**; [`fomc-2026-09-16`](fomc-2026-09-16.md) is **one day before**. The ECB
   held this exact data — as the flash — for **nine days** before it decided, and the sibling ledger
   already records the flash as material to that decision ("August's headline **3.3%** was energy
   while **services fell 3.3% → 3.0%** and **core printed 2.4%**"). So the 09-17 document is a
   receipt on a decision already taken, published into a post-FOMC tape. This is the single sharpest
   fact about the event and it is why the tier is `low`.

4. **The one artefact the complete release adds — the contribution table — is precomputable today.**
   SUPPORTED, and this is the finding. The complete releases publish rate *and* contribution per
   component, which pins the implied weights:

   | Component | July rate → contribution | Implied weight | April rate → contribution | Implied weight |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | Services | 3.3% → +1.55pp | 47.0% | 3.0% → +1.38pp | 46.0% |
   | Energy | 10.3% → +0.94pp | 9.1% | 10.9% → +0.99pp | 9.1% |
   | Non-energy industrial goods | 0.9% → +0.23pp | 25.6% | 0.8% → +0.20pp | 25.0% |
   | Food, alcohol & tobacco | 1.2% → +0.23pp | 19.2% | 2.5% → +0.46pp | 18.4% |

   The energy weight is stable to **0.0pp** across the two months and the others to **≤1.0pp**.
   Applying them to the August flash rates gives the table 09-17 will print: **energy ≈ +1.30pp**
   (from +0.94), **services ≈ +1.40pp** (from +1.55), **NEIG ≈ +0.30pp**, **food ≈ +0.23pp**. That
   is the whole story of the month in one line — **energy alone accounts for ≈ +0.36pp of the
   +0.4pp headline acceleration, while services subtracted ≈ 0.15pp.** Registered as
   **FT-eurostat-hicp-final-2026-09-17-2**. **The honest wrinkle is kept, not smoothed:** the four
   reconstructed contributions sum to **≈3.23pp**, marginally under the **3.25** floor a 3.3% print
   rounds from — so either the weights drifted a touch or one component's unrounded rate sits above
   its published rounding. Either way the table is pinned to about **±0.05pp**, which is why the
   forward test is written as inequalities and not as four point estimates.

5. **What is left that is genuinely new is national dispersion and item detail — real content, none
   of it ours.** SUPPORTED. The complete release adds the member-state breakdown, and that breakdown
   is where the euro aggregate hides the most: July's complete showed **Sweden 0.3%** against
   **Romania 8.2%** — a **7.9pp** spread — after June's **Sweden 1.0% / Romania 9.2%** (8.2pp). The
   **EU** rate also runs above the euro-area rate (**3.0% vs 2.9%** in July, **2.9% vs 2.8%** in
   June), so "European inflation" is two different numbers depending on the aggregate quoted. This
   is worth knowing and worth *nothing* to a US-only, equity-and-earnings-keyed book.

6. **The item-level detail sits on a 2026 methodological break, which is a trap for anyone reading
   it against older vintages.** SUPPORTED, primary. Eurostat's information-on-data page (fetched
   today) records three changes effective **February 2026**: the HICP is compiled on **ECOICOP 2**
   (aligned to UN COICOP 2018), **games of chance** are folded into recreation services, and the
   index reference period is **2025 = 100**. Any sub-index comparison across that break — including
   the "services ex-something" cuts a reader would naturally build from a complete release — is not
   apples-to-apples. Recorded here so the first session to open 09-17's item table does not walk
   into it.

7. **No tracked symbol carries a channel, and the argument is weaker here than for the ECB.**
   SUPPORTED. `symbols: []`. The house playbooks
   ([`trade-playbooks.md`](../../plans/trade-playbooks.md)) are equity- and earnings-keyed; none is
   rates-keyed, let alone euro-inflation-keyed. [`ecb-decision-2026-09-10`](ecb-decision-2026-09-10.md)
   already argued the null for a euro *policy* event with a live 50bp terminal disagreement inside
   it; this event has no disagreement inside it at all, because its number is published. The two
   second-order channels that exist — dollar translation and global term premium — are both owned by
   [`fomc-2026-09-16`](fomc-2026-09-16.md), and inventing a position from a `low`-tiered `estimate`

8. **What the entry actually buys is coverage, and the coverage gap was measured before it was
   claimed.** SUPPORTED from the calendar itself. Eight ECB entries tracked, zero Eurostat inflation
   releases until this one — and the observable cost of that gap is a week: the 09-01 flash, the
   first hard August-window inflation reading from a comparable advanced economy inside the identical
   energy shock, did not reach the US CPI ledger until 09-08. With this entry on the calendar, the
   September flash (**10-02**) and complete (**10-16**) are on the board *before*
   [`cpi-2026-10-14`](cpi-2026-10-14.md) needs them, which is the entire practical return.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `estimate`):** **stand aside completely, and treat 09-17 as a receipt rather than a
print.** The August euro-area headline has been public since 2026-09-01 at **3.3%**; the complete
release has not moved a headline in the four months this session verified; it clears at **06:00 ET**
before the US open; and it lands **after** both the ECB's 09-10 decision and the FOMC's 09-16, so it
cannot inform either. `symbols: []`, no rates-keyed playbook, no position, no play. The ledger's two
substantive positions are **analytical**, not positional. First, it **publishes the contribution
table nine days early** (energy ≈ +1.30pp, services ≈ +1.40pp, NEIG ≈ +0.30pp, food ≈ +0.23pp) and
registers it, so 09-17 either confirms an already-computed table or reveals a weight drift worth
knowing. Second, it **fixes the attribution boundary**: a euro statistical confirmation at 06:00 ET
on a post-FOMC, pre-quad-witching session is not an explanation any future row may reach for without
showing a Bund move first. Estimates widen caution and license nothing.

**Kill switches:**

- **Channel kill (the one that would rebuild this doc):** a tracked name (NVDA/AVGO/MRVL/CRWV)
  moving **>1%** in the **06:00–09:30 ET** window on **2026-09-17** in a way the tape attributes to
  this release. Leg 7's "no price channel" claim would be false. Score by **2026-09-18**.
- **Receipt kill (registered):** the complete release printing the August euro-area headline
  **outside 3.2–3.4%**, i.e. more than **±0.1pp** from the 3.3% flash. Leg 1 dies, complete releases
  become real prints, and the `low` tier is wrong. Registered as
  **FT-eurostat-hicp-final-2026-09-17-1**, score by **2026-09-18**.
- **Reconstruction kill (registered):** the 09-17 contribution table printing **energy below
  +1.25pp** or **services above +1.45pp**. The implied weights in leg 4 drifted, the "precomputable"
  claim loses, and the same arithmetic must be re-derived before the **10-16** September complete.
  Registered as **FT-eurostat-hicp-final-2026-09-17-2**, score by **2026-09-18**.
- **Core kill:** the complete release printing **core (ex energy, food, alcohol & tobacco) ≥ 2.6%**,
  against the flash's **2.4%**. It does not kill this stance — it kills the *sibling* reading, since
  the decelerating-core-under-an-energy-headline shape is what
  [`ecb-decision-2026-09-10`](ecb-decision-2026-09-10.md) leans on for its terminal call. Score by
  **2026-09-18**, and the change gets written there, not here.
- **Cadence kill:** the **2026-10-16** September complete release revising a euro-area headline by
  **≥0.2pp**, or Eurostat moving the flash-then-complete pair off the ECB calendar's published
  clock. The class changes and this entry is re-tiered. Re-check at the next pulse.
- **Date kill:** the 09-17 release moving or the ECB calendar row changing. Breaks the header.
  Re-check every pulse.
