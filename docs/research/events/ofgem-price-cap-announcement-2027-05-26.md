### Leg 1 — "this cap is the control quarter for the VAT reversion" — **REFUTED, independently of the sibling that already refuted it**

The `2027-02-23` lane killed this from Ofgem's **press releases**: Ofgem itemises cost components in
pounds as a matter of practice, and named the VAT counterfactual unprompted. That is a behavioural
argument — "a regulator that has always done X will do X."

The **cost-allowance annex** makes it structural. Ofgem's 1 July 2026 letter, Annex 1 (Direct Debit
price cap cost breakdown), publishes this table:

| Cost | April–June 2026 | July–Sept 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Wholesale incl. contracts for difference | 40% | 45% |
| Networks | 28% | 25% |
| Operating, debt and industry costs | 17% | 16% |
| Policy | 6% | 6% |
| EBIT allowance | 2.6% | 2.6% |
| Headroom | 1.0% | 1.0% |
| Levelisation allowance | 0.6% | 0.5% |
| **VAT** | **5%** | **5%** |
| Total | £1,477 | £1,663 |

**VAT is a standing line item in the published cost stack**, with its own percentage, every quarter, for
every payment method (Annex 2 repeats it for Standard Credit). It is not a courtesy sentence in a press
release that Ofgem might omit next time — it is a row in the model output. The Oct–Dec 2026 through
Mar 2027 annexes will carry a *reduced* VAT line (gas only); the Apr–Jun 2027 annex restores it. The
April 2027 tax-versus-wholesale split is therefore **arithmetic on the day, from a table**, and the
proposal's "it needs two observations, not one" is false on the strongest available evidence.

**This is worth recording as a distinct refutation rather than a "me too."** The sibling's argument
would weaken if Ofgem changed press-release style; this one only weakens if Ofgem changes its published
cost model, which is a licence-condition artefact.

### Leg 4 — "the announcement is a confirmation, not a print" (the siblings' central claim) — **SUPPORTED, and now primary-sourced rather than derived**

Both siblings reached this by construction: the observation window closes ~30 working days before the
cap period starts, so the number is arithmetic about a week before Ofgem says it. Neither had Ofgem
saying so. The 1 July 2026 letter does, in terms that are stronger than the construction:

> "We are obliged to update the price cap level at intervals by applying updated inputs for items such
> as wholesale costs to the price cap formulae which have been determined by our previous decisions.
> That is what we have done today. **In updating the price cap in this way, we are not making a policy
> decision or exercising a judgment.**"

A quarterly cap update is, by the regulator's own account, **mechanical application of a predetermined
formula to observed inputs**. There is no discretion in it to surprise anyone with. This upgrades the
siblings' inference to a sourced statement and is the single most reusable sentence this lane found.

### Leg 5 — "no precedent was located for Ofgem revising an already-announced cap" — **the gap is now FILLED and BOUNDED**

The `2027-02-23` lane flagged this explicitly as "a gap in this research, not a finding that none

> "**Only this letter has been updated and the cost allowance models/annexes remain unchanged from our
