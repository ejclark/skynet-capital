## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This entry arrived with a job written on it, and the job is a claim about **statistical power**. The
[`jgb-20y-auction-2026-10-20`](jgb-20y-auction-2026-10-20.md) ledger was itself created as a
falsifier — [`jgb-30y-auction-2026-10-08`](jgb-30y-auction-2026-10-08.md) had argued that the BoJ's
taper of its *"more than 10 years and up to 25 years"* purchase bucket should damage 20-year auction
demand first — and it returned a null with the sign reversed (difference-in-differences **−0.412bp at
t = −0.77**), explicitly limited: *"the test only reliably detects an effect above roughly 1.5bp.
This is no evidence of an effect, not evidence of no effect."* Its adjacency sweep then proposed this
event, in its own words, because *"a second post-10-20 print is exactly what that power limit
needs."*

So the question is not "will the auction go well." It is: **does one more auction actually fix the
power problem it was proposed to fix — and if it does not, what is this event worth?**

**One-line verdict:** it does **not** — one month-matched pair moves the minimum detectable effect
from **1.50bp to 1.47bp** against a permanent floor of **0.81bp** — and what the event is worth
instead is three things nobody had measured: it proves the sibling's DiD is **position-matched by
construction**, it is the **tightest same-tenor sequencing date** the calendar offers (the opposite
of how the sibling ranked it), and it is the print at which this tenor's famous **cover persistence
is demonstrably dead**.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The date is MOF's and the size is not announced — SUPPORTED.** MOF's November calendar row reads
`Nov. 18, 2026 | 20-year`. Parsing every November row for an announcement href returns **none** —
against an August page whose 20-year row already links `auct20260813e.htm` and a September page whose
09-15 row links `auct20260908e.htm`. MOF's own note on the page reads *"Each issue amount will be
announced about one week prior to each auction date,"* putting terms around **2026-11-11**. ¥700bn is
the base case from three independent MOF primaries: the workbook (¥700bn every month since
2026-04-14), the 08-20 result page (¥532.1bn competitive + ¥167.4bn non-price-competitive I =
**¥699.5bn**), and the 09-08 announcement, which names **"About 700 billion yen."**

**2. This is issue 198's FIRST REOPENING, and MOF's 09-08 announcement is new primary evidence for
the rhythm — SUPPORTED.** The 10-20 ledger inferred the three-auction issue rhythm from the workbook
alone and registered it as a prediction. A primary published *after* that ledger's fetch settles the
current cycle directly: `auct20260908e.htm` states **"20-year JGBs to be issued in September will be
a reopening issue of the July 2026 issue,"** maturity **2046-06-20**, ¥700bn. That closes issue 197
as **Jul 14 (new) → Aug 20 (first reopening) → Sep 15 (second reopening)**, which puts a **new issue
198 on 2026-10-20** and makes **2026-11-18 its first reopening**, maturity **2046-09-20**. The
workbook confirms the rhythm is unbroken across issues 187 → 197. Registered as
`FT-jgb-20y-auction-2026-11-18-1`.

**3. The event's stated rationale — "the second observation this DiD's power needs" — is REFUTED on
arithmetic. This is the leg the event was created for, and it fails.** The 10-20 ledger's DiD
replicates exactly from source here, with the 08-20 print added from MOF's result page:

| Measure | Period A (2024-01→2025-03) | Period B (2025-04→2026-09) | DiD | t |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tail gap (20Y − 30Y)** | **+0.453bp** (n=15) | **+0.041bp** (n=17) | **−0.412bp** | **−0.77** |
| **Cover gap (20Y − 30Y)** | +0.029 | +0.077 | +0.049 | +0.29 |

Standard error on the tail-gap DiD: **0.535bp**, i.e. a minimum detectable effect (80% power, 5%
two-sided) of **1.50bp** — the sibling's own "roughly 1.5bp," independently reproduced. Now the
question the proposal never asked: **what does one more pair buy?**

| Pairs added to period B | n_B | SE | Minimum detectable effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 (today)** | 17 | 0.535bp | **1.50bp** |
| **+1 (this auction)** | 18 | 0.525bp | **1.47bp** |
| +3 | 20 | 0.506bp | 1.42bp |
| +12 (one more year) | 29 | 0.450bp | 1.26bp |
| +48 (four more years) | 65 | 0.369bp | 1.03bp |
| **+∞** | ∞ | 0.290bp | **0.81bp — the floor** |

Read plainly: **this auction improves the test by 2%**, and the improvement is asymptotically capped.
Because the DiD's variance carries period A's 15 observations — a closed historical window that the
BoJ's own taper schedule fixed and no future auction can extend — the test can **never** detect an
effect below **0.81bp**, however long it runs. Getting even to 1.2bp needs **35** post-taper pairs,
about **a year and a half** more of monthly auctions past the 17 already banked.

The honest consequence for this calendar: **the 20-year auction series cannot settle the bucket
question, and no schedule of further 20-year ledgers changes that.** Two escape routes exist and both
are the sibling's own suggestions rather than this one's — a cleaner control (the 40-year, 89.6% of
its float in the untapered bucket, at the cost of printing no tail because it is single-price), or a
different dependent variable entirely (the curve, leg 7). Registered as
`FT-jgb-20y-auction-2026-11-18-2`.

**4. What the event IS worth, first: the sibling's DiD is position-matched by construction, and
nobody had checked — SUPPORTED, and it is a new result.** The DiD matches 20- and 30-year auctions by
calendar month. If the two tenors' issue cycles ever drift out of phase, a month-match silently
compares a *new issue* against a *reopening* — and on the full history the new-issue penalty is real
(leg 5), so a phase drift would contaminate the gap directly. Measured across every month-matched
pair in the workbook:

| Sample | Pairs | Position-matched |
|---|---|---|
| 2003-11-20 → 2026-09 (all) | 214 | 182 (85.0%) — **32 mismatches** |
| **2013-06-18 → 2026-09** | **158** | **158 (100%)** |
| DiD period A (2024-01→2025-03) | 15 | **15 (100%)** |
| DiD period B (2025-04→2026-09) | 17 | **17 (100%)** |

The last mismatch was **2013-05-28**; since then MOF has opened both tenors' new issues in the same
months (January, April, July, October) without a single break in **158** consecutive pairs. Both DiD
windows sit wholly inside that lockstep, so the sibling's month-matching is *automatically*
position-matching and the new-issue penalty cancels exactly in the gap. That is a confound removed
rather than found — and the November pair extends it: the 20-year on 11-18 is issue 198's first
reopening against the 30-year on 11-10 as issue 92's. Registered as
`FT-jgb-20y-auction-2026-11-18-4`.

**5. The new-issue penalty, decomposed by issue position — SUPPORTED, and it sharpens the sibling's
"do not handicap" rule rather than repeating it.** The 10-20 ledger tested new issues against *all*
reopenings pooled. Splitting reopenings by position is what this event needs, because it is a **first**
reopening:

| Position | n | Mean tail | Mean cover |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — new issue** | 193 | **1.131bp** | **3.093** |
| **2 — first reopening** | 91 | **0.767bp** | 3.395 |
| 3 — second reopening | 58 | 0.871bp | 3.540 |

New vs first reopening: tail **+0.364bp, t = +2.21**; cover **−0.302, t = −3.15**. First vs second
reopening: tail **−0.104bp, t = −0.54**; cover −0.145, t = −1.44. So on the full history the entire
effect is the step from new to first reopening, and nothing distinguishes the two reopenings — which
would make **2026-11-18 the single best-positioned slot in the cycle**, if the effect were real.

It is not, on the modern sample. From **2015**: tail **0.800 / 0.911 / 0.930bp** across positions 1 /
2 / 3 (n=46 each), new-vs-all-reopenings **−0.121bp, t = −0.64**; cover **3.609 / 3.614 / 3.527**,
**t = +0.35**. Same verdict as the sibling's, one level finer: **do not credit this print for being a
first reopening.** The step is a pre-2015 composition artifact, and the positional decomposition does
not rescue it.

**6. You cannot forecast this auction from the 10-20 print — SUPPORTED, and it kills the most natural
reading of a two-auction pair.** With two auctions four weeks apart and the second proposed as the
"second observation," the obvious question is whether the first one tells you anything about the
second. It used to. It does not now:

| Sample | n | AR(1) **tail** | AR(1) **cover** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full history | 343 | **+0.407 (t = +8.23)** | **+0.627 (t = +14.87)** |
| 2015 onward | 139 | +0.169 (t = +2.01) | **+0.581 (t = +8.35)** |
| **2023 onward** | **43** | **−0.059 (t = −0.38)** | **+0.025 (t = +0.16)** |

The 2023+ sample detects |r| ≳ **0.31** at 5%, so "no persistence" needs a real test rather than a
failed one. Fisher-z, 2023+ against the sample it replaces:

- **Cover**: z = **+4.26** vs the full history and **+3.55** vs 2015+. Both differences are real.
  **Cover persistence is dead**, and dead against the modern benchmark, not just the ancient one.
- **Tail**: z = **+2.94** vs the full history — the strong version is gone — but only **+1.28** vs
  2015+, so a *weak* (r ≈ 0.17) tail persistence cannot be excluded and is not claimed either way.

Practical rule for 11-18: **carry nothing from 10-20's cover, and treat its tail as at most weakly
informative.** Note this cuts both ways — a bad 10-20 print does not forecast a bad 11-18 one, which
is precisely the reading a "second observation" framing invites.

**7. The clock reverses the sibling's sequencing ranking — and this is a correction, not a
refinement.** The 10-20 ledger preferred its own date over 09-15's on the grounds that the US 20-year
was **D+1** rather than same-day: *"Japan's result publishes inside the Tokyo session roughly 18
hours before the US 1:00pm ET stop — complete rather than concurrent."* The implication is that a
same-calendar-date pairing is *concurrent* and therefore contaminated. It is not, and the reason is
only arithmetic:

- Japan Standard Time is **UTC+9**. US Eastern is **UTC−5** in November (DST ends 2026-11-01) and
  **UTC−4** in October. So **JST = EST+14** on 2026-11-18 and JST = EDT+13 on 2026-10-20.
- Tokyo's cash session closes **15:00 JST**, which is **01:00 EST the same calendar date**. MOF
  publishes 20-year results around **12:35 JST** by convention (not separately sourced here), i.e.
  roughly **22:35 EST the previous evening**.
- The US 20-year auction stops at **1:00pm ET**.

So on **2026-11-18** Japan's result precedes the US stop by roughly **14 hours** — and on
**2026-10-20 → 10-21** by roughly **35**. A same-calendar-date JGB auction is *always* complete
before the US session opens; the timezone offset guarantees it. **Same-day is the tighter pairing,
not the concurrent one**, and 2026-11-18 is therefore the better sequencing instrument of the two,
reversing the sibling's ranking of its own date.

The offsetting cost is real and specific, and it lands on measurement rather than on ordering: the
[`treasury-20y-bond-2026-11-18`](treasury-20y-bond-2026-11-18.md) ledger records that this US auction
is a **new issue** bidding one hour before **`fomc-minutes-2026-11-18`** at 2:00pm ET, with
**`tic-monthly-2026-11-18`** at 4:00pm behind it. So the US **close-to-close** CMT on 11-18 is a
joint observation and cannot be attributed to either auction. The clean read is auction-to-auction —
JGB result → US bid-to-cover and tail — not the CMT print the spillover regression uses. Worth noting
in passing that the two 20-year sales sit at **opposite ends of their issue cycles** that day:
Japan's is a first reopening, America's is a new issue.

**8. The spillover null holds at the horizon that applies here — and the placebo says the family's
control specification is shakier than three ledgers have treated it.** Re-running the 09-15 and 10-20
ledgers' test from source, **279** matched auctions **2003-10-21 → 2026-08-20**, against 5,925 daily
US 20Y CMT observations:

| Horizon | n | Raw corr(tail, US move) | Partial (\| same-date JGB move) | Soft (tail ≥2bp, n=20) − firm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D−1 (placebo)** | 279 | −0.026 (t = −0.44) | **−0.148 (t = −2.48)** | +0.50bp (t = +0.33) |
| **D+0 (applies to 11-18)** | 279 | +0.083 (t = +1.38) | **+0.035 (t = +0.59)** | **+1.78bp (t = +1.26)** |
| D+1 (applied to 10-20) | 279 | +0.043 (t = +0.71) | +0.037 (t = +0.62) | +0.51bp (t = +0.32) |
| D+2 | 344 | +0.010 (t = +0.18) | +0.001 (t = +0.02) | +0.42bp (t = +0.53) |

Two pipeline agreements first: the 2023+ subsample reproduces the 09-15 sibling's cell to the decimal
(n=44, soft **+1.43** vs firm **−0.10**, diff **+1.53**, **t = +0.80**), and the same-date raw split
reproduces the 10-20 ledger's (+1.78 vs +1.76bp, t = +1.26 vs +1.24; n differs by 4 because this run
includes the 08-20 print). corr(tail, same-date JGB 20Y par move) = **+0.412 (t = +7.53)** — the tail
is mostly a symptom of that day's own Tokyo selloff, exactly as both siblings found. US 20Y CMT daily
sd on auction dates: **5.59bp**.

**The null holds. The specification does not.** The **D−1 placebo** is the check nobody ran, and it
should have been the first one: the US session *before* the auction cannot be caused by it, so any
coefficient there is spurious by construction. The **raw** placebo behaves (t = −0.44). The
**partial** placebo does not — **−0.148 at t = −2.48**, nominally significant and larger in magnitude
than the D+0 estimate it is supposed to validate. The reason is structural: the US session on D−1
closes at 16:00 ET, which is **06:00 JST on D**, so it *feeds into* the same-date JGB move the partial
correlation conditions on. Conditioning on a mediator manufactures exactly this kind of coefficient.
The same objection applies, more weakly, at D+0 — the auction-day JGB move contains the auction's own
effect, so partialling it out removes part of what the test is looking for.

Consequence, stated narrowly: **the null survives** (raw and partial agree at D+0, D+1 and D+2, and
nothing is significant at any horizon in either form), but the *partial* specification the 30-year
ledger introduced should not be quoted as though its t-statistics were trustworthy. Registered as
`FT-jgb-20y-auction-2026-11-18-3`.

### Honest limits

- **The power arithmetic assumes the period-B variance holds.** The MDE table holds sd_B at its
  measured 1.861bp. A quieter or noisier post-taper regime moves the numbers; it does not move the
  **floor**, which depends only on period A's fifteen fixed observations and is the load-bearing
  part of leg 3.
- **The floor is a property of this design, not of the question.** A different estimator — pooling
  tenors, a longer pre-period, the 40-year as control, or the curve as the dependent variable —
  faces a different bound. Leg 3 refutes "one more 20-year auction fixes this," not "the bucket
  question is unanswerable."
- **The lockstep in leg 4 is descriptive.** 158 consecutive matched pairs is MOF's revealed practice,
  not a published commitment; the November pair is a *prediction* on that practice
  (`FT-jgb-20y-auction-2026-11-18-4`), and the 30-year's own announcement around 2026-11-03 is what
  settles it.
- **The persistence null in leg 6 is honest about what it cannot exclude.** n=43 detects |r| ≳ 0.31.
  The cover result is significant against both benchmarks; the tail result rules out the full-history
  value and not the 2015+ one, and is reported that way.
- **The D−1 placebo failure is diagnosed, not proven.** The mediator story (US D−1 close → Tokyo D
  open → same-date JGB move) is mechanically forced by the timezone arithmetic, but no instrument was
  constructed to test it, and an alternative explanation — a slow common factor across the two
  markets — would produce a similar pattern.
- **The 12:35 JST publication convention in leg 7 is not separately sourced.** The *bound* the leg
  rests on needs only the UTC offsets and Tokyo's 15:00 close, both of which are facts; the exact

- **The auction result does not exist**, the size is not announced, and the issue identity in leg 2

  cycle further than the 10-20 ledger could, but still an inference. MOF's calendar note allows

- **VIX and USD/JPY are Yahoo, i.e. secondary**, and the 09-09 readings are intraday rather than

- **The date is `estimate`.** MOF is a primary source and the date is not in doubt; the label
