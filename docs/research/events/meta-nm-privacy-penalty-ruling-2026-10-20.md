## At a glance

**TL;DR.** A New Mexico judge is due to rule by ~2026-10-20 on a civil penalty against Meta that
state prosecutors want set at **$35–40B** and Meta wants capped at **$345M–$3.45B** — four orders
of magnitude apart — eight days before Meta's Q3 print. The tape has so far treated this as a
low-probability tail, not a priced catalyst: the 09-25 jury verdict and the 10-01 penalty hearing
both produced **sub-1% standalone reactions** against a stock that is otherwise swinging on AI-capex
sentiment. **Stand aside on a directional bet keyed to the ruling itself** — the modal outcome
(precedent + Meta's own signaled appeal) is a number far below the headline ask, and the market's
muted reaction so far is consistent with pricing exactly that. The honest trade is defensive: anyone
already long META into the Oct 20–28 corridor is holding two uncorrelated catalysts back-to-back,
and that is a sizing question, not a directional one.

## Initial research

**Question, plainly:** New Mexico wants a judge to fine Meta $35–40B over the Cambridge Analytica
data-sharing scandal, eight days before Meta's Q3 print. Is the date solid, what does the market
think the real number will be, and does any of this license a trade?

**Verdict in one line: the date is better-sourced than the proposing lane found, and the market has
already told you its modal answer — a low number — by barely reacting to a $219B headline.**

**Method:** sourced web research (primary-source re-fetch of the court reporting, re-verification
of the proposing lane's date claim, and the stock's own reaction to the three dated catalysts this
case has already produced — the 09-25 verdict, the 10-01 hearing, and precedent from Meta's 08-26
youth-safety settlement). This is a `sector`/legal-catalyst event, not an earnings print, so no
`earnings-cycle`/`intraday-edges` instrument run applies (see EVENT-RESEARCH.md's instrument
guidance) — the analogue to "consensus/whisper" here is the market's own revealed pricing of the
dollar range.

### Leg (a) — the 2026-10-20 date is real and derivable from a primary → **SUPPORTED**

The proposing lane (`meta-2026-10-28-print`'s 10-03 pulse) sourced the date to a press paraphrase
("one account giving 2026-10-20 specifically") it flagged as worth re-verifying rather than
inheriting. Direct fetch of the Santa Fe New Mexican (HTTP 200, 2026-10-03) quotes Judge Francis
Mathew in court: he "extended the deadline for the parties to file briefs on the issues until
Tuesday, telling them he intends to have 'something' for them two weeks after that." The Oct 1
hearing was a Thursday; the next Tuesday is 2026-10-06; two weeks after that is **2026-10-20** —
the date is arithmetic on the primary's own quote, not a second-hand paraphrase of an oral estimate.
Two other fetches (krqe.com, cnbc.com) returned HTTP 403 this session (recorded in `probe-ref.blocked`);
every WebSearch snippet of KRQE's own coverage corroborates only the vaguer "by/before late October"
framing. `NEWS:` stays the honest prefix (no `ECF:` slot exists for a state district court, and this
lane never fetched a filed scheduling order) — but confidence in the specific day is now higher than
the proposal had it, for a reason worth recording: a derivation from a quoted in-court statement,
not a second paraphrase of one.

### Leg (b) — the operative dollar range is $345M–$40B, not the $219B headline → **SUPPORTED**

Every outlet's "$219 billion" figure is Bloomberg Law's and others' computation of the raw statutory
ceiling (up to $5,000/violation × the jury's ~1.3–2.1M-violations-per-statement range, taken at the
high end) — a number **neither party has asked the court to impose**. The number actually argued in
court on 2026-10-01: New Mexico's attorney Randi McGinn asked for $35–40B specifically because (per
reporting) it "would provide a meaningful penalty while avoiding due-process concerns" — i.e., even
the state's own counsel is anchoring below the statutory max on purpose. Meta's counsel Matt
Nicholson called the $35–40B ask "astronomical" and argued for a $345M–$3.45B cap, and has signaled
appeal regardless of outcome. One sourced comparison worth weighting: Meta's own 2019 FTC privacy
settlement opened with public damages estimates "into the tens of billions" before landing at **$5B**
— precedent for civil-penalty negotiations/rulings settling well below the opening ask.

The **unconfounded** data point — 10-01, when $35–40B was the live number argued in the room — is a
**+0.1%/+0.4%** non-reaction. That is the cleanest read available: the market is not pricing a
material probability of the ruling landing anywhere near the state's ask. Precedent agrees: the
proposing lane's note on Meta's 08-26 youth-safety settlement (a different, already-quantified
~$10B case) found a net **+1.5%** reaction after an initial **+4.1%** pop — single-digit, not the
print's own 10–25% class, even for a settlement Meta had already agreed to pay.

### Counter-evidence worth holding

1. **The confound cuts both ways.** Because 09-25's drop is attributable to a Goldman note, this
   session cannot rule out that the market is simply *under*-attending to the legal tail risk, not
   correctly pricing it low. Muted ≠ informed; it may just mean nobody has done the math yet.
2. **$35–40B is ~2% of META's ~$1.86T market cap at the top end** — large enough that even a
   10–20% probability-weighted outcome is a non-trivial expected cost, which is why this stays
   `high` impact rather than `medium` despite the muted tape.
3. **Appeal is signaled regardless of outcome**, which means even a high ruling does not create a
   near-term cash event — but it does create a headline-risk event, and headline risk has its own,
   separately-muted-so-far reaction function (see Leg (c)).
