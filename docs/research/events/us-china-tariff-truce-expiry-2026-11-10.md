## At a glance

**TL;DR.** This event is real but its headline mechanism is dead, and that inversion is the whole
finding. The calendar filed it as "the additional 24% US tariff snaps back on Nov 10" — that cannot
happen. The Supreme Court held on **2026-02-20** that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs
(*Learning Resources v. Trump*, 6–3), the President terminated the IEEPA duties by EO the same day,
and collection stopped **2026-02-24**. EO 14358 suspended a rate that no longer exists, so its
Nov-10 clock schedules nothing on the US side. What *does* still expire that day is **China's** half:
MOFCOM/GAC Announcement No. 70 of 2025 suspended the six Oct-9-2025 export-control announcements —
the 0.1% extraterritorial rule and the rare-earth foreign-direct-product rule — "until November 10,
2026," and a US court ruling has no effect on a Chinese administrative act. So the risk points at
rare-earth-dependent supply chains (semis, autos, robotics, defense, aerospace), not at
tariff-sensitive importers. What keeps this a stand-aside rather than a trade: this administration
has now papered over two tariff cliffs *within the same minute* they lapsed (IEEPA→Section 122 on
2026-02-24; Section 122→Section 301 on 2026-07-24), the market's reaction to the single largest
possible tariff shock — SCOTUS voiding the entire regime — was S&P 500 **+0.69%**, and the
rare-earth complex itself is *easing* into the deadline (NdPr's first monthly decline since May;
MP Materials −8.4% over the eight sessions to 2026-09-15). **Amended 2026-09-15:** the initial
research's most uncomfortable structural fact — "the extension venue sits *after* the deadline" —

2026-09-24 summit, the G20 trade ministerial 9/30, G20 FMCBG Bangkok 10/15, G20 foreign ministers

## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** the calendar carries 2026-11-10 as the day the US–China tariff truce
expires and an additional 24% duty returns. Is that mechanism still real after everything that
happened to US tariff authority in 2026, what actually lapses on that date, and which of the names
this research tracks carry the exposure?

**One-line verdict:** the date is real and primary-quotable but the risk has *inverted* — the US-side
snapback was extinguished by the Supreme Court on 2026-02-20 and terminated in fact on 2026-02-24,
leaving China's rare-earth export-control suspension as the only live thing on the Nov-10 clock, so
this is a supply-chain-restriction watch, not a tariff trade, and the extension venue sits eight days
past the deadline.

**Method:** sourced web research only — no price/options instruments run (geopolitical kind, no
symbols keyed, per the macro-print/geopolitical method in
[`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md)). Primary-source reads: the EO 14358 text via
the American Presidency Project's reproduction and the whitehouse.gov PDF, and the 2026-02-20
"Ending Certain Tariff Actions" presidential action on whitehouse.gov directly. Cross-checked against
law-firm and trade-press analysis (K&L Gates, Ropes & Gray, WilmerHale, White & Case, Skadden, Wiley,
Honigman, Pillsbury, Clark Hill), Global Trade Alert, CSIS, CNBC/NBC/Forbes/Yahoo for market
reaction, and mining-technology/InvestorNews/China Briefing for the rare-earth leg. VIX/SPX/SOXX
closes pulled live 2026-09-04. All figures as-checked 2026-09-04 and drift.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The Nov-10-2026 date is real and primary-quotable.** SUPPORTED. Executive Order 14358,
   "Modifying Reciprocal Tariff Rates Consistent with the Economic and Trade Arrangement Between the
   United States and the People's Republic of China," signed **2025-11-04** and published in the
   Federal Register **2025-11-07**, continues the suspension of the heightened EO-14257 reciprocal
   rate in favour of a flat additional 10% until, verbatim, **"12:01 a.m. eastern standard time on
   November 10, 2026."** It implements the Kuala Lumpur Joint Arrangement struck at the Trump–Xi
   meeting of 2025-10-30. The "24%" in this event's title is the arithmetic gap: the heightened
   reciprocal rate was 34%, the applicable floor 10%.

2. **The US-side snapback the title describes can no longer fire.** SUPPORTED, and this is the
   load-bearing finding. On **2026-02-20** the Supreme Court decided *Learning Resources, Inc. v.
   Trump* (No. 24-1287), holding 6–3 that IEEPA does not authorize the President to impose tariffs —
   Kavanaugh dissenting, joined by Thomas and Alito. The same day the President issued **"Ending
   Certain Tariff Actions"**, terminating the additional ad valorem duties imposed under IEEPA by
   EOs 14193, 14194, 14195, 14245, **14257**, 14323, 14329, 14380 and 14382; CBP confirmed collection
   stopped **2026-02-24 at 12:00 a.m. ET**. EO 14358 is a *modifying* order — it suspends a rate set
   by EO 14257. With EO 14257's duties terminated, there is no heightened reciprocal rate left for
   the Nov-10 expiry to restore. (See Honest limits: 14358 is not itself named in the termination
   order; the mooting is inferred from the termination of the rate it modifies.)

3. **The US tariff wall was rebuilt on other authorities — but with no Nov-10 hook.** SUPPORTED.
   Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 (15% cap, hard 150-day limit) carried a flat 10% global
   surcharge from **2026-02-24**, expiring by operation of law at **12:01 a.m. EDT on 2026-07-24**.
   A replacement took effect the same minute: USTR announced on **2026-07-23** new Section 301
   forced-labor duties of 10–12.5% on ~60 economies. China sits at the 12.5% tier, which stacks with
   the pre-existing Section 301 duties up to 25% for an aggregate Section 301 exposure of ~37.5%,
   separate from Section 232 metals (50% steel, 50% aluminium, copper). None of that architecture is
   dated to 2026-11-10. Bessent's framing in May 2026 — that China would accept "restoration of prior
   US tariff rates through new Section 301 duties, as long as they don't go higher" — is exactly the
   substitution that happened.

4. **China's half of the arrangement IS still on the Nov-10 clock, and a US court cannot touch it.**
   SUPPORTED. On **2025-11-07** MOFCOM and the General Administration of Customs issued joint
   Announcement **No. 70 of 2025**, suspending MOFCOM/GAC Announcements Nos. 55, 56, 57 and 58 and
   MOFCOM Announcements Nos. 61 and 62 — all issued **2025-10-09** — effective immediately and
   **until 2026-11-10**. Announcement No. 61 is the extraterritorial one: foreign-made products
   containing at least **0.1% by value** of specified Chinese-origin rare-earth material, or produced
   using covered Chinese rare-earth technologies, would again require a MOFCOM export licence, with
   technology controls spanning mining, beneficiation, smelting, separation, magnet manufacturing and
   equipment. A delayed second wave covering holmium, erbium, thulium, europium and ytterbium is
   scheduled to the same date. This is Chinese administrative action; the SCOTUS ruling is irrelevant
   to it.

5. **The exposure is supply-chain, not tariff pass-through, and it is concentrated.** SUPPORTED.
   China produced 270kt of 390kt global rare-earth mine output in 2025 (**~69%**) and handles **up to
   ~90%** of global processing; Bloomberg Intelligence still projects a ~36% global shortfall by 2030
   despite a 4.4-fold rise in non-Chinese NdPr output (mining-technology, 2026-04-27). Sectors named
   at risk if the controls return: automotive, robotics, aerospace, semiconductors, AI and advanced
   electronics, with defense contractors facing presumptive denial. The Western build-out names — MP
   Materials, USA Rare Earth, Lynas, Iluka — are explicitly described as lacking the scale to offset.
   Notably, China **added MP Materials and USA Rare Earth to its own export-control list on
   2026-06-22**, barring China-origin dual-use exports to them: the pressure mechanism has already
   been used *during* the suspension, which is evidence the suspension is tactical rather than a
   settled détente.

6. **The market's reaction function to tariff cliffs in this series is a fizzle, not a shock.**
   SUPPORTED, and it is why the honest call is stand-aside rather than hedge. The largest possible
   tariff event — the Supreme Court voiding the entire IEEPA regime — produced, on 2026-02-20,
   S&P 500 **+0.69% to 6,909.51**, Nasdaq **+0.9% to 22,886.07**, Dow **+0.47% to 49,625.97**;
   contemporaneous reporting characterised it as largely priced in *because the replacement was
   announced the same day*. The 2026-07-24 Section 122 lapse likewise had its successor live in the
   same minute. n=2 is thin, but both observations point the same way: this administration does not
   leave gaps, and the tape has learned that.

7. **The extension venue sits after the deadline — the one genuinely uncomfortable structural fact.**
   SUPPORTED. The 33rd APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting is **2026-11-18/19 in Shenzhen** (apec.org
   press release; sz.gov.cn and english.www.gov.cn, both dated 2025-12), i.e. **eight days past**
   the 11/10 expiry. Bessent said in May 2026 the administration was "not in a hurry" to extend, with
   time to renew "in meetings later this year," naming the September Washington summit, APEC in
   November, and a G20 in Florida in December. Two of those three fall *after* the clock runs out. So
   either the 2026-09-24 summit (estimate) carries it, or a bridging instrument appears, or the
   suspension lapses into an eight-day window before leaders next meet. A high-level He Lifeng /
   Bessent / Greer video call reported 2026-08-02 had China voicing "serious concern" over new US
   curbs while both sides worked deliverables for the September visit — engagement is live, an
   outcome is not evidenced.

**What the conditions support:** nothing directional, and specifically *not* the trade the event's
own title implies. The tariff-snapback play is against a terminated duty. The rare-earth play is
real but is a foreign-regulatory coin flip 67 days out with a specialist base case (selective
licensing, not embargo) that is itself the moderate outcome. The correct posture is a watch with two
dated triggers — the 9/24 summit's output and any MOFCOM publication on Announcement No. 70 — and no

**Honest limits.**
- The 2026-02-20 "Ending Certain Tariff Actions" order does **not** name EO 14358 among the terminated
  orders. The conclusion that 14358's Nov-10 clock is moot is an inference from the termination of
  EO 14257's duties — the rate 14358 suspends. A formal revocation of 14358, or a residual legal
  effect, was not established this pass. If a Nov-10-keyed US action does surface, that inference is
  where it will have failed.
- MOFCOM/GAC Announcement No. 70's Chinese-language text was not read directly; the "until
  2026-11-10" scope rests on English-language law-firm and industry summaries (Pillsbury, Clark Hill,
  CIRS, China Briefing) plus CNBC's 2025-11-10 contemporaneous report. Consistent across sources, but
  aggregator-grade, not primary.
- **The anniversary trap is live and it caught several sources.** The Aug-2025 extension ran to
  Nov 10 **2025**; EO 14358 then ran to Nov 10 **2026**. Aggregators conflate the two constantly (one
  search result presented the Aug-2025 order as an Aug-2026 event). Every date in this doc is stated
  with its year for exactly that reason, and any future pulse should distrust an undated "Nov 10"
  citation.
- No price/options instruments were run (geopolitical kind, no symbols keyed). The VIX/SPX/SOXX
  readings above are context, not an instrument pass.
- The reaction-function sample is n=2 within 2026 and both instances had an immediate substitute
  announced — which is the pattern, but also means it has never been tested against a cliff that was
  actually allowed to bite.
- APEC Shenzhen's 11/18–19 dates come from apec.org and Chinese state sources dated 2025-12; leaders'
  attendance, and specifically whether Trump attends, was not established.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date: estimate, primary-quotable).** No position keyed to this event, and an explicit
refusal of the trade its title implies: the additional 24% reciprocal duty cannot return on
2026-11-10 because the IEEPA authority behind it was held unlawful on 2026-02-20 and the duties
terminated 2026-02-24. Treat 2026-11-10 instead as a **Chinese export-control renewal date**. The
exposed leg is rare-earth-dependent supply chains — semis, autos, robotics, aerospace, defense — via
MOFCOM/GAC Announcement No. 70's expiry, not tariff-sensitive importers. Carry no exposure taken on
the date; the two dated things that would change this doc are the 2026-09-24 summit's output and any
MOFCOM publication on the suspension. Estimate status widens caution only — nothing here is
date-keyed action.

**Amendment (2026-09-15 pulse, D-56) — one kill switch fired and the call got *more* confident, not
less.** The stand-aside is unchanged; two of its supports moved.

*Retired: "the extension venue sits after the deadline."* That was the initial research's one
genuinely uncomfortable structural fact and it does not survive contact with this repo's own
calendar. It was written from **leader-level** meetings alone, where APEC Shenzhen (11/18–19) really
does post-date the clock. At **ministerial** level the picture inverts: `g20-trade-ministerial-milwaukee-2026-09-30`

*Upgraded: the US-side refutation is now primary-sourced, not inferred.* The initial research flagged

exactly those controls. Together with the 2026-06-22 listing of MP Materials and USA Rare Earth,
that is the second use of the lever *while it was supposedly suspended*, and it is the single
strongest argument that the benign base case is wrong. It is also why the quarter-horizon call stays
Medium while the month-horizon call moves to High: the near-dated evidence (a falling physical
market, a named expected deliverable) is much cleaner than the far-dated evidence.

Worth banking as a follow-up: a `WH:`/`FR:` confirmed prefix would fix a recurring blind spot for
