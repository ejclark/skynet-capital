## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **This print is a snapshot of a window that had already closed before the week's central
banks spoke — read it that way, and expect it to give back part of August's two-year high.** Every
September fieldwork window in the three-year primary sample ended on or before the **13th** (2023:
Sep 1–13 · 2024: Aug 30–Sep 13 · 2025: Sep 1–11), while **FOMC 09-16**, **UK CPI 09-16** and the
**BoE 09-17** all land *after* that. So the 2026-09-25 release is a **pre-policy-week** read
published a week *after* the policy week, and any recap calling it the consumer's reaction to that
week is wrong — that reaction arrives in the **October 23** edition (proposed here as a new
calendar entry). On the number itself: August printed **−14**, the best in two years, after a
**+9** two-month run. Three of four August-to-September changes in the 2022–26 series are negative
(**−5, +4, −7, −2; mean −2.50**) against an unconditional monthly mean of **+0.55** across 49
changes — and the two negative years are exactly the two whose September fieldwork sat inside
**pre-Budget tax-rise speculation**, which 2026's does too (`uk-autumn-budget-2026-10-28`, 33 days
out). A secondary aggregator's consensus sits at **−17**. **And none of it is ours to trade**
(`symbols: []`). The date is **estimate** on a taxonomy gap, not on the evidence.

### Leg 2 — the August print is a two-year high built on a +9 two-month run — **SUPPORTED**

From the validated series, the 2026 path: Jan **−16** → Feb **−19** → Mar **−21** → Apr **−25** →
May **−23** → Jun **−23** → Jul **−17** → Aug **−14**. The July move was **+6**, which NIQ's own
commentator called "the largest increase in consumer confidence since November 2023"; August added
**+3** to "its highest level for two years."

The August sub-measures, all from the NIQ primary:

| Measure | Jun 2026 | Jul 2026 | Aug 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headline | −23 | −17 | **−14** |
| Personal finances, past 12m | −10 | −8 | −6 |
| Personal finances, next 12m | −2 | +1 | **+4** |
| General economy, past 12m | −49 | −39 | −40 |
| General economy, next 12m | −36 | −28 | **−23** |
| Major purchase index | −20 | −12 | **−7** |
| Savings index (not in headline) | 20 | +27 | +22 |

Two things worth naming. First, the **major purchase index at −7 is the best since December 2021** —
the sub-measure that is supposed to track the till, printing its best in nearly five years in the
month the till printed its worst since October 2024. Second, the **savings index fell five points**
while everything else rose, which is the one internal tell that the August reading was about mood
rather than capacity.

And the level still is not good: **−14 against a 1981–2026 average of −11.24** (secondary). A
two-year high in this series is a less-bad reading, not a strong one.

call negative is the **calendar cut**, and only because of what the calendar cut has in common.

### Leg 4 — the two negative Septembers are the two pre-Budget ones — **SUPPORTED**

The Aug→Sep sample is n=4 and would be worthless as a bare frequency. It is worth something because
the two large negatives share a mechanism NIQ itself named:

- **September 2024: −7 points** (−13 → **−20**), fieldwork Aug 30 – Sep 13, with the forward-looking
  general-economy measure down **12** and major purchases down **10**. The Budget was **2024-10-30**.
  The chill ran a **second** month (October **−21**) and only reversed after the Budget resolved
  (November **−18**, December **−17**).
- **September 2025: −2 points** (−17 → **−19**), fieldwork Sep 1 – 11, *all five measures down*. NIQ's
  own commentator: "There's an autumnal chill in the air this month," with confidence falling on
  anticipated **November tax increases**. The Budget was **2025-11-26**; October recovered to **−17**
  before the Budget month printed **−19**.
- **September 2023: +4 points** (−25 → **−21**) — the control. No tax-raising Budget was in prospect;
  the November 2023 Autumn Statement was a tax-**cutting** one, and NIQ's commentary named falling
  inflation and wage growth.
- **September 2022: −5 points** (−44 → **−49**, the record low) — the mini-budget month, a different
  and far more violent fiscal shock.

**2026 has the 2024/2025 configuration, earlier.** The Budget is **2026-10-28** — two days earlier
than 2024's and a month earlier than 2025's — which puts the September fieldwork window *deeper* into
the run-up than 2025's was. Secondary wire coverage dated **2026-09-07**, inside the window, reports
Chancellor **John Healey** declining to rule out tax rises ahead of his first Budget and describes
rising gilt yields having cut his fiscal headroom from about **£23.6bn to ~£13bn**; PwC's mid-August
survey, also secondary, frames the autumn as one where "the pressure on household finances will
increase as energy and food prices rise" with "added uncertainty as the new Chancellor assesses if
any tax changes may be needed."

The honest limit, stated: everything in this paragraph except the Budget date is **secondary press**,
and the Budget date itself is `estimate` on a taxonomy gap (HM Treasury primaries, fetched
2026-09-09 by [`uk-autumn-budget-2026-10-28`](uk-autumn-budget-2026-10-28.md)). The **mechanism** is
supported by two NIQ-primary precedents; the **2026 intensity** is not primary-sourced and is
labelled accordingly.
