## Initial research

### The question, plainly

A voting Reserve Bank president who dissented for a hike five weeks ago is reported to speak two days
from now, on a date carried by one aggregator and corroborated nowhere. Three questions, in order:
**is the date real**, **can it be made real**, and **would it matter if it were?**

**One-line verdict:** the date cannot be confirmed *in principle* — the Cleveland Fed publishes no
forward speaking calendar, so the 403 that blocked the last session was never the real obstacle —
and it does not matter anyway, because the one new detail recovered this session is that the remarks
are **pre-recorded**, which makes them mechanically incapable of reacting to the week's data.

1. **The 403 was a user-agent block, not an outage — and reaching the primary still cannot confirm

   returns **HTTP 200** (157KB) to a browser User-Agent from this runner today, having returned 403 to
   two prior attempts on 2026-09-01 and to a default-agent fetch again today. So the calendar entry's
   standing instruction — *"a session that can reach clevelandfed.org should confirm or drop it"* — was
   built on a false premise about *why* confirmation failed. Having reached it: the site carries a
   **`President's upcoming events`** heading whose entire body is *"Subscribe to receive notifications
   about President Hammack's upcoming speaking engagements"* — inspected in the raw markup, it is a
   `cf-promo` block wrapping a HubSpot signup form, **not** an events listing. The speeches collection
   is a post-hoc archive (newest: the **07-31-2026** dissent statement; her last delivered speech with a
   venue and time is **06-02-2026**, City Club of Cleveland). The events listing on `/news-and-events`

   Stability 11-19–20** — no 9/3 anything. **The correct conclusion is not "unconfirmed pending a better
   fetch" but "unconfirmable at this primary, permanently."**

2. **The 9/3 slot rests on exactly one aggregator — which supplies a time and a decisive
   qualifier.** SUPPORTED as a single-source claim, and materially sharper than what the entry was
   filed with. ZeroHedge's "Key Events This Week: Jobs, JOLTS, Beige Book And ISM," **published
   2026-08-29**, gives a day-by-day schedule whose Thursday 9/3 block reads: 08:30 trade balance ·

   **15:00 "Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack will give pre-recorded opening remarks."** The Waller
   line matches federalreserve.gov's own September calendar as fetched by the sibling ledger, which is
   evidence the list is not fabricated. But the Hammack line **names no venue**, and no venue surfaced
   in search — so the claim remains one publication deep.

3. **The one professional calendar that covers the same week omits her — consistently, not
   contradictorily.** SUPPORTED, and this is the reconciliation the doc turns on. Newsquawk's *Weekly
   Economic Calendar, 31st August – 4th September 2026* lists Thursday 9/3 as "Chinese RatingDog

   EU PPI, US Challenger Layoffs, Trade Balance, Jobless Claims, ISM Services PMI, Atlanta Fed GDP,
   Canadian Trade Balance" — and names **Waller as the week's only Thursday Fed speaker**. A naive read
   calls that a contradiction and drops the event. The better read: an institutional calendar lists
   *policy-relevant speakers*, and a taped ceremonial opener is not one. **Both sources can be right at
   once**, and the omission is itself corroboration of leg 4's character claim rather than evidence
   against the date. Recorded as MIXED on the date, SUPPORTED on the character.

4. **"Pre-recorded" is the finding that decides the tier — taped remarks cannot carry a reaction

5. **Her position is not merely known — it is fresher than this calendar entry records.** SUPPORTED,
