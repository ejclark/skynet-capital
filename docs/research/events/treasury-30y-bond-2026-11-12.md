## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **The one thing that looks unusual about this auction is the one thing that is completely
routine, and the primary record settles it three ways.** A closed bond market sits inside the
November refunding block — 3-Year Monday 11-09, 10-Year Tuesday 11-10, **market shut Wednesday
11-11 for Veterans Day**, 30-Year Thursday 11-12 — which reads like a disrupted block. It is not.
**(1) The shape is Treasury's standing answer, not an improvisation:** every time Veterans Day has
fallen on a Wednesday, Treasury has run exactly this Mon / Tue / [shut] / Thu block — **3 for 3**
(2009, 2015, 2020), and across all six holiday-inside Novembers since 2009 the 30-Year anchored on
**Thursday 6/6**. **(2) Nothing is compressed:** the market is *closed* on the interposed day, so
the three legs still run on **three consecutive bond sessions**, exactly as a normal Tue/Wed/Thu
block does — the closure moves the calendar, not the session sequence. **(3) Demand shows no
penalty:** the five holiday-inside November 30-Years cleared bid-to-cover **2.308** against
**2.314** for the other twelve, with a *higher* indirect share (56.2% vs 52.8%). What is left is an
ordinary $25B refunding new issue whose size is over-determined (**11 straight $25B new issues since
2024-02-08**; no nominal coupon size has moved anywhere since April 2024) and whose only genuinely
unpublished number arrives on **2026-11-04**. Date is `estimate`, `symbols: []`, and nothing here is
a trade.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Two sibling ledgers already own most of what a 30-Year auction doc could say. The September one
([`treasury-30y-bond-2026-09-10.md`](treasury-30y-bond-2026-09-10.md)) argued the long-end
term-premium case at length; the October one
([`treasury-30y-bond-2026-10-08.md`](treasury-30y-bond-2026-10-08.md)) established the demand
mechanics across a full sale cycle and the buyback eligibility rules. Repeating either would be a
non-question.

What is genuinely new about **2026-11-12** is structural, and it is the thing a reader will notice
first: **the US bond market is closed on Wednesday 2026-11-11 for Veterans Day, in the middle of the
refunding block.** The block runs 3-Year Monday 11-09, 10-Year Tuesday 11-10, nothing Wednesday,
30-Year Thursday 11-12. This event's own calendar note flagged that as anomalous — "a closed day
sits inside a block that ran three consecutive business days in every other 2026 cycle."

So the question this session actually asked was: **does the interposed closure change anything about
this auction — its placement, its mechanics, or its demand — and is the shape even unusual?** Three
candidate readings, all intuitive, all testable:

1. The closure **displaces or compresses** the block, so the 30-Year leg is out of position.
2. A shut Wednesday means the market has **less time to build a concession** between the 10-Year and
   the 30-Year, and more information arrives at once when it reopens — so Thursday should **gap**.
3. Selling the quarter's largest duration into a **shortened, holiday-broken week** should show up as
   **weaker demand**.

**One-line verdict:** none survives. The shape is Treasury's deterministic answer to the Veterans Day
weekday and has occurred in exactly this form three times before; the closure changes the *calendar*
distance between the legs while leaving the *session* distance unchanged; and demand in the
holiday-inside Novembers is indistinguishable from the others on every published bidder statistic.
The residual is an ordinary refunding new issue whose only unpublished number lands **2026-11-04**.

**Method caveat, reproduced independently.** `auctions_query`'s `security_type` does **not**
separate 30-Year TIPS from nominal bonds — both come back as `Bond`, and the TIPS rows ($8–9B, high
yields near 2%) contaminate any month-level average taken naively. Filtering on
`ref_cpi_on_issue_date` is what separates them; every statistic below is nominal-only. This is the
same class of field-reliability trap the 11-12 coupon-announcement sibling recorded for
`original_security_term`.

**Cycle vocabulary.** A nominal 30-Year is sold three times — a **NEW** issue in the refunding month
(February, May, August, November), then reopened in each of the two following months. **2026-11-12
is a NEW issue**: the tentative schedule's row carries no `R`, and every one of the **17** November
30-Year auctions since 2009 has been a new issue, 17/17.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The date, security and settlement are what the calendar says — SUPPORTED, primary.** The
tentative schedule's row reads verbatim `30-Year BOND / Wednesday, November 04, 2026 / Thursday,
November 12, 2026 / Monday, November 16, 2026`, with no `R` and the PDF's own legend confirming
`R --denotes reopening`. Settlement is Monday 11-16 rather than the customary 15th because
2026-11-15 is a Sunday; the bond will be dated 2026-11-15 and back-dated, as the 2026-08-13 sale was
(dated 08-15, issued 08-17). Its first interest payment date will be **2027-05-15**. 1:00pm ET is
Treasury's standing coupon convention and is **not** separately sourced. Status correctly stays
`estimate`: a tentative schedule is tentative by construction, and the 11-04 refunding statement is
what fixes it. **This ledger deliberately predicts no CUSIP** — the October sibling could, because a
reopening's CUSIP already exists; a new issue's does not, and guessing at the 912810U-- sequence
would be inference dressed as a reading.

**2. This is announced by the refunding statement, not by the coupon announcement it shares a
session with — SUPPORTED, and it is a correction worth stating.** The event's title records that the
auction runs "two hours after the same day's coupon announcement," which is true as a time relation
and inviting as a causal one. It is not causal. The schedule's own announcement column puts this
bond at **Wednesday, November 04, 2026** — the quarterly refunding. The ~11:00 ET announcement on
2026-11-12 sizes an entirely different set of securities (the 20-Year new issue, the 10-Year TIPS
reopening and three bills), which is exactly what
[`treasury-coupon-announcement-2026-11-12.md`](treasury-coupon-announcement-2026-11-12.md) covers.
Two events, one session, no information flowing between them on the day.

**3. The block shape is Treasury's deterministic answer to the Veterans Day weekday, not a
disruption — REFUTED as an anomaly, on 18 years of reconstruction.** This is the session's central
finding. Reconstructing every November refunding block 2009–2025 from `auctions_query` and pairing
it with the weekday Veterans Day fell on:

| Veterans Day weekday | Years | Block shape | 30-Year day |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wednesday** | 2009, 2015, 2020 | **Mon 3Y · Tue 10Y · [shut Wed] · Thu 30Y** | Thursday **3/3** |
| Tuesday | 2014, 2025 | Mon 3Y · [shut Tue] · Wed 10Y · Thu 30Y | Thursday **2/2** |
| Monday | 2013, 2019, 2024 | block sits entirely before or after the holiday | Thu / Thu / Wed |
| Thu, Fri, weekend | 2010–12, 2016–18, 2021–23 | ordinary Mon/Tue/Wed or Tue/Wed/Thu | — |

**2026 is the fourth instance of the Wednesday shape and it matches the prior three exactly**: 3-Year
Monday November 9, 10-Year Tuesday November 10, 30-Year Thursday November 12 — the same three
calendar dates as 2009, 2015 and 2020, because those are the same four years in which November 11
fell on a Wednesday. Across all **six** holiday-inside Novembers the 30-Year anchored on **Thursday,
6/6**. So the closure does not displace this leg; Treasury pulls the *front* legs earlier and leaves
the long end where it always is. **Honest limit:** n=3 for the exact shape and n=6 for the broader
"holiday inside the block" class — small, but the mechanism is a calendar identity rather than a
behavioural regularity, which is why it is worth a high-confidence call at n=3.

**4. Nothing is compressed — the block still runs three consecutive bond sessions — SUPPORTED,
mechanically.** The calendar note's phrasing ("a closed day sits inside a block that ran three
consecutive business days in every other 2026 cycle") is true of *calendar* days and misleading
about *sessions*, and the distinction is load-bearing for every intuition in this doc. The bond
market is **shut** on 11-11 (the tentative schedule carries its own line `Holiday - Wednesday,
November 11, 2026 - Veterans Day`, a Treasury primary corroborating the SIFMA-sourced
[`sifma-bond-market-closure-2026-11-11`](sifma-bond-market-closure-2026-11-11.md) entry). A closed
day is not a session in which the block failed to auction; it is a day the block's calendar skips.
Counted in sessions rather than dates, the November block runs **Monday, Tuesday, Thursday = three
consecutive bond sessions**, precisely like an ordinary Tuesday/Wednesday/Thursday block. In
particular the 10-Year and the 30-Year sit **zero intervening cash sessions** apart in both shapes.

**5. There is no closure gap in the long end — SUPPORTED directionally, and small-n by
construction.** Leg 4 removes the *mechanical* reason to expect a gap, but not the informational
one: equities and global markets trade Wednesday while the cash Treasury market is dark, so Thursday
must absorb two calendar days of news in one open. Measured on Treasury's own par curve, the
30-Year's move from the **10-Year auction day close** to the **30-Year auction day close**:

| Block class | n | Mean signed | Mean absolute | Max absolute |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Closure inside the block | 5 | **−2.0 bp** | **3.2 bp** | 11.0 bp |
| Ordinary block | 11 | +1.5 bp | **8.5 bp** | 28.0 bp |

The three exact-shape years moved **0.0** (2009), **−1.0** (2015) and **−11.0 bp** (2020); the
largest ordinary-block move in the sample is **−28 bp** (2022, a CPI week). **What this establishes
and what it does not:** at n=5 (n=3 for the exact shape) it does *not* establish that closures damp
the long end — that reading would be over-fitting a handful of Novembers. It does establish that
**there is no precedent for a large adverse gap into this leg**, which is the only form of the claim
a stance can rest on. It also corroborates, from a different instrument and a different series, what
[`sifma-bond-market-closure-2026-11-11`](sifma-bond-market-closure-2026-11-11.md) already refuted
from the ETF side across 42 bonds-shut sessions: the next session's move runs **0.946×** its
trailing-20 median, i.e. ordinary. Two independent measurements, same conclusion, neither derived
from the other.

**6. Demand in holiday-inside Novembers is indistinguishable from the rest — REFUTED as a
hypothesis, on every published bidder statistic.** Nominal November 30-Year new issues, 2009–2025:

| Class | n | Bid-to-cover | σ | Indirect | Dealer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Closure inside the block | 5 | **2.308** | 0.052 | **56.2%** | 30.4% |
| No closure | 12 | **2.314** | 0.203 | 52.8% | 31.8% |

A gap of **0.006** on bid-to-cover, with the closure years carrying the *higher* end-investor share
and the *lower* dealer absorption — the opposite of what "shortened week, forced duration" predicts.
Their dispersion is also markedly tighter (σ 0.052 vs 0.203), which is suggestive and **not** claimed
here: five observations cannot separate a real effect from three quiet Novembers.

**7. November is not a weak refunding month — SUPPORTED, and it removes the last seasonal story.**
Nominal 30-Year new issues by refunding month, 2009–2026 (TIPS excluded):

| Month | n | Bid-to-cover mean | σ |
|---|---|---|---|
| February | 18 | 2.336 | 0.175 |
| May | 18 | 2.335 | 0.164 |
| August | 18 | 2.327 | 0.170 |
| **November** | **17** | **2.312** | 0.173 |

A **2.4 bp-of-cover** spread across four months and eighteen years — noise. Whatever else the
November long end is, it is not seasonally under-bid.

**8. The size is over-determined and is not the question — SUPPORTED.** Eleven consecutive nominal
30-Year new issues have printed **$25B**, from 2024-02-08 through 2026-08-13, across clearing yields
**4.314% → 5.216%** — a 90bp span the size did not respond to. That sits inside the broader fact the
coupon-announcement sibling established and this session re-derived on its own pull: **no nominal
coupon or FRN size has changed anywhere since April 2024**, ~29 months and ten quarterly refundings,
each carrying *"maintaining … for at least the next several quarters."* The number is not published
today — `sb0590`'s anticipated-size table ends at Oct-26 — and becomes published **2026-11-04**. The
same eleven auctions give the usable demand band: bid-to-cover mean **2.392**, σ 0.130, range
**2.27 – 2.66**; bidder shares indirect **65.6%** / direct **20.7%** / dealer **13.7%**.

**9. The headline supply number understates the line by about $6B — SUPPORTED, and it is the one
number in this doc a reader is most likely to have wrong.** Total accepted has exceeded the offering
amount at **15 of 15** nominal 30-Year new issues since 2023-02, because Treasury adds SOMA
roll-in awards on top of the announced size. On the $25B era the add-on has run **$1.85B – $8.82B**,
mean **$5.75B**, median **$6.08B**; accepted/offering averages **1.24×**. The most recent new issue
(2026-08-13) offered $25B and accepted **$31.32B** including a **$6.32B** SOMA add-on. The last
nominal 30-Year new issue with a zero add-on was **2014-08-14**. Registered as
**FT-treasury-30y-bond-2026-11-12-4**.

**10. There is no buyback question on this auction — SUPPORTED, and the reason is coverage, not
policy.** Both prior 30-Year ledgers spent their sharpest legs on whether a same-day buyback bids
for the bond being sold. Here the question does not arise: the published **Tentative Buyback
Schedule**, re-fetched this session, ends at the **11/05/2026** operation (1 Mo–2 Y, announce 11/04,
settle 11/06). The week of 11-09 has **no published operation calendar at all** — the 11-04 refunding
publishes the next quarter's. Two things follow. First, any buyback claim about this auction today
would be invention, and this doc makes none. Second, when that calendar does publish, the October
sibling's eligibility finding applies unchanged: the new bond will be on-the-run **and** short of its
first coupon (**2027-05-15**), failing both TreasuryDirect exclusion clauses, which held **20/20**
across every nominal 20Y–30Y operation on record. Separately worth recording: `sb0607`'s (2026-08-19)
promised updated buyback schedule is now **20 days unfulfilled** — the PDF re-fetched this session is
**byte-identical at 125,547 bytes** to the 09-03 and 09-05 pulls, still mastheaded "For Publication
August 5, 2026."

**11. The corridor's real risk is the 11-10 session, not the auction — SUPPORTED.** Eighteen tracked
entries sit within ±5 days. The dense one is **Tuesday 11-10**, which carries the **October CPI**
print (high impact), the **US-China tariff truce expiry** (high impact) and the 10-Year refunding
leg — and is the **last bond session before the market shuts**. Its close is the reference against
which Thursday's auction prices, with no cash session in between. The 30-Year's own session (11-12)
is crowded but with low-information items: the coupon announcement (a different set of securities,
leg 2), the October MTS, and existing-home sales. PPI follows 11-13, settlement 11-16, retail sales
11-17. The sequence that can actually move this event is earlier and shorter: **borrowing estimates
11-02 → midterms 11-03 → refunding 11-04**.

**12. The tape going in is calm, and 65 days stale by construction — SUPPORTED, primary.** Par curve
at the **2026-09-08** close: 2Y **4.39** · 10Y **4.80** · 20Y **5.26** · 30Y **5.25**, with 2s30s at
**86bp**. Cross-asset: **VIX 15.72** (09-08 close, vs 14.53 on 09-04), **MOVE 76.14** (vs 73.10 on

### What plays the conditions support

None directional, and here the guard-shaped answer is the *finding* rather than a default.
`symbols: []`, the date is `estimate`, and the three readings that would motivate a position around
this auction — a displaced block, a reopening gap, holiday-week demand weakness — are the three this
session refuted against primaries. No new duration-sensitive exposure into the 1:00pm ET release,

### Honest limits

The 11-04 refunding statement, the when-issued yield, and the auction's CUSIP and coupon **do not
exist yet**; this doc is written 65 days out and says so rather than implying otherwise. The
structural legs rest on small samples by construction — n=3 for the exact Wednesday shape, n=5 for
the holiday-inside class, n=17 for November 30-Year new issues — and leg 5's dispersion result is
explicitly *not* claimed as an effect. Bid-to-cover is a demand proxy; the tail (stop vs. when-issued)
is the sharper metric and is **not** in the primary dataset, because it needs a dealer when-issued
quote Treasury does not publish, so this ledger makes no tail claim. The par-curve measurement in
leg 5 uses close-to-close constant-maturity yields, not the on-the-run bond and not intraday marks
around the 1:00pm auction, so it measures the *session*, not the auction. Nothing here forecasts a
clearing yield; every claim is about **structure, size and demand mechanics**, which are the parts
the primary record can actually settle. All sources returned HTTP 200 this session; nothing was
blocked, and `probe-ref.blocked` is empty accordingly.
