### The question

**So: is the implementation session really an inference? And does the quarterly IPO leg do anything to the
tape that this house could act on?**

**One-line verdict:** the caveat is retired — the owner states the implementation session in its own words,
in a table the proposing session did not quote — and the event does nothing measurable, on evidence that is
stronger than a null usually gets, because **this event has a twin and the twin disagrees**.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The implementation session is owner-published, not inferred — the proposal's own caveat is REFUTED.**
   The FAQ carries a second table the proposing session did not read, **"Quarterly Addition Guidelines"**,
   which states the *standing rule* rather than one year's dates:

   | Row | Rule as published | 2026 Q1 | 2026 Q3 |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Rank Date\* | "Last working day in January and July." | 30 January 2026 | **31 July 2026** |
   | Announce Date\* | "Fourth Friday prior to the effective date (after market close)" | 20 February 2026 | **21 August 2026** |
   | Effective Date\*\* | "Third Friday of the month." | 23 March 2026 | **21 September 2026** |

   with the footnotes **"\* If a date falls on a holiday the previous business day is used"** and — the line
   that settles it — **"\*\* After the close on the third Friday of the month."** The owner therefore states
   that the trade prints after the close of the third Friday, which for September 2026 is **2026-09-18**,
   effective the following open, **2026-09-21**. The rule reproduces the owner's own published values for both
   2026 legs to the day (third Fridays 20 March / 18 September; announce Fridays 20 February / 21 August, each
   exactly 28 days earlier), which is the internal check. What remains `estimate` is the **prefix**, not the fact.

2. **The owner runs exactly TWO quarterly IPO legs a year, and that is what makes this event testable —
   SUPPORTED, and it is the hinge of the whole sheet.** The same table has only two columns, headed **"2026
   First-Quarter Additions"** and **"2026 Third-Quarter Additions"**; June and December are the semi-annual
   reconstitutions. So March and September are not an analyst's convenient pairing — they are *the owner's own
   two instances of one event*, sharing the rule, the geometry, the flow mechanic and the announcement lead
   time, and differing only in which calendar month they land on. That is a **mechanism-matched control**, and
   it is the thing the [December lock-down sibling](russell-recon-lockdown-2026-11-30.md) explicitly did not
   have (no December reconstitution has ever happened, so its 21-year sample carried zero event content).

3. **HEADLINE — the September effect is real-looking and the March leg refutes it — SUPPORTED, and it is the
   finding.** IWM minus SPY, session excess on the effective open, 2005–2025, by leg:

   | | n | mean | median | negative | sign p |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | **September** | 21 | **−0.311%** | −0.164% | **15 / 21** | **3.92%** |
   | **March** | 21 | **+0.182%** | +0.277% | 6 / 21 (**15 positive**) | **3.92%** *(the other way)* |
   | **Pooled** | 42 | −0.064% | −0.009% | 21 / 42 | **56.12%** |

   Two legs of one event, each clearing 5% — in opposite directions — and summing to an exact coin flip. The
   per-year pairs make the point without statistics: 2008 prints **−1.80%** in September and **+1.89%** in
   March; 2020 prints **−2.39%** and **+1.08%**. **The mechanism is present in both columns and the sign is
   not**, so the sign is not coming from the mechanism.

4. **The September number is a September number, and it does not survive its own outliers — SUPPORTED.**
   Dropping the two macro-crisis Mondays that dominate the mean (2008-09-22, the week the short-selling ban
   and TARP landed; 2020-09-21, a global second-wave selloff) leaves **−0.123% mean, −0.146% median, 13 of 19,
   p=8.35%** — the effect halves and stops clearing. The surrounding tape is mildly soft in the same window
   anyway (every session 18–30 September, n=196: **−0.047%, 105/196, p=17.7%**), so the slot is the most
   negative draw inside a weakly negative fortnight, on n=21, in one of two legs.

5. **The placebo panel is CLEAN — and that is the methodological finding, because it is also wrong —
   SUPPORTED.** Running the sibling chain's standard calibration, the September slot shifted by whole weeks:

   | Mean | +0.12% | +0.03% | +0.10% | **−0.31%** | +0.15% | −0.09% | −0.12% |
   | Negative | 9/21 | 9/21 | 11/21 | **15/21** | 9/21 | 10/21 | 12/21 |
   | sign p(neg) | 80.8% | 80.8% | 50.0% | **3.92%** | 80.8% | 66.8% | 33.2% |

   No shifted slot comes close. By the calibration this chain has been using, that panel *endorses* the
   September result — and leg 3 shows the result is spurious anyway. **The reason is structural: shifting a

### Honest limits

**The refutation is as small a sample as the effect.** n=21 per leg; a permutation test on the September−March
difference returns **p=5.8%**, so the two legs are not *proven* different either. The honest claim is the
pooled one — 21 of 42, p=56% — which is a statement that the event has no measurable sign, not a proof that
the September slot is empty. **The 5-session post-effective window is the one measure that does not fully
die:** September runs **−0.85% at 16/21 (p=1.33%)**, March **−0.33% at 10/21 (p=66.8%)**, and pooled
**−0.59% at 26/42 (p=8.2%)** — pooled it fails, but it fails less cleanly than the day itself, and a reader
who wanted to argue for a slow post-effective drift would start there. **The instrument is near-blind (leg 8)**,
which cuts both ways: it is why the nulls are honest about tradability and why they say nothing about
single-name adds. **No turnover figure for a quarterly IPO leg could be sourced** — the owner publishes
reconstitution turnover but not this, so "far smaller than reconstitution" is a mechanical inference from the
review's scope, not a measured quantity. **The 2026 print will be near-worthless as evidence** either way: 26
tracked events sit within five days and the effective open is the first session after an FOMC, a BoJ decision
and a quad-witching expiry. **Leg 7 is n=1.** **The date is `estimate`** and every trading-adjacent statement
above carries that label; shorting is blocked house-wide, `symbols` is empty by design, and no house playbook
is index-flow-keyed.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `estimate`, `EST:` owner-published in the July 2026 FAQ's schedule table *and* its standing-rule
Quarterly Addition Guidelines table, prefix-gapped rather than unpublished).** Treat 2026-09-21 as **a
mechanically certain, owner-published, entirely untradeable boundary whose apparent tape signature is a
calendar artefact** — and treat the *method* that established that as this ledger's larger contribution. Four
legs. **(a) The provenance improved.** The owner states the implementation session verbatim — *"After the
close on the third Friday of the month"* — retiring the proposal's own capitalised inference caveat; the
`estimate` label survives on a missing prefix and the FAQ's *"dates may be subject to change"*, not on absence
of publication. **(b) The event has a twin and the twin refutes it.** September's effective open runs −0.31%
at 15-of-21 (p=3.92%); March's, the same review under the same published rule, runs +0.18% at 15-of-21
positive; pooled, 21 of 42 at p=56%. **(c) Therefore the calibration this chain uses is not sufficient.** The
September result passes a clean week-shift placebo panel — no shifted slot within an order of magnitude — and
is spurious regardless, because week-shifting varies the calendar while holding the mechanism at zero. **The
mechanism-matched control is the same event's other instance, and it must be run before any Russell slot
result is believed.** **(d) And the instrument is near-blind, measured.** June reconstitution — 10.43% R2000
turnover, the largest index-flow day of the year — moves IWM's relative turnover by ~+0.10× at 12-of-21, so
every null in this chain is a claim about index-level tradability and not about the underlying flow. Carry
forward one thing that outlives this event: **when an event recurs on more than one calendar slot, the other
slot is the free control that separates the mechanism from the calendar — run it before believing either the
effect or its placebo panel.**
