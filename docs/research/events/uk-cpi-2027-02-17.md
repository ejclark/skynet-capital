### Leg 2 — "This print publishes the 2027 electricity weight." **REFUTED — ONS publishes two, and this is the other one.**

ONS's *Consumer price inflation, updating weights: 2026* article (released 16 March 2026, fetched
today) states the design verbatim:

> "There are two separate updates to 2026 weights, which is in line with our **"double update"
> methodology introduced in 2017**. These are: the **first update** of weights for the **January 2026**
> set of indices, where expenditure for 2024 is price updated to **December 2025** … the **second
> update** of weights for the **February to December 2026** indices, where the expenditure for 2024 is
> price updated to **January 2026**."

Its Table 2 publishes both, per division — CPI division 04 (housing, water, electricity, gas and other
fuels) reads **130.008** for the January update against **131.251** for February-to-December, a gap of
1.24 ppt on one division. The accompanying annex goes to class level. Parsed from the 2026 edition
xlsx, sheet `W1-CPI`, row `CJXA 04.5.1 Electricity`:

| February–December | **17** | 17 | **17** | 18 | 19 | 20 | **27** | **23.3142** | **19.1488** | **20.6065** |
| MM23 `CJXA` publishes | 17.0 | 17.0 | 17.0 | 18.0 | 19.0 | 20.0 | 27.0 | 23.3142 | 19.1488 | 20.6065 |

**Six of the ten double-update years discriminate** (2017, 2019, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 — the rest have

### Leg 5 — "The exact 5/105 cancellation depends on the January stage." **MIXED — right answer, wrong stage, and the correct route is stronger.**

`FT-uk-cpi-2027-11-17-3` argues the reversion's tax arithmetic (+5/100 = 5.0%) is offset against the
cut's (−5/105 = 4.7619%) because ONS price-updates the weight to **December of the prior year**, which
sits inside the zero-rate window, so the weight comes in at ≈1/1.05 of its counterfactual. It cites the
CPI technical manual's January rule — and Leg 2 shows the January rule governs the weight that lane is
**not** using.

Read the correct stage and the conclusion survives, more robustly: the **second** update price-updates
to **January of the current year**, i.e. **January 2027** — which is *also* inside the zero-rate window
(2026-10-01 → 2027-03-31), and closer to the reversion than December is. **Both** stages are VAT-free,
so the cancellation does not depend on which one you pick. Graded MIXED rather than SUPPORTED because
the mechanism as written cites a stage that does not govern the outcome; the *number* is unaffected.

**What could still break it** is not the stage but a **special adjustment**: if ONS treats the VAT
distortion as requiring intervention rather than letting price-updating handle it, the reversion step
grows ~5% and every band moves. That is now dated to the March 2027 article, not to this print, and
registered as **FT-uk-cpi-2027-02-17-2**.

### Honest limits

- **The double-update finding is identification from 6 discriminating years, not a statement of ONS
  policy about which column MM23 carries.** ONS does not publish a sentence saying "the MM23 weights
  series is the February-to-December column"; the inference is that CJXA matched it 6 times out of 6 and
  the January value 0 times, over the only years where the two differ. Four further years agree
  trivially. It is a strong empirical read, and it is a read.
- **Whether the January-only weight is published *anywhere* on 2027-02-17 is not established.** The ONS
  release page's summary line does say "Price indices, percentage changes **and weights**", and the
  detailed CPI tables workbook was not parsed (2.0 MB, and the dataset landing page carries no weights
  language). So "the operative weight is not published on 2027-02-17" is verified; "no weight at all is"
