## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **This print carries a +5.0% year-on-year jump in the UK electricity index that comes
entirely from tax, and the reason it will fool people is not that it is thirteen months old — it is
that the tax cut it reverses never appeared as a fall in the first place.** SI 2026/987
(legislation.gov.uk, fetched) zero-rates GB domestic electricity *"beginning with 1st October 2026
and ending with 31st March 2027"*, so October 2027 measures a 5%-rated month against a **zero-rated
base**: +5/100 = **5.0000%** on CPI 04.5.1, holding through the March 2028 print. On headline that is
**+0.090 to +0.108pp** — a band this lane tightens from the proposing lane's 0.091–0.129pp, because
the 2027 weight is price-updated through a **zero-rated December 2026** and the top of that range
(27.0 per 1000, 2023) is an energy-crisis vintage, not a base case. **The finding that earns this
entry its place is the invisibility.** Ofgem's 26-Aug-2026 release (fetched) says *"The Government's
decision to remove VAT from all domestic electricity bills is reflected in this update"* and that
electricity-only households *"will see a much smaller **increase** of less than 1%"* — an *increase*,
after the relief. The net-of-VAT price rose ~5% on the same date the 4.7619% cut landed. So there is
no October 2026 electricity plunge for anyone to remember, and a forecaster reading the observed base
sees nothing anomalous in it. **None of it is ours to trade.** `symbols: []`; the calendar tracks

## Initial research

**The question.** The October 2027 UK CPI print sits 434 days out, on a calendar with no UK
instrument. Is there anything in it worth carrying — and is the proposing lane's thesis right?

**Verdict in one line.** The thesis is right and its stated reason is the weaker of the two available:
the base effect is hidden not because it is thirteen months old, but because the cut that created it
was offset, on the day, by a coincident ~5% rise in the net-of-VAT cap — so nothing visible ever
happened for anyone to diarise.

### Leg 2 — the base effect is real and is +5.0000% — **SUPPORTED**

Article 1 of SI 2026/987, fetched: the Order *"comes into force on 1st October 2026"* and the zero
rate applies *"between 1st October 2026 and ending with 31st March 2027"*. Four dated steps follow in
the year-on-year path, and the proposing lane has all four right:

| Print (published) | Current month | Base month | VAT term on CPI 04.5.1 y/y |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oct 2026 (2026-11-18) → Mar 2027 (2027-04-22) | 0% | 5% | **−4.7619%** (1/1.05) |
| Apr 2027 (2027-05-19) → Sep 2027 (2027-10-20) | 5% | 5% | **0** — the drag drops out |
| **Oct 2027 (2027-11-17)** → Mar 2028 | **5%** | **0%** | **+5.0000%** (5/100) |
| Apr 2028 onwards | 5% | 5% | 0 — it drops out again |

The asymmetry is not a rounding artefact: removing 5% VAT from a VAT-inclusive price cuts it by
5/105 = 4.7619%; putting it back onto the now-VAT-free price adds 5/100 = 5.0%. ONS uses purchasers'
prices — the CPI technical manual, fetched, requires prices to *"include any taxes less subsidies on
the products"* — so both moves land in the index.

### Leg 3 — the headline band is right but too wide — **MIXED**, tightened

Headline effect = 2027 CJXA weight × 5.0%. CJXA, fetched direct today (parts per 1000): **20.0**
(2015), **17.0** (2016–19), **18.0** (2020), **19.0** (2021), **20.0** (2022), **27.0** (2023),
**23.3142** (2024), **19.1488** (2025), **20.6065** (2026). The 2027 weight is undrawn until the
January print on **2027-02-17**.

The proposing lane already established the mechanism that shapes it, and the credit is theirs: ONS
price-updates each year's weights to **December of the prior year** — the technical manual, fetched:
*"The weight for January is calculated by price updating the expenditure (V) in the weight reference
period to the December of the previous year"* — and December 2026 sits **inside** the zero-rate
window, so the 2027 electricity weight is price-updated through a VAT-free price and lands ≈1/1.05 of
its counterfactual. The tax cut mechanically shrinks the weight that scales its own reversal.

What this lane adds is that the same mechanism argues the band should be **narrower and lower**. The
27.0 top of the 2023–2026 spread is the basket set at the peak of the 2022 energy crisis, when
electricity prices had roughly doubled; reaching it again in 2027 requires a second crisis, which is
a different event and not this one's base case. With 2026 at 20.6065 and a ~4.76% VAT depression
applied to the price-update, the defensible band is **18.0–21.5 per 1000**:

| 2027 CJXA (per 1000) | Headline VAT contribution, Oct-2027 print |
|---|---|
| 18.0 | **+0.090pp** |
| 19.63 (2026 weight, VAT-depressed) | **+0.098pp** |
| 20.6065 (2026 published) | **+0.103pp** |
| 21.5 | **+0.108pp** |

So **+0.090 to +0.108pp**, inside and in the lower half of the proposing lane's 0.091–0.129pp. This
is a forecast of a weight, not a fact, and it is registered as **FT-uk-cpi-2027-11-17-3** rather than
asserted.

### Leg 4 — "nobody diarised it" — **SUPPORTED, with a stronger reason than the proposal gave**

The proposal's reason is elapsed time: by November 2027 anyone who diarised the October 2026 cut is
thirteen months past it. True, but the sharper reason is that **there was nothing to diarise.**

Ofgem's 26-Aug-2026 press release, fetched: *"The Government's decision to remove VAT from all
domestic electricity bills is reflected in this update"* — so the announced **+4% to £1,723** is
*after* the relief — and *"households which do not use gas will see a much smaller **increase** of
less than 1%"*. An **increase**, in the quarter the tax came off. Ofgem's cap-change page, fetched,
puts the Oct–Dec 2026 electricity rate at **26.32p/kWh** with a **54.83p/day** standing charge and
states it *"does not include VAT from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027"*.

Run it backwards: if a VAT-inclusive September bill and a VAT-free October bill differ by under 1%,
the net-of-VAT electricity price rose by roughly 5% on the same date the 4.7619% relief landed. The
release's own cross-check agrees — without the removal the cap *"would have been around £45 higher"*,
and £45 is 5/105 of the ~£950 electricity share of a £1,723 dual-fuel bill.

Two consequences, and the second is the one that matters here:

1. The October 2026 electricity index should print roughly **flat to modestly up**, not down ~4.3%.
   This is *not* a correction to `uk-cpi-2026-11-18.md`, which already computed the energy block from
   Ofgem's post-relief percentages and called the −0.098pp explicitly *"a **counterfactual** delta,
   not a year-on-year base effect"*. That ledger is internally consistent; this lane is drawing out
   the consequence it did not need to state.
2. **The base for this print looks entirely ordinary.** A forecaster building an October 2027
   year-on-year off the observed October 2026 index sees a month that rose about as much as a routine
   cap step — no plunge, no anomaly, no reason to check the tax treatment. The level is nonetheless
   ~4.76% below its counterfactual, and that gap comes back in full on 2027-11-17. A base effect
   whose *cause* was visible gets diarised; this one's cause was cancelled out on the day.

Registered as **FT-uk-cpi-2027-11-17-1**, scoreable on **2026-11-18** — 70 days from today, and 364
days before the event it bears on.

### Leg 5 — the confound structure is cleaner than the April sibling's — **SUPPORTED**

`uk-cpi-2027-05-19` carries a real confound the proposing lane named: the Apr–Jun 2027 Ofgem cap
resets on the same date as the reversion, so the April month-on-month electricity move mixes tax with
cap. This print does not inherit it. VAT has been back at 5% since April 2027, so:

- the **month-on-month** move in October 2027 is **pure** Oct–Dec 2027 cap reset (announced ~late
  August 2027 on Ofgem's cadence; the date is not published and is deliberately not proposed here);
- the **year-on-year** move carries the VAT step, and it is separable by construction — divide the
  electricity y/y by 1.05 and the tax term is gone.

That is a better-conditioned observation than the April one, which is worth knowing when the two
prints get compared.

The asymmetry is the finding, not the dates. The Bank's staff model consumption-tax changes
explicitly, so the step should already sit in the November projection; the tape, thirteen months past
a cut that never showed as a fall, will not have it in the narrative. A hot October 2027 UK print is
therefore not a hawkish surprise to the committee that has just published its forecast. Registered as
**FT-uk-cpi-2027-11-17-2**, scoreable **2027-11-04**.
