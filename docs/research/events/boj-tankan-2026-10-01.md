## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This event is one day old. It was created on 2026-09-05 by the
[boj-summary-of-opinions-2026-10-01](boj-summary-of-opinions-2026-10-01.md) initial research, which
needed a **positive control**: to argue that its own null result ("the Summary of Opinions does not
move the Tokyo open") was a limit of the measurement rather than a fact about the world, it needed
another 08:50 JST Bank of Japan release that certainly *does* move it. It chose the Tankan, measured
it at **1.28× baseline on the Nikkei opening gap over 41 releases (2016+), p = 0.159**, observed
that this sits below the test's own bootstrap detection floor of 1.46× at that n, and concluded:
*"a known-material 08:50 JST BoJ release does not clear this instrument's floor… the null bounds the
channel rather than excluding it. Every 'no channel' this calendar has written about the BoJ should
carry that qualifier."*

That is a load-bearing conclusion — it was written as an instruction to every future BoJ ledger on
this calendar — and it rests entirely on the premise that **the Tankan is elevated**. So the
question this session asks is not the generic one. It is: **is the control a control?**

**One-line verdict: no. Extended from 41 releases to the Bank's full published history of 90, the
Tankan's opening-gap elevation is 0.88× — below an ordinary session — and the 1.28× the sibling
measured is a 2021-2026 sub-period that also happens to contain all four of its collision days; the
Tankan's own published surprise has no measurable relationship to the gap either, so the correct
reading is not "the instrument is blind" but "this release does not price into the Tokyo open," and
the calendar's BoJ nulls should go back to being plain nulls.**

**Method:** sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md), plus
measured legs run entirely in this session. Primaries fetched raw and machine-parsed today
(2026-09-05), never through a summariser: the **BoJ's six TANKAN (Summary) year archives** —
`boj.or.jp/en/statistics/tk/gaiyo/{2001,2006,2011,2016,2021,2026}/index.htm`, HTTP 200 each,

double-listing — yielding **90 releases, 2004-04-01 → 2026-07-01**; the **forward release schedule**

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The sibling's number replicates exactly, on an independent pipeline — SUPPORTED, and this leg
   exists to license the rest.** Restricting to 2016+ and using the same series, the Tankan's Nikkei
   \|opening gap\| median is **0.610%** against a non-Tankan baseline of **0.477%** = **1.28×**, at
   **p = 0.19** (theirs: 0.610%, 1.28×, p = 0.159 — the p differs only by test construction). **The
   disagreement below is not a data disagreement.**

2. **On the Bank's full published history the same statistic is below baseline — SUPPORTED, and it
   is this session's headline.** 90 releases; 89 Nikkei bars (the TSE halted the entire session on
   **2020-10-01**, that year's release date, so one release has no bar at all):

   | Series | Baseline 2004+ (median) | Tankan days (median) | Ratio | p |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | **Nikkei \|opening gap\|** | 0.501% (n=5,460) | **0.440%** (n=89) | **0.88×** | 0.30 |
   | Nikkei \|intraday\| | 0.492% | 0.501% | 1.02× | 0.91 |
   | Nikkei \|close-to-close\| | 0.721% | 0.720% | 1.00× | 1.00 |
   | S&P 500 \|close-to-close\| | 0.500% (n=5,615) | 0.517% (n=90) | 1.03× | 0.84 |
   | USD/JPY \|close-to-close\| | 0.327% (n=5,804) | 0.319% (n=80) | 0.98× | 0.87 |

   **Every series is at or below an ordinary session.** A release that "unambiguously matters" is
   supposed to look like something.

3. **The 1.28× is one sub-period, and three of four eras run below baseline — SUPPORTED, and it is
   the mechanism of leg 2.** Nikkei \|opening gap\|, Tankan days vs the same era's own baseline:

   | Era | n | Tankan median | Era baseline | Ratio | p |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | 2004-2010 | 28 | 0.355% | 0.498% | **0.71×** | 0.16 |
   | 2011-2015 | 20 | 0.469% | 0.556% | **0.84×** | 0.45 |
   | 2016-2020 | 19 | 0.322% | 0.429% | **0.75×** | 0.26 |
   | **2021-2026** | 22 | 0.698% | 0.509% | **1.37×** | 0.19 |
   | 2004-2015 | 48 | 0.402% | 0.521% | 0.77× | 0.10 |
   | 2016-2026 (the sibling's window) | 41 | 0.610% | 0.477% | **1.28×** | 0.19 |

   The sibling's window is **2016-2020 (0.75×) plus 2021-2026 (1.37×)**, and the second half
   dominates because gaps are larger there in level terms. **Nothing here is individually
   significant in either direction** — which is the point: the control was never measured as
   elevated, only *observed* as elevated in one window.

4. **The sibling's other suggestive finding is the same sub-period — SUPPORTED, and it collapses two
   hints into one.** That ledger reported the four Tankan/Summary-of-Opinions collision days at a
   Nikkei gap median of **0.753% = 1.51-1.58× baseline**, three of four above the floor, and flagged
   honestly that "the collision years are exactly 2021-2024, so a period effect and a Tankan effect
   are inseparable." **They are also inseparable from the control's elevation**, because 2021-2024 ⊂
   2021-2026, the only era in which Tankan gaps exceed baseline at all. The two independent-looking
   pieces of evidence for a Tankan channel are one observation of a recent-period regime.

5. **The Tankan's own surprise has no measurable relationship to the Tokyo opening gap — SUPPORTED,
   and it is the leg that speaks to the mechanism rather than the window.** Each release prints, in
   the same table as the result, the **previous survey's forecast for the quarter now being
   reported** — a published-in-advance anchor from the primary source, available for the 22 releases
   the Bank ships as machine-readable data (2021-03 → 2026-06). Surprise = actual − that forecast,
   large-manufacturer business-conditions DI:

   | Statistic | Value |
   |---|---|
   | Spearman(\|surprise\|, \|opening gap\|) | **+0.173**, permutation **p = 0.440** |
   | Spearman(\|surprise\|, \|close-to-close\|) | −0.130 |
   | Sign agreement (surprise direction = gap direction) | **10 / 19** |
   | Median \|gap\|, \|surprise\| ≥ 4 (n=6) | 0.820% |
   | Median \|gap\|, \|surprise\| ≤ 1 (n=7) | 0.709% |

   The two largest surprises in the sample are the tell: **+13 (2021-04-01) → a 0.90% gap**, and
   **+10 (2021-07-01) → a 0.14% gap**. **The information content of the release does not explain the
   size of the move that follows it**, which is what a real channel would require.

6. **A Tankan day is no more likely to clear the detection floor than a random session — SUPPORTED,
   and it is the least technical statement of the null.** Bootstrap floors (p97.5 of the median of
   *n* draws from the 2004+ non-Tankan pool): **n=22 → 1.56× · n=41 → 1.41× · n=89 → 1.28×**
   (0.641%). Rather than compare medians to floors, ask the direct question — **P(\|gap\| ≥ 0.641%)
   is 0.382 on Tankan days and 0.396 across all 2004+ sessions**; September-survey days clear it
   **7 of 21**. The sibling's framing ("the control lands below its own floor, so the instrument is
   underpowered") and this one describe the same data, but only the second is free of the assumption
   that the control has an effect to detect.

7. **Three of four Tankans a year are perfectly collinear with the quarter's first trading day —
   SUPPORTED, and it is why the Apr/Jul/Oct cuts can never be clean.** March, June and September
   surveys publish on the first business day of April, July and October. Across 2004-2026 there are
   exactly **two** April/July/October quarter-start sessions without a Tankan: **2010-10-01** (the
   2010 September survey printed on 09-29) and **2020-10-02** (the TSE halted 10-01 entirely). At
   n=2 the quarter-start effect and the Tankan effect are not separable on those dates by any test.
   **The one Tankan cut that is structurally clean is December** — mid-month, no quarter boundary —
   and it runs **0.399% median = 0.80× baseline, p = 0.34 (n=22)**.

8. **This session's own leading hypothesis, REFUTED and reported as such.** The obvious worry was
   that a quarter-start effect was inflating the Tankan cuts, and the "quarter-start without a
   Tankan" cell looked like proof: **median \|gap\| 0.995% = 1.99× baseline, p = 0.001 (n=25)**. It
   is not a quarter effect. **23 of those 25 sessions are January** — Tokyo's first bar after a 4-6

   session**, against **1 day** for every Tankan day, quarter-start or not. Restricted to January

   way**: there is no clean quarter-start control to compare Apr/Jul/Oct Tankans against, and leg 7
   states that as an identification limit rather than pretending leg 8 solved it.

### Honest limits

**The refutation is a null, and nulls are weak claims.** Nothing here is significant at conventional
thresholds in *either* direction: 0.88× at p = 0.30 is "not distinguishable from an ordinary
session," not "proven inert." The strong statement is comparative — the sibling's 1.28× and this
session's 0.88× are the same estimator on nested samples, so the *window choice*, not the data, was
carrying its conclusion. **The surprise leg is n=22 and its anchor is not the market's.** The
Bank's published forecast is the *surveyed firms' own* expectation, not the economists' consensus
the tape trades against; the two are correlated but not identical, and a consensus feed would be the
better test. It is used because it is primary, published a quarter in advance, and free — the
alternative was no surprise measure at all. **Pooling 2004-2026 spans several distinct BoJ regimes**
(ZIRP, QQE, YCC, normalisation) and every p-value is a same-day association, not a causal
identification. **The bootstrap draws i.i.d.**, ignoring volatility clustering, which makes extreme
cohort medians somewhat more likely by chance than the floors imply. **The 2021-2026 era is not
shown to be spurious** — it is shown to be the only era with the effect, which is a different and
weaker statement; if something genuinely changed in 2021 (larger Tankan revisions, a more
rate-sensitive Nikkei, higher gap volatility generally), the sibling's window would be the right one
and this ledger's would be wrong. **Leg 7's collinearity has no fix in this data**, only two
observations, and this ledger does not pretend otherwise. **2020-10-01 has no Nikkei bar at all**
(TSE system failure, whole session halted), so n=89 rather than 90 on every Nikkei cut. **The
release date is `estimate`** — the confirmed-prefix taxonomy has no slot for the Bank of Japan and
this lane may not self-confirm — even though the source is the Bank's own forward schedule. And this
is **D-26** initial research on a survey still in the field: nothing here forecasts the DI, only what
the print is worth to a book that does not trade it.
