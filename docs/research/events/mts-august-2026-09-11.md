**TL;DR.** **Never trade this print, and this session measured why rather than assuming it.** Across
**56 releases since 2022** the Monthly Treasury Statement moves nothing — TLT p=0.249, SPY p=0.689,
10-year yield p=0.839 on the day — and across the **35 releases** for which intraday bars exist, its

realized dispersion in that window *lower* than an ordinary day's. The reason is structural, not
statistical: the MTS is an accounting restatement of the **Daily Treasury Statement**, which publishes
every business day, so the number is knowable ten days early by anyone who adds it up. This session
added it up. **From primary DTS data through 2026-08-31, August's ex-debt cash gap is $202.1B; mapped

totaled **$99.4B in Aug-2025 and $25.3B in Aug-2026**. The one genuinely new thing in this statement

deficit is roughly four-fifths done. Read this print for that line and for **net interest** (FY2026 is
already **$931B through ten months**); ignore its headline, and ignore the release hour entirely —
CPI lands the same morning at 8:30 and owns the day.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

What is the Monthly Treasury Statement, is the 2026-09-11 date real, what will the August 2026 edition
say, does it move anything, and how should a paper-trading book that holds long-duration AI names
(NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL AMZN) use a monthly deficit print at all?

**One-line verdict:** the date is now primary-`confirmed` at 2:00 p.m. ET and the print is a measured
market non-event — its headline is pre-computable from daily Treasury data (this session computed it:
**~$230B**, band $200–265B, against $344.8B a year ago) and essentially all of the y/y improvement is
payment-calendar arithmetic plus a decaying court-ordered tariff-refund wave, so the only honest use
of this document is as a monthly fiscal *input* to the long-end ledgers, never as an event to trade.

### Method

Macro-print mode per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md) — `symbols: []`, so the
symbol-keyed instruments (`earnings-cycle.mjs`, `intraday-edges.mjs`) do not apply and the
cache-busting rule has no target. Everything below is primary except where flagged:

- **Treasury Fiscal Data API**, fetched direct 2026-09-03 (plain `curl`, HTTP 200 on every call):
  `accounting/mts/mts_table_1` for the FY2025/FY2026 monthly deficit series;
  `accounting/dts/deposits_withdrawals_operating_cash` for twelve month-end Daily Treasury Statement
  snapshots; `od/debt_to_penny`; `od/avg_interest_rates`.
- **The MTS publications themselves**, PDFs downloaded from
  `fiscaldata.treasury.gov/static-data/published-reports/mts/` and text-extracted with `pdftotext
  -layout`: the **July 2026** edition (its Highlight box and its Scheduled Release page), the **June
  2026** edition, the **November 2025** edition (which carries the full 2026 release calendar) and the
  **November 2023** edition (the 2024 calendar).
- **Market data**: Yahoo daily and hourly bars for TLT, SPY and ^TNX, 200,000-iteration two-sided
  permutation tests, hourly bars bucketed to `America/New_York`.
- **Secondary, and labeled as such**: the Supreme Court IEEPA ruling and the CIT refund order are
  press- and law-firm-reported (cbo.gov returns a JS-challenge wall to `curl` and 403 to WebFetch, so
  CBO's own Monthly Budget Review could not be read this session).

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is real, the time is 2:00 p.m. ET, and the entry is PROMOTED to `confirmed` —
   SUPPORTED by two independent Treasury primaries.** The July 2026 MTS, page 39, reads verbatim:
   *"The release date for the August 2026 Statement will be 2:00 p.m. EST September 11, 2026."*
   ("EST" is Treasury's own typo; September is EDT.) Independently, the November 2025 edition carries
   the full annual table — *"Listed below are the scheduled release dates for the Monthly Treasury
   Statement. The Statement is released at 2:00 p.m."* — with **August 2026 → September 11, 2026**.
   `TSY:` is an authorized confirmed-tier prefix and these are Treasury's own publications, so the
   `estimate` label filed on 2026-09-03 no longer has grounds. **Two corrections to the entry as
   filed**, both banked honestly: it claimed the July edition published **2026-08-13** and that this
   was *"itself the 8th August workday."* Treasury's own schedule says **August 12** in two separate
   PDFs, and August 13 is the **9th** August workday, not the 8th — the arithmetic was wrong in a way
   that happened not to change the answer.

2. **The 8th-workday rule is exact, not approximate — SUPPORTED, and it is now a reusable tool.**
   Computing the 8th federal workday of each month against the published schedules reproduces **20 of
   20** dates across the 2024 and 2026 calendars with **zero** mismatches. That is what let this doc
   date all 56 historical releases for leg 4 without fetching 56 PDFs, and it is why the "subject to
   change" caveat on Treasury's table is not a real risk here.

3. **The August deficit is already knowable, and this session computed it — SUPPORTED.** The MTS's own
   Introduction calls the Daily Treasury Statement part of the same "triad," published every working
   day. Summing DTS operating-cash flows for August 2026 and stripping public-debt issuance and
   redemptions gives **deposits $420.9B, withdrawals $623.0B, net gap $202.1B**. That is not the MTS
   number — the MTS is modified-cash with accrued interest on public issues — so the gap was
   calibrated against eleven months where both exist:

   | Month | DTS ex-debt gap | Published MTS deficit | MTS − DTS |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Aug 2025 | 349.4 | 344.8 | −4.6 |
   | Oct 2025 | 215.1 | 284.3 | +69.2 |
   | Nov 2025 | 139.6 | 173.3 | +33.7 |
   | Dec 2025 | 131.4 | 144.8 | +13.4 |
   | Jan 2026 | 36.2 | 94.6 | +58.4 |
   | Feb 2026 | 341.1 | 307.5 | −33.6 |
   | Mar 2026 | 115.5 | 164.1 | +48.6 |
   | Apr 2026 | −270.9 | −215.0 | +55.9 |
   | May 2026 | 263.8 | 292.6 | +28.8 |
   | Jun 2026 | 115.2 | 120.3 | +5.1 |
   | Jul 2026 | 370.4 | 432.3 | +61.9 |

   Mean bias **+$30.6B**, σ **$31.9B**. Applied to August's $202.1B: **central estimate ≈ $233B, 1σ
   band $200–265B**. The σ is the honest headline here — this maps the *level* well and the *last
   $30B* poorly, which is exactly why the print is not a surprise generator.

4. **It does not move anything, and the null holds in its own release window — SUPPORTED, and this is
   the load-bearing measurement.** Daily close-to-close across **56 releases (2022-01 → 2026-08)**,
   200k-iteration two-sided permutation:

   | Series | n | Mean on release day | Up days | p | All-day mean |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | TLT | 56 | −0.156% | 21/56 | **0.249** | −0.028% |
   | SPY | 56 | −0.061% | 27/56 | **0.689** | +0.052% |
   | ^TNX | 56 | +0.054% | 30/56 | **0.839** | +0.108% |

   A daily bar is a weak test for a 2:00 p.m. release, so the sharper one: hourly bars back to
   2023-10-05 give **35** releases with a clean **1:30 p.m.→close** window (the 13:30 ET bar's open to
   the session close — it contains the 2:00 p.m. release; the 30-minute lead-in is disclosed, not
   hidden). TLT **mean −0.038%, 15/35 up, p=0.413**, window σ 0.304% vs an all-day base of 0.267%.
   SPY **mean +0.004%, 17/35 up, p=0.946**, window σ **0.359% vs an all-day base of 0.402%** — that is
   *below* base, i.e. release-day afternoons are, if anything, quieter than ordinary ones. No drift, no
   volatility premium, nothing. The mechanism in leg 3 explains it: there is no surprise to react to.

5. **August 2026 is a zero-benefit-batch month and August 2025 held two — SUPPORTED by Treasury's own
   words and by daily data, and it is why the headline will mislead.** The July 2026 MTS Highlight box
   states it directly: *"Outlays for military active duty and retirement, veterans benefits,
   Supplemental Security Income, and Medicare payments to health maintenance organizations and
   prescription drug plans accelerated into July, because August 1, 2026, the normal payment date, fell
   on a non-business day."* August 1, 2026 was a **Saturday**; September 1, 2026 is a **Tuesday**, so
   nothing pulls back in. Those five categories, MTD on the DTS:

   | Category | Jul 2026 (double) | Aug 2026 (zero) | Aug 2025 (double) |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Medicare Prescription Drugs | 41.8 | **0.0** | 30.4 |
   | VA – Benefits | 37.0 | 19.7 | 33.2 |
   | DoD – Military Active Duty Pay | 14.7 | 4.7 | 13.9 |
   | DoD – Military Retirement | 11.9 | **0.5** | 11.5 |
   | SSA – Supplemental Security Income | 10.7 | **0.4** | 10.4 |
   | **Total** | **116.1** | **25.3** | **99.4** |

   August 2025 was the mirror case: **September 1, 2025 was Labor Day**, so that batch was paid Friday
   **2025-08-29** — and the DTS shows it landing on that single day (Medicare Rx $15.14B, VA $15.78B,
   military retirement $5.53B, SSI $5.00B, active duty $4.76B = **$46.2B in one session**). So the
   y/y comparison is two batches against zero, a **$74.1B swing** in these five lines alone, before
   anything economic happens.

6. **The tariff-refund wave is the one genuinely new fact, and it is decaying fast — SUPPORTED on
   Treasury data, with a secondary-sourced cause.** On 2026-02-20 the Supreme Court held **6–3** that
   IEEPA does not authorize tariffs; the Court of International Trade ordered CBP to refund roughly
   **$165B**, and CBP activated phase one of its CAPE refund system on **2026-04-20** (all
   press/law-firm sourced — cbo.gov and CBP primaries were not reachable this session). The DTS
   withdrawal line **"DHS – Customs & Border Protection (CBP)"** shows the consequence with a step
   change exactly where the cause predicts, MTD $B:

   | | Oct | Nov | Dec | Jan | Feb | Mar | Apr | **May** | **Jun** | **Jul** | **Aug** |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | FY2026 | 1.9 | 1.1 | 1.8 | 2.1 | 1.4 | 3.0 | 3.1 | **23.3** | **51.0** | **35.5** | **12.5** |

   FY2025's whole-year run-rate on this line was ~$1B/month (**$9.6B** through 2025-08-29). FY2026 is
   at **$136.5B** fiscal-year-to-date. May–August alone is **$122.3B** of the court-ordered ~$165B, so
   roughly **$43B remains** and the monthly outflow has fallen **76% in two months**. Meanwhile gross
   customs *receipts* have not collapsed at all — **$25.0B in August vs $26.1B in July**, FYTD
   **$305.9B** — so on a net basis July was a **negative $9.4B** tariff month and August a positive
   **$12.6B** one. This, not the headline, is the line worth reading on 9/11.

7. **The structural deterioration is in interest, and it is not improving — SUPPORTED.** The July 2026
   MTS puts **net interest at $931B through ten months** and ~**$104B in July alone**. On the DTS,
   August's *"Interest on Treasury Securities"* was **$110.6B** against **$75.5B** in August 2025 —
   though that y/y gap is itself partly calendar (2025-08-31 was a Sunday, pushing month-end interest
   into September 2025), so it is quoted as context, not as a +46% structural jump. Public debt
   outstanding was **$40.1125T on 2026-09-01** against the **$41.1T** limit — ~**$0.99T** of headroom,
   tightening from the ~$1.02T the sibling
   [`treasury-borrowing-estimates`](treasury-borrowing-estimates-2026-11-02.md) logged on 2026-08-27.
   Weighted average rates as of 2026-07-31: bills **3.758%**, notes **3.309%**, bonds **3.442%** — the
   coupon stock is still repricing upward into a 4.8% 10-year, which is why interest compounds even
   if the primary deficit does not.

8. **FY2026 is running slightly worse than FY2025 and the August print will disguise that —
   SUPPORTED.** Ten-month totals: FY2026 receipts **$4,485.4B**, outlays **$6,284.2B**, deficit
   **$1,798.8B**; FY2025 same period **$5,234.6B / $7,010.0B / $1,775.4B** — a deficit **+1.3% y/y**
   despite the refund drain. Add the ~$230B estimate and FY2026's eleven-month deficit is ≈**$2,029B**
   against FY2025's **$2,120.2B**, i.e. the YTD comparison **flips from +1.3% to roughly −4%** in a
   single month. That flip is an artifact: FY2025's eleven months contain a batch that economically
   belonged to September 2025, FY2026's do not. CBO's Monthly Budget Review normalizes for exactly
   this; the raw MTS does not.

9. **Tracked-name sensitivity is indirect and thin, and this print is not the way to trade it —
   SUPPORTED.** `symbols: []`. The only channel to nine software/semis/platform names is the rate

10. **The 9/11 session belongs to CPI, and that is permanent, not incidental — SUPPORTED.** CPI
    releases at **08:30 ET**, the MTS at **14:00 ET**, and in this calendar's own schedule **two of the
    next four** MTS releases share their day with CPI (**2026-09-11** and **2026-12-10**; the October

### What the conditions support

Nothing directional. What it supports is a **reading discipline and a calendar habit**:

- **Skip the headline; read two lines.** The CBP refund outflow (is the one-off finished?) and net
  interest (is the structural cost still compounding?). Both feed the long-end ledgers directly.
- **Discount the y/y comparison to zero for August.** Two mechanical effects — zero benefit batches
  against a two-batch prior year, and a refund wave decaying from $51B to $12B — will make August look
  like fiscal consolidation. It is not.
- **Use the DTS instead of waiting.** Leg 3 shows the number is derivable ten days early to ±$32B from
  a free public API. For any future month where the deficit matters to a decision, compute it; do not
  wait on the 8th workday.
- **Do not price event risk into 9/11 afternoon.** Leg 4 measured the release window as quieter than
  an ordinary afternoon.

### Honest limits

The $230B estimate is a **calibrated mapping, not a forecast of Treasury's arithmetic** — its σ is
$31.9B on eleven observations, and one of those eleven (Feb 2026, −$33.6B) sits nearly 2σ the wrong
way, so a print at $190B or $275B would be unusual but not disqualifying. The single prior *August*
in the calibration set maps at **−$4.6B**, which alone would imply ~$198B; the doc quotes the pooled
figure and flags the disagreement rather than picking the flattering one. The month-end DTS snapshots
use each month's last business day, which for a month ending on a weekend is not the calendar month
end — a known small source of mapping error, not corrected here. The IEEPA refund story's **cause**
(SCOTUS 6–3 on 2026-02-20, ~$165B CIT order, CAPE phase one 2026-04-20) is entirely
press/law-firm-sourced: cbo.gov serves a JavaScript challenge to `curl` and 403 to WebFetch, so CBO's
own Monthly Budget Review — which would both corroborate the refund figures and publish days *before*
this print — could not be read; it is a known leading release this calendar still cannot date, and it
is deliberately **not** proposed as a calendar entry on an unverified date. The CBP withdrawal line
also carries ordinary agency disbursements, so $136.5B FYTD is an upper bound on refunds, not a
refund total. The August interest y/y comparison is calendar-contaminated in the opposite direction to
the benefits (2025-08-31 fell on a Sunday) and is quoted as context only. The market-impact null is
measured on TLT/SPY/^TNX close data and a 1:30 p.m. window that includes 30 minutes of pre-release
drift; no options-implied or MOVE-based test was run, and no attempt was made to separate MTS days
that collided with CPI from those that did not — with n=35 that split would not have been powered.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `confirmed`, TSY:, promoted this session on two Treasury primaries).** Treat
**2026-09-11 14:00 ET as a fiscal data point, not an event**: no position is opened, closed, sized or
hedged around it, and no house playbook targets it. This is a *measured* stand-aside, not a cautious
one — 56 daily observations and 35 intraday release-window observations all return nulls, and the
mechanism (the DTS pre-publishes the inputs daily) explains why. **Base case for the August print,
`confirmed`-date but model-derived: a deficit near $230B, band $200–265B**, versus $344.8B in August
2025 — and the resulting ~$110–145B y/y "improvement" is judged **mechanical**: zero shifted benefit
batches against two in the prior year (a $74.1B swing in five DTS lines alone), plus a court-ordered
tariff-refund wave decaying from $51.0B in June to $12.5B in August. The report's genuine content is
two lines — **CBP refund outflows** (~$43B of the ~$165B order still to run, on secondary sourcing)
and **net interest** ($931B through ten months) — both of which feed the long-end term-premium story
that [`treasury-30y-bond-2026-09-10`](treasury-30y-bond-2026-09-10.md) and
[`treasury-borrowing-estimates-2026-11-02`](treasury-borrowing-estimates-2026-11-02.md) already track.
The auctions on 9/8, 9/9 and 9/10 price that story before this document reports it.

**Kill switches:**

- **The 2026-09-11 print lands outside $195–270B** — the DTS→MTS mapping in leg 3 is broken, this
  print carries real surprise after all, and the whole "pre-computable, therefore inert" argument needs
  re-deriving before it is repeated. Registered as **FT-50**.
- **TLT's 1:30 p.m.→close move on 2026-11-12 exceeds ±0.60% (≈2σ of the measured release-window
  distribution)** — the market-impact null fails on the one clean, CPI-free release day in this
  calendar's forward window. Registered as **FT-51**; **2026-09-11 is excluded in advance** as
  unfalsifiably confounded by CPI at 08:30.
- **CBP refund outflows re-accelerate above ~$25B/month** — the one-off is not winding down, the drain
  extends into FY2027, and the fiscal leg of the long-end story gets worse rather than better.
- **The improvement persists on a calendar-neutral month** (the 2026-10-14 September-data edition or
  the 2026-11-12 October-data edition) — the "mechanical, therefore ignore it" framing in leg 5 is
  wrong and the deficit trajectory really is bending; the long-end ledgers should hear about it.
- **A debt-limit constraint becomes live** — headroom is ~$0.99T on $40.1125T against a $41.1T limit
  and shrinking; the sibling borrowing-estimates ledger puts exhaustion near Feb–Apr 2027. Once
  Treasury is managing to a ceiling, the MTS stops being a backward-looking accounting release and
  starts being a countdown, and this doc's whole "inert" stance would need rewriting.
- **cbo.gov becomes fetchable** — the Monthly Budget Review both corroborates the refund figures and
  publishes days *ahead* of the MTS. Once it can be read on a primary, it should be dated and added to
  this calendar, which would make this print the *second* look at the month rather than the first.
