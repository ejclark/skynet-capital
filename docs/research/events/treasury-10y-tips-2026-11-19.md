## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The 09-17 sibling already researched a 10-Year TIPS reopening of this exact CUSIP, and concluded the
breakeven is the load-bearing signal. Re-running that would be a duplicate. So the question here is
the one that ledger cannot answer: **what does the second reopening carry that the first does not,
and is a more-seasoned auction of an already-tested security a worse auction?** That splits into a
structural question (what is 11-19 *for*, in the calendar's own terms) and an empirical one (does
the seasoning penalty everyone assumes actually exist in eight years of prints).

**One-line verdict:** 11-19 is the **cleaner** of the CUSIP's two reopenings, not the tireder one —
it is the last 10Y real-yield print of 2026 and the only one of the year that prices with no policy
decision inside its window, while the assumed seasoning penalty is **absent from the record** (median
Δ bid-to-cover of +0.01 across 15 pairs). Medium impact, no symbols; this licenses reading, never an
entry.

**30 of 30 reopenings across 15 fully-reopened CUSIPs printed exactly $2B below their own new issue,
and 15 of 15 second reopenings equalled the first.** The cycle is also wider than the proposal
recorded: the 10-Year TIPS series runs **two** new issues a year — January reopened in March and May,
July reopened in September and November — **15 of 15** since 2019, not the "every July new issue is
reopened twice, 3 of 3" a 2023-only window sees.

**4. The seasoning penalty does not exist in the record — SUPPORTED, and it is this doc's central
empirical finding.** The intuitive read is that a second reopening of an eight-month-old security
should clear worse than the first. Across the 15 pairs above, **Δ bid-to-cover has a median of +0.01
and a mean of +0.021, with 8 of 15 positive** — a coin flip, not a penalty. The full observed range
is **−0.29 → +0.64**, so **15 of 15 second reopenings came in no worse than 0.29 below their own
first**, and 13 of 15 no worse than 0.15 below. The one axis that does drift is **primary-dealer
takedown**, which rises from a mean of **12.2%** at first reopenings to **14.3%** at seconds (up in 9
of 15 pairs) — real but small, and indirect share is essentially flat (69.3% → 67.8%). **Why this
matters practically:** it fixes the benchmark. A weak 11-19 print should be judged against the 09-17
result, and only a bid-to-cover more than ~0.30 below it would be outside eight years of precedent.

**5. This is the policy-clean auction of the pair — SUPPORTED, and it inverts which sibling is the
better signal.** The [09-17 reopening](treasury-10y-tips-2026-09-17.md) prices **D+1 to an FOMC
decision** whose modal branch flipped to a hike, which its own ledger states lifts real yields
directly into the stop. 11-19 has no such contamination: it sits **22 days after `fomc-2026-10-28`**,
**20 days before `fomc-2026-12-09`**, and **9 days before `fomc-blackout-start-2026-11-28`**. The only
Fed input in the window is `fomc-minutes-2026-11-18` — a second-order release about a meeting three
weeks past, not a decision. So the September stop measures "supply + inflation expectations + the
Fed"; the November stop measures the first two. For a doc whose signal is the breakeven, that is a
material improvement in the instrument, and it is a calendar fact rather than a forecast.

**6. The session is also a Treasury announcement day, and that is structural — SUPPORTED, measured,
and it corrects a tempting wrong explanation.** Eight securities are announced the morning of
2026-11-19 — `2-Year NOTE`, `5-Year NOTE`, `7-Year NOTE`, `2-Year FRN R`, `52-Week BILL` and the
13/26/6-week bills — the entire Thanksgiving-week block, at ~11:00 ET, **two hours before this

a full bond closure) and **it is not**: reading `announcemt_date` on every 2-Year note since 2019, the
month-end announcement has shared the session with the 10Y TIPS November second reopening in

## Stance & kill switches

**Kill switches:**

- **A 11-19 bid-to-cover more than 0.30 below the 09-17 result** — unprecedented in eight years and
  15 pairs. It would retire this doc's central finding, and the honest reading would then be that
  seasoning *does* bite once the real yield is at multi-year highs, a conditional nobody has tested.
- **A 10-year breakeven below 2.30% into the auction** — the five-week inflation leg unwound, and
  the 09-17 sibling's real-rate framing reclaims the whole move rather than 64% of it.
- **A policy event landing inside 11-16 → 11-20** — an intermeeting action, or a December-FOMC
  repricing sharp enough to dominate the session. Voids the "policy-clean" contrast that is this
  doc's reason for grading 11-19 above 09-17 as a signal.
- **A TIPS size printed at a reopening rather than a new issue** — at 11-12 or anywhere. Never
