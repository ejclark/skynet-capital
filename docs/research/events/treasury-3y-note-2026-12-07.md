## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The December 3-Year note auction (1:00pm ET) opens the December mid-quarter coupon block and stops on a
**Monday, two days before the FOMC decision on 2026-12-09**. Three siblings are already on the shelf —
[September](treasury-3y-note-2026-09-08.md), [October](treasury-3y-note-2026-10-06.md) and
[November](treasury-3y-note-2026-11-09.md) — and between them they have established the reframe (a
3-Year is a **policy-path** auction, not a term-premium one), the base-rate machinery, and two null
results on structure. So the question for this one is narrow: **does stopping two days before a policy
decision change what this auction costs, or what it attracts — and is the December block shape actually
unusual, or does it just look that way?**

**One-line verdict:** the block shape is **ordinary** (7 of 7 December Mondays are same-week-FOMC
Mondays; the December seat measures identical to every other month), and the proximity effect is
**real in the price and unproven in the demand** — a pre-FOMC 3-Year pays roughly **+1.3 to +1.6bp** more
concession in every era cut tested, always with the same sign, never at significance, while the
bid-to-cover comparison flips sign between the long and the modern sample.

2. **"Nothing on Wednesday 12-09" is false as written — REFUTED, and it is the proposal's own claim.**
   The [seeding proposal](../../../src/domain/market-events/proposals/treasury-3y-note-2026-12-07.from-treasury-3y-note-2026-11-09.json)

   day, which is the interesting fact; the calendar is not.

3. **The Monday slot is FOMC-driven in December — SUPPORTED, and it narrows the November sibling rather
   than contradicting it.** That ledger cited "the same PDF puts the December (12-07) and January
   (2027-01-11) 3-Years on Mondays too, so the slot is a scheduling pattern rather than a Veterans-Day
   artifact." True as far as it goes, and it now has a mechanism for the December half. Across the 17
   December 3-Years since 2009:

   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 2011-12-12 · 2012-12-11 | Mon · Tue | 12-13 · 12-12 | D-1 · D-1 | 3.620 · 3.360 |

   *a December Monday implies a same-week FOMC*, not the reverse. **2026 is the Monday/D-2 shape**, and it
   is the modal December shape of the last decade, not an anomaly. The January 2027 Monday (auction
   2027-01-11, FOMC 2027-01-27, **D-16**) is *not* FOMC-driven, which is the honest boundary on how far
   this mechanism generalises.

### What the conditions support

Nothing directional, for the ordinary reasons: `symbols: []`, the date is `estimate`, and no house
playbook is rates-keyed. What the conditions support is a **reading discipline**, four items of it.
**(a)** Do not treat the December block shape as information — legs 3 and 4 measured it to nothing, and
the value of that null is that three siblings' base rates transfer here untouched. **(b)** **Read the

and the reason both forward tests are split across those two legs: leg 6 found a consistently-signed
