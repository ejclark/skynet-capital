## Initial research

### The question, plainly

What is the **2027-05-03** ISM Manufacturing PMI actually about for a paper book holding NVDA MRVL
AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL AMZN — the seventh entry in a chain whose six siblings have measured
this release's reaction function from several directions? And specifically, the two questions this
date raises that no sibling could: **does the May edition differ in kind from its siblings**, and
**is ISM the explanation for the unattributed first-business-day equity gap the
[borrowing-estimates lane](treasury-borrowing-estimates-2027-05-03.md) nominated it for on the day
it proposed this entry?**

**One-line verdict.** It differs in kind — it is the **triple-header**, the one session of the year
that carries ISM, SLOOS and the Treasury borrowing estimates together — and the answer to the
nomination is **no, by construction and by measurement**. Both findings push the same way, and both
are refusals: the May slot's apparent bond edge belongs to a different subset of years than this
one, and the equity gap this print was nominated to own cannot be ISM's because ISM is present on
both sides of the comparison that produced it.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is right and it stays `estimate` — SUPPORTED, and the cadence rule now has a
   mechanical validation this chain never ran.** May 2027 opens on **Saturday 2027-05-01**, so the
   first business day is **Monday 2027-05-03**; it is not a US federal holiday (the nearest is
   Memorial Day, tracked as `memorial-day-market-closure-2027-05-31`) and the NYSE trades a normal
   session. It is not January, so the second-business-day exception does not apply. What is new is
   that leg (b)'s reconstruction lets the rule be *checked* rather than asserted: deriving the
   release day from the trading calendar reproduces **22 of 22** May editions since 2005 and **261
   of 261** across all months, with the NYSE-closure footnote handled automatically. `EST:`
   nevertheless remains the honest prefix — the rule is validated, **this specific date is not
   fetched from ISM**, and promoting it needs a credentialed fetch this lane does not have.
   **The promotion path is dated:** ISM's report text names its successor one edition ahead (the
   02-01 sibling verified the verbatim sentence against a primary), so the **2027-04-01** report,
   carrying March-2027 data, should name 2027-05-03. Registered as
   **FT-ism-manufacturing-2027-05-03-2**, scored 2027-04-02.

2. **This is the triple-header, and it is calendar geometry rather than a coincidence of 2027 —
   SUPPORTED, and it is the structural fact that distinguishes this entry from its six siblings.**
   Three tracked releases land on this one session: **ISM Manufacturing 10:00 ET**,
   [**SLOOS 14:00 ET**](sloos-2027-05-03.md), [**Treasury quarterly borrowing estimates 15:00
   ET**](treasury-borrowing-estimates-2027-05-03.md) — with the [refunding
   statement](treasury-refunding-2027-05-05.md) on 05-05 and [payrolls](jobs-2027-05-07.md) on
   05-07. The mechanism is deterministic. SLOOS is timed to the FOMC meeting and lands the Monday
   after; the borrowing estimates open refunding week on the same Monday (every 2026 edition shared
   its date with SLOOS — 2026-02-02, 05-04, 08-03, 11-02). So when the April/May FOMC decides on a
   **Wednesday** in the last week of April, both land on the following **Monday** — and when May 1
   falls on a Saturday or Sunday, that Monday is also May's first *trading* day, hence ISM's. The
   arithmetic is exact: three trading sessions after a Wednesday is the next Monday, so **"May's ISM
   day sits 3 sessions after the FOMC" and "May's ISM day is the SLOOS / borrowing-estimates Monday"
   are the same statement**. Over 2005–2026 that has happened **four times — 2010-05-03, 2011-05-02,
   2016-05-02, 2021-05-03** — and **2027-05-03 is the fifth**. The practical consequence is a
   refusal: a close-to-close read of that session contains all three releases and cannot be assigned
   to any of them.

3. **The chain's release-day bond finding is half artifact, and this is the correction to bank —
   SUPPORTED, and it is the most transferable result in this document.** The magnitude half
   replicates and now has three independent points: TLT clears its own year's **p78** on
   **36.9%** of the 141 releases 2015 → 2026-09-08 (^TNX **39.0%**, IEF **32.6%**) against
   **21.8% / 21.6% / 22.0%** on the other 2,796 sessions — where the [12-01
   sibling](ism-manufacturing-2026-12-01.md) reported **40.7%** on n = 118 and the [02-01
   sibling](ism-manufacturing-2027-02-01.md) **37.9%** on n = 132. The three runs agree
   qualitatively and disagree on n, and the honest read is a **range of 36.9–40.7%**, not a number.
   **The directional half does not survive.** Decomposing the release day into its overnight gap
   (previous raw close → open) and its intraday leg (open → raw close):

   | Window | TLT overnight gap | vs other sessions | TLT intraday | vs other sessions |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 2015 → 2026-09-08 (n = 141) | **−0.283%** | −0.005% (**p < 0.001**) | +0.098% | +0.003% (p = 0.087) |
   | 2005 → 2026-09-08 (n = 260) | **−0.299%** | +0.003% (**p < 0.001**) | +0.070% | +0.010% (p = 0.146) |

   A statistically overwhelming bond move that happens **hours before a 10:00 ET print** is not
   caused by the print. It is TLT's distribution: the fund's ex-date falls at the start of the
   month, and this session measured the size independently as the adjusted-minus-raw return drift —
   **0.233% per month** (2.79% annualised, n = 2,937) — which accounts for roughly four-fifths of
   the −0.283%, the remainder inside noise. This confirms and quantifies **FT-38**'s warning that
   raw-close sign studies of this release measure TLT's own first-business-day distribution, and
   **extends it**: the contamination reaches gap and open-to-close decompositions too, not only
   sign studies. The equity side is clean and null on both legs — SPY gap **+0.075%** vs +0.028%
   (p = 0.431), intraday **+0.065%** vs +0.019% (p = 0.515).

4. **ISM does not own the borrowing-estimate lane's unexplained first-business-day equity gap —
   REFUTED, by construction and then by measurement.** The [proposing
   ledger](treasury-borrowing-estimates-2027-05-03.md) measured that on the **17** borrowing-estimate

   +0.478%**, against **+0.092%** on the 120 first-business-days that were not release days, and

   business day of every month**, so it is present on essentially **100%** of that treatment arm —
   the borrowing estimates run on the February/May/August/November cycle and never in January — and
   on roughly **91%** of the control arm, the only exceptions being the ~11 Januaries where ISM
   moves to the second business day. A variable that is present in both arms cannot generate the
   difference between them. Two measurements confirm the structural point rather than resting on it:

   | Test (ISM held constant across the split) | Window | Treatment | Control | perm p |
