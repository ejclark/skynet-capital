## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This event reached the calendar as **one proposal and no canonical file**:
`proposals/uk-quarterly-national-accounts-2026-09-30.from-uk-public-sector-finances-2026-09-22.json`,
filed 2026-09-09 by the UK borrowing lane during its own initial research. It was read in full before
anything here was written, and the canonical
`src/domain/market-events/uk-quarterly-national-accounts-2026-09-30.json` is written in this PR. The
proposal makes one claim, and it is a claim about a *different* event:

> *"it is the exact 2026 analogue of a document the OBR is on the record as having EXCLUDED in 2024, and
> it is therefore the cleanest available test of when the 2026-10-28 Budget's forecast actually closes."*

So the question this document has to answer is two questions. **Is this release anything to this book on
its own terms?** And **is it the clean test the proposing lane registered it as (FT-uk-public-sector-
finances-2026-09-22-4)?**

**One-line verdict:** on its own terms it is a **measured non-event twice over** — the tape has never
moved on a QNA release in ten years, and this year's news went public **41 days early** — and as a test
of the Budget's forecast close it is **narrower than registered**, because the 2024 precedent has a
**third outcome** the proposal's binary did not anticipate: excluded from the forecast, but assessed in a
published box and judged immaterial.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md), plus a

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The date rests on two publisher primaries and a ten-year cadence with no exception.**
**SUPPORTED.** `ons.gov.uk/releases/gdpquarterlynationalaccountsukapriltojune2026` reads *"GDP quarterly
national accounts, UK: April to June 2026"*, *"30 September 2026 at 7:00am"*, *"This release is not yet
published"*, with the accredited-official-statistics kitemark. Independently, the **live first-estimate
bulletin** (Q2 2026, published 2026-08-13) names its own *"Next release: 30 September 2026"* — a
different publisher page, not a restatement. Cadence, scraped direct across four pages of the ONS's own
past-release listing: **40** consecutive editions from **2016-09-30** to **2026-06-30**, and all **10**
September editions fall on the **last working day of September** — the 30th when it is a weekday
(2016, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2024, 2025), the Friday before when it is not (**2017-09-29**,
**2018-09-28**, **2023-09-29**). **2026-09-30 is a Wednesday.** The `estimate` label is the taxonomy gap
and the no-self-confirm limit, not a doubt.

**Correction to the proposal, on the record.** The proposing lane's source note reads *"the Q2 edition
lands on the last day of September every year in the listing — 30 September 2025, 30 September 2024,
**30 September 2023**"*. The third is wrong: the ONS listing shows **29 September 2023** (30 September
2023 was a Saturday). The rule is the last **working** day, and the corrected rule is what makes
2026-09-30 safe rather than the uncorrected one — this is a strengthening correction, not a weakening
one, and it is recorded because a cadence claim nobody checks is how a wrong date survives.

**2. The news this release carries went public 41 days before it prints.** **SUPPORTED**, publisher
primary, and it is this document's first substantive finding. `ons.gov.uk/economy/grossdomesticproduct
gdp/articles/bluebook2026impactongdpandmaincomponents/2026-08-20`, published **20 August 2026**, sets out
the Blue Book 2026 revisions in full for **1997–2024**: nominal GDP for 2024 revised **up 0.5%** to
**£2,905bn**; real 2024 annual growth **+0.1pp to 1.1%**; 2023 real growth **+0.1pp to 0.4%**; 2023
nominal growth **+0.2pp to 6.8%**; cumulative real growth 1997–2024 now **61.7%**, up from **59.2%**;
average annual growth 1998–2024 **unchanged at 1.8%**. The article states plainly: *"The next quarterly
national accounts release on 30 September 2026 will incorporate these improvements into our official
estimates,"* with **2024** becoming the new fully-balanced baseline year under the supply-and-use-tables
framework for the first time. So the **level and history** revisions are already in the public record;
what **09-30** adds is their **carry-through into 2025 and H1 2026**, plus the ordinary second estimate of
Q2 2026. That is a materially thinner payload than "the Blue Book lands on 09-30" implies, and it is the
reason the tier here is argued rather than inherited.

### Honest limits

**First, the Budget finding is a search relay and the governing document was never read.**
`obr.uk/box/blue-book-2024-revisions/` returned **403** (recorded in `probe-ref.blocked`), so Box 2.2's
wording, the 2024-09-26 close date and the *"broadly unchanged"* conclusion are all relayed through
search of the OBR's own published material. Leg 3 is graded **MIXED** and registered as **FT-…-3** for
exactly that reason. The **autumn 2026** timetable — the only one that actually governs 2026-10-28 — was
never obtained by this lane or by any of the four before it.

**Second, "the news is already public" is an argument about content, not a guarantee about price.** Leg 2
establishes that the aggregate revisions were published 41 days early. It does **not** establish that
market participants read them, nor that the carry-through into 2025/H1-2026 quarters is uninteresting.
It is offered as the mechanism *behind* the measured null in leg 4, not as a substitute for it.

**Third, every market number here is a daily close-to-close move.** IGLT.L stops trading at 16:30 London,
so a 07:00 release's signature is diluted by a full session of everything else; IGLT.L is also an
**all-maturity** gilt index and under-weights the long end where UK fiscal stress shows. `GBPUSD=X` has
only **28** usable QNA bars against IGLT.L's 40 and only **5** September ones, its daily-close timing is
not dependable, and no weight is placed on it — the September GBP/USD result (**0.42x**, t = -2.91) is
reported nowhere in the call sheet for that reason.

**Fourth, the September subset is n = 10 and its headline ratio is not significant either way.** 1.18x at
t = 0.47 is a null, not evidence of elevation; the ex-2022 figure of 0.85x is a null too. Ten prints
cannot distinguish "the release is a non-event" from "ten quiet Septembers." The **40**-day full-sample
result (leg 4) is the one carrying the weight, and it is what **FT-…-2** tests.

**Fifth, the quarter-end/December identification in leg 6 is imperfect by construction.** Every QNA-free
