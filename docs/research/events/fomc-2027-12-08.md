## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This entry did not arrive with an open question. It arrived with a **commission**. The proposal that
created it — filed by [`fomc-2027-10-26`](fomc-2027-10-26.md)'s initial research, which is also the
meeting that will *confirm* this date — put the instruction in its own `notes` field:

> December carries the HIGHEST monthly mean absolute S&P decision-day move in the table, 1.265% at
> n=19 against 0.925% across all 156 decision days 2007-2026, and unlike September's and October's
> cells that one has not been shown to be a mixture or a bin-split artifact — **whoever researches
> this id should test it before quoting it, because the two cells this family has tested both
> failed.**

So the question is exactly one question: **is December's 1.265% a property of the December meeting,
or is it, like the two cells before it, an artifact of how the sample was cut?**

**One-line verdict.** It is an artifact, it is the third failure in three attempts, and it fails
*differently* from the first two in a way that generalises past December: September's cell was a
**mixture** and October's was a **bin split** — both objections to how one cell was assembled.
December's cell is assembled correctly. It fails because **it was selected for being large**, and
once that selection is priced, **no cell in the table is distinguishable from noise** — including
August's, which is the cell that is actually the largest. The table should be retired as a source of
per-month base rates, not corrected a fourth time.
