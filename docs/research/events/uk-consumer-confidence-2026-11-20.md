### Leg 2 — October's −24 had no named driver — **SUPPORTED**

October 2026: **−24, down 11**, fieldwork Oct 1–14, n=2,003; every measure fell (general economy next-12m **−36**, major purchases **−22**, savings **+28** outside the headline). Neil Bellamy: "no single major economic event driving the decline", weakness "across the full spectrum of income, age and gender", cost of living and "unresolved regional conflicts" feeding inflation of 3.5%. Re-fetched direct today: no Budget or tax wording (the page carries a "Budget support" link only in its related-content list). The collapse is therefore not attributable to the Budget by NIQ, which cuts both ways: it cannot be expected to reverse *because* the Budget passed, and it has no named cause to persist for either.

### Leg 3 — the Budget was received as priced in — **MIXED**

Secondary (Trading Economics, investinglive, dated 2026-10-28 to 10-30; the Budget ledger's close-out read the gov.uk primaries): Chancellor John Healey kept the fiscal rules, ruled out broad-based tax rises, raised taxes on banks, gambling, savings and landlords, and added energy-bill cushioning; OBR headroom about **£15bn** (against £23.6bn in March; EY had warned of £11bn pre-Budget); OBR cut 2027 growth to **1.0%** from 1.6% citing the Iran war; 10y gilt **5.34%**, **−1.4bp**, lowest since September. Pre-Budget, press expected tax rises and consumer gloom was building (City AM and BRC–Opinium, October). No post-Budget household survey was found, so reception by *households* is inferred from markets and press, not measured. **Mixed:** relief is plausible, unproven.

### Leg 4 — post-Budget editions have risen, n=2 — **MIXED**

| Edition | Change | NIQ's stated reason |
|---|---|---|
| Nov 2024 (window opens on Budget day) | **−21 → −18** (+3) | Consumers "moved past" the Budget and the US election; Major Purchase Index +5 ahead of Black Friday |
| Dec 2025 (Dec 1–11; Budget Nov 26) | **−19 → −17** (+2) | Relief that the Budget was less damaging than expected |
| Nov 2025 (pre-Budget, Oct 30–Nov 14) | −17 → −19 (−2) | "Bracing for difficult news" — all five measures down |

The 2025 pair is a clean before/after: pre-Budget down two, post-Budget up two. The 2024 edition overlaps the Budget day itself. A third, non-Budget analogue: Nov 2023 rose **+6** to −24 from about −30 (implied by NIQ's release), a bounce after a trough. Three observations, all up; none was a −11 shock, and the cause named each time was the Budget, which October 2026's release did not name.

### Leg 5 — headwinds inside the window — **MIXED**

For a further fall: the BoE decision on Nov 5 (a 25bp hike to 4.00% priced at roughly 85–100% in the sibling ledgers; Rabobank, secondary, expects 7–2) lands mid-window, UK CPI is **3.5%** (published 2026-10-21), and the Iran-war energy backdrop is unresolved. For a rebound: October's fall was the largest in a decade with no named cause, savings sentiment is **+28**, and the series rises in 61% of months. Net: a lean, not a call.
