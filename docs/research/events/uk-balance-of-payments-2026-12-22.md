## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This event reached the calendar as **one proposal and no canonical file** —
[`from-uk-public-sector-finances-2026-12-22`](uk-public-sector-finances-2026-12-22.md), filed
2026-09-09 — and was read in full before anything here was written. Its central contribution is that
07:00 on 2026-12-22 is a five-release ONS stack, and it ranks the members:

> *"the quarterly current account — the UK's external financing requirement, the clearest sterling
> channel of the five"*

…and, in the ledger that filed it: *"a 07:00 gilt or sterling move on 2026-12-22 belongs to GDP or the
current account first and to a monthly borrowing print last."*

**The question this lane owes the calendar is the one the proposal could not ask about itself:** is
the current account actually the sterling-relevant member of that stack, and how would anyone know?

**One-line verdict:** the ranking is **untestable on this calendar and directionally unsupported by
what evidence exists** — ONS balance-of-payments and quarterly-national-accounts releases are
**perfectly collinear** (47 of 47 dates), so a release-day study cannot ever separate them, and on
those days sterling is *quieter* than an ordinary session, not more active. A second finding falls out
of the same sourcing: this print is **not** a re-benchmarking print, because the annual benchmark
lands at the September edition.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md), plus a
first-party event study against this repo's own market-data layer
(`scripts/research/market-data.mjs`, split/dividend-adjusted daily bars). Primaries fetched direct

### Honest limits

**First, the central finding is a negative one, and negatives are cheap to overstate.** Leg 2
establishes that BoP and QNA have never been published apart in 47 tries; it does **not** establish
that they never will be, and a single future exception would begin to identify them. What leg 3
concludes from it is deliberately narrow — that *no release-day study on the data that exists* can
apportion between them — not that the current account is irrelevant to sterling. A tick-level study of
the 07:00–07:05 window, which this repo has no data for, would be a different instrument and might
answer a different question.

**Second, leg 5 is a failed identification reported as one.** The Pink Book lever is confounded by the
Blue Book on the same day, by month-end, by n = 10, and in 2026 by a Budget two days earlier. Its
numbers are in this document to close a door, not to open one, and nothing downstream leans on them.

**Third, leg 6's slice is n = 12 against a control of n = 39.** Neither is a sample from which a
distribution should be estimated, and the claim made from it is correspondingly weak: the gilt quiet
*survives directionally* under the corridor control, which is a statement about a point estimate
moving from 0.69x to 0.65x, not about significance. The **±0.80%** threshold in FT-…-4 is the
**full-sample p90** (2,973 sessions), never those twelve.

**Fourth, leg 7 corrects a plausible assumption using two years of precedent and one verbatim
sentence.** The 2025-09-30 quotation is decisive for 2025. The 2026 analogue has not published yet, so
FT-…-2 is graded **medium** rather than high, and its kill switch is written so the December bulletin
itself adjudicates.

**Fifth, the market study's limits are the sibling's, inherited honestly.** IGLT.L is an
**all-maturity** gilt index, so it under-weights the long end where UK external-financing stress would
show; it stops trading at 16:30 London, so every figure above is a **daily close-to-close** move in
which a 07:00 release's signature is diluted by a full session — and on this particular morning by
four other releases. `GBPUSD=X` is a Yahoo spot series with **35** usable BoP bars against IGLT.L's 47
and unreliable close timing; its rows carry less weight than their t-statistics suggest, which matters
because sterling is the series the headline claim is about.

**Sixth, no consensus or whisper was findable** for the Q3 2026 current account at D-103, and none is
likely to become findable: the UK current account is not a surveyed consensus print in the way CPI or
payrolls are. Leg 10 is a **null claim**, and its failure mode is a channel that exists and is simply
not instrumented in this book.

## Stance & kill switches

Pink Book 2026-consistent re-benchmark and it is **twenty days away**; **2026-12-22** is the second

printed.

in the same minute it reported a **£5.0bn** flow change. A print whose history moves further than its
news cannot be positioned into. That is the durable argument for `low`, and it survives any change in
time zone, tier or calendar crowding.
