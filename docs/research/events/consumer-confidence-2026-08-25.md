10:00 ET, six days out and one day before the compound-risk NVDA/PCE/GDP session. Confidence has
been sliding for three straight months (May 90.6 → June 92.2-revised → July 90.8, a miss vs. a

exposure and to the broader recession-odds narrative feeding the Sep-16 FOMC, not a standalone

## Initial research

**The question.** What is the consensus expectation for the August 2026 Conference Board Consumer
Confidence reading, what does the July print and the recent trend say, how has the market typically
reacted to this print in each direction, and which tracked names carry the most sensitivity?

**One-line verdict:** confidence is on a real but gradual three-month slide driven by a cooling
labor read (not a fresh shock), no August street consensus exists yet at D-6, this print
historically moves markets meaningfully less than CPI/jobs/FOMC, and among the nine tracked names
only AMZN and AAPL carry direct consumer-facing exposure — the rest feel it only through the same
rate-path channel every macro print shares.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md)
(macro-print mode: no price instruments). Primary source is conference-board.org (checked
2026-08-18/19); press (Yahoo Finance, PRNewswire, Advisor Perspectives/dshort, InvestingLive, qz.com,
CNBC) used for reaction narration and revision history, each claim dated. One aggregator
(fxstreet.com) surfaced a stale 2024 article under a live-looking URL when queried for June
figures — the exact aggregator-staleness trap the CPI doc's Dec-CPI catch warned about; that figure
was discarded and the Conference Board / PRNewswire-sourced June number used instead.

**Conviction legs, tested:**

1. **The date and time are right — SUPPORTED.** Multiple independent aggregations (fxstreet.com,
   investing.com economic calendar, forexfactory.com) converge on "Tuesday, August 25, 2026, 10:00
   AM ET" for the August release, matching the checked-in calendar entry exactly; the Conference
   Board's own press page (conference-board.org/topics/consumer-confidence/press, fetched
   2026-08-19) confirms the July 28 release cadence this projects from (last-Tuesday-of-month
   pattern). No primary-source calendar page with the explicit August 25 line item was directly
   fetchable (it sits behind Data Central), so this leg rests on convergent secondary aggregation
   of the CB's own schedule rather than a single fetched primary line — noted as a limit, not
   treated as unconfirmed.

2. **Recent trend is a real, gradual slide — SUPPORTED.** May: 90.6 (revised down, "edged down in
   May"). June: initially reported +0.6 to 91.2, later revised up to 92.2. July (released
   2026-07-28): fell 1.4 pts to **90.8**, missing a ~92.3–92.4 consensus (InvestingLive,
   PRNewswire, both 2026-07-28) — the third straight monthly decline in the Present Situation
   Index specifically (down 3.6 pts to 114.9). The Expectations Index has sat at/below the
   Conference-Board-flagged **80.0 recession-signal threshold since February 2025** — six straight
   months — and held flat at 74.7 in July. Dana Peterson (CB chief economist, quoted in the July
   release) frames it as "a general downward sloping trajectory since late 2021," i.e., not a
   discrete shock.

3. **The driver is labor cooling, not a fresh price shock — SUPPORTED, with a live caveat.** July's
   "jobs plentiful minus jobs hard to get" differential fell to +3.1%, the weakest since February
   2021 (Detroit News/Jefferson City News-Tribune reporting the CB release, 2026-07-29) — consistent
   with the July jobs-report miss already logged in the [`CPI doc`](cpi-2026-09-11.md) and
   [`FOMC doc`](fomc-2026-09-16.md). Separately, the July release noted 12-month inflation
   expectations "fell back to their prewar rate" — but that survey window closed *before* the
   renewed Hormuz tanker attacks and failed ceasefire reported mid-August (RBOB ~+15%/wk into
   2026-08-17, per the sibling CPI doc). The August survey window is the first to fully overlap the
   re-escalated energy shock, so a reversal in the inflation-expectations subcomponent specifically
   is a live open question this print will answer, not something already known.

4. **A different, more-cited survey (UMich) already flagged a sharper August drop —
   MIXED/CAUTION.** The University of Michigan's *Consumer Sentiment* index (a related but
   distinct survey, run separately from the Conference Board's) fell to a preliminary 51 in early
   August from 55.2 in July, below a ~54.5 Reuters/Bloomberg consensus (Yahoo Finance/TradingEconomics,
   fetched 2026-08-19) — a broad-based deterioration concentrated among lower-income and
   less-educated respondents. This is genuinely informative as a same-window read-through (both
   surveys sample the same broad consumer mood), but the two indices diverge often enough in level
   and magnitude that UMich's plunge should not be read as a preview of the CB number — flagged as
   directional color, not a CB estimate.

5. **This print moves markets less than CPI/jobs/FOMC — SUPPORTED.** Cross-indicator research
   converges: employment reports draw the largest trader reaction of the major releases, followed
   by retail sales; Consumer Confidence/Sentiment-type releases generate comparatively muted
   immediate reaction and matter more as a leading-indicator/trend signal than a single-print
   volatility event (CME Group research summary; multiple 2026 recaps found no notable equity
   volatility tied to the June or July CB releases specifically). The one 2026 counterexample found
   was the UMich *March* sentiment collapse (53.3, well below the preliminary read), which did
   coincide with a broad equity sell-off and a yields spike on "stagflationary" framing — evidence
   that an outsized, surprising miss *can* move tape, just that the June/July prints (both misses,
   both muted) show that's the exception, not the rule.

6. **Tracked-name sensitivity — ranked, with mechanism.** `symbols: []` on this event — it is
   market-wide, and the calendar's own note already flags the expectations + inflation-expectations
   subcomponents as the parts rate desks watch. Within the nine-name roster (NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV
   MSFT GOOG META AAPL AMZN), only two carry *direct* consumer-facing exposure: **AAPL** (device
   demand is discretionary spend, and this print lands 15 days before the rumored Sep 9 iPhone 18
   event/pricing-increase catalyst already on the calendar) and **AMZN** (e-commerce volumes track
   discretionary spending directly, and AMZN eats fuel/logistics cost on the same energy shock
   pressuring the confidence read). The other seven — the AI-infra/duration names — feel this print
   only through the same rate-path/recession-odds channel every macro print shares (a weak
   confidence read supports the "labor is cracking → cuts sooner" read that has been *bullish* for
   long-duration names on recent misses per the CPI doc's reaction-function evidence), not through
   any consumer-facing mechanism of their own.

**What plays the conditions support.** Nothing directional, and less urgency than the CPI/FOMC

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date confirmed, aggregator-convergence checked 2026-08-19).** Treat 2026-08-25 10:00 ET
as a medium-impact, second-tier macro print — real regime information, not a trading event. No new
AMZN/AAPL discretionary-adjacent entries sized around the release; no directional macro position on
the print itself. Base case (estimate-labeled — no street consensus found, this is trend
extrapolation only): a print in the high-80s/low-90s continuing the three-month slide, driven by the
same labor-cooling read as July, with the inflation-expectations subcomponent as the genuine
open question given the energy shock re-escalation since the July survey closed.

**Kill switches:**

- **A sharp Expectations-Index break below ~70** (accelerating away from the 80-level threshold
  it's held at/under for six months) kills the "gradual drift" framing and elevates this from
  regime-context to a recession-odds signal worth a dedicated reassessment ahead of schedule.
- **Two consecutive prints (Aug + Sep) back above ~92–93** kills the "consumer is cracking"
  caution overlay on AMZN/AAPL.
- **Energy de-escalation** (the CPI doc's own kill switch — a holding ceasefire, Hormuz reopening)
  kills the concern that the August survey catches a fresh inflation-expectations spike; a
  continuation or renewed re-shock instead would be the leg to watch hardest in this print.
- **A published August consensus emerging before 8/25** resolves this doc's biggest honest limit —
  re-run the "surprise" framing against it rather than the trend extrapolation above.
