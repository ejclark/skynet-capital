## At a glance

correction is the finding.** The id says `nsa` because the discovering sweep read the MTIS
announcement's "not adjusted estimates and corresponding adjusted estimates … September 28, 2026 and

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This id reached the calendar from the `mtis-2026-12-16` lane's adjacency sweep, which read one
sentence on Census's MTIS page and filed the nearest untracked dated item it found. That sentence
carries a "respectively" doing a lot of work. **What actually publishes on 2026-09-28 — and does any
of it reach a number this book trades?**

**One-line verdict:** the proposal's date is right and its *description* is wrong — 09-28 is the
retail benchmark revision entire, not the not-adjusted half of a two-step — and nothing about it is
tradeable, because a restatement of history has no consensus to surprise against and its session
slot measures indistinguishable from an ordinary day.

### Method

Macro-print mode per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md) — `symbols: []`, so
neither `earnings-cycle.mjs` nor `intraday-edges.mjs` applies and the cache-busting rule has no
target. Everything below was fetched direct on 2026-09-06 by this session; nothing is carried from a
sibling ledger except where the text says whose it is.

1. **Three Census program pages** — `census.gov/retail/index.html` (HTTP 200, 743,183 bytes),
   `census.gov/mtis/index.html` (HTTP 200, 684,348 bytes), `census.gov/wholesale/index.html`
   (HTTP 200, 527,648 bytes), each parsed for its announcements block. These three are the whole of
   Leg 1, and they are what disagree.
2. **Seven Census release calendars** — `economic-indicators/calendar-listview.html` (2026, 179
   rows) plus `calendar-listview-2020…2025.html` (145/165/165/165/165/151 rows), **1,135 rows**,
   each row regex-parsed for `revis|benchmark|annual integrated`.
3. **`census.gov/programs-surveys/aies.html`** (HTTP 200, 325,372 bytes) — the survey's own
   description and its news list, for the upstream release dates.
4. **FRED `RSAFSNA`** — retail sales NSA, 414 monthly observations 1992-01 → 2026-07, aggregated to
   annual totals to size the restated window.
5. **Yahoo daily bars, SPY and `^VIX`** — **4,189 SPY sessions, 2010-01-04 → 2026-08-28**, with a

6. **This repo** — `assessment-cadence.json`, all 200-odd `src/domain/market-events/*.json`, and a

`blocked` array, and the consequence is stated in Leg 4 rather than papered over.

### Leg 1 — "2026-09-28 is the NOT-ADJUSTED half of a two-step revision" · **REFUTED**

The sentence that named this event, on `census.gov/mtis/index.html`, verbatim:

> Intention to Revise - Monthly retail and monthly wholesale estimates are anticipated to be revised
> based on historical corrections and the results of the 2023 and 2024 Annual Integrated Economic
> Survey. Revised not adjusted estimates and corresponding adjusted estimates are tentatively
> scheduled for release on September 28, 2026 and October 26, 2026, respectively.

Read alone, "respectively" plausibly maps *not adjusted → 09-28* and *adjusted → 10-26*. That is the
reading the sweep took, and it is where the `-nsa-` in this id comes from. Each program's own page
says otherwise, in the program's own words:

| Page | Scope of the sentence | Estimate types | Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| `census.gov/retail` | "Monthly **retail** estimates" | "Revised not adjusted estimates **and corresponding adjusted estimates**" | **September 28, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. EDT** |
| `census.gov/wholesale` | "Monthly **Wholesale** sales, inventories, and inventories/sales ratios" | "Revised not adjusted estimates **and corresponding adjusted estimates**" | **October 26, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. EDT** |

Both program pages give **one** date for **both** estimate types. The only reading consistent with
all three pages is that the MTIS sentence compresses the two program announcements into one, and its
"respectively" maps to the *retail and wholesale* of its own first sentence. **09-28 is retail,
entire. 10-26 is wholesale, entire.** They are two program halves of one AIES restatement, not two
sequential steps, and the pair is not a duplicate to be collapsed.

The consequence for this calendar is small but exact: the seasonally adjusted retail history — the
basis every month-over-month retail comparison runs on — moves on **09-28**, not four weeks later.
Registered as `-1`, scoreable the day after the release.

### Leg 2 — "the date is soft" · **SUPPORTED**, and now measured rather than asserted

Three independent grounds, in descending strength:

1. **Census's own word is "tentatively"** — identically on all three pages.
2. **The date is absent from Census's release schedule.** The 2026 `calendar-listview.html` has 179
   rows; September runs **09-25 → 09-30** and October runs **10-20 → 10-27**. Neither **09-28** nor
   **10-26** appears. Both are Mondays with no scheduled Census release at all.
3. **And that absence is the norm, not a signal.** Across all seven schedules 2020–2026 — **1,135
   rows** — **zero** carry `revis`, `benchmark` or `annual integrated` in any field. Census's
   Economic Indicator Release Schedule has never listed a benchmark revision in the archive this
   lane can reach.

Point 3 cuts both ways and both ways matter. It removes "not on the calendar" as evidence the date
will slip — it is simply how this class is published. It also means **no calendar-scraping lane can
ever discover an event like this**: it exists only in a program page's announcements block, which is
why it took an adjacency sweep to find, and why the two sweeps that found it produced two ids.

What *does* support the date: the upstream inputs both exist and both landed on time. AIES "replaced
and integrated seven annual business surveys into one survey" (`aies.html`), and its news list dates
the 2023 main release **2026-02-26** and the 2024 release **2026-09-03** — three days before this
assessment, seven weeks ahead of the announced 09-28. The material was in hand.

Status therefore stays **`estimate`**, on Census's hedge and the schedule absence — not merely on
this lane's no-self-confirm rule, which would have been the weaker reason.

### Leg 3 — "this restates a lot" · **SUPPORTED**, sized from the published series

The window Census names is two survey years, not the usual one. From `RSAFSNA`, NSA retail and food
services sales:

| 2022 | $7,873.5B | prior basis, for scale |

About **$16.5 trillion** of retail history across **24 reference months**, on a survey instrument
that did not exist in its current form for the prior benchmark. That is the honest size statement
this document can make: the *width* of the restated window, not the *depth* of the revisions in it.

### Leg 4 — "how big are the revisions?" · **UNMEASURABLE FROM HERE**, and not borrowed

The wholesale sibling answered exactly this question for its own series by walking 255 ALFRED
vintages. That route is closed this session: ALFRED returned 404 on three URL shapes and timed out
three times at 120 seconds on a fourth, and FRED's CSV endpoint accepts `vintage_date` and returns
the current vintage regardless (byte-identical, 7,495 bytes). All of it is in `blocked`.

So this ledger states the gap instead of filling it. The wholesale ledger's numbers — 12 revisions
since 2014, a median 172 months restated at a median 0.126% each — describe **Monthly Wholesale
Trade**, a different survey with a different sample and a different benchmark history. Quoting them
as a retail expectation would be exactly the kind of unlabelled substitution the honesty rules
forbid. The next pulse should retry ALFRED on `RSAFSNA` / `RETAILIRSA`; if it answers, the magnitude
table belongs in that row.

One thing the gap does **not** block, because it can be pinned prospectively rather than
historically: whether the restatement reaches today's level at all. `RSAFSNA` for **2026-07** reads
**$784,610M** on this session's FRED pull. If a benchmark keyed to 2023–2024 carries forward, that
number moves; if it is confined to the survey years, it does not. Registered as `-2`, scoreable
2026-10-16 by re-pulling the same series.

### Leg 5 — "the session is worth watching" · **REFUTED**

2026-09-28 is a Monday, two sessions before quarter-end, with **zero** scheduled Census releases and
one low-impact tracked event (`dallas-fed-mfg-2026-09-28`). Testing the slot rather than the story,
over 4,189 SPY sessions since 2010:

| Class | n | Median session range |
|---|---|---|
| Quarter-end **minus 2 sessions** | 66 | **0.910%** |
| Every other session | 4,123 | **0.904%** |

A permutation test on the median difference over 20,000 iterations gives **p = 0.942**. The slot is
indistinguishable from an ordinary day. The September-only subset (n=16, the 09-26-to-09-28 Mondays
and Fridays of 2010–2025) runs a 0.975% median — higher, but on sixteen observations that is noise,
and it is quoted here only so a future reader does not rediscover it as a signal.

What *is* in the corridor is everything behind 09-28, not on it: the **09-30** government funding
deadline and PCE, **10-01** ISM manufacturing, **10-02** jobs — four high-impact tracked events in
five days — plus quarter-end and the S&P select-sector secondary reweight. Any width on 09-28 is
positioning for those. Registered as `-3` in the quiet direction, with its base rate stated: SPY's
range has been ≤1.5% on **80.0%** of sessions since 2010 and **85.9%** since 2024.

### Leg 6 — "it feeds something this book trades" · **MIXED**, and this is the one live thread

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date is `estimate`).** Stand aside on 2026-09-28, and hold three frames.

**On what this is:** a **basis change, not a print** — a restatement of 2023–2024 retail history with
no consensus, no whisper, no new reference month and no direction to be surprised in. The refusal is
structural rather than statistical: the surprise-vs-expectation apparatus every other macro-print
ledger here uses has nothing to attach to.

**On what it actually covers:** the retail programme entire — not-adjusted *and* seasonally adjusted,
both on 09-28 — which contradicts the reading that produced this event's id. The `-nsa-` token is a
misnomer kept only because it is the branch, ledger and tracking-issue key; the title carries the
truth and `-1` scores it. Its sibling `mwts-benchmark-revision-2026-10-26` is the wholesale half in
full, on the same terms.

**On the corridor:** 09-28 is an empty Monday standing in front of the loudest week of the quarter.
The slot is measured inert (0.910% vs 0.904%, n=66, p=0.942); the four high-impact events behind it
are not. Any width that day belongs to them.

**One thing this lane surfaced and cannot fix.** The `mwts-benchmark-revision-2026-10-26` lane
proposed this same release as `retail-benchmark-revision-2026-09-28` (impact `medium`) in PR #1768.
Both sweeps were right; they simply named the same thing differently, an hour apart. From this merge
the calendar carries one Census release under two ids, and the newer one will draw its own
`never-assessed` research lane and its own tracking issue. This lane may neither delete another
lane's proposal nor write another id's canonical file, so it is flagged rather than absorbed. The
recommended direction is to retire `retail-benchmark-revision-2026-09-28`: its slug is the more
accurate of the two, but this id already carries the ledger, the forward tests and the tracking
issue, and a slug is not worth breaking those links for.

**Kill switches** — any one of these ends the stance above and earns a ledger row:

- **The 09-28 release publishes not-adjusted retail estimates only,** with the adjusted deferred.
  That would vindicate the MTIS sentence's "respectively" reading, make this id's `-nsa-` correct,
  and mean the seasonally adjusted retail basis does not move until 10-26. Scored as `-1`.
- **The restatement carries forward into 2026 months** — `RSAFSNA` 2026-07 moving off **$784,610M**.
  Then "a revision to history" understates it, and every retail level comparison spanning 09-28 is
  comparing two series rather than two months. Scored as `-2`.
- **The 2026-10-15 08:30 advance retail sales report publishes revised pre-2025 history.** The
  high-impact print of that morning would then be carrying the benchmark directly, which is the one
  route by which this event reaches a number the book actually watches.
- **Census re-dates or withdraws the intention-to-revise notice on `census.gov/retail`.** The event
  becomes undated, and an undated event is not assessable on a cadence.
- **Census puts 2026-09-28 on `economic-indicators/calendar-listview.html`.** Unprecedented in 1,135
  rows across seven years; it would be the cleanest route to a `CENSUS:`-confirmed status flip, which
  this lane may not perform itself.
- **`retail-benchmark-revision-2026-09-28` is promoted to a canonical file by any lane.** The
  duplicate stops being a proposal-level annoyance and becomes two permanent research lanes on one
  release.
- **A macro-keyed house playbook lands in `docs/plans/trade-playbooks.md` before 2026-09-28.** The
  stand-aside is partly an absence-of-instrument argument; a macro-keyed playbook makes it live.
