## At a glance

**TL;DR.** This is the **long end of the sell-off, reopened** — the Sep-10 sale reopens the Aug-13
30-year bond that cleared at **5.216%, its highest auction yield in ~25 years (since 2001)** and
**tailed** (stopped +0.4bp above when-issued) on a below-average bid-to-cover of 2.39 with dealers
absorbing more than their 12-month norm. Unlike the 10Y (which is well-bid), the 30Y is exactly
where the **term-premium/fiscal weakness concentrates** — the 30Y yield hit a fresh **19-year high
~5.33% intraday on 8/18**. This auction is the day *after* the 10Y reopening (9/9) and the day
*before* August CPI (9/11): the middle of a three-day compound-rate corridor. A soft print
extending the tailing pattern is the base-case risk, and it hits high-duration, debt-financed AI
names first. Auction **date confirmed**; every size/when-issued/demand figure below is
**estimate/press-sourced** and drifts. No symbol-keyed action — guard-shaped, no-new-duration-risk.

**Signals & conditions** — the buy/sell/hold triggers:

- **Weak print** (bid-to-cover materially below ~2.39, a positive tail wider than Aug-13's +0.4bp,
  or indirect share falling below the mid-60s) — extends the tailing long-end pattern (Aug-13 was
  the third tailed long-end auction in a row per the TIPS sibling doc); validates the pre-auction
  duration selloff, caution widens on high-duration names into the 9/11 CPI.

  ~68%) — near-term relief for the long-end fear, but does **not** resolve the structural fiscal/

## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** the 30-year bond reopening (1:00pm ET, confirmed) is the single sharpest
expression of the long-end term-premium sell-off — 30Y yields at a 19-year high — reopening the
Aug-13 issue that already cleared at a 25-year-high yield and tailed. It sits the day after the 10Y
