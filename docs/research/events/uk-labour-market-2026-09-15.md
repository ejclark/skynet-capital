## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **The wires will print the wrong number, and the tape already knows it.** This release's
headline wage figure is a **composition artifact**: in the last print (Apr–Jun 2026) whole-economy
regular pay was **3.5%** y/y — public sector **6.1%** averaged against **private sector 2.8%**, the
weakest private reading since **Aug–Oct 2020**. The MPC's second-round-effects argument is a
*private-sector* argument, so **2.8%** is the cell that settles the **09-17** vote split, not 3.5%.
The evidence that the market reads it this way is dated: on **2026-08-18** whole-economy regular pay
**beat** the 3.4% Reuters consensus and sterling still **fell 0.1% to $1.352**. The release's second
weakness is its most-quoted cell — the **unemployment rate** comes from a survey ONS told the
regulator on **2026-08-11** it *"will not be seeking reaccreditation"* for, and the same bulletin says
plainly *"RTI currently provides the most reliable measure of employees."* **None of this is ours to
trade** — `symbols: []`, no house playbook is rates- or sterling-keyed, and the measured reaction to
the last print was ~0.1% in cable. Date is **estimate**; it widens caution and licenses nothing.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date and time are the publisher's own, twice over.** SUPPORTED, primary, two independent
   ONS pages. The release-calendar entry `ons.gov.uk/releases/uklabourmarketseptember2026` (fetched
   today) reads *"UK Labour Market: September 2026"*, *"15 September 2026 at 7:00am"*, *"Estimates of
   employment, unemployment, inactivity, average weekly earnings, vacancies and other labour market
   related statistics for the UK"*, and is marked not yet published. The **August 2026** bulletin
   (fetched today) independently states *"Released: 18 August 2026"* and *"Next release: 15 September
   2026"*. The date is not in doubt; the `estimate` label is about this lane's no-self-confirm limit
   and the taxonomy's missing ONS prefix, not about the evidence.

2. **The headline wage number is a composition artifact, and this is the finding.** SUPPORTED,
   primary on the split, press-cited on the historical comparison. The August bulletin states
   verbatim: *"Annual average regular earnings growth was 6.1% for the public sector and 2.8% for the
   private sector"* (Apr–Jun 2026), against a whole-economy **3.5%**. A **3.3pp** gap means the
   headline is a weighted average of two different economies — one setting pay by public settlement,
   one by labour-market clearing. Reuters' read of the same series: *"the last time private sector
   regular earnings growth was lower than 2.8% was August to October 2020, when it was 2.4%"*. The
   MPC's live question is whether the energy shock is feeding **wage bargaining**; that is a private
   -sector mechanism, and 2.8% is the number that answers it. Registered as
   **FT-uk-labour-market-2026-09-15-1**.

3. **The market already reads the private cell, not the headline — measured, not asserted.**
   SUPPORTED, press-cited with a date. On **2026-08-18**, whole-economy regular pay printed **3.5%**
   against a **3.4%** Reuters consensus and total pay **4.1%** against **4.0%** — a beat on both — and
   the unemployment rate printed **4.9%** against **4.8%**. Sterling *fell*: *"Sterling was last 0.1%
   lower against the dollar at $1.352."* A wage beat that moves cable the wrong way is a tape saying
   the beat was public-sector noise. This is the ledger's one piece of hard reaction-function
   evidence and it is a **single print**, which is why the "This week" call is medium and not high.

4. **The most-quoted cell is the least reliable one, and the publisher says so.** SUPPORTED,
   primary, two ONS documents. The **11 August 2026** ONS letter to OSR (fetched today) states
   verbatim: *"We will not be seeking reaccreditation for LFS and APS-derived outputs"*, and that
   *"LFS and APS-derived outputs that are currently official statistics in development will move to
   official statistics"* — a designation change one step below accredited, with priority instead on
   the **Transformed Labour Force Survey** achieving *"the standards required to become accredited
   official statistics."* The **August 2026** transformation update (fetched today) dates that road:
   the **July 2026** readiness assessment concluded *"now is not the right time to transition to the
   TLFS for our headline labour market statistics"*, a progress check follows in **January 2027**, the
   decision point is **July 2027**, and the earliest transition is **November 2027**. Meanwhile the
   bulletin's own guidance is unambiguous: *"Our view remains that RTI currently provides the most
   reliable measure of employees."* Registered as **FT-uk-labour-market-2026-09-15-3**.

5. **The labour market is contracting in levels, not just decelerating in rates.** SUPPORTED,
   primary. Vacancies: *"Early estimates for May to July 2026 suggest a decrease of 6,000 (0.8%) to
   707,000"*, and — the sentence that makes it a level story — *"Outside of the coronavirus (COVID-19)
   pandemic period, the last time there were 707,000 or fewer vacancies was in September to November
   2014, when there were 703,000 vacancies."* Payrolled employees: *"The early estimate of payrolled
   employees for July 2026 decreased by 94,000 (0.3%) on the year, but was largely unchanged on the
   month, decreasing by 13,000 (0.0%) to 30.3 million."* Employment rate **75.1%**, inactivity
   **20.9%**, unemployment **4.9%**. A twelve-year low in vacancies alongside a shrinking payroll is
   the loose labour market the July MPC minutes rest their hold on, arriving in levels.

6. **The one number most likely to be revised is the one most likely to be reported.** MIXED — the
   direction of the revision bias is on the record, its size for this specific print is not. ONS's
   own guidance on the PAYE series: early months in the tax year *"carry a greater degree of
   uncertainty in their initial estimates and have received larger-than-average upward revisions in
   recent years"*, and the mechanism is documented (*"the incorporation of additional real time
   information (RTI) submissions"* revised May 2026's monthly change from +2,000 to +3,000). So the
   **−94,000** y/y figure that anchors leg 5 is the provisional value of a series that revises **up**.
   The honest reading is that the *direction* (payroll shrinking) is robust across five months of
   prints and the *magnitude* is not. Registered as **FT-uk-labour-market-2026-09-15-2**.

7. **The forward-looking wage evidence agrees with the backward-looking evidence — and the energy
   channel nets to roughly zero.** SUPPORTED on the figures, press-cited on their relay. The
   **July 2026** BoE Decision Maker Panel: firms expected **3.4%** average wage growth one year ahead,
   *below* realised **4%** over the past year, and on the energy question **19%** of firms expected
   wages to increase against **17%** expecting them lower. That near-cancellation is the survey
   evidence sitting under the MPC majority's *"little evidence so far"* of second-round effects,
   quoted from the 30 July minutes in the [`boe-decision-2026-09-17`](boe-decision-2026-09-17.md)
   ledger. **Named limit:** the July DMP page itself was not fetched this session — these figures are
   a press relay of a BoE release, and the September DMP page (fetched today) carries no data, stating
   only *"This monthly Decision Maker Panel data will be published on 2 October 2026."* That date is
   proposed to the calendar in this PR.

8. **The print is inside the committee's information set, and the corridor around it is crowded but
   cleanly ordered.** SUPPORTED. The MPC meeting ends **09-16** and announces **09-17**
   ([`boe-decision-2026-09-17`](boe-decision-2026-09-17.md)), so a **07:00 London / 02:00 ET** release

   the *first* macro release of the ET session by six hours, so a pre-dawn gilt or cable move on
   09-15 has exactly one candidate explanation, and anything after 08:30 ET has several.

9. **No tracked symbol carries a sterling or UK-labour channel, and no play is proposed.**
   SUPPORTED, and this is where the honesty sits. `symbols: []`. The house playbooks (S1/S2/E1/S3/S4
