take effect at 12:01 a.m. ET on 2026-09-08 — 15/25/50% on 600+ classifications covering C$27.6B of US

equipment — 554 HTS lines, ~$20B), which this calendar does not track and which is the

channel to this book is **nil and measurably so**, and the framing this calendar filed the event

and **nothing dated comes next**, which is the finding that most changes what a future session

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The tariff took effect on 2026-09-08 exactly as filed — SUPPORTED.** Canada's own backgrounder
   carries the in-force wording *"as of 12:01 a.m., September 8, 2026"*; 09-08 was a Tuesday and
   press of that day reports the surtax live from that minute. Scope: **600+ tariff classifications**
   (reported variously as ~700 items) covering **C$27.6B** of US-origin imports, at **15% / 25% /
   50%**, *"with individual product rates based on the matching U.S. rate for the same goods"* — a
   line-by-line mirror, not a flat retaliation. Tiering as reported: **50%** on steel and aluminum,
   pulp, paper, plywood, electronics, clothing, cosmetics, sports equipment; **25%** on wood and
   lumber, household steel/aluminum items, appliance parts, carpets, cutlery, cheeses; **15%** on
   forklift trucks, agricultural machinery parts, air conditioners, molds. Two carve-outs and no
   more: goods **in transit** on the coming-into-force day are exempt, and *"Canada's tariff
   remission framework also remains available to assess requests for exceptional relief."* Alongside
   it, a **C$7.5B** domestic package — C$3.5B rapid-response worker/employer supports, C$2B Canada
   Strong Diversification Fund, C$1.5B Regional Tariff Response Initiative, C$0.5B BDC liquidity.
   **Status stays `estimate` for a schema reason, not a doubt reason:** the confirmed-prefix
   taxonomy in `market-events-data.ts` admits `IR|CAL|BLS|FED|PJM|SEC|TSY|OCC|BEA|CENSUS|ISM|CB` and
   has no slot for a foreign government's own release — the same mechanical gap
   [boj-decision-2026-09-18](boj-decision-2026-09-18.md) and
   [us-china-tariff-truce-expiry-2026-11-10](us-china-tariff-truce-expiry-2026-11-10.md) already
   record. Understating the label only widens caution.

2. **THE FRAMING CORRECTION — Canada's counter-tariffs are a Canadian price event, not a US CPI
   event. REFUTED, and this is the leg that most changes what the entry means.** The proposal that
   put this event on the calendar argued it is *"the goods-inflation half of the stagflation pairing
   those ledgers name (soft labor 10/02 + hot CPI 10/14)."* That cannot be right by construction: a
   Canadian surtax is levied **by Canada, at the Canadian border, on US-origin goods entering
   Canada**. Its incidence falls on Canadian importers and Canadian consumers. It is arithmetically
   incapable of appearing in a US consumer price index. **What the proposal was reaching for exists,
   but it is the other instrument:** the **US** Section 338 duty of 2026-08-22 — 50% on ~$20B of
   Canadian goods across **554 eight-digit HTS provisions**, and per Davis Wright Tremaine the basket
   is consumer-facing (alcohol, ice hockey equipment, maple syrup, plywood, dairy), with Section
   232-tariffed goods and civil aircraft excluded and **USMCA-qualifying goods *not* exempted**. That
   is the leg that touches US shelf prices, and **this calendar has no entry for it.** The
   *payroll* half of the proposal's rationale survives better than the inflation half — a US exporter
   losing Canadian demand can shed goods-producing jobs inside the September reference month — but
   see leg 5 for how small that is.

3. **The US authority is novel and legally untested, and that — not the economics — is the live risk
   — SUPPORTED.** Proclamations **11046, 11047, 11048** of **2026-07-20** invoked **Section 338 of
   the Tariff Act of 1930 (19 U.S.C. § 1338)**, which permits the President to proclaim, *"effective
   no sooner than 30 days later, a tariff of up to 50% on imports"* where a foreign country places
   *"any burden or disadvantage"* on US commerce through *"unequal impositions or discriminations."*
   Per DWT it *"is understood to require no precedent administrative investigation, making it a
   flexible tool"* — and it had never been used since enactment in 1930. **Why it exists now is the
   point:** the Supreme Court held on **2026-02-20** that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs
   (*Learning Resources v. Trump*, 6–3), a holding the
   [us-china ledger](us-china-tariff-truce-expiry-2026-11-10.md) already carries; Section 338 is the
   replacement authority. Proclamation **11056** of 2026-08-18 slipped the start from 08-19 to 08-22
   to buy three days for a deal; talks collapsed the evening of **08-21** and the duty took effect
   **12:01 a.m. 08-22**. **As of 2026-08-29 reporting, no lawsuit had been filed** — practitioners
   called challenges *"surely days (if not hours) away"* and one described the statute as *"literally
   a blank canvas because it's never been litigated,"* while the Liberty Justice Center said it
   *"[hadn't] had a lot of response from plaintiffs."* **No docket, therefore no date, therefore no
   calendar entry is proposed for it** — recorded here so a later reader does not mistake the absence
   of an entry for the absence of the risk.

4. **There is no dated forward hook on either side of this exchange — SUPPORTED, and it is the most
   actionable finding for the calendar.** Checked four ways and each came back empty: Canada's own
   announcement and backgrounder carry **no review date, no sunset and no removal condition**; Blakes'
   timeline *"contains no mention of scheduled negotiations, reviews, or future-dated deadlines"* and
   ends at 09-08; DWT records **"no expiration/termination dates stated"** for the Section 338
   tariffs; and the political track is explicitly undated — Carney on **2026-09-03**: *"We're ready to
   sit down and strike that deal when the Americans are ready,"* against Trump's *"we'll see"* on
   whether to restart talks. **An undated escalation is a headline risk, not a calendar position**, and
   a lane that keeps hunting for the next US–Canada date will keep finding nothing. The one dated
   venue that does exist is institutional, not bilateral — see leg 6.

5. **The economic footprint is real but small against a US book, and the payroll channel is smaller
   than the headline — SUPPORTED.** C$27.6B ≈ **US$20B** at the ~0.726 CAD/USD spot this session, against
   annual US goods exports on the order of **$2T** — roughly **1%** of the US export base, and that is the
   *gross* value exposed, not the demand actually destroyed (a 15% surtax on a forklift part does not
   zero the order). The two legs together are ~US$40B of bilateral trade taxed out of a relationship
   that runs several hundred billion. **This is why the tier is carried, not measured:** `medium` is the
   proposer's call on the combined footprint and an unresolved escalation, and it stays — twelve
   sessions of flat tape is far too thin a base to re-tier a structural trade conflict on, in either
   direction.

6. **The only scheduled venue where this wall is institutionally on the table is the USMCA annual
   joint review — SUPPORTED, and proposed same-PR.** The Free Trade Commission held the mandatory
   six-year joint review under **art. 34.7** on **2026-07-01** (six years from entry into force,
   2020-07-01), and **the United States declined to confirm it wished to extend** the Agreement for a
   further 16 years. That activates **art. 34.7.4**: a joint review **every year** until the parties
   agree to extend or the Agreement reaches its 16-year term on **2036-07-01**. All USMCA rights and
   obligations — preferential tariffs, rules of origin, investment protections, dispute settlement —
   **remain fully operative** in the meantime, and Canada's own page is careful that the review *"is not
   an expiry date, but rather a scheduled check-in."* The implementing statute is primary and was read:
   **19 U.S.C. § 4611** refers to *"any subsequent annual joint review meeting conducted as required
   under article 34.7 of the USMCA"* and attaches a **70-day-prior** US consultation obligation to it.
   **Proposed same-PR:** `usmca-joint-review-2027-07-01`, `estimate` / `EST:`, `medium`, `symbols: []`
   — **with its soft spot stated in the entry itself:** no source fetched this session names
   2027-07-01 explicitly. The year is solid, the anniversary rule supports the month, the day is
   *derived*. The ~2027-04-22 consultation lead date implied by the 70-day rule is deliberately **not**
   proposed, because it would be a derivation stacked on a derivation.

7. **The measured market channel is nil, and two apparent counterexamples dissolve on inspection —
   SUPPORTED; the numbers are in §5 of the Outcome.** S&P 500 across the whole escalation, 08-21
   (talks collapse) → 09-08 (surtax live): **7674.37 → 7673.52, −0.01%**. No tracked name of this
   calendar's roster carries the exposure. The exposed basket's two worst performers over that window
   — **IP −12.1%** and **TSN −10.6%** — both have named non-tariff drivers on the record (Q2
   containerboard de-rating and a **2026-09-03** beef guidance cut respectively), and attributing them
   to a trade measure would be exactly the inference this leg exists to forbid.

### 3. THE FINDING — in a tariff exchange, the protected domestic producer is a winner, and the word "steel" hides it

This is the transferable lesson and it is worth more than the event.

Canada's largest tier is **50% on steel and aluminum**. A reader who scans "Canada slaps 50% on US
steel" and reaches for a short in US steel equities has the sign backwards, and the tape says so:

| Name | 2026-08-21 (talks collapse) | 2026-09-08 (surtax live) | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| CLF | 11.27 | **12.29** | **+9.05%** |
| NUE | 243.63 | **256.40** | **+5.24%** |
| STLD | 228.68 | **240.31** | **+5.09%** |
| DE | 647.47 | **680.73** | **+5.14%** |
| S&P 500 | 7674.37 | **7673.52** | **−0.01%** |

The mechanism is not subtle once named. A US steelmaker sells overwhelmingly into the **US** market,
which the Section 232/338 wall protects; its exposure to Canada's surtax is the small **export** leg.
So the same policy that is a cost to a US *exporter* is a moat for a US *domestic producer*, and both
get filed under "steel tariffs."

**The rule for the next session, stated so it generalises past this case:** a tariff's incidence
follows **who levies it on whose goods crossing which border** — never the sector name in the
headline. Before sizing anything off a tariff story, write one sentence naming the levying
government, the origin of the taxed good, and the border it crosses. If that sentence cannot be
written, the trade is a headline, not a position. **The same test is what catches leg 2's error**:
written out, "Canada taxes US-origin goods entering Canada" makes the US-CPI claim collapse on
sight.

**Registered as FT-canada-counter-tariffs-effective-2026-09-08-1** so the claim is falsifiable rather
than merely plausible: the equal-weight CLF/NUE/STLD basket does **not** underperform SPX by more
than 5 percentage points from the 2026-09-08 close to the 2026-11-10 close. Five prices, one date,
anyone can score it.

### 4. The legs, re-scored

1. **In force on 09-08 — SUPPORTED**, from Canada's own release plus 09-08 reporting. The one gap is
   named in *Honest limits*: no post-event gazette or CBSA notice was fetched.
2. **The US-CPI framing — REFUTED, and this is the document's central correction.** The incidence is
   Canadian by construction. It is worth being precise about what is *not* claimed: Canadian
   retaliation can still reach US firms through **demand destruction** on the export leg, and that is
   a real (small) earnings channel. What it cannot do is raise a US consumer price. The proposal
   conflated the two, and so would anyone reading "tariffs → inflation" without asking whose border.
3. **Section 338 is novel and untested — SUPPORTED, and it is the only live risk left.** The duty
   rests on a 1930 statute used for the first time in 96 years, chosen because the Supreme Court took
   IEEPA away on 2026-02-20. No suit had been filed as of 2026-08-29 reporting. **A ruling voiding it
   would unwind Canada's surtax too**, since the Canadian order is explicitly rate-matched to the US
   line and has no independent rationale once the thing it mirrors is gone. That is the asymmetry a
   future session should carry: the downside of this exchange is gradual and small; the *upside* — a
   fast, total unwind on a court order — is the tail with the shape.
4. **No dated forward hook — SUPPORTED**, checked four independent ways (Canada's order, Blakes'
   timeline, DWT's alert, the political track). This is the finding that should change behaviour: an
   adjacency sweep hunting a US–Canada date will keep coming back empty, and should take the
   institutional venue instead.
5. **Footprint small against a US book — SUPPORTED.** ~US$20B of US exports at the ~0.726 CAD/USD
   spot this session, ~1% of the US goods-export base; both legs together ~US$40B.
6. **USMCA annual review is the successor — SUPPORTED and proposed**, `usmca-joint-review-2027-07-01`,
   with its derived-date weakness written into the entry rather than left for a reader to discover.
7. **Nil measured channel — SUPPORTED.** §5.
