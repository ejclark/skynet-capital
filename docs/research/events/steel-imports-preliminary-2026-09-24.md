on that question the last four editions say the same thing with increasing clarity.

### Leg 2 — does this print move steel equities? · **REFUTED** (the claim that it does)

Measured, not assumed. The release lands **10:00 ET**, inside the cash session, so the informative window
is the release-day **open→close** — the only daily window that contains 10:00.

Thirty-nine realized release dates came from Census's own **four** calendar-year grids (2023 and 2024
complete at 12 each, 2025 at 9 before the suspension, 2026 at 6 through 08-27). Every one is a trading day
with a bar. Bars are Nasdaq's own historical API; the benchmark is **XLB**, the materials sector, so
"abnormal" means the name's move net of its own sector that day.

| Test (NUE, open→close net of XLB) | Result |
|---|---|
| Release days, mean | **+0.264%**, sd 1.42, **t=+1.16, p=0.245** (n=39) |
| Release days, mean absolute | **1.06%** |
| All 922 sessions, mean absolute | **1.05%** |
| **Day-of-month 23–28, non-release control** | **+0.197%** (n=142) |

| Earnings-free subset, variance ratio | **0.80** |
| Up-days | **19 of 39** |

Four readings, all pointing the same way. There is **no mean effect** once the calendar is controlled for:
this release always lands on the 23rd–28th, materials drift mildly positive in that window, and the
release-day mean is statistically indistinguishable from the control days that share the window and carry
no release. There is **no excess magnitude** — the mean absolute move is a hundredth of a point *smaller*
than an ordinary session. There is **no excess tail**: |abnormal| exceeds 1.5% on **22%** of release days
against **22%** of ordinary non-earnings days, 2.0% on **9%** against **12%**, 2.5% on **6%** against
**7%**. And the direction is a **coin flip**, 19 up out of 39.

The **7** release days that do carry outliers are the ones contaminated by earnings, not by steel imports:
2023-01-26, 2023-04-25, 2024-01-25, 2024-04-23, 2025-01-27, 2025-04-23 and 2026-07-27 each sit within a
±1 day window of an 8-K Item 2.02 filing by NUE, STLD or CLF (EDGAR, fetched direct). The largest single
abnormal move in the whole sample — NUE **+4.81%** on 2023-01-26 — is Nucor's own Q4 report. Steel names
have reported in the last week of January, April, July and October for the whole sample; that is the same
week this release always occupies, which is exactly why the confound had to be removed rather than noted.

Cross-checks on the other two steel names point the same way and are reported because the one near-miss
belongs on the record: **STLD** shows +0.461% (t=+1.73, **p=0.084**) on the raw 39, which is the only
result in this study that flirts with significance — and it dies on the same calendar control
(release-vs-control **t=+1.09, p=0.28**), it is one of three names tested, and its sign story is backwards
(more imports arriving → domestic mills rally). **CLF** is −0.341% (t=−0.84, p=0.40). For scale, the same

### Leg 6 — the macro triangulation, and a correction handed back to the sibling · **SUPPORTED**

Three FRED primaries, fetched direct this session, on the same monthly grid through **July 2026**:

| Series | Jul-2025 | Jul-2026 | y/y |
|---|---|---|---|
| PPI, iron and steel (`WPU101`) | 318.105 | **374.100** | **+17.6%** |
| PPI, metals and metal products (`WPU10`) | 331.281 | 384.941 | +16.2% |
| Industrial production, iron and steel products (`IPG331S`) | 99.288 | **100.885** | **+1.6%** |

`WPU101` has risen in **every month** since November 2025 (307.944 → 374.100, **+21.5%** over eight
months). Domestic output is flat. Imports are **−22.6% YTD**. Those three facts only fit together one way:
the domestic steel price is being set by the **absence of the marginal imported ton**, not by a demand boom
(output would have risen) and not by a supply expansion (the price would have fallen). That is the whole
reason this document exists at `low` tier rather than not existing.

It also **settles the sibling's open channel, in the opposite direction from the framing**.
[`durable-goods-2026-09-25`](durable-goods-2026-09-25.md)'s leg 9 banked primary-metals inventories at
**+1.5% to $51.9B, up seventeen consecutive months**, calling it *"a tariff-era stockpiling signature worth
watching but not evidenced here."* Two things now bear on it, and both cut against the alarming reading:

1. **The build cannot be import-fed.** Imports ran **−22.6%** year-to-date over the same window. Whatever
   is accumulating in primary-metals inventories arrived from a domestic mill or was already in-country.
2. **Most of it is price, not tons.** M3 inventories are **nominal dollars**; the relevant deflator, PPI
   iron and steel, is **+17.6% y/y** and has been rising every month for eight. A nominal series climbing
   ~1.5% a month against a deflator climbing ~1.5–2.4% a month is not obviously accumulating any metal at
   all.

Stated as an honest limit: the M3 inventory figures are **quoted from that ledger, not re-derived here**,
and this leg does not attempt a real-terms reconstruction of the series — it observes that the two
published facts are in tension with the phrase "stockpiling signature" and hands that back to the ledger
that owns the claim.
