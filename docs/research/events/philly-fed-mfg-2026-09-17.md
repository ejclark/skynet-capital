2. **What August actually said, in the bank's own words — SUPPORTED, and it is quotable here for the
   first time.** From the August 2026 report: *"The survey's indicator for general activity rose to a

### What the conditions support

Nothing directional. What the conditions support is a **narrow set of dated expectations, three of
them registered**, and a reading discipline. The expectations: a **lower September headline** with an
AR(1) central of 40.6 and a base-rate median of 41.0, where "lower but still hot" is confirmation
rather than weakness; a **prices-paid line that holds** near 40 rather than continuing down, on a
4-of-12 continuation base rate and a Summary of Returns showing zero firms reporting decreases; and a
**pair that converges** on 09-15/09-17 rather than in October, on 16/17 and 32/33 one-month base
rates. The discipline is three refusals. **Do not read the firms' forward price indices as evidence**
(leg 7 measures them as worse than persistence). **Do not read the September print as a verdict on
the FOMC** — its collection closed before the decision (leg 9). **Do not attempt any release-hour
measurement on this date** — FOMC+1 into witching eve is a worse confound than October's co-release,
which is why the sibling's declined Dallas-style test is declined here too, for a different reason.
The one genuinely readable MBOS morning of 2026 remains **11-19**, and that is where a tape test
belongs.

### Honest limits

The measurement is of the **published series and the bank's own release text**, not of the tape — no
release-hour percentile test is run, because leg 8 shows it would be unidentifiable on this date, and
an unidentifiable null reported as a null is worse than no test. So the claim "this release does not
move the market" is **not made here**; the weaker and defensible claim is that on 2026-09-17 you
could not tell. **The September collection window in leg 9 is an inference from one observed
release** — the August report's stated 3-day gap between "data received through August 17" and its
August 20 publication — not a published rule; if the September window ran longer it could in
principle catch the 09-16 decision, and the leg would then be wrong in the one direction that
matters. **A source oddity is recorded rather than smoothed over:** the August report's own Summary
paragraph opens *"Responses to the May Manufacturing Business Outlook Survey…"* — evidently a
template slip in the Fed's own text, since every figure in it is August's. Nothing above depends on
that sentence, and it is noted so a later session does not treat it as a data problem. The AR(1)
bands in leg 4 assume a linear Gaussian process on a bounded diffusion index, so true bands are
somewhat tighter at the extremes than 23.1/58.1 suggests; they are model output, not history. The
base rate in leg 4 rests on **n=6** at the tight threshold — thin, and graded as such in the forward
test that depends on it. Leg 5's conditional cut is **n=12** and spans five decades and several
inflation regimes with no control for which. Leg 6's convergence rates (n=17, n=33) are the
best-powered numbers here but are unconditional on *why* a gap opened; nothing tests whether an
energy-driven divergence closes differently from a demand-driven one. Leg 10's two records come from
**one survey's forward block**, so they are correlated instruments and not two independent draws on
history — the analogue remains n=1. Leg 13's representativeness figures are **cited from the sibling,
not recomputed**, and inherit its limits. `blocked` is empty because every *cited* primary returned
real content; the sibling's guessed August-note URL still 404s and is not cited. A **VIX9D** term-
structure reading was attempted and abandoned — the source returned a single usable observation for
the trailing month, so no claim rests on it. **No September consensus is published at D-8** and, on
this series' pattern, none will be before release week. Finally, everything above describes a
**sub-state qualitative diffusion survey** whose national explanatory power is R² ≈ 0.20 on the recent
window.
