## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Three questions, and the first two are about the calendar entry rather than the survey. (1) This id
reached the calendar as a *proposal* from the `treasury-2y-note-2026-12-28` lane, filed on a corridor
argument — while the [`dallas-fed-tssos-2026-12-01`](dallas-fed-tssos-2026-12-01.md) research had
**explicitly declined** to file this exact date six weeks of calendar-time earlier, on the ground that
"filing further `low` rows for a series this doc has just measured as market-neutral would contradict
the finding." Two lanes, opposite calls, same fact. Which is right? (2) The proposal's own reasoning
contains two factual claims about the schedule and the corridor — do they hold? (3) Does anything
about the *December* edition, as opposed to the November one already researched, change the stance?

**One-line verdict:** the entry earns its row but not for the reason proposed — the corridor is
*less* crowded than the proposal thought, not more, and that is precisely what makes it interesting:
removing a phantom 10:00 print leaves **2026-12-29 as the only date in 2026 where this survey's
10:30 slot has a 90-minute cordon on both sides**, the first clean attribution window the series
gets — which then turns out to be worth little, because the window sits in the Dec 26–31 stretch
where SPY's cash session is up only 41.3% of the time on 0.70× volume; the stand-aside is unchanged,
the wage leg survives to t+4 at 88% against 92% at t+3, and December carries no seasonal.
