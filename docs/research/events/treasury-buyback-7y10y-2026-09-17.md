## At a glance

**TL;DR.** A **read, not a trade** — and the read is that this operation is the **least-used
instrument in the entire Treasury buyback program**, which corrects the reason it was filed. The
tentative schedule (**estimate**) puts a routine 7-10Y nominal liquidity-support buyback at
**1:40–2:00pm ET on 2026-09-17**, cap **$4B**. It was proposed as the "$4B parity benchmark"
showing that sb0607 merely lifts the long end to the size the rest of the curve already carried.
That is true about **caps** and false about **purchases**: across all nine operations this bucket
has ever run it has filled its cap **zero times**, accepting a mean of **$414M** and a median of
**$210M** — while the 10Y-20Y bucket buys its entire $2B cap **21 times out of 21**. On accepted
par, the long end at its *old* cap already purchases four times what this bucket purchases at
$4B. Second correction: despite the label, **no 7-Year note has ever been eligible** — every list
is ten 10-Year notes, and the count is derivable rather than awaited. Trade nothing; 09-17 sits one
day after the FOMC with four other dated events on it.

## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** a 7-10Y nominal liquidity-support buyback is scheduled for 1:40pm ET on
2026-09-17, the day after the FOMC. It entered this calendar as the control that proves sb0607's
long-end increase is normalization rather than intervention. Is that framing right, what will the
operation actually do, and does any of it justify a position?

**One-line verdict:** the framing is **half right and misleading in the half that matters** — this
bucket's $4B cap has never been binding, never been filled, and buys about a tenth of what the long
end buys at half the cap, so "parity" holds only on paper; read the 09-16 list and the 09-17

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The operation exists with these parameters — SUPPORTED, from the primary itself.** The
   decompressed row reads verbatim: announce **9/16/2026**, operation **9/17/2026 1:40 pm – 2:00
   pm**, settle **9/18/2026**, Liquidity Support, Nominal Coupons **7Y to 10Y**, maturity range
   **09/18/2033 – 09/17/2036**, min $0, **max $4 billion**. The entry stays `status: "estimate"`: a
   tentative schedule is tentative by construction, and the confirming primary is the 09-16
   announcement. **One aggregator correction, recorded because it is the class of error this
   calendar was seeded to catch:** a search relay for this operation this session returned "announce
   September 17, operation September 18" — one day late on both. The PDF text layer decompressed
   here says otherwise and is primary.

2. **This is the least-used operation in the whole program — SUPPORTED, and it is the finding.**
   All nine 7Y-10Y operations ever run (`buybacks_operations`, primary, 2026-09-08), offered /
   accepted par against cap: 2024-07-24 $3.71B / **$0** ($2B cap) · 2024-09-10 $3.07B / $0.449B ·
   2024-12-09 $3.46B / $0.195B · 2025-03-18 $4.90B / $0.985B · 2025-06-17 $3.96B / **$1.066B** ·
   2025-10-16 $10.44B / $0.192B · 2026-01-16 $5.94B / $0.210B · 2026-03-12 $3.39B / $0.055B ·
   2026-06-16 $5.07B / $0.570B. **Zero full fills in nine**, mean accepted **$414M**, median
   **$210M**, mean fill **10.3%** of cap. And the cap has been *structurally* irrelevant: cover
   (offered ÷ cap) ran **0.77x–2.61x**, median 1.22x, and was **below 1.0x four times** — dealers
   did not offer enough to fill the cap even if Treasury had taken everything.

3. **Cap parity is not purchase parity — this REFUTES the framing the event was filed under.** The
   proposal note reads down the schedule's caps and concludes sb0607 "does not create a new size
   tier; it lifts the two long-end buckets to the size the rest of the nominal curve already had."
   Correct about caps. Now the same comparison on **accepted par**, the only number that touches the
   market — mean per operation since 2025-01-01, by nominal bucket: 1Mo-2Y **$3.433B** · 3Y-5Y
   **$3.076B** · 2Y-3Y **$2.148B** · 10Y-20Y **$2.000B** · 20Y-30Y **$1.863B** · 5Y-7Y **$1.465B**
   · **7Y-10Y $0.513B**, last by a factor of nearly three. 10Y-20Y filled its $2B cap **21 of 21**;
   20Y-30Y **20 of 22**. So the long end at its *old* $2B cap already bought **four times** what
   this bucket buys at $4B. If the doubled long-end cap fills the way the $2B cap did, sb0607 is the
   **largest actual-purchase change anywhere in the nominal program**, not a return to a standing
   size. The parity story is an artifact of reading caps in a program where most caps are not
   binding.

4. **"7Y to 10Y" describes the maturity window, not what is bought — SUPPORTED, and no 7-Year note
   has ever been eligible.** Reconstructed eligible lists (`buybacks_security_details`, per
   operation): 2026-01-16 ran ten notes 2033-02-15 → 2035-05-15; 2026-03-12 ran 2033-05-15 →
   2035-08-15; 2026-06-16 ran 2033-08-15 → 2035-11-15 — in every case **ten 10-Year notes on the
   Feb/May/Aug/Nov cycle, and not one 7-Year note**. That is mechanically necessary rather than
   coincidental: a 7-Year note maturing inside 09/18/2033–09/17/2036 was issued between 09/2026 and
   09/2029, so on operation day it is either unissued or fails TreasuryDirect's on-the-run and
   first-coupon exclusions. **Every one of the nine operations had exactly 10 eligible issues and a
   `max_nbr_offers` of 9** — an invariant across two years.

5. **The eligible list is derivable, not merely awaited — SUPPORTED, 3 of 3.** The window is three
   years wide, so it spans **twelve** quarterly 10-Year maturities; subtract the newest two, which
   have not passed a first coupon, and 12 − 2 = **10**, the observed invariant. The model reproduces
   all three 2026 lists exactly, including *which* issues were dropped (2026-01-16 excluded
   2035-08-15 and 2035-11-15, first coupons 2026-02-15 and 2026-05-15, both unpaid on the day).
   Applied to 09/18/2033–09/17/2036 it predicts **ten notes, 2033-11-15 → 2036-02-15**: the
   2036-05-15 and 2036-08-15 notes (first coupons 2026-11-15 and 2027-02-15) are excluded, the
   latter also on-the-run. Registered as `FT-…-1`. **Honest caveat:** three observations, and the
   sibling `treasury-buyback-2y3y-2026-10-06` logged an unexplained pre-2011 exclusion in its own
   bucket that this model would not have caught.

6. **Treasury buys near-par paper here, so the long end's cash-vs-par nuance does not read across —
   SUPPORTED.** The sibling ledgers carry the point that a "$2B operation" is roughly $1.3B of cash
   because Treasury takes deep-discount long bonds at $52–71. Accepted prices in this bucket:
   103.516 and 100.578 (2026-01-16), 95.738 / 103.227 / 100.203 (2026-03-12), 97.488 and 96.902
   (2026-06-16). Par ≈ cash here — and both numbers are small either way.

7. **Nothing on 2026-09-17 is attributable to this operation — SUPPORTED.** The day carries
   **housing starts 8:30am** (`confirmed`), the **monthly coupon announcement** (`estimate`), the
   **10-Year TIPS auction at 1:00pm** (`confirmed`) forty minutes before this operation, and a
   **UNSC Iran panel mandate vote** (`estimate`) — after the **FOMC decision on 09-16**
   (`confirmed`), with opex and the BoJ on 09-18. **27 other tracked events sit inside ±5 days.**
   One structural detail worth keeping: the preliminary CUSIP list posts **11:00am ET on 09-16,
   three hours before the FOMC statement**, so the announced maximum is a pre-decision number and
   cannot respond to it. The 1:00pm TIPS auction is also *not* an eligibility link — TIPS are a
   different security type and cannot be bought in a nominal-coupon operation.

8. **Scale — SUPPORTED, and it is what makes this a read.** The bucket bought **$835M combined
   across its three 2026 operations**. The single week of 09-09/09-10 issues **$39B of 10-Year and
   $22B of 30-Year** (`auctions_query`, via the `treasury-buyback-increase-2026-09-09` ledger,
   2026-09-08). Whatever this operation accepts on 09-17 is a rounding error against that.

9. **Tracked-name sensitivity — none distinguishable.** `symbols: []` — market-wide through the
   rate-duration channel, and at this size not even that. The corridor's duration-sensitive names

**What the conditions support.** Nothing directional — the house playbooks (S1/S2/E1/S3/S4 + G1) are
symbol- and earnings-keyed and no rates playbook exists. What travels is a reading discipline the

**Honest limits.** The bucket's *why* has no published explanation. Treasury's acceptance rule is a
relative-value price test, which explains **how** a 1.4%-of-cap fill can happen but not **why** the
belly 10-Year sector has priced away from Treasury's marks nine times running; the 2024-07-24
zero-accept operation likewise has no published account. Treat the pattern as a demonstrated
regularity with an unknown mechanism — the same shape of gap the 20-30Y sibling recorded for the
2026-03-19 under-fill. The eligible-list model is reconstructed from three observed lists plus
published exclusion rules, not from a rule Treasury states as a count. sb0607's terms are inherited
from the sibling ledgers' reads rather than re-fetched here. And **no 09-17-specific forecast exists
anywhere**; the 09-16 announcement is this event's first hard number.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date and parameters `estimate`-labeled — tentative schedule; the confirming primary is the
09-16 announcement).** The 2026-09-17 7-10Y buyback is a **data release, not a catalyst**: no
position keyed to it, and no attribution of that afternoon's tape to it. Base case
(**estimate**-labeled): the 09-16 11:00am ET preliminary list posts **ten 10-Year notes running
2033-11-15 → 2036-02-15** at a **$4B** maximum, and the operation accepts a **low-hundreds-of-
millions** figure across two or three issues near par — under the bucket's all-time high of $1.066B
— passing without a tape effect separable from the FOMC 23½ hours earlier, the 1:00pm TIPS auction,
the coupon announcement and opex the next day.

**Two corrections this doc owns.** (1) **Cap parity is not purchase parity.** The proposal note and
the `treasury-buyback-increase-2026-09-09` ledger's "it is parity, not escalation" read both compare
caps. On accepted par the long end at $2B already bought 4x this bucket at $4B, so sb0607 plausibly
*is* an escalation in purchases even while being parity in caps — the two claims are not in tension
and the ledger should carry both. (2) **No 7-Year note is ever eligible here**, and the eligible
count is derivable from the maturity window and the first-coupon rule rather than awaited.

**Kill switches:**

- **The 09-16 preliminary list is not ten CUSIPs running 2033-11-15 → 2036-02-15, or contains a
  7-Year note** — legs 4 and 5 are wrong; correct this doc and the calendar note the same day, and
  flag it to the `treasury-buyback-2y3y-2026-10-06` sibling, whose eligibility model is the same shape.
- **The 09-16 announced maximum is anything other than $4B** — the standing cap moved without a
  published release; re-read the program's terms before any further inference, and check whether the
  replacement schedule finally posted.
- **09-17 par accepted is ≥ $1.2B** — above every observation in the bucket's history; the belly's
  price gap would be closing, which is the one outcome that would make these operations worth
  tracking as more than plumbing.
- **The 2026-09-10 10-20Y operation accepts well under $4B** — the long end would be behaving like
  this bucket, which weakens leg 3's asymmetry and reopens the parity question in the other direction.
- **Treasury publishes the replacement buyback schedule, or the 2026-11-04 refunding adds sectors,
  frequency or size to the belly** — a genuinely different program; propose the changed operations
  as their own dated entries rather than folding them into this stance.
- **Any single 7-10Y operation clears $2B accepted** — leg 2's nine-operation regularity breaks and
  the "least-used instrument" framing goes with it.
