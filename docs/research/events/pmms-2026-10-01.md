## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **This print has no surprise in it — and that is the finding, not a shrug.** Paired
against the 10-year Treasury over its own stated survey window, the PMMS 30-year average is a
**lagged restatement of the 10Y plus ~199bp**: 36 published 2026 weeks give a spread of mean
**1.967pp** (sd 0.054), the last 13 weeks **1.987pp** with **sd under 2bp**, and a strictly
out-of-sample rule lands **MAE 6.6bp** over 75 paired weeks with the last six inside 3bp each. So
there is nothing to lean against at 12:00 ET on **2026-10-01** — by then the 9/24–9/30 curve will
already be public and the print computable. **What IS live is the level.** The 10Y ran to **5.00**
(09-15) from the **4.795** average behind the last published 6.76%, so roughly **+20bp of mortgage
rise is already banked and unpublished**; the 09-17 print projects **6.96%** on four of five known
days, a bigger weekly move than any of 2026 (max **+0.16pp**). At an unchanged 10Y the 10-01 print
lands **~6.99%** — and **7.00% has not printed since 2025-01-16 (7.04%), 86 weeks**. The 7-handle
needs the 10Y to average just **≥5.01** across 9/24–9/30. Date is **estimate** on taxonomy, not
doubt (no confirmed-tier prefix exists for a GSE survey). Estimates widen caution; nothing here
licenses an entry.

## Initial research

**The question.** What will the 2026-10-01 PMMS 30-year fixed-rate average be, does its arrival
carry information worth positioning for, and should the weekly series join this calendar?

**The verdict, in one line.** The print is a **~7bp-precision restatement of an already-public
Treasury average** — untradeable as a surprise, genuinely interesting as a *level*, because the
banked 10Y move puts the **first 7-handle in 86 weeks** within a single basis point of the current
curve; and the weekly series should **not** be backfilled.

### Method

Three primaries, all fetched direct on **2026-09-16** from this runner, none cited second-hand:

- `freddiemac.com/pmms` — HTTP 200, 103,786 bytes. The publishing rule and the live reading.
- `freddiemac.com/pmms/docs/PMMS_Publication_Calendar.pdf` — HTTP 200, 93,314 bytes. The forward
  calendar, inflated in-session by a pure-Python FlateDecode pass over its **8** compressed streams
  (306,198 chars) and text-extracted from the show-text operators.
- `freddiemac.com/pmms/docs/PMMS_history.csv` — HTTP 200, 97,403 bytes. **2,894** weekly rows back
  to 1971-04-02.

Plus `home.treasury.gov` daily par yield curve CSVs for 2026 (**177** sessions, 01/02→09/15) and
2025 (**248** sessions), and `cdn.cboe.com` `VIX_History.csv` (HTTP 200, 472,666 bytes) for the
volatility regime. Nothing was blocked; `probe-ref.blocked` is empty and honestly so.

The analytical step is a **pairing** nobody here had done: for every PMMS publication date, average
the CMT 10Y over that print's *own stated window* (prior Thursday through Wednesday = pub−7 … pub−1)
and take the difference. That difference is the primary-secondary spread, and it is the whole model.

### Leg 1 — the date and the window are the publisher's own, twice over → **SUPPORTED**

The forward calendar's row reads verbatim `Publication Date 10/1/2026 | Week Beginning 9/24/2026 |
Week Ending 9/30/2026`, and its neighbours reproduce to the day — `9/17/2026 | 9/10/2026 |
9/16/2026` and `9/24/2026 | 9/17/2026 | 9/23/2026`. Independently, the site states the standing rule
(*"released weekly on Thursdays at 12 p.m. ET … an average of loan rates offered the prior Thursday
through Wednesday"*), and 9/24/2026 is a Thursday with 9/30/2026 a Wednesday. Structurally, the
history CSV's last published row is **9/10/2026** and 9/10 + 21d = 2026-10-01.

**Status stays `estimate`, and the reason is taxonomy rather than doubt.** `CONFIRMED_PREFIX` in
`scripts/event-scan-validation.mjs` (`IR|CAL|BLS|FED|PJM|SEC|TSY|OCC|BEA|CENSUS|ISM|CB|UMICH|FHFA`)
has no member for a GSE's own weekly survey — the same gap `case-shiller-hpi-2026-09-29` recorded
for an index provider. Every trading-adjacent line here carries that label.

### Leg 2 — the print is a lagged restatement of the 10Y, not new information → **SUPPORTED**

Thirty-six paired 2026 weeks:

| Sample | n | Mean spread | sd | Range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026 YTD | 36 | **1.967pp** | 0.054 | 1.838 (02-05) → 2.092 (04-02) |
| Last 13 weeks | 13 | **1.987pp** | **0.019** | — |
| 2025 | 52 | 2.295pp | 0.125 | — |
| 2025+2026 | 88 | 2.161pp | 0.191 | 1.838 → 2.520 (2025-05-01) |

The last thirteen weeks disperse by **under two basis points**. The eight most recent spreads read
1.976 · 1.994 · 2.012 · 1.982 · 1.972 · 1.974 · 1.964 · 1.965.

**Tested out-of-sample, with no peeking.** Predict week *t* as (window-average 10Y) + (mean spread
of the **13 weeks strictly before** *t*), over 75 paired weeks:

- **MAE 6.6bp**, median absolute error **5.3bp**, max **23.1bp**
- **47%** inside 5bp, **73%** inside 10bp
- last six weeks: −2.8 · +0.6 · +1.9 · +2.1 · +2.9 · +2.4 bp

### Leg 3 — the model's one real failure is a regime shift, not noise → **MIXED**, stated as a limit

The four worst errors in the entire sample are **2026-01-22 (+23.1bp)**, **2026-01-15 (+22.1)**,
**2026-01-29 (+19.5)** and **2026-02-05 (+19.1)** — consecutive, all one-signed, all inside the
window where the spread compressed from 2025's **2.295pp** to 2026's **1.967pp**. A trailing mean
is structurally late through a regime change, and this is what that looks like. So the ±10bp band
below is a claim about a *stable* spread regime, and its kill switch is a spread break rather than
a level miss. Honest limit, not a hedge.

### Leg 4 — a policy meeting inside the survey window does not break transmission → **SUPPORTED**

The obvious objection to trusting the rule across an FOMC is that a decision inside the window
should dislocate primary pricing. Tested on the ten paired weeks whose window contains a 2025–2026
decision date: **MAE 7.2bp** against **6.5bp** for the other 65. Barely distinguishable — and the
five FOMC weeks of 2026 read **+19.5 · −3.6 · +1.6 · −1.5 · −1.4 bp**, with the outlier being the
January regime transition, not the meeting. The 2025 cluster (+11.8 · +10.0 · +9.4) is the same
compression showing through.

**And this window contains no meeting at all.** The 2026-09-16 decision precedes it; the next is
2026-10-28. The 9/24–9/30 window is the **first full post-decision week**, which is the one genuine
reason this edition earns a calendar slot.

### Leg 5 — the level is already moving, and 7% is one basis point away → **SUPPORTED**

Published 30-year averages: **6.66** (08-27) → **6.71** (09-03) → **6.76** (09-10), against **6.35**
a year earlier — **+41bp y/y**. The window behind that 6.76% averaged a 10Y of **4.795**. Since
then: 4.95 (09-10) · 4.96 (09-11) · 4.97 (09-14) · **5.00** (09-15).

So roughly **+20bp of mortgage-rate rise is already banked and unpublished**, and the grid at the
current trailing spread of **1.987pp** is arithmetic, not forecast:

| 9/24–9/30 average 10Y | Implied 10-01 PMMS 30Y |
|---|---|
| 4.80 | 6.79% |
| 4.90 | 6.89% |
| **5.00** (09-15 level) | **6.99%** |
| **5.01** | **7.00% — the threshold** |
| 5.10 | 7.09% |
| 5.20 | 7.19% |

Two dated anchors make the threshold matter. **7.00% has not printed since 2025-01-16 (7.04%)** —
86 weeks, with a post-peak maximum of **6.96%** on 2025-01-23. And the nearer 09-17 print projects
**6.96%** (+19bp), which would be **the largest single weekly move of 2026**, whose maximum to date
is **+0.16pp** (2026-03-26). A 6.99% print would report a y/y gap near **+65bp** against **6.34%**
on 2025-10-02.

### Leg 6 — the weekly series should not be backfilled → **SUPPORTED**, and this is the open question answered

The proposing sweep wrote: *"a lane taking it up should decide whether a weekly survey belongs on
this calendar as a series or only where it brackets a tracked print."* Legs 2–4 answer it. A
weekly series would buy **52 sessions a year** for a number derivable to **~7bp** from Treasury
data this repo already fetches — the definition of the clutter `#2946`'s horizon leash exists to
stop. **No proposals are filed by this research**, including the 09-17 and 09-24 editions, and
that refusal is the deliverable rather than an omission.

The gap the proposal identified is nonetheless real and stays closed by this one file: across
**666** canonical events the calendar carries no other mortgage-rate release (re-grepped
2026-09-16), while `fhfa-hpi-2026-09-29`, `fhfa-hpi-2026-10-27`, `fhfa-hpi-2026-11-24` and the
Case-Shiller ledgers all quote a PMMS reading as the live transmission channel. What they need is
the *number*, which this ledger now derives, not a weekly session.

### What the conditions support

**Nothing, as a position on this event** — and that is a first-class call. Concretely:

- **No trade on the print.** Its content is public beforehand to ~7bp. Any "surprise" is a spread break, which is a 2-in-75-weeks event outside a regime change.
- **The 10Y is the tradeable object**, and it belongs to `fomc-2026-09-16`, `pce-2026-09-30` and `jobs-2026-10-02` — not here.
- **The 7-handle is a watch item with a real number attached** (10Y ≥5.01 over 9/24–9/30), useful to the housing ledgers in the same corridor and to nothing else.

### Honest limits

- **The spread regime is the single load-bearing assumption.** It shifted 33bp between 2025 and 2026; a re-widening invalidates every level in Leg 5 while leaving the *method* intact.
- **The window is entirely in the future.** Every 10-01 figure here is conditional on a 10Y path nobody has, including across a **high-impact, `estimate`-status** government-funding deadline on **2026-09-30** that sits inside the window.
- **CMT par yields are not MBS.** The spread absorbs the whole primary-secondary stack — MBS basis, servicing, capacity, points. Its stability is empirical, not structural, and it is exactly what breaks first under stress.
- **No instrument run.** `earnings-cycle.mjs`/`intraday-edges.mjs` are symbol-keyed and this event has none, so there is no cache to bust and none was claimed.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (2026-09-16, `estimate` date).** **Stand aside on the print; watch the level.** The
2026-10-01 PMMS 30-year average is modelled as (average CMT 10Y over 9/24–9/30) **+ 1.987pp**, and
at an unchanged curve that is **~6.99%** — one basis point of 10Y away from the **first 7-handle in
86 weeks**. No position is warranted on this event at any horizon: its information is public before
it publishes. The weekly series is **declined**; this edition stands alone because its window is the
first full post-FOMC week and brackets quarter-end.

**Kill switches** — any one of these and the stance is rebuilt, not patched:

1. **Spread break.** The trailing-13-week spread printing outside **1.94–2.04pp** on any publication through 2026-10-01. Kills Legs 2–5 and every level above.
2. **Fast falsifier.** The **2026-09-17** print coming in below **6.85%** against a **6.96%** projection built on four of five known days.
3. **Regime re-widening.** Two consecutive out-of-sample errors past **15bp** by **2026-12-31** — which would also *reverse* Leg 6 and argue the series deserves its own slot.
4. **Publisher change.** Freddie Mac moving, suspending or redefining the PMMS window before 2026-10-01 — the calendar and the rule are the only two things holding the date.
