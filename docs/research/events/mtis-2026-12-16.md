### Primary content read — what the last published edition actually says

CB26-132 (released 2026-08-14, June 2026 data): combined distributive trade sales and manufacturers'
shipments **$2,111.3B**, down **1.1%** m/m (±0.2) and up **10.0%** y/y (±0.3); inventories
**$2,740.2B**, *virtually unchanged* m/m — the 90% confidence interval includes zero — and up
**3.0%** y/y; **total business inventories/sales ratio 1.30**, against **1.39** in June 2025.

Sales growing 10.0% y/y against inventories at 3.0% is a destocking regime, and FRED's 414-month
history puts 1.30 at the **28.3rd percentile** since 1992 (32.8th since 2010), reached from **1.39**
a year earlier. The obvious read is that the slide continues into October. Two measurements say
otherwise. First, **it has already stopped**: the ratio troughed at **1.28** in May-2026 and ticked
back up to 1.30 in June. Second, conditioning on a comparable regime (12-month ratio change ≤ −0.08;
today's is **−0.09**; **n=25** episodes across 1997/2002/2004/2009/2010/2020/2021), the ratio four
months later is at or below its level then only **44.0%** of the time, median change **+0.010** —
and, more sharply, the *monthly* non-increase rate inside a destocking regime is **57.7%** (n=26)
against a **67.8%** unconditional rate (n=401). **Conditioning on destocking makes a further decline
less likely, not more**, because the unconditional series has its own secular downtrend. That is
what makes `-3` worth registering rather than assuming.

### Honest limits

- **The archive is release *dates*, not release *contents*.** Legs 2, 3 and 5 measure whether, when

- **The advance-to-final revision size for retail inventories is not measured here.** How much
