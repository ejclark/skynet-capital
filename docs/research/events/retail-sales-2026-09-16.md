## Initial research

**The question.** What is the consensus for the August 2026 advance retail sales report, what does
the July print and recent trend say, how has the market reacted to this release in each direction
during 2026, and — since it lands the morning of the Sep-16 FOMC decision — how should we read that
timing and which tracked names (NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL AMZN) carry the most
sensitivity?

**One-line verdict:** the consumer-strength read (second-largest trader-reaction print after jobs)
landing *on FOMC morning* hours before a live hold-vs-hike decision — the July print was a
Prime-Day-distorted −0.6% miss that notably did *not* get cleanly bought (stagflation tinge); no firm
August street consensus exists at D-28; hot means yields up / duration pressure and soft is
supportive-but-muddied, and the house answer is guard-shaped, not directional — the play is
known-date variance discipline across a 9/16 session governed by the FOMC decision, not retail sales
alone.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md)
(macro-print mode: no price instruments). Primary source is census.gov (the retail release schedule
and the advance monthly report / marts_current.pdf); press (CNBC, TradingKey, ADM Investor Services,
Retail Dive, Seeking Alpha) used only for reaction narration and component detail, each claim dated.
Date re-verified against the Census retail release-schedule primary, fetched today, 2026-08-19. Two
stale/aggregator figures surfaced (Retail Dive "+5.4%" and a Seeking Alpha "+0.5%" that mismatch the
Census −0.6% primary) — discarded in favor of the Census number.

**Conviction legs, tested:**

1. **The date and time are right — SUPPORTED.** census.gov/retail/release_schedule.html (fetched
   2026-08-19) lists the August 2026 advance report for **Sep. 16, 2026 at 08:30 ET**, matching the
   checked-in calendar entry exactly. Re-verification matters (the 2026-08-15 seeding caught an
   aggregator carrying a wrong CPI date), so the Census primary is re-checked at each assessment.

2. **It lands *on* the FOMC decision day — SUPPORTED, and it is the load-bearing framing.** Sep 16 is
   the September FOMC decision day (statement 14:00 ET, SEP + dot plot — [`FOMC doc`](fomc-2026-09-16.md),
   confirmed). Retail sales at 08:30 ET is the **last major data print before the Fed decides**, and
   the FOMC is a live two-sided hold-vs-*hike* meeting (~60–70% hold / ~30–40% hike / 0% cut, per the
   FOMC doc). So any retail-driven yield move that morning feeds directly into the afternoon decision
   and its dot-plot reaction — the two events are not separable on the tape, and the 9/16 session
   should be read through the FOMC stance, with retail sales as its morning input.

3. **The July print was a distorted miss — SUPPORTED, with a base-effect caveat.** July advance
   retail sales (released 2026-08-14) fell **0.6% m/m** to $763.6B — the first decline since October
   2025 and a miss versus a +0.1% Dow Jones consensus (census.gov / CNBC / TradingKey, 2026-08-14).
   Ex-autos −0.3% (vs +0.2% expected); ex-autos-and-gas −0.2%. But the miss was mechanically
   distorted: **nonstore retailers fell 2.2%**, reflecting spend pulled forward into June after Amazon
   moved Prime Day from July to June this year; motor vehicles (−1.8%), gas stations (−0.9%), and
   electronics (−0.5%) also dragged. Year-over-year sales were still **+5.0%**. So the headline
   overstates consumer weakness — the August print carries a likely bounce-back in nonstore as the
   Prime-Day base effect unwinds, which should not be read as fresh strength.

4. **A soft retail print is *not* cleanly bought — SUPPORTED, and it is the key asymmetry nuance.**
   On the July release day (8/14), the weak −0.6% number did not trigger a clean dovish rally:
   Treasury yields were little changed, as lingering inflation and rising U.S. fiscal deficits kept
   upward pressure on yields even against soft data, and coverage framed it as a stagflation-tinged
   read — weak consumer *and* sticky inflation (TradingKey / ADM Investor Services, 2026-08-14). This
   is the important contrast with the inflation prints: a soft CPI/PPI is bought on the hike-relief
   channel, but a soft *retail* print signals demand weakness that, against sticky inflation, reads
   stagflationary — worse for our long-duration names than a clean dovish print. Conversely a *hot*
   retail print lifts yields on the consumer-strength channel (the calendar's own note: "hot consumer
   → yields up → duration pressure; soft → supportive").

5. **Retail sales is a high-reaction print — SUPPORTED.** Cross-indicator research converges that
   employment reports draw the largest trader reaction of the major releases, **followed by retail
   sales** (CME Group research summary, cited in the [`consumer-confidence doc`](consumer-confidence-2026-08-25.md)
   leg 5) — well above second-tier reads like consumer confidence. That justifies the high-impact tag
   and, combined with the FOMC-morning timing (leg 2), makes this the most market-relevant of the four
   activity prints in the pre-FOMC set.

6. **Tracked-name sensitivity — ranked, with mechanism.** `symbols: []` — market-wide; the ranking is
   about *our* risk. All nine trade as long-duration assets on the rate path (the calendar's own CPI
   note), and on that channel the ranking is the usual one: **CRWV** most sensitive (debt-financed
   datacenter build — a hawkish repricing hits discount rate *and* cost of capital), then the
   high-multiple semis **NVDA / AVGO / MRVL** (AI-beta, longest-duration cash flows), then **MSFT /
   GOOG / META** (mega-cap duration, fortress balance sheets). But retail sales is the one macro print
   where **AAPL and AMZN** carry *direct* exposure on top of that channel: AAPL (device demand is
   discretionary consumer spend, and the rumored Sep 9 iPhone 18 event sits days before this print),
   AMZN (e-commerce volumes track retail sales directly — and its nonstore category is the one the
   Prime-Day distortion runs through). So for this release specifically, AAPL/AMZN move up the
   sensitivity ranking from "least direct" to "dual-exposed." Sympathy plumbing on the rate channel
   still transmits QQQ-level reactions to every name ([`multi-symbol-sweep.md`](../multi-symbol-sweep.md)).

**What the conditions support.** Nothing directional — no macro playbook exists in the house set
(S1/S2/E1/S3/S4 + G1 are symbol/earnings-keyed). What travels is the *shape* of S2's logic, applied
to an FOMC-day session: 09-16 08:30 ET is a known-date variance event whose move is inseparable from
the 14:00 ET FOMC decision — positioning "for retail sales" on 9/16 is really positioning for the
FOMC, and should be governed by the [`FOMC stance doc`](fomc-2026-09-16.md)'s no-new-risk-day framing.
Support = discipline: no new rate-duration-sensitive paper entries into the 9/16 session, and no new
AAPL/AMZN consumer-adjacent exposure sized around the 08:30 ET print, without a reason that survives
both a hot and a soft outcome *and* both FOMC outcomes.

**Honest limits.** D-28: no August street consensus exists yet (retail consensus firms in the days
just ahead of release) and no whisper is findable — the July trend is the earliest honest proxy, and
it is distorted by the Prime-Day base effect (leg 3), so even the trend read carries a mechanical
asterisk. Reaction-day characterizations come from press narration, not our own price instruments
(macro-print mode has none). Two aggregator figures (+5.4%, +0.5%) mismatched the Census −0.6%
primary and were discarded — the exact staleness trap the CPI doc warned about; every number above
was date-checked against the Census primary or a dated press source. The advance estimate itself is
subject to revision in the subsequent monthly report — any pre-release "surprise" framing should net
against the revised prior, not the first-reported one.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date confirmed, Census primary re-checked 2026-08-19).** Treat 2026-09-16 08:30 ET as a
high-impact known-date variance event that is inseparable from the same-day FOMC decision — not a
tradeable edge, and governed by the FOMC stance doc's no-new-risk-day framing. No directional macro
position, no new rate-sensitive paper entries into the 9/16 session, no new AAPL/AMZN
consumer-adjacent exposure sized around the print, and any open position sized as if both retail
outcomes (hot → yield lift into the decision; soft → supportive-but-stagflation-tinged) *and* both
FOMC outcomes are live. Base case (**estimate-labeled**: trend extrapolation, no street consensus at
D-28, and distorted by the Prime-Day base effect): a headline recovering off the July −0.6% as the
nonstore pull-forward unwinds, with the control group the cleaner trend read — a bounce that should
not be mistaken for fresh consumer strength. The asymmetric risk is a genuinely hot control-group
print lifting yields into a live hike decision, hitting CRWV and the high-multiple semis hardest and
adding a direct consumer overlay on AAPL/AMZN.

**Stance sharpening (2026-08-29, D-18; receipt: the 2026-08-29 ledger row below).** The guard is
unchanged — the 9/16 session stays a no-new-risk day governed by the FOMC doc — but two things
moved and the doc should carry both rather than let the D-28 framing stand. **First, the
reaction function went one-sided.** The original stance treated hot and soft as mirror images
("hot → yield lift; soft → supportive, stagflation-tinged"). Warsh's 2026-08-28 keynote pinned the
hawkish case explicitly to inflation (PCE 3.7% 12-mo, 4.1% 6-mo, financial conditions judged
accommodative) and abolished forward guidance; the very next data point, Chicago PMI's −10.5pt
collapse to **47.1**, moved the tape not at all. On the current evidence a growth/demand miss does
not buy this Fed's relief — so soft retail is downgraded to *muted*, while hot retail keeps its
full hawkish weight. **Second, the same-day decision got more dangerous, not less:** ~73% hold
(Kalshi, 8/26) → roughly a coin flip (~56–59% hike on CME/FedWatch, 8/28). Both statements are
`confirmed`-date-keyed for 9/16 and neither licenses an entry.

**Stance sharpening (2026-09-01, D-15; receipt: the 2026-09-01 ledger row below).** The guard is
unchanged and firmer — the 9/16 session stays a no-new-risk day governed by the FOMC doc — but the
*base case* moves, and it moves toward the branch this doc already named as its asymmetric risk.
Through D-18 the base case was "a bounce that should not be mistaken for fresh strength," carried
loosely. It can now be quantified from primaries, and it is larger than "a bounce." Census Table 1's
own footnote states the series is adjusted for seasonal, holiday and trading-day variation **but not
for price changes**, and August set the most expensive August at the pump on record (AAA: national
average above $4/gal every day for the first time ever, ~$4.07 for the month against July's $3.93).
Gasoline stations were **$59.9B of the $763.6B** July headline — **7.8%** — and printed −0.9%; a
~+3.5% price move at constant gallons is worth roughly **+0.27pp**. Nonstore was **$136.9B**
(**17.9%**) and printed −2.2% on the Prime-Day base effect; un-doing that alone is worth roughly
**+0.39pp**. So ~**+0.6–0.7pp** of purely mechanical lift sits on top of a −0.6% base *before any
demand change*, and the one genuinely August-covering demand read — Redbook at **+9.1% y/y** — points
firm rather than soft. The arithmetic is this doc's own, from the Census primary and AAA monthly
averages; it is **not** a street forecast, and no August street consensus exists at the fourth
consecutive pulse. Two consequences. **First, the probability mass shifted to the hot branch** — the
branch that, after 8/28, still carries full hawkish weight into a decision the tape priced at **62%
hike** on 8/31 (up from ~40% a week earlier, 10Y ~4.716%). **Second, the D-18 restorer reversed:**
Brent went from sub-$87 and de-escalating to **$90.69** (+2.93%) on 8/31 after a US strike on Iranian
rocket launchers staged to mine Hormuz — so the energy path that could have made a soft print read
