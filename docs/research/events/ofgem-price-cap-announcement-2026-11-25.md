## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Two things about this entry are wrong in the way a calendar entry is usually wrong, and
fixing them is most of its value.** First, **2026-11-25 is a deadline, not a slot.** Ofgem's own words
are "will be announced **by**", and it beat the identical "by 25 November" deadline last year by four
days — publishing the Jan–Mar 2026 cap on **21 November 2025**. Two of the three cycles this lane could
source landed *on* the stated day, so the base rate favours 25 November, but the entry must be read as a
ceiling. Second, and more useful, **25 November is not when the number is decided.** Ofgem's wholesale
indexation is **3-1.5-12[3]** — a three-month observation window closing roughly 30 working days before
the cap period starts. Cornwall Insight dated the equivalent window exactly one cycle ago ("following
the closure of the observation window on **18 November**", for a cap announced 21 November). By that
construction the Jan–Mar 2027 window closes **~17–18 November 2026** and the cap is arithmetically
fixed about a week before Ofgem says it out loud. **The announcement is a confirmation, not a print.**
The live forecast is Cornwall Insight's **+9% to ~£1,872** (26 August 2026), driven by Middle East
conflict, a European heatwave, Norwegian supply disruption and low winter gas stocks — with ~9 weeks of
the window still to run at the time of writing, so direction is leanable and magnitude is not.
**None of it reaches this book.** `symbols: []`; the calendar's universe is eight US mega-caps with no
UK listing, utility, gilt or sterling instrument, and no house playbook is energy- or sterling-keyed.
The date is **estimate**; it widens caution and licenses nothing.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This entry arrived by **proposal**: the [`uk-cpi-2026-10-21`](uk-cpi-2026-10-21.md) lane filed
`proposals/ofgem-price-cap-announcement-2026-11-25.from-uk-cpi-2026-10-21.json` on 2026-09-09, closing a

[`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md).

So the question is not "does a UK energy price cap matter". It is: **what does a session actually learn
on 25 November 2026 that it did not know on 24 November — and does any of it reach a US-only,
equity-and-earnings-keyed book?**

**One-line verdict:** almost nothing, and none of it — but the *reasons* are worth the file, because two
of them correct claims the proposal carried and one of them changes how every future Ofgem entry on this
calendar should be dated.

### Leg 1 — "the date is 25 November" — **MIXED**, and this is the finding that generalises

Ofgem's October-2026 release, under *Energy price cap level dates*, reads verbatim:

> "We review and update the price cap level on how much an energy supplier can charge for each unit of
> energy, including the standing charge, every 3 months. The levels for the next periods will be
> announced by:
> 25 November 2026 – period 1 January 2027 to 31 March 2027
> 23 February 2027 – period 1 April 2027 to 30 June 2027
> 26 May 2027 – period 1 July 2027 to 30 September 2027"

The operative word is **by**, and Ofgem does not leave it implicit. Its January-2026 release (published
21 November 2025) states the discretion outright: *"We may publish before this date if we need to because
of external reasons."*

Three cycles were sourceable, and they split:

| Ofgem's stated ceiling | Actually published | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| by **25 November 2025** (Jan–Mar 2026) | **21 November 2025** — +0.2%, £1,758 | **4 days early** |
| by **25 February 2026** (Apr–Jun 2026) | **25 February 2026** — −7%, £1,641 | on the day |
| by **26 August 2026** (Oct–Dec 2026) | **26 August 2026** — +4% | on the day |

So the base rate is **2 of 3 on the stated day**, which is why the call is *keep the date* rather than
*move it*. But a ceiling that has been beaten once is not a slot, and the one early case has no stated
cause — Ofgem's "external reasons" language was not invoked publicly. This is registered as
**FT-ofgem-price-cap-announcement-2026-11-25-2** rather than argued: if 2026-11-25 is beaten too, the
right fix is to date every future Ofgem entry at the deadline and carry the early-publication risk in the
notes, which is what this file now does for the two proposals it ships.

### Leg 2 — "the announcement is when the number becomes knowable" — **REFUTED**

Ofgem's wholesale allowance runs on a **3-1.5-12[3]** indexation formula: a three-month observation
window, a ~1.5-month (30 working day) lag between the window closing and the cap period starting, a
12-month price reference period, and a three-month cap period. Ofgem's own methodology documents carry
the formula; the *dates* for a specific cycle are best evidenced by Cornwall Insight, which publishes a
"final forecast" pinned to the window:

> "Cornwall Insight has released its final forecast for the January – March 2026 Default Tariff Cap
> (price cap), following the closure of the observation window on 18 November." (18 November 2025)

Ofgem announced that cap **three days later**, on 21 November 2025. The number was determined before the
forecast was even published.

Apply the same construction to a cap period starting **1 January 2027** and the window closes
**~17–18 November 2026** — about **a week** before this event's ceiling date, a slightly wider gap than
last cycle's three days because the announcement sits later in the month. **By roughly 18 November the
January cap is arithmetic.** The announcement ratifies it.

**Honest limit, and it is the softest link in this document:** the ~17–18 November 2026 date is derived
by analogy from a dated prior cycle plus Ofgem's published formula. No Ofgem primary naming *this*
cycle's window was located. The `This month` falsifier is written against exactly that.

**An aggregator claim rejected on the record.** A search-result synthesis asserted the January 2027 cap
window ran "19 May to 18 August 2026". It traces to no primary, contradicts Cornwall Insight's own dated
statement, and contradicts the 3-1.5-12[3] lag. Not used. (This calendar's seeding already caught an
aggregator publishing a wrong CPI date; the discipline is cheap and it paid again here.)

### Leg 3 — the proposal's VAT claim — **REFUTED**, and the corrected version is more useful

The proposal wrote that this is "the last cap set while the electricity VAT zero-rating is in force" and
that "the January cap is the one quarter where a headline cap number and the bill a household actually
pays diverge by the full 5% VAT". Both need correcting.

- **On divergence.** Ofgem quotes the cap **VAT-inclusive**. With domestic electricity zero-rated
  1 October 2026 – 31 March 2027 under SI 2026/987
  ([`uk-electricity-vat-zero-rate-2026-10-01`](uk-electricity-vat-zero-rate-2026-10-01.md)), inclusive
  *equals* exclusive on the electricity leg — so **headline equals paid**. Ofgem says so plainly: "VAT is
  not included in electricity bills for people covered under the energy price cap between 1 October and
  31 December 2026", while gas stays at 5%. There is no divergence, and nothing about it is unique to
  January: **Oct–Dec 2026 is identically zero-rated**.
- **On "last cap set".** The 23 February 2027 announcement is also *made* while the zero rate is in
  force. The precise statement is that **Jan–Mar 2027 is the last cap _period_ the zero rate covers**.

**The consequence the proposal missed, and it is the point.** Because both quarters sit inside the zero
rate, **the October→January step is VAT-clean** — pure wholesale, network and policy cost. The tax
distortion lands one quarter later, in the **Apr–Jun 2027** cap, which carries a wholesale move *plus*
the mechanical 0%→5% snap-back on the electricity leg. That announcement (by **2027-02-23**) and the
control quarter after it (by **2027-05-26**) are both proposed in this PR, because without the second one
the April step cannot be decomposed into tax versus wholesale.

### Leg 4 — "the market has no view yet" — **REFUTED**

Cornwall Insight, 26 August 2026: *"bills currently forecast to rise a further 9% in the new year. This
would put an average January bill up to £1,872 a year"* — against the £1,723 October cap. The drivers it
names are wholesale, not policy: *"the conflict in the Middle East continues to keep wholesale markets
elevated"*, alongside *"a summer heatwave across Europe, supply disruption in Norway and strong demand
from Asia"* producing *"low winter gas stock levels"*.

Its own caveat is quoted rather than paraphrased, because it is the reason this document's quarter call
is medium and names no number: *"The January figure will not be confirmed until November, and there
remains a lot of time for wholesale market conditions to shift."* At the time of writing roughly **nine
weeks** of the observation window still ran.

### Leg 5 — does it reach the Bank of England, and does the Bank reach us? — **MIXED, then no**

The announcement falls **20 days after** the 2026-11-05 MPC and its November Monetary Policy Report, and
**22 days before** [`boe-decision-2026-12-17`](boe-decision-2026-12-17.md). The tempting framing — "the
Bank must set its forecast path without it" — is technically true and practically weak: the Bank
*forecasts* Ofgem cap steps from the same futures curves Ofgem indexes to, so 25 November removes the
December committee's forecast **error** on one line rather than delivering it new information. That is a
real but small effect and it is where this lane parts company with a stronger reading of the proposal.

Whether any of it reaches *this* book is not mixed at all. `symbols: []`. The calendar tracks eight US
mega-caps — AAPL, AMZN, AVGO, CRWV, GOOG, META, MRVL, MSFT — with no UK listing, no utility, no gilt and
no sterling instrument, and none of the house playbooks (S1/S2/E1/S3/S4, G1) is energy- or
sterling-keyed. There is no transmission path to size.

### What plays the conditions support

**None.** The honest sheet here is entirely refusals. What the file buys is three attribution rules — the
date is a ceiling, the number is fixed a week early, the January step is VAT-clean — plus four dated
adjacent events that did not exist on this calendar this morning.

### Honest limits

- The observation-window close for *this* cycle is derived, not fetched (Leg 2). It is the one claim here
  a future pulse should try to replace with an Ofgem primary.
- The deadline base rate is **n=3**. Three cycles is enough to say "by" is real and not enough to price it.
- The Cornwall Insight forecast is a consultancy's, not a primary; it is used as *the market's stated
  view*, which is exactly what it is, and never as a fact about the cap.
- The £1,872 and £1,758 figures may rest on different typical-consumption definitions (Ofgem revised
  its own during this period). No forward test in this file turns on comparing them.

instrument entering this calendar's universe. Absent that, no cap announcement can be sized, and this
