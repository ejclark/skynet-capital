**Is that experiment worth running, and if so what does it say — and is there anything about a March

### Leg 2 — but the arithmetic under the estimate is far stronger than the proposal claimed · **SUPPORTED**, and this corrects it

The proposal filed the estimate partly on the grounds that *"the cadence it rests on was interrupted

That framing is too pessimistic, and the seven calendars say so directly.

Computing each release date against the **first business day** of its own month (federal holidays

| Class | Count |
|---|---|
| Releases on the month's first business day | **75 of 81 (92.6%)** |

The sub-case 2027-05-03 actually needs is tighter than the general rule, because a Saturday first-of-

Year's observed, 2024-09-03 after Labor Day). The three exceptions are all backlog dates.

And the geometry is not hypothetical. **May 1 last fell on a Saturday in 2021, and the March-2021
edition released Monday 2021-05-03** — the identical shape, at the identical position in the month,
for the identical reference month. **2027-05-01 is a Saturday**, 2027-05-02 a Sunday, **2027-05-03 a
Monday** with no federal holiday anywhere near it (Memorial Day 2027 is 05-31) and a normal NYSE
session.

The honest residual is the one the proposal named correctly even if it priced it high: the *risk* to
this date is a second data lapse, not a scheduling quirk — and a second lapse would move the print by
weeks and be visible on the Census calendar months ahead.

| Measure | WITH (n=72) | WITHOUT (n=9) | p (20k permutation on medians) |

| ITB session range | 2.244% | 2.022% | 0.42 |

Eight-for-eight directional consistency is weak evidence, not strong, because these are eight highly
correlated readings of one thing. And the confound is visible in the last row: **the 9 controls are
drawn almost entirely from one contiguous post-lapse window** (2025-10 → 2026-05) that was simply a
quieter market than the pooled 72 — median VIX **16.99** against **18.94**.

Controlling for that regime **weakens the result further**, which is the decisive check. Ranking each
session's range against the 42 sessions surrounding it removes the level of volatility and keeps only
"was this session unusual *for its own moment*":

| Regime-normalised (median within-window percentile) | WITH | WITHOUT | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| SPY | 0.643 | 0.548 | **0.69** |
| ITB | 0.619 | 0.405 | 0.15 |
| XHB | 0.595 | 0.500 | 0.53 |

SPY's gap essentially disappears; XHB's halves; only ITB firms up, and to p=0.15.

**And the power run is what actually closes the question.** Bootstrapping from the observed
`WITH` distribution, imposing a true effect on a 9-session control group and re-running the same
permutation test:

| True `WITHOUT` range, as a fraction of `WITH` | Power, SPY | Power, XHB |
|---|---|---|
| 100% (no effect) | 4% | 5% |
| 80% | 2% | 11% |
| 70% | 3% | 27% |
| 60% | 10% | 55% |
| **50%** | 20% | **80%** |
| 40% | 39% | 95% |

The observed gaps sit at **77% (SPY)** and **84% (XHB)** — squarely in the region where this design
detects a real effect **2–11%** of the time. A test that reaches conventional power only if removing
one low-impact macro print *halves* a sector ETF's daily range was never going to answer this
question, and the null it returns carries almost no information about the world.

**The window is closed.** Cadence resumed 2026-06-01 and has held for seven editions; the control
group cannot grow without another shutdown. So this is the final answer available on this calendar's
data, not an interim one: **the same-minute ISM/Construction confound is permanently unresolvable
here, and the honest posture is the one the 2026-12-01 ledger already took — decline the tape claim
rather than make it.** The value of running it is that the next four construction ledgers, and the
next seven ISM ledgers, can stop reserving the test.

### Leg 4 — what is actually distinct about a **March** reference month · **SUPPORTED**, and it argues for less confidence in the headline, not more

Four construction ledgers now exist and none has looked at the reference month. This edition reports
**March 2027**, and March is not an ordinary month for this statistic.

Deriving the implied seasonal factor directly — `NSA × 12 ÷ SAAR`, from FRED's `TTLCON` against
`TTLCONS`, monthly since 2015:

| Reference month | Median unadjusted m/m | Implied seasonal factor | Factor's year-to-year sd |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jan | −4.90% | 0.8505 | 1.11pp |
| Feb | +1.10% | 0.8512 | 1.04pp |
| **Mar** | **+10.94%** | **0.9397** | **0.85pp** |
| Apr | +5.34% | 0.9869 | 0.53pp |
| May | +6.26% | 1.0402 | 0.53pp |
| Jun | +4.80% | 1.0886 | 0.37pp |
| Jul | +0.82% | 1.0987 | 0.31pp |
| Aug | +1.19% | 1.1086 | 0.19pp |
| Sep | −2.27% | 1.0796 | 0.67pp |
| Oct | −1.87% | 1.0541 | 1.20pp |
| Nov | −4.87% | 0.9928 | 0.70pp |
| Dec | −8.50% | 0.9025 | 0.54pp |

**March is the spring ramp, and it is the largest raw move of the calendar year — +10.94% median,
roughly double May's +6.26% and April's +5.34%.** All of that has to come out to produce a headline
whose median absolute move, across the 29 editions the sibling parsed, is **0.30%**.

The number that makes this a *call* rather than a curiosity: March's seasonal factor has a
year-to-year standard deviation of **0.85pp**, which on a factor of 0.9397 is ≈**0.9% of the reported
level**. That is **~3× the median headline move the report prints** and slightly **above Census's own
median stated margin of error of ±0.80%**. In plain terms — for a March reference month, plausible
year-to-year variation in the seasonal filter alone is larger than the signal the headline is
reporting.

Two honest qualifications. **March is not the worst month on factor stability** — October (1.20pp),
January (1.11pp) and February (1.04pp) are less stable; March ranks 4th. The distinction is that
March combines a middling-to-high instability with by far the largest quantity being adjusted away.
And the corroboration is **n=2**: the only March-reference months in the sibling's 19-pair
first→revised set are **Mar-2023 at +2.74% (the maximum of the set)** and **Mar-2025 at −1.55%**,
ranking 1st and 5th of 19 against a median of 0.74%. Two observations are a flag, not evidence — but
the mechanism was derived from the seasonal arithmetic *first* and the two revisions checked after,
which is the right order, and `-3` registers the prediction rather than the retrofit.

Two consequences, one methodological and one worth a reader's eye.

**Methodologically it closes off the last escape route.** A close-to-close read of 2027-05-03 contains
four scheduled releases and belongs to none of them; Leg 3 has just shown the only design that could
have isolated the 10:00 pair is dead. There is no version of this session that yields an attributable
construction claim.

**Substantively, one adjacency is genuinely apt and costs nothing to note.** SLOOS at 14:00 reports
banks' net tightening on **commercial-real-estate and residential-mortgage lending standards** — the
credit input to exactly the activity this 10:00 print measures as output, six-to-eighteen months
downstream. Reading the two together on the same Monday is a better use of the session than reading
either headline alone. That is a reading suggestion, not a signal, and it produces no trigger.

One structural note on the nowcast, flagged as **derived**: BEA's advance Q1-2027 estimate would fall
in late April 2027 on the ordinary cadence, so a 2027-05-03 construction print would land among the
**first** inputs to the Q2-2027 GDPNow rather than mid-quarter as the 2026-12-01 edition did. The
tempting inference — that an early-quarter print therefore moves the model more — was **pre-tested by
the sibling and failed**: first-of-forecast-quarter construction vintages (n=49) move structures
0.0547pp against 0.0510pp for later ones (n=89), **p=0.869**. It is recorded here so the next session
does not re-derive the story, and `-4` is registered at the pooled bar rather than an early-quarter
one. `bea.gov/news/schedule` carries no 2027 rows (fetched today, recorded in `probe-ref.blocked`),
