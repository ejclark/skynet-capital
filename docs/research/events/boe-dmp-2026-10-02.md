### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is the publisher's own, three times over.** SUPPORTED, primary, three independent
   pages fetched today. The Bank's **September 2026** DMP page carries no data and reads verbatim
   *"This monthly Decision Maker Panel data will be published on 2 October 2026."* The **August
   2026** release page (*"Published on 04 September 2026"*) carries *"Next Release: 2 October
   2026."* The panel's own site reads *"Next month's Decision Maker Panel data release – 2nd
   October 2026."* The date is not in doubt; the `estimate` label is about this lane's
   no-self-confirm limit and the taxonomy's missing non-Fed central-bank prefix, not the evidence.

2. **The proposal named the wrong cell, and the committee's own minutes say which one is right.**
   SUPPORTED, primary, and this is the finding. The **30 July 2026** MPC minutes (fetched today)
   mention the DMP exactly once, verbatim: *"own-price expectations in the DMP survey, wage-price
   feedback and food inflation, pointed to a likelihood of limited second-round effects to come."*
   The load-bearing cell is **expected own-price growth one year ahead** — what firms expect to
   charge — not expected wage growth. That is coherent with the committee's actual worry as its own
   minutes state it: *"The risk of material second-round effects in price and wage-setting, against
   which policy needs to lean, is greater the longer higher energy prices persist."* Second-round
   effects run energy → prices → wages; the DMP's price cell sits at the front of that chain and the
   ONS wage data sits at the back. The proposal inherited the wage framing from its parent lane,
   which was researching a **wage** print. Registered as **FT-boe-dmp-2026-10-02-1**.

3. **The release is damped by construction, on two independent mechanisms.** SUPPORTED, primary on
   both. **(a) Three-month moving averages.** The Bank's own release wording is not a point
   estimate: the August page reads *"3.1% in the three months to August, down from 3.4% in the three
   months to July"*, and the July page reads *"fell to 3.4% in the three months to July, down from
   3.7%."* Two of the 10-02 release's three months were therefore already published on 09-04.
   Arithmetically, moving a 3mma headline by 0.3pp requires the newly-added September month to come
   in roughly **0.9pp** away from the July month it displaces. **(b) A rotating third of the panel.**
   `decisionmakerpanel.co.uk/about-the-panel` (fetched today) states *"Each month we send one of
   three sets of questions to one third of the panel members"*, with questions *"focussing on one or
   more of these topics on a rotating basis"*, against *"approximately 10,400 businesses"* on the
   panel; the August release drew **2,012** responses (fieldwork 7–21 August) and July **1,982**
   (fieldwork 3–17 July). Smoothing over three months a survey whose sample rotates monthly is what
   makes these series move in 0.1pp steps, which is exactly what they have done.

4. **The series confirms the damping — and the fast cell is CPI expectations, not wages.**
   SUPPORTED, primary, both release pages fetched today. Across the last two publications:
   expected own-price growth **3.9% → 3.8%**; realised own-price growth **3.8% → 3.7%**; expected
   wage growth **3.4% → 3.4%**; realised wage growth **4.0% → 4.0%**; CPI expectations three years
   ahead **2.8% → 2.8%**. One cell moved fast: CPI expectations one year ahead ran **3.7% → 3.4% →
   3.1%** across the three months to June, July and August — **−0.6pp in two publications** — while
   the three-year anchor did not move at all. That combination is the textbook read of an energy
   shock as a **level shift rather than a regime change**, and it is the most supportive evidence in
   this survey for the six-member majority's hold.

5. **One cell is moving the other way, and it is the one the dissenters would cite.** SUPPORTED,
   primary, with a named limit. On the energy-shock supplementary questions, the **July** release
   reports *"55% of firms expected to increase prices (down 9 percentage points since April), while
   5% expected to lower prices"*; the **August** release reports *"price increases expected by 59%
   of firms (down 5 percentage points since April)"*. Both anchor to the same April base — 64% —
   which cross-checks the two readings against each other, and the July→August move is a **4pp
   re-acceleration**. Alongside it, margin compression eased (**61%** expecting lower profit
   margins, *"down from 68%"*) and the wage channel netted near zero (**19%** expecting energy to
   push wages up against **17%** down). **Named limit:** these are supplementary questions asked
   against an April baseline, not obviously reported on the same three-month-average basis as the
   headline cells, and the rotating question sets mean there is no guarantee they are asked again on
   10-02. Registered as **FT-boe-dmp-2026-10-02-2**.

6. **The publication calendar is opaque beyond one release, and it has already broken its own
   pattern this year.** SUPPORTED, primary. The dates listed on the September announcement page and
   confirmed on the release pages themselves: May-2026 data published **5 June**, June-2026 data
   **3 July**, July-2026 data **24 July** — *"Published on 24 July 2026"*, same month as its own
   reference period — and then **nothing until 4 September**, a six-week gap with no August
   publication. Each release names only the next one (*"Next Release: 2 October 2026"*). So a reader
   standing on 2026-09-09 **cannot know** whether a public DMP lands before the **2026-11-05** MPC
   decision and Monetary Policy Report; assuming a first-Friday cadence would have predicted a
   release in August that did not happen. The 10-02 release's own next-release line is the one place
   that answer will exist. Registered as **FT-boe-dmp-2026-10-02-3**.

7. **The forward employment cell disagrees with the ONS backward one, and that is worth watching
   rather than resolving.** MIXED. The August DMP reports realised annual employment growth
   **−0.2%** (*"up from −0.4%"*) and expectations for the year ahead at **+0.1%** — a turn from
   negative to positive. The ONS August bulletin, per the

8. **The day is crowded and cleanly ordered, and the ordering is the practical output.** SUPPORTED.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `estimate`):** **stand aside completely, and re-point the reader from the wage cell
to the price cell.** This book holds nothing with a sterling or UK-policy channel and none is
proposed; the `low` tier is argued in leg 9, not inherited. The ledger's three substantive positions
are all *analytical*, not positional. First, the **cell correction**: the proposal that created this
entry framed the DMP as the MPC's forward *wage* survey, and the committee's own July minutes name
**own-price expectations in the DMP survey** — last **3.8%** year-ahead — as the leg of its
second-round-effects argument. Second, this release is **damped by construction** and should be read
with expectations set accordingly: three-month moving averages over a rotating third of a ~10,400-firm
panel produce 0.1pp steps, and the ledger's month call (own-price 3.6–3.9%, expected wage growth
3.3–3.5%) is a prediction about the *mechanism*, not about the UK economy. Third, the **calendar is
the scarce information**: the DMP publishes one release ahead, already skipped a month in 2026
(24 July, then 4 September), and whether the **11-05** MPC decision and MPR get a fresh public read
is unknown until 10-02 says so. Estimates widen caution and license nothing.
