## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **"Third estimate" is a misnomer here.** 2026-09-30 is the day BEA's **2026 Annual
Update** lands — for the first time ever, national, industry *and* regional accounts on one day —
rewriting GDP, GDI, corporate profits and the **PCE price index from Q1 2021 through Q1 2026**,
including a changed deflation method for three PCE categories. So the "revisions this late are
noise" prior is wrong twice over: the two most recent third estimates each moved **+0.5pp**
(Q2 2025 3.3→3.8; Q1 2026 1.6→2.1), and this one arrives with five years of history moving under
it. None of that is a trade — `symbols: []`, no house playbook is macro-keyed. **What changed on
09-05: the shutdown branch is gone.** The House passed the Senate CR to **Dec 11** on 09-01 by
**370–48** and it is reported signed, so this release is no longer "the last federal GDP vintage
before a data blackout" — 10-02 payrolls, 10-14 CPI and the 10-29 Q3 advance estimate all survive,
and the scarcity half of this doc's thesis retires. What remains is the plain one: the revised
**history** is what the **10-28 FOMC** decides on, and that meeting now sits behind a genuinely live
09-16 hike-or-hold vote. Date **confirmed**, BEA schedule re-fetched verbatim 2026-09-15; every
magnitude below is estimate-grade.

**What changed on 09-15: the magnitude stopped being unknown, and the policy channel opened 27 days
early.** Leg 5 said the PCE-price channel was "SUPPORTED as a mechanism, UNKNOWN as a magnitude."
Four independent sizings now exist and they do not agree: Underlying Inflation LLC ~**10bp**
(component-by-component, 06-29), Bloomberg Economics **−13bp** on May data, Goldman Sachs and
JPMorgan both **May core 3.4% → 3.2–3.3%** — and Governor **Waller**, on **09-03**, **"a few tenths
of a percentage point"**, above every published estimate. He cited the *pending, unpublished*
revision as a reason he is comfortable holding. So the revision is already inside a governor's

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

What will the Q2 2026 GDP third estimate say on 2026-09-30, how much can a "third" estimate really
move — and does anything in that release change how we read the consumer, the inflation path, or the
AI-capex cycle we actually hold?

**One-line verdict.** The release is mislabelled by its own schedule line: it is the **2026 Annual
Update**, which rewrites five years of GDP, GDI, corporate profits and PCE prices, so the small-
revision prior does not apply — but the payload that matters is the revised *history* (PCE price
index, saving rate), not the Q2 headline, and none of it is tradeable in a `symbols: []` macro print.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md)
(macro-print mode: `symbols: []`, so no `earnings-cycle.mjs` / `intraday-edges.mjs` run and the
cache-bust rule does not apply). Primary sources preferred and fetched directly — bea.gov's release
schedule, its 2026-annual-update information page, its 2026-08-17 blog post, the June 2026 *Survey
of Current Business* preview, and the BEA news releases for the Q2 2025 and Q1 2026 third estimates.
The Q2 2025 third-estimate PDF returned as unreadable binary; its figures were taken from BEA's own
HTML news page instead. Sibling ledgers (`gdp-q2-2026-second-2026-08-26`,
`gdp-q3-2026-advance-2026-10-29`, `government-funding-deadline-2026-09-30`, `fomc-2026-09-16`) are
cited for regime rather than re-litigated.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date, time and payload are right — SUPPORTED.** bea.gov/news/schedule (fetched 2026-08-29)
   lists verbatim "**September 30, 8:30 AM — GDP (Third Estimate), Industries, Corporate Profits,
   State GDP, and State Personal Income, 2nd Quarter 2026; State PCE, 2025**", matching the calendar
   entry's `confirmed`/`BEA:` label exactly. Note the schedule line does **not** carry the words
   "Annual Update" the way the 2025 equivalent's release title did — it names the *contents*
   (industries, state GDP, state personal income, state PCE) instead. Anyone screening the schedule
   by title alone would mis-size this release; that is leg 2.

2. **It is the 2026 Annual Update, and the update period is Q1 2021 → Q1 2026 — SUPPORTED
   (three primary sources).** BEA's own information page: "the 2026 annual updates of national,
   industry, and regional data will begin on the same day for the first time: **September 30,
   2026**", with the update running "from the first quarter of 2021 through the first quarter of
   2026". BEA's 2026-08-17 blog post repeats the date and adds that "some state and county
   statistics will be updated back to 2009 or 2013". The June 2026 SCB preview confirms scope —
   "revisions to gross domestic product (GDP), GDP by industry, gross domestic income, and related
   components" — plus new source data (Census Annual Integrated Economic Survey 2023, IRS Statistics
   of Income 2023, federal budget data) and **methodological changes**: composite rather than single
   price indexes for portfolio management services, legal services, and computer software and
   accessories. The concurrent national+industry+regional release is a first.

3. **The "revisions this late are noise" prior is REFUTED for this cycle.** BEA's own news releases:
   the Q2 2025 third estimate (2025-09-25 — itself an annual-update release) revised real GDP **up
   0.5pp, 3.3% → 3.8%**, "primarily reflecting an upward revision to consumer spending"; the Q1 2026
   third estimate (2026-06-25) revised **up 0.5pp, 1.6% → 2.1%**, primarily a downward revision to
   imports. The Q3 2025 updated estimate moved only +0.1pp, so the recent set is +0.5 / +0.1 / +0.5 —
   two of the last three at 0.5pp, both upward. The sibling second-estimate ledger's ~0.59pp
   advance→second stdev is therefore the *right order of magnitude* for this leg of the revision
   chain too, not an over-estimate. **Estimate-grade:** three prints are a pattern, not a base rate,
   and no consensus for the Q2 2026 figure exists at D-32.

4. **But the annual update historically moves *history*, not *growth rates* — MIXED, and that split
   is the whole read.** The 2025 annual update (SCB, Nov 2025) left average annual real GDP growth
   over 2019–2024 **unchanged at 2.4%**; the largest single-year revisions were **0.1pp** (2020
   −2.2%→−2.1%, 2021 6.1%→6.2%), 2022–2024 unrevised, and even the pandemic collapse moved only
   0.1pp. What *did* move: the **personal saving rate**, revised up **+0.4 / +0.3 / +0.9 / +0.9pp**
   for 2021/2022/2023/2024; **real GDI** growth revised down 2.3% → 2.2%; the **PCE price index**
   revised **up 0.1pp** over 2019Q4–2025Q1. So the honest expectation for 09-30 is a broadly intact
   growth path with the consumer's balance sheet and the inflation history re-cut underneath it.

5. **The PCE-price channel is the one with a policy consumer — SUPPORTED as a mechanism, UNKNOWN as
   a magnitude.** The same 08:30 tick carries "Personal Income and Outlays, August 2026"
   (sibling `pce-2026-09-30`), whose monthly series the annual update also revises. Method changes
   to three PCE categories propagate into the **Fed's preferred inflation gauge back to Q1 2021**,
   and the first meeting to decide on the rewritten series is **2026-10-28** — after the 09-16 FOMC,
   which decides on the old one. Chair Warsh's 2026-08-28 Jackson Hole keynote leaned on AI as a
   growth "hinge point" while questioning published forecasts as a tool ("works better in theory
   than in practice"), which raises rather than lowers the weight a revised *actual* series carries
   in that reaction function. **Honest scope:** the software method change is documented on the
   **consumer** (PCE) side; nothing found confirms a matching change to business software-investment
   deflators, so no AI-capex measurement claim is made from it.

6. **This print is lapse-proof; it is the corridor around it that is fragile — SUPPORTED.** FY2026
   funds lapse at the end of 2026-09-30 (sibling `government-funding-deadline-2026-09-30`; Senate CR
   to Dec 11 passed 08-08 90–6, House CR to Dec 4 passed 07-21 220–205, neither enacted as of
   08-29, House back ~08-31). An 08:30 ET release on 09-30 publishes **hours before** any lapse
   begins — so in the shutdown branch this becomes the **frozen official picture of the US economy**,
   the vintage every model runs on until BEA resumes, with the 10-02 payrolls gone and the 10-29 Q3
   advance estimate at risk of outright cancellation (BEA cancelled the Q3 2025 advance estimate in
   the FY2026 lapse). Four tracked events land 09-30 alone (this, PCE, Chicago PMI, the deadline),
   eight within the 09-29 → 10-02 corridor including MU's print and payrolls.

### What the conditions support

Nothing directional. `symbols: []`, no house playbook applies, and the market-reaction precedent from
the sibling second-estimate close-out is unambiguous: on 2026-08-26 the GDP revision drew a one-line
mention in every recap checked while PCE and NVDA owned the narrative. Expect the same burial here,
with the same-day PCE print again the louder input. What travels is a **reading protocol**: on 09-30,
read the annual update's revision tables before the Q2 headline — the PCE price index path, the
personal saving rate, and the restated corporate-profits series (Q2's +$400.9B, which already
restated Q1 from +$40.4B to +$74.4B between vintages) — and carry those into the 10-28 FOMC read and
the next NVDA/AVGO/MRVL pulse checks as context, never as an entry.

### Honest limits

No consensus or whisper exists for the Q2 2026 third estimate at D-32, and none is expected before
mid-September (revision whispers are rare). BEA publishes no forecast of its own update's direction —
the SCB preview explicitly quantifies nothing — so leg 4's expectation is a one-year base rate from
2025, not a forecast; a five-year rewrite incorporating a new Census annual survey can in principle
move more than 2025's did, in either direction. The Q2 2025 third-estimate PDF was unreadable to the
fetch tool (figures taken from BEA's HTML page instead); Atlanta Fed GDPNow was not consulted this
pass (it nowcasts Q3, not a Q2 revision). No claim is made about business-investment software
deflators (leg 5). Macro-print mode runs no price instruments — every figure above is sourced
reporting or a primary BEA release, dated 2026-08-29, not our own price data.
