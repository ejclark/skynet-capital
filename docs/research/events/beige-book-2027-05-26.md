### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is right, but the rule the proposal used to get it is wrong 18% of the time —
   SUPPORTED, with the inherited leg corrected.** The proposing sweep
   ([`fomc-minutes-2027-05-19`](fomc-minutes-2027-05-19.md), 2026-09-08) asserted "the pre-FOMC
   Beige Book publishes at exactly D-14 from the decision day, reproduced 8 of 8 against 2026."
   Re-measured here from scratch rather than inherited: release dates for **129 editions
   2007–2026** were parsed out of the Board's own archive pages (three URL conventions coexist —
   `BeigeBook_YYYYMMDD.pdf`, `/fomc/beigebook/YYYY/YYYYMMDD/`, and `fullreport<YYYYMMDD>.pdf`) and
   paired against **107 FOMC meetings** parsed from the live `fomccalendars.htm` panels plus all
   fourteen `fomchistorical<year>.htm` pages. **Decision-day-minus-14 holds 88 of 107 (82.2%).**
   The gap distribution is 13 days ×14, 14 days ×44, 15 days ×3 on the complete pre-2021 subsample
   — it varies with the meeting's own weekday, which is the tell. The invariant it is a shadow of:
   **all 129 releases are a Wednesday, without a single exception**, and the edition is the
   Wednesday of the second week before the meeting's week, which holds **107 of 107**. (One
   apparent miss is a parse artefact of the Board's own `May 30-1` rendering of the April 30 – May 1
   2019 meeting; predicted 2019-04-17, actual 2019-04-17.) Applied to 2027: **Jan 13 · Mar 3 · Apr
   14 · May 26 · Jul 14 · Sep 1 · Oct 13 · Nov 24**. The two formulations agree on **2027-05-26**
   only because June 8–9 is a Tuesday–Wednesday meeting — so the date survives a stronger test than
   the one that produced it, and the correction is worth banking for every future edition where the
   meeting is not Tue–Wed.

2. **The status stays `estimate`, and not for want of a fetch — SUPPORTED.** Three independent
   reasons, all re-checked today. `beige-book-default.htm` (200, 86,202 bytes) carries **no 2027
   editions at all** — its list ends at the 2026 set (Jan 14 · Mar 4 · Apr 15 · Jun 3 · Jul 15 ·
   Sep 2 · Oct 14 · Nov 25, the first six now carrying HTML/PDF links). `newsevents/2027-may.htm`
   and `newsevents/2027-june.htm` both **404**. And `fomccalendars.htm` states verbatim that "Each
   meeting date is tentative until confirmed at the meeting immediately preceding it," so the
   underlying June 8–9 meeting is itself tentative until the **2027-04-27/28** meeting ratifies it.
   This is the opposite situation from [`beige-book-2026-11-25`](beige-book-2026-11-25.md), which
   promoted to `confirmed` on 2026-08-31 because that session held **two** Board primaries naming
   the date. Here there are zero. The promotion trigger is the Board posting a 2027 schedule, not a
   better fetch.

3. **The release time is 2:00 p.m. ET, read rather than assumed — SUPPORTED.** The 2027 month pages
   404, so the convention was verified on the most recent month page that exists:

   `Beige Book | Time: | Release Date(s): | 2:00 p.m. | Beige Book | 2`. Every sibling ledger in
   this series carries the same time. Recorded as convention-plus-one-primary, not as a 2027 fact.

4. **The preparing Reserve Bank is predictable nine editions out, and it is Dallas — SUPPORTED, and
   this is the leg that gives the edition a lens.** Each edition's national summary opens "This
   report was prepared at the Federal Reserve Bank of ⟨X⟩ based on information collected on or
   before ⟨date⟩." Twenty-seven summaries were fetched and parsed. From **2024-07-17 onward the
   rotation is an exact, unbroken 12-District cycle**: Richmond → Cleveland → New York → Kansas
   City → Chicago → Minneapolis → Atlanta → St. Louis → Boston → Philadelphia → San Francisco →
   Dallas. Eighteen consecutive editions confirm it, and the second lap reproduces the first
   exactly — 2024-07-17 Richmond / 2026-01-14 Richmond, 2024-09-04 Cleveland / 2026-03-04
   Cleveland, 2024-10-23 New York / 2026-04-15 New York, 2024-12-04 Kansas City / 2026-06-03 Kansas
   City, 2025-01-15 Chicago / 2026-07-15 Chicago, 2025-03-05 Minneapolis / 2026-09-02 Minneapolis.
   Nine steps on from Minneapolis (2026-10-14 Atlanta · 2026-11-25 St. Louis · 2027-01-13 Boston ·
   2027-03-03 Philadelphia · 2027-04-14 San Francisco) puts **Dallas** on **2027-05-26**. Why it
   matters rather than being trivia: the preparing Bank writes the national summary and chooses its
   framing, and Dallas's District contains the ERCOT power and data-center build-out this book's
   AI-infra sleeve tracks. Dallas also prepared **2025-11-26**, the edition

   2023 sequence runs Richmond → Chicago → Minneapolis → St. Louis → Atlanta, which is not this
   permutation. The cycle is a convention the Board can re-shuffle, which is exactly why this is

5. **The collection cutoff is a modal convention, not the nine-day rule the siblings recorded —
   MIXED, correcting an inherited leg.** [`beige-book-2026-11-25`](beige-book-2026-11-25.md) leg 2
   called nine days "a strong convention" on three data points. Measured here on **27 editions**
   (2023-04-19 through 2026-09-02), release-minus-cutoff is **9 days in 20**, **12 days in 6**, and
   **7 days once** (the 2026-06-03 edition, cutoff May 27). The nine-day cases land on a **Monday**
   and the twelve-day cases on the preceding **Friday** — so the convention is better stated as *the
   Monday nine days before, slipping to the prior Friday about a quarter of the time*, and the tail
   runs **earlier**, never later. Applied here: this edition's books shut on or about **2027-05-17**,
   with **2027-05-14** the plausible early case and **2027-05-19** the outer bound on this entire
   record. All three fall **on or before 2027-05-19**, the day the April-meeting minutes publish —
   so the two scheduled Fed communications in this corridor are sequential but not cumulative: the
   Beige Book's District evidence closes before the minutes reach the tape, and it publishes seven
   days after them.

6. **It does not move the tape, and the decomposition says why the appearance of movement is
   spurious — SUPPORTED, and this replaces a carried-forward 2005 citation with a direct
   measurement.** The sibling ledgers rest this leg on Zavodny & Ginther (*Southern Economic
   Journal* 2005), read from source by the 2026-09-02 session. Measured directly here on `^GSPC`
   daily bars (cache busted, last bar 2026-09-08), **128 Beige Book Wednesdays vs 888
   non-Beige-Book Wednesdays**, 2007-01-01 → 2026-09-08, permutation p-values at 20,000 shuffles:

   | Window | Release Wednesdays | Base Wednesdays | Ratio | p |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | Overnight (prior close → open) | 0.268% | 0.198% | 1.35× | **0.016** |
   | Session (open → close) — *contains the 14:00 release* | 0.770% | 0.690% | 1.12× | 0.31 |
   | Full day (close → close) | 0.920% | 0.763% | 1.21× | 0.070 |

   The causal direction is pinned by the split: **a 2:00 p.m. release cannot cause the gap that
   opened at 09:30 that morning.** The only significant elevation sits entirely in the pre-release
   overnight, which means Beige Book Wednesdays are *calendar-dense* days — they sit two weeks
   before an FOMC meeting, in the macro-heavy stretch that implies — and the marginal full-day
   result (p=0.07) inherits that clustering rather than measuring the report. Directionally there is
   nothing either: up-share 57.8% (n=128) vs 54.4% (n=888). **Honest limits on this measurement:**
   daily bars cannot isolate the 14:00–16:00 window, so the session test is an upper bound — if the
   release moved the tape it would have to show up inside `open → close`, and it does not; and the
   base includes FOMC decision Wednesdays, which *inflates* the base and therefore biases the test
   toward finding an effect that still is not there.
