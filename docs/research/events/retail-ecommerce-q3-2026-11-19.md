## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The proposal that created this event made one substantive claim: the monthly `retail-sales-2026-09-16`
ledger's entire base case rests on ~**+0.41pp** of headline lift from nonstore normalizing after
Amazon moved Prime Day out of July, and *"both of its registered forward tests depend on that
assumption without measuring it."* The Q3 2026 quarterly e-commerce report is, it argued, *"the
quarterly settlement of the monthly argument"* — the reference quarter that *"CONTAINS the Prime-Day
shift on both sides."* **Is the date right, is this print the settlement it claims to be, and does
anything downstream read it?**

**One-line verdict:** the date is right and now rests on three primaries instead of one; the
settlement claim is **REFUTED four separate ways**, one of which is a sign error in the proposal's own
premise; and nothing downstream reads this print at all.

### Leg 2 — "the quarter that contains the shift on both sides" · **REFUTED — the sign is inverted**

Prime Day 2026 ran **June 23–26**. June is in **Q2**. The consequences run the other way from the
proposal's framing:

- **Q2 2026 (Apr–Jun) gained** an event it did not have in Q2 2025 → its **+3.8% q/q** and **+12.2%
  y/y**, both the highest of the last five quarters, are *flattered*.
- **Q3 2026 (Jul–Sep) lost** an event it *did* have in Q3 2025 (Prime Day 2025 ran in July) → the Q3
  print faces a hit on **both** the q/q (against a flattered Q2) and the y/y (against a Prime-Day
  base).
- **No fall Amazon event is announced for 2026**, so nothing offsets inside Q3. (Prime Big Deal Days
  has run in early October — Q4 — every year since 2022 anyway.)

So 2026-11-19 is the **payback quarter**, not the settlement quarter. It is where the distortion
*lands*, which is precisely why it cannot be the referee for whether the distortion was real.

### Leg 3 — can a quarterly aggregate detect a Prime-Day move at all? · **REFUTED, on the one natural experiment that exists**

**2021 is the only prior year Prime Day left July for June** (June 21–22, 2021; the 2020 move was to
October and is hopelessly confounded by COVID — Q2 2020 e-commerce q/q was **+32.6%**). If the
quarterly series can see this at all, 2021 is where it shows.

Measured from `tsadjustedsales.xlsx`, e-commerce SA q/q, third quarters since 2015:

| 2015 | 2016 | 2017 | 2018 | 2019 | 2020 | **2021** | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| +3.5 | +3.3 | +3.3 | +2.2 | +6.3 | +2.0 | **−1.6** | +1.6 | +2.0 | +2.2 | +2.5 |

At first read that looks like a clean hit: **−1.6% is the only negative Q3 in eleven years**. It is
not, and the disproof is one column over. Netting out total retail (e-commerce q/q **minus** total
q/q) isolates what e-commerce did *relative to the shelf it sits on*, and across 2021 that series
reads:

| 2021Q1 | **2021Q2** | 2021Q3 | 2021Q4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| −0.9 | **−2.3** | −0.3 | +0.4 |

**Q2 2021 — the quarter that gained Prime Day and should have been the flattered one — posted the
worst relative growth of the entire year, six times worse than Q3's.** Against a Q3 relative median
of **+2.00pp** ex-2020/21 (n=9, range +0.6 to +5.1), 2021's Q3 shortfall is ~2.3pp — but 2021's Q1
and Q2 shortfalls, with no payback to explain them, are 2.9pp and 4.3pp. The 2021 reopening rotation
out of goods and online, into services, swamps the Prime-Day term completely and points the *wrong
way* in the quarter the hypothesis needs.

**The mechanical reason this generalizes**: CB26-133's Table 2 puts the standard error on the
e-commerce q/q change at **0.2–0.5pp**, with the release's own 90% confidence statement at **±0.4pp**.
A 4-day promotional event redistributed across a 92-day quarter is a small multiple of that, and
anything else happening in the quarter is a large one. The quarterly report can *report* Q3; it
cannot *attribute* it.

### Leg 4 — does this report isolate the monthly nonstore line? · **REFUTED, from the report's own survey description**

The proposal called this *"the only published Census measure that isolates the single line item the
proposing ledger's entire arithmetic rests on"* — the monthly **nonstore retailers** line, NAICS 454.
CB26-133's survey description says otherwise, twice:

> "A stratified simple random sampling method is used to select approximately 10,800 retail firms
> **excluding food services** … **Coverage includes all retailers whether or not they are engaged in
> e-commerce.**"

Two distinct mismatches:

- **The numerator is wider than nonstore.** E-commerce is collected from every retailer, so it
  includes the online channel of store-based retailers (a department store's website) and excludes
  parts of nonstore that are not online. It overlaps NAICS 454; it is not NAICS 454.
- **The denominator is narrower than the monthly headline.** This report's "total retail sales" —
  **$1,986.5B** for Q2 2026, ~$662B/month — **excludes food services**, where the monthly headline
  (July 2026: **$763.6B**) includes them. The share this report publishes is therefore taken against
  a different base than any monthly figure in the sibling ledger.

A third mismatch is worth flagging for anyone comparing across the 2025 boundary: **nonemployers were
removed from this series at the April 2025 annual revision**, to align with the AIES. Census restated
the history, so the series is internally consistent — but any pre-2025 quote of it from an outside
source is not comparable to today's.

### Leg 5 — is the +12.2% y/y the corroboration the sibling ledger took it for? · **MIXED**

The `retail-sales-2026-09-16` ledger cited Q2's **+12.2% y/y** against the monthly headline's
**+5.0%** as *"the first independent support for the mechanical base case."* The direction survives;
the magnitude does not.

Stacking two years to strip the base effect (same series, same table):

| Window | Level change | Two-year annualized | Headline y/y |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q2 2022 → Q2 2024 | $247,967M → $288,822M | **7.92%** | 7.1% |
| Q2 2023 → Q2 2025 | $269,738M → $303,284M | **6.04%** | 5.0% |
| **Q2 2024 → Q2 2026** | **$288,822M → $340,244M** | **8.54%** | **12.2%** |

E-commerce **is** accelerating — 8.54% annualized against 6.04% a year ago is a real trend change.
But the honest number is **~8.5%, not 12.2%**; the headline is flattered by roughly 3.7pp of soft
2025 base plus the pulled-in June event. **MIXED**, not REFUTED: the sibling's leg holds in sign and
is overstated in size, and the correct restatement is that nonstore's trend is ~3.5pp above the
monthly headline, not ~7pp.

### Leg 7 — the share, and the denominator nobody has priced · **the one live question**

The proposal's most interesting claim is that the *share* is the clean metric, because *"a share is
unmoved by an inflator that lifts numerator and denominator together."* The symmetry does not hold
here, and it fails in the direction that matters.

This report's total retail is NAICS 44–45 excluding food services, which **includes gasoline
stations**. Gasoline is ~0% e-commerce. So a gas-price rise inflates the **denominator only** and
mechanically pushes the e-commerce share **down** — the exact inflator the sibling ledger has been
sizing all quarter (Brent **$98.61** on 2026-09-08 after strikes on Saudi facilities; ~**+0.23pp** of
mechanical August headline lift from pump prices alone).

Against that, the base rate is strongly one-directional. SA e-commerce share, Q2 → Q3 change:

| 2015 | 2016 | 2017 | 2018 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| +0.2 | +0.2 | +0.2 | +0.1 | +0.5 | **−1.4** | **−0.1** | +0.3 | +0.1 | +0.1 | +0.1 |

**Nine of eleven Q3s gained share**; the two exceptions are COVID's reopening (2020) and the same
confounded 2021. So the share test has a real base rate on one side and a named, sized mechanism on
the other — which is what makes it worth registering rather than asserting.

### Honest limits

- **No release-day tape test was run.** FRED, Yahoo and stooq were all unavailable (recorded above),
  so this ledger has no daily index closes and therefore no session-behavior measurement. It does not
  substitute a sibling's number for one it could not fetch. The stand-aside call rests on structure
  (no PFEI, no nowcast, no playbook, `symbols: []`), not on a tape null.
- **One natural experiment is one observation.** Leg 3's refutation rests on 2021 alone — 2020's move
  is unusable. It is corroborated by the report's own published error bars, but "n=1 plus an error
  bar" is what it is.
- **The Prime Day 2026 date is inherited, not re-fetched.** `aboutamazon.com` 404'd here; June 23–26

  Leg 2 inverts again.
- **No Q3 in the base-rate sample except 2021 lost a July Prime Day.** The +1.6%-to-+6.3% band is
  drawn from ten quarters that all had one, which is why `FT-…-1` is graded medium rather than high.
- **The Q4 2026 edition has no published date.** `E-Creleasedates.xls` stops at the Q3 row, so this
  ledger cannot propose the February 2027 edition it names as the genuinely lapse-exposed one.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stand aside on the event, and stop treating 2026-11-19 as the referee for the monthly retail
argument.** The date is **`confirmed`** on three Census primaries. The report is not a Principal
Federal Economic Indicator, carries no AIES benchmark (this series benchmarks in April), is named
**zero** times in **1,871** GDPNow vintages with no vintage posted on its date, attaches to **zero**
house playbooks, and lands **two days after** the complete Q3 monthly nonstore series is already
public.

The proposal's date work stands and was extended from one primary to three. Its analytic premise
does not: **Q3 2026 is the quarter that lost Prime Day, not the quarter that settles it**; the one
prior July→June move (2021) left no separable fingerprint in this series, and pointed the wrong way
in the quarter the hypothesis needed; the report measures a wider numerator and a narrower
denominator than the monthly line it was said to isolate; and the **+12.2%** y/y that corroborated
the sibling's base case annualizes to **8.54%** on a two-year stack. The useful residue is real and
smaller than claimed: nonstore's trend runs ~3.5pp above the monthly headline, and the honest way to
read 11-19 is the **share**, whose denominator carries a gasoline term the proposal's symmetry
argument missed.

**Kill switches — what would move this stance:**

- A macro- or commerce-keyed playbook landing in `docs/plans/trade-playbooks.md` before
  **2026-11-01**; today there are none.
