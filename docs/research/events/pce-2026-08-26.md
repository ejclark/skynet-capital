## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** what does July 2026 PCE — the Fed's actual preferred inflation
gauge, distinct from CPI — look likely to show, how has the market reacted to recent PCE prints,
and does the new Fed chair's stated skepticism of PCE itself change the reaction function, on a
day that also carries GDP, a Treasury auction, and NVDA's earnings?

**One-line verdict:** the print itself is whisper-priced flat-to-slightly-cooler with almost no
upside tail, so in isolation it's a low-drama release — but it lands inside the single most
crowded macro-plus-earnings day on the calendar, two days before a new chair's first Jackson Hole
keynote, and that chair has gone on record discounting the exact metric this event delivers.
House answer is guard-shaped, not directional: no new rate-duration entries into 08-26, and
NVDA-adjacent risk is governed by the NVDA doc's own S1/S2 discipline, not this one.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md)
(macro-print mode: no price instruments). Primary sources over aggregators — bea.gov schedule and
the actual May/June 2026 BEA press releases fetched directly; a live prediction market
(Polymarket) used for the whisper since no broad economist survey for July PCE was findable at
D-7; press (CNBC, Fortune, CNN, techtimes) used for reaction narration and Fed-chair framing, each
claim dated. All figures checked 2026-08-19.

**Conviction legs, tested:**

1. **The date and slot are right — SUPPORTED.** bea.gov/news/schedule (fetched 2026-08-19) lists
   "Personal Income and Outlays, July 2026" at 08:30 AM ET on **2026-08-26**, matching the
   calendar entry exactly — and the same schedule page lists the **GDP (Second Estimate), Q2
   2026** at the identical date/time slot, confirming the calendar's separate
   `gdp-q2-2026-second-2026-08-26` entry is correctly paired.
2. **The whisper is flat, with almost no upside tail — SUPPORTED, thin source.** A Polymarket
   market titled "Core PCE YoY – July 2026" (fetched 2026-08-19, last updated same day, ~$46.5k
   total volume) prices **3.3% y/y at 83%**, **3.2% at 16%**, every other bucket (≤3.0% through
   ≥3.6%) under 1% combined. That's a single thin prediction market standing in for a street
   survey — directional, not authoritative (see Honest limits).
3. **The trend line supports the flat whisper — SUPPORTED (BEA primary, two data points).** May
   2026 data (bea.gov, released 2026-06-25): headline PCE **+0.4% m/m / 4.1% y/y**, core **+0.3%
   m/m / 3.4% y/y**. June 2026 data (bea.gov, released 2026-07-30): headline **-0.1% m/m / 3.7%
   y/y**, core **+0.1% m/m / 3.3% y/y** — the lightest headline monthly print since April 2020.
   Core decelerated one tick (3.4%→3.3%) between the two most recent prints; the July whisper
   (3.3% flat / 3.2% down-tick) is a straight-line continuation, not a reversal call.
4. **PCE is running meaningfully hotter than CPI this cycle — SUPPORTED, a persistent gap worth
   flagging.** July 2026 CPI (CNBC, 2026-08-12): headline **3.4% y/y**, core **2.5% y/y**. June
   2026 PCE (BEA, 2026-07-30): headline **3.7% y/y**, core **3.3% y/y** — an **~80bp core gap**
   against the metric the Fed actually targets. The [`cpi-2026-09-11.md`](cpi-2026-09-11.md)
   doc's "core is still tame" read is a CPI-only read; core PCE sits materially further from the
   2% target than core CPI does, which matters because it's PCE, not CPI, the FOMC's own SEP is
   built around.
5. **A one-off pass-through risk exists but is flagged as likely noise — MIXED.** July 2026 PPI
   (BLS via press, released 2026-08-13) showed a flat headline but core (ex food/energy/trade)
   **+0.4% m/m**, driven by a 6.5% spike in portfolio-management fees — a component that flows
   mechanically into core PCE (fee income scales with asset prices, not real demand) and that the
   BEA is reportedly revising its methodology to de-distort on 2026-09-30. An economist quoted in
   coverage (Chris Rupkey, Fwdbonds) called it non-signal for broader pipeline pressure. Read: if
   July core PCE ticks hot, check whether it's this fee artifact before treating it as
   re-acceleration.
6. **Warsh's own reaction function to PCE specifically is unsettled — MIXED, the load-bearing
   leg.** At his confirmation hearing Warsh dismissed PCE as "rough swag" (Fortune, 2026-07-31)
   and has voiced a preference for the trimmed-mean measure (2.2% in June 2026, same source); he
   has named task forces reviewing how the Fed measures inflation altogether, with a possible
   framework change floated for after January 2027 (Yahoo Finance, CNBC, both 2026-07-31). At the
   same July 29 press conference he reaffirmed "there is no soft target inflation, only 2% goal"
   and said the Fed is "sticking with" PCE for now (stayathomemacro, 2026-07-29 context) — so the
   print still lands as the official gauge, but the chair steering the reaction has told markets,
   on record, he doesn't fully trust the number this event produces. He's also stated he's "not
   constrained by market prices" (goldsilver.com framing of his July comments) — read by that
   outlet as a hawkish-surprise tell.
7. **Market reaction to the last two PCE prints — SUPPORTED, in-line/cool gets bought.** June
   data (2026-07-30): cooler than trend, landed 18 hours after the hawkish July 29 FOMC hold and
   same morning as the Q2 GDP advance estimate; equities rallied on the day (Dow +135, S&P +26,
   Nasdaq +289 at time of reporting; TradingView/Zacks recap, 2026-07-30). May data (2026-06-25):
   in-line-to-slightly-hot on core, muted cross-asset reaction (10Y little changed per CNBC,
   2026-06-25). Two data points, both consistent with the CPI/FOMC docs' "cool/in-line gets
   bought" pattern — but thin, and neither print carried the compound-day risk 08-26 does.
8. **08-26 is a four-catalyst compound-risk day — SUPPORTED, cross-checked against the calendar
   itself.** Independently confirmed in `src/domain/market-events-data.ts` (checked 2026-08-19):
   PCE (this event, BEA, 08:30 ET) + GDP 2nd estimate (BEA, same 08:30 slot) +
   `treasury-5y-note-2026-08-26` (TSY, 1:00pm ET) + NVDA earnings, after close (IR, `critical`
   impact per [`nvda-2026-08-26-print.md`](nvda-2026-08-26-print.md)) all land the same session.
   Two days later, `jackson-hole-2026-08-28` (estimate-status, Warsh's first keynote) follows —
   but press previews (regardsofwallstreet, riviera wealth, goldsilver.com, all fetched
   2026-08-19) frame the speech as "big picture" long-term structural remarks (monetary policy in
   the age of AI/deglobalization), explicitly *not* near-term guidance, per Warsh's own July 29
   comments. So the print's Fed read-through may not resolve even at the next big Fed-communication
   moment.
9. **VIX regime, checked today.** VIX closed **15.86 on 2026-08-18** (Yahoo/CNBC via search,
   fetched 2026-08-19) — up modestly from the 14.56 2026-low cited in the sibling CPI/FOMC/NVDA
   docs' 2026-08-15/17 rows; no distinct regime shift, still a calm print heading into a loaded
   week.

**What the conditions support.** Nothing directional — consistent with the house macro stance
(no CPI/FOMC/PCE-shaped playbook exists; S1/S2/E1/S3/S4 + G1 are symbol/earnings-keyed). What

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date confirmed, BEA primary re-checked 2026-08-19).** Treat 2026-08-26 08:30 ET as a
high-impact print riding inside a four-catalyst compound-risk session (PCE + GDP 2nd estimate +
5Y Treasury auction, all before NVDA's after-close print), two days ahead of Warsh's first
Jackson Hole keynote. No directional macro position on the print itself: no new rate-duration
paper entries initiated into the 08-26 session for this reason alone. Base case (estimate-labeled:
one thin prediction market, not a survey): core PCE y/y **3.3% flat or 3.2% one-tick cooler**,
consistent with the May→June deceleration — a likely non-event to small relief move, the way the
last two prints traded. The asymmetric risk is a core print **≥3.4%** (the <1%-priced tail),
which would force a hike-odds repricing on the same day NVDA reports and two days before Jackson
Hole — but flag that a hot core reading should first be checked against the July-PPI
portfolio-fee artifact before being read as genuine re-acceleration. Separately: Warsh's on-record
skepticism of PCE itself ("rough swag," a stated trimmed-mean preference) means the standard
hike-odds reaction function this event assumes (per the CPI/FOMC docs) may transmit weaker under
this specific chair than it did historically — an open question this doc leaves MIXED, not
resolved, and the next pulse should watch for any signal on it.

**Kill switches:**

- **Core PCE y/y ≥3.4%** — the priced <1% tail — flips this from a likely non-event to a
  hawkish-repricing forcing print landing on NVDA's print day; reassess rate-sensitive exposure
  same day, net of the portfolio-fee-artifact check (leg 5).
- **Core PCE y/y ≤3.1%** — beyond the priced 3.2% floor — a genuine downside surprise that would
  stack with (and be hard to cleanly separate from) any NVDA post-print reaction that evening.
- **Warsh explicitly names PCE (vs trimmed-mean or a broader basket) as the operative gauge**
  before 08-26 — would resolve leg 6 toward "standard reaction function applies" and firm up the
  stance; continued silence keeps it open.
- **The 5Y auction tails badly (weak demand, a real yield spike)** same morning — a supply-driven
  move that could get misattributed to the PCE print; the next pulse's adjacency sweep should
  separate the two before reading either in isolation.
- **Jackson Hole (08-28) delivers near-term policy guidance** despite Warsh's own "long-term
  structural" framing — would mean the print's Fed read-through resolves two days later than this
  doc assumes; note explicitly at the next pulse if so.
