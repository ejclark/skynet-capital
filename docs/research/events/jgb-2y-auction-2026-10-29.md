## At a glance

**TL;DR.** Japan sells roughly **¥2.8tn** of new 2-year JGBs on **2026-10-29**. **Nothing here is ours to
trade** and this ledger does not pretend otherwise. The proposal that created this event framed it as
*"the second of the two issues that close the 1-5y menu's 2-Year coupon channel"* — true, and the least
interesting thing about it. From **BoJ's own published schedule**, the October 2026 Monetary Policy
Meeting runs **Oct. 29–30 with an Outlook Report**: MOF sells this bond on **day one of a live MPM**,
after the **2026-10-28 FOMC** statement (≈03:00 JST the same morning) and before the BoJ's own decision.
Across MOF's **44 published 2-Year results** that has happened **three times** — 2023-07-27, 2023-10-30
and 2026-07-30 — and those three hold the **three lowest covers** among the seven July/October auctions
(**3.540** mean vs **4.470** for the near-misses; exact permutation **p = 0.029**, the smallest value
C(7,3) can produce). It is filed **MIXED, not supported**: n = 3, six comparisons were run to find it, and
two of the three precedents sit on documented 2023 YCC changes, so "MPM day one" and "the eve of a policy
change" cannot be separated. **The operational output is a warning, not a call:** when this print is
scored, compare its cover to the day-one peer set (**3.540**), not to the sibling's ¥2.8tn-era mean
(**3.885**) — an ordinary day-one auction reads weak against the wrong baseline. Two inherited rules are
corrected on 4× the sample: the coupon rule is **one-sided, not exact** (34/34 coupons at or above the
rounded auction-day close, **7 of 34** a full step above), and **"¥2,800bn in 8 of 8" is a regime, not a
constant** — MOF has moved the 2-Year offering **three times in 44 prints**, twice in the last 14 months,
every move **upward**. Date and issue number are `estimate`; they widen caution and license nothing.

**Signals & conditions** — the buy/sell/hold triggers:

- **Never** — no entry, exit or hedge is keyed to this auction or to the 2026-10-29 session.
- **The comparison-set rule, and the single most useful line in this document.** Score a 2-Year auction
  that clears inside an MPM against **day-one peers**, not the era mean. Day-one covers: **3.045**
  (No.454), **3.626** (No.487), **3.950** (No.451) — mean **3.540**. July/October near-misses: **4.187**,
  **4.351**, **4.467**, **4.874** — mean **4.470**. ¥2.8tn-era mean, all prints: **3.885**.
- **The collision is structural and therefore predictable.** A 2-Year auction can land inside an MPM only
  when BoJ schedules that meeting within ~2 business days of month-end, and BoJ only ever does that at an
  **Outlook-Report** MPM (Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct). Every non-Outlook meeting in 2023–2027 sits mid-month. In this
  window the collisions are **July and October** (2023–2026) and, from BoJ's published 2027 table,
  **April and October** in 2027.
- **The coupon rule is one-sided, not exact — this corrects the sibling at n = 34 vs its n = 8.** Across
  every auction with a coupon ≥ 0.1%, the printed coupon is **at or above** the 2Y CME close rounded to
  the nearest 0.1% in **34 of 34** cases; it is **exactly** the rounded value in **32 of 34** on the
  auction-day close and **27 of 34** on the prior session's close. **It has never printed below.** So the
  honest form is a floor plus one step: `coupon ∈ {round(y), round(y)+0.1}`.
- **The 2-Year offering is a regime, not a constant.** ¥2,900bn (2023-01→2024-02, 14 prints) · ¥2,600bn

- **The saturation call survives at enormous margin, and needs no watching.** Whatever this prints, it is

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

`jgb-2y-auction-2026-09-30` proposed this event on 2026-09-09 for a specific, honest reason: its own
saturation call needs **both** issues entering the 2026-11-20 liquidity-enhancement menu to price ≥1.0%,
and registering only one would leave that forward test half-blind. Fine — but that question is answered in
a line (it will; see leg 7), and it does not need a session.

So the question this ledger has to answer is: **is there anything about this print that is not true of
every other 2-Year auction?**

**One-line verdict: yes, exactly one thing, and the proposal's title missed it.** MOF sells this bond on
**day one of a live BoJ Outlook-Report MPM** — a condition that has occurred **three times in MOF's 44
published 2-Year results** and that is *structurally* confined to two months of the year. What that
condition does to auction quality is **suggestive and under-powered**, and this document refuses to call
it. What it does to *how the next session should read the result* is settled, and that is the deliverable.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. This auction clears on day one of a live BoJ MPM — SUPPORTED, and it is the finding.** BoJ's own
2026 table reads `Oct. 29 (Thurs.), 30 (Fri.)` with an Outlook Report and "The Bank's View" released
Oct. 30. MOF's `2610e.htm` reads `Oct. 29, 2026 | 2-year`. The 2-Year clears in Tokyo early afternoon; the
BoJ statement lands the next day. Sequenced across time zones the print sits inside a ~30-hour window:

| Time (JST) | Event |
|---|---|
| 2026-10-29 ~03:00 | **FOMC** statement (`fomc-2026-10-28`, 14:00 ET the prior day) |
| 2026-10-29 morning | **BoJ MPM day one** convenes |
| 2026-10-29 ~12:45 | **This auction** |
| 2026-10-29 evening | ECB decision, US Q3 advance GDP, PCE |
| 2026-10-30 ~midday | **BoJ statement + Outlook Report** |

Our calendar carries `boj-decision-2026-10-30`, which captures day two only — the day-one overlap is not
represented anywhere on it, and that is why the proposal's title missed it.

**2. And the collision is structural, not coincidence — SUPPORTED, and it is the more durable half.**
Of the 44 held 2-Year auctions, exactly **three** fall on an MPM day, and every one is an **Outlook-Report**
meeting (Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct). Broken out by calendar month:

| Month | 2-Year auctions | Inside an MPM |
|---|---|---|
| Jan–Jun, Aug, Sep, Nov, Dec | 37 | **0** |
| **July** | 4 | **2** (2023-07-27, 2026-07-30) |
| **October** | 3 | **1** (2023-10-30) |

The mechanism is a calendar overlap, not a market one: MOF places the 2-Year on the **last business days**
of a month, and BoJ schedules a meeting that close to month-end **only** at an Outlook MPM. Every
non-Outlook meeting (Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec) in 2023–2027 sits mid-month — 13 to 19 business days clear of
month-end. Running the same arithmetic forward on BoJ's published **2027** table, the only two meetings
inside two business days of month-end are **Apr. 27–28** and **Oct. 28–29** — so the collision months
shift, which is what makes this a rule rather than a coincidence about July and October.

**3. Day-one auctions print thinner cover than their month-matched peers — MIXED, and deliberately not
called.** Restricting to July and October (the only months where the comparison is possible at all, per
leg 2) gives seven auctions, three treated:

| Date | Issue | MPM day one | Auction vs day one | Cover | Tail | Allotment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023-07-27 | No.451 | 2023-07-27 | **0** | **3.950** | 0.3bp | 66.59% |
| 2023-10-30 | No.454 | 2023-10-30 | **0** | **3.045** | 0.9bp | 90.68% |
| 2024-07-26 | No.463 | 2024-07-30 | −4 | 4.187 | 0.3bp | 63.12% |
| 2024-10-29 | No.466 | 2024-10-30 | −1 | 4.874 | 0.3bp | 98.61% |
| 2025-07-29 | No.475 | 2025-07-30 | −1 | 4.467 | 0.3bp | 35.82% |
| 2025-10-31 | No.478 | 2025-10-29 | +2 | 4.351 | 0.1bp | 93.06% |
| 2026-07-30 | No.487 | 2026-07-30 | **0** | **3.626** | 0.4bp | 89.13% |

The three treated auctions are the three lowest covers in the table — the **minimum attainable**
arrangement, so the exact permutation over C(7,3) = 35 gives **p = 0.029** and cannot give less. Tail
(p = 0.11) and allotment (p = 0.40) show nothing.

**Why this is still MIXED, in three counts.** (a) **n = 3.** (b) **Six comparisons were run** — three
metrics × two control definitions — and a Bonferroni-honest reading of the best is p ≈ 0.17, which is not
a finding. (c) **The precedents are confounded with policy**: 2023-07-28 and 2023-10-31 are the two MPMs at
which BoJ loosened its yield-curve-control band, so "an auction on MPM day one" and "an auction on the eve
of a policy change" describe the same three observations. The 2Y rose into the decision every time
(+1.5bp, +0.6bp, +5.5bp on the statement day; +6.1bp, −0.2bp, +11.4bp over the following week) — a coherent
mechanism for a thin bid stack, and equally coherent under either reading.

**4. And the first version of leg 3 was wrong, which is worth recording — REFUTED, by me, this session.**
Against the **full** 44-auction sample the metric that fired was not cover but **allotment at the lowest
accepted price** (day-one mean **82.13%** vs **53.80%**; permutation p = 0.043, 200k draws) — thin demand
at the margin, an appealing story. It **does not survive the month-matched control**: within July/October
the same comparison is 82.13% vs 72.65%, p = 0.40. The allotment signal was **the month, not the meeting**
— end-of-month July/October auctions run a high marginal allotment whether or not the BoJ is sitting. The
signal that survived the harder test is cover, and it is the one that failed the easier one (full-sample
p = 0.25). Reporting both directions is the only honest way to present a search over six comparisons.

**5. The coupon rule is one-sided, not exact — SUPPORTED at n = 34, and it corrects the sibling.** The
sibling tested `coupon = round(2Y close, 0.1%)` on its eight 2026 prints and got 7/8 on the prior close and
**8/8** on the auction-day close. Extended to every auction with a coupon ≥ 0.1%:

| Basis | Exact fit | One step **above** | Any print **below** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Auction-day close | **32 / 34** | 2 (No.475, No.478) | **0 / 34** |
| Prior session's close | **27 / 34** | 7 | **0 / 34** |

**All nine misses go the same way.** MOF has never set a 2-Year coupon *below* the rounded close in this
sample; when it deviates it takes the higher 0.1% step. The sibling's 8/8 was a small-sample artifact — at
n = 34 the same rule is 94%, and the correct form is a floor plus one step, `{round(y), round(y)+0.1}`.
Two scope limits: the ten pre-2023-10 prints carry a **0.005%** floor coupon in a zero/negative-yield tape
where no rounding rule applies, and they are excluded rather than counted as fits; and MOF publishes no
rounding rule this ledger found, so this is a described convention, not policy.

**6. "¥2,800bn in 8 of 8" is a regime, not a constant — SUPPORTED, and it is the second correction.**
Parsing the offering amount out of all 44 announcements:

| Offering | Prints | Span |
|---|---|---|
| ¥2,900bn | 14 | 2023-01-31 → 2024-02-29 |
| ¥2,600bn | 18 | 2024-03-29 → 2025-08-28 |
| ¥2,700bn | 3 | 2025-09-30 → 2025-11-28 |
| **¥2,800bn** | **9** | **2025-12-25 → 2026-08-28** |

Three step changes in 3.7 years, **two of them inside the last 14 months**, and every move upward. The
2024-03 step lands on a fiscal-year boundary and is schedulable; the 2025-09 and 2025-12 steps do not, and
are the ones that make the ¥2,800bn call medium rather than high. This is why the sibling's `FT-…-3` framing
("an 8-of-8 size record") overstates: eight identical prints inside one regime say nothing about how long
the regime lasts.

**7. The saturation call survives, and needs no monitoring — SUPPORTED, trivially.** The proposal's stated
reason for creating this event is that the family's ≥1%-coupon saturation call needs both No.489 and No.490
to price ≥1.0%. The 2Y closed **1.848%** on 2026-09-08; a sub-1% coupon needs roughly a **90bp** fall in the
51 days to the auction. Against MOF's own series the worst 51-day decline since 2024-01 is **−25.2bp**, and
since 2025-09 the 5th percentile of 51-day changes is **−0.3bp** — the distribution barely has a left tail
in this regime. The margin is about **3.6× the worst historical adverse move**, and the honest way to say
that is that the call is safe on this leg and its real risk sits entirely in the window rule the sibling
already registered, not in either coupon.

**8. The corridor is the densest this family has met, and none of it touches the auction — SUPPORTED.**
**34** tracked ids sit within ±5 days. Same date: `ecb-decision-2026-10-29`, **`gdp-q3-2026-advance`**
and **`pce-2026-10-29`** (both high), `treasury-7y-note`, `nar-metro-home-prices`. D-1: **`fomc-2026-10-28`**
(high), `treasury-2y-frn`, `uk-autumn-budget`, `advance-economic-indicators`. D+1: **`boj-decision-2026-10-30`**,
`eci-q3` (high), `chicago-pmi`. D+5: **`midterm-elections-2026-11-03`** (high). The auction clears in
Tokyo hours — after the FOMC statement, before every US print listed and before the BoJ's own. Density is
context for the tape, not a channel: none of these has a mechanical bearing on a ¥2.8tn front-end JGB sale.

(09-08) and 15.30 (09-07); twenty-three-session range **14.25–16.34**. Inside the material-screen threshold

### What plays the conditions support

**None.** No tracked symbol has yen-rates exposure and no house playbook is rates-keyed. The refusal is
inherited from the family rather than re-tested, and a front-end sovereign auction has the weakest claim on
the spillover channel of anything in it.

What this entry is *for* is that it hands the family a **conditioning variable it did not have**. The
sibling's `FT-jgb-2y-auction-2026-09-30-4` asks whether No.488's weak print was a regime, and it will be
scored against an era mean of 3.885 that silently contains a day-one auction (No.487). The contamination is
small in this instance — dropping No.487 moves the era mean from **3.885** to **3.922** — but the same
baseline is about to be pointed at **this** auction, where it is not small, because this auction *is* a
day-one print. A cover of 3.5 here is unremarkable against its own cohort and looks like a second
deterioration against the era. Naming the cohort before the result lands is the entire contribution.

### Honest limits

- **The auction has not happened and its terms are not published.** Everything here is prior. The
  announcement due ~2026-10-22 settles the issue number and the size; the result page on 10-29 settles the
  rest.
- **Leg 3 is under-powered and is labelled MIXED for three separate reasons**, any one of which would be
  sufficient (n = 3, six comparisons, policy confound). It is registered as an out-of-sample test rather
  than asserted, and the control arm is proposed in the same PR because the treatment alone cannot separate
  the meeting from the regime.
- **The MPM-collision census is bounded by what MOF publishes**, not by history. `2201e`–`2212e` are 404,
  so the window is 2023-01 → 2026-11 — 47 scheduled 2-Year auctions, 44 held. BoJ's schedule reaches back
  to 2010; MOF's calendars do not, and no attempt was made to reconstruct pre-2023 auction dates from
  another source.
- **"~12:45 JST" for the auction time is convention, not a figure this session read from MOF.** The
  ordering it supports (auction before the BoJ statement, after the FOMC statement) does not depend on it:
  the statement lands the next day.
- **The 51-day yield distribution is drawn from a one-way tape.** Since 2025-09 the 2Y has essentially not
  fallen over any 51-day window (p5 = −0.3bp), which is a description of a normalisation cycle, not a
  property of the 2Y. Every band quoted from it — including the coupon band — is momentum extrapolation and
  should be read as such. Leg 7's margin is robust to this precisely because it is 3.6× the worst observed
  move; the coupon band is not.
- **n = 34 for the coupon rule, and it is still a described convention**, not MOF policy. A 15bp+ move
  through the Oct. 29–30 MPM breaks any band without breaking the convention.
- **The offering-size regimes are read from announcement text, not from an issuance plan.** This session
  did not read MOF's FY2026 or FY2027 JGB issuance plan, which is the document that would actually date
  the next step. That is the obvious next piece of work and it is not done here.
- **This ledger did not re-derive the family's eligible-stock basis.** The ¥323.95tn / 39.731% figures,
  the sector saturation map and the NPC-II procedure are the 1-5y and 2026-09-30 siblings' work, read as
  input and cited. What it did re-derive independently is the **auction-quality series** (n = 44), because
  every claim here is computed on it.
- **VIX is Yahoo, i.e. secondary**, and a US reading with no mechanical bearing on a JGB auction; it is
  carried because the probe-ref contract asks for it.
- **The date and the issue number are `estimate`.** MOF is primary and the date is not in doubt; the label
  reflects this lane's no-self-confirm limit, a taxonomy in `market-events-data.ts` with no slot for a
  non-US sovereign debt office, and an announcement that has not published. Estimates widen caution and
  license nothing.
