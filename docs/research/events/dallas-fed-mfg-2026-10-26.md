## Initial research

### The question, plainly

What will the Dallas Fed's October Texas Manufacturing Outlook Survey likely show, does the release
move the market, and how should a paper book use it? This is the fourth edition of a series this
calendar already researches ([`09-28`](dallas-fed-mfg-2026-09-28.md) closed out 09-29;
[`11-30`](dallas-fed-mfg-2026-11-30.md) and [`12-28`](dallas-fed-mfg-2026-12-28.md) are filed), so
this document inherits that ledger's measurements and adds only what October changes.

**One-line verdict:** same permanent stand-aside; the date is confirmed; the reading order is
prices → wages → outlook → headline; and the live fork is whether the oil/ISM input shock keeps
reaching Texas factories while the Fed's own near-term path softened.

### Method

Macro-print mode per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md) — `symbols: []`, so

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date and hour are right — SUPPORTED, primary-verified twice.** The release table lists
   `October | Monday, October 26` under `Release (9:30 a.m. CT)` and the September report states
   `Next release: Monday, October 26`. 10-26 is an ordinary Monday and the last Monday of the month,
   matching the cadence rule the 09-28 ledger validated. Promoted to `confirmed` on `FRB:`.
2. **The field window is a function of the date — SUPPORTED, 3-for-3.** July (Jul 14–22 → Jul 27),
   August (Aug 18–26 → Aug 31) and September (Sept. 15–23 → Sep 28) all hit release −13 / −5 on both
   edges. October's window derives to **Oct 13–21**, which puts **CPI 10-14** and the Beige Book
   inside it and the **10-17** FOMC blackout and Oct 21 close at its end. Registered as FT-1 for
   the fourth instance; a pass is a dating-method result, not a signal.
3. **Inputs: the September cost shock has not faded — SUPPORTED.** Dallas September raw-materials
   prices **52.2** (+8.1; 28.0 series average); ISM Manufacturing September **54.5** with Prices
   **77.9** (+6.8) on steel, aluminum, tariffs and petroleum products (ISM report, 10-01); Chicago
   September **58.8** (+11.7) but with its Prices Paid index down **3.7 points** — the one soft input read.
   Brent: **$106.60 (09-24) → $100.20 (10-07) → $104.72 (10-09)**, a +4.1% jump on 10-08 alone;
   still above the ~$97–100 of September's field period (own Yahoo daily closes, 2026-10-11).
4. **Wages: two-sided, not settled — MIXED.** Dallas manufacturing wages **27.4** (Aug 21.1) killed
   the 09-15 "wage cooling" correction at the 09-29 close-out; the sibling services survey printed
   wages **15.1** (Aug 18.8, −4) on 09-29 and input prices **42.1**, highest since Dec 2022.
   National payrolls were weak (**+29k**, July/August revised −60k) while AHE was already the
   softest since mid-2021. A goods-sector wage line firm and a services line soft is a real split.
5. **The outlook leg broke in September and is the one to watch — SUPPORTED as observation.**
   Future general business activity **20.8** (Aug 37.2, **−16.4**), company outlook **8.7** (−10.5),
   future production **40.3** unchanged. A second fall would say Texas firms priced the 09-16
   hike and the oil shock into expectations; a rebound would say September was noise on ~63
   respondents. n is small; a 3-point move is not a signal.
6. **This release does not move the market — CARRIED (n=21), not re-run.** The 09-28 ledger's
   Yahoo hourly measurement: median release-hour percentile **52.5 (SPY) / 55.5 (QQQ)** vs a null of
   50, and the 09-28 release hour itself (SPY **−0.309%**, QQQ **−0.183%**) sat under both p90s
   (0.464% / 0.640%). The October release has a crowding feature that makes any move less
   attributable: it is the Monday of FOMC week, with the 2-Year Treasury Note auction (`estimate`,
   `medium`) and the MWTS benchmark revision (`estimate`, `low`) on the same date, and MSFT's
   `estimate`-labeled print the next day. Auction and revision times were not verified here.
7. **Fed path: the survey's prices lines are worth less to a hold-modal Fed — SUPPORTED.** The FOMC
   ledger (checked 2026-10-09) puts the Oct 28 decision at **hold ~84% / hike ~16%** with a **Dec 9
   hike ~75%**, after core PCE **3.0%** vs 3.3% and payrolls **+29k**; the Sep 15–16 minutes said
   *most participants* see another increase likely by year-end. A regional prices line moves this
   Fed least at a hold-modal meeting and most between meetings, which is why the October print is
   "the last regional read before the meeting" and still a read, not a trigger.
8. **Tracked-name sensitivity: none directly — SUPPORTED.** No tracked name is a Texas
   manufacturer; the only channels are the rate path (CRWV, then NVDA/AVGO/MRVL, then MSFT/GOOG/META,
   least AAPL/AMZN) and capex sentiment, and leg 6 shows neither has ever appeared in a measured
   release-hour move. Neither channel licenses a position off this print.

### What the conditions support

Nothing directional. A reading discipline: raw-materials prices and wages first, six-month outlook
second, headline last; compare against the September print (52.2 / 27.4 / 20.8 / 9.8), not against
a consensus that does not exist yet and, on this series' pattern, will not until release week.
Do not let a single-state survey change the weight given to CPI 10-14 or the FOMC 10-28.

**Stance (date `confirmed`, 2026-10-11, D-15).** Treat 2026-10-26 10:30 ET as a **low-impact
reading exercise, never an event**: no position is opened, closed or sized off it. Reading order
and base case (Low confidence, `confirmed`-date, not action): raw materials stay ≥ 45 (input leg
persists), wages revert somewhere between 21 and 27, headline stays positive but below the 11.6

[`forward-tests/dallas-fed-mfg-2026-10-26.md`](../forward-tests/dallas-fed-mfg-2026-10-26.md)).

**Kill switches:**

- **A release-hour move above the 90th percentile on 2026-10-26 with a session wrap naming this
  survey as the cause** — the null breaks and the `low` tier is wrong; re-run the leg-6
  measurement before repeating any of it.
- **Raw-materials prices below 45.0** — the input transmission this reading order assumes failed;
  retire the prices-first order and re-rank the outlook lines.
- **The Dallas Fed re-dates the release off 10-26 or publishes a window other than Oct 13–21** —
  voids the derived dates, and the `confirmed` label is revisited.
- **October FOMC hike odds back above 40% (direct venue read) before 10-26** — the prices lines
  become more rate-relevant; the call does not become a trade.
