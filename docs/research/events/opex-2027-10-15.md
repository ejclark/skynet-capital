## Initial research

### The question

This event arrived proposed, `estimate`, from [`fomc-blackout-start-2027-10-16`](fomc-blackout-start-2027-10-16.md)'s
adjacency sweep — because the October blackout's deadline session *is* October monthly opex. That
parent measured the coincidence from its own side (57 opex-deadline gates, 0.844% mean absolute S&P
move against 0.745%, VIX −0.361 against +0.062) and refused it in one sentence: **"A post-expiration
vol drop is an opex property, not a gate property."** Six opex ledgers and three blackout ledgers now
rest on some version of that sentence, and it has never had a control of any kind.

Two questions follow. **Does the gate survive the conditioning the parent could not run** — not
"FOMC gates whose deadline is opex versus gates whose deadline is not", but *expirations that are
deadlines versus expirations that are not*? And **is the residual actually an opex property**, or is
it a Friday property wearing an expiration label?

**One-line verdict:** the gate is inert to three decimals and the parent's conclusion is safe; its
*attribution* is not — roughly half the drop is the day of the week, and the expiration-specific
remainder does not clear P = 0.05.

### Honest limits

**The Friday confound is structural, not incidental.** Only **6** of 236 expirations in the sample are
not Fridays, so "expiration" and "Friday" cannot be fully separated in this data at all — leg 5
bounds the effect, it does not decompose it, and a cleaner test needs intraday or option-level data
this repo does not have. **Nineteen comparisons, none multiplicity-adjusted.** The one that reads
significant under a control (leg 6) is reported as unproven for exactly that reason, and the September
sibling's era-artifact lesson is the reason the control was run at all. **The month control in leg 6
rests on n = 21** non-deadline January/April/July/October expirations, and that cohort is
era-imbalanced (deadline monthlies are 79% dated 2013+ against 64% for the rest). **The VIX channel is
a daily close-to-close change on a 30-day index**, not a realized-volatility measurement; the
