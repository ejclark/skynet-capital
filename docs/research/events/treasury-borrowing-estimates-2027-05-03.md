## At a glance

**TL;DR.** Stand aside on the release and read it for one sentence. This is the **quantity** half of
May-2027 refunding week — the aggregate dollar figure Treasury publishes at 15:00 ET on the Monday,
which [treasury-refunding-2027-05-05](treasury-refunding-2027-05-05.md) (`estimate`) allocates across
the curve 48 hours later. The date is an **`estimate`** and stays one: Treasury's schedule page
publishes exactly one cycle ahead. But this session did not take the date on faith — it audited it
against **48 refunding cycles** of Treasury's own auction-announcement data (2014-11 → 2026-08) and
found the rule the proposal cited (*"the first Wednesday of February, May, August and November"*)
**wrong in 10 of 48**. The corrected rule puts the announcement on Wednesday **2027-05-05** and the
estimates release on Monday **2027-05-03** — same answer, sounder derivation, and May 2027 falls in
the one weekday class that is **8 for 8** with no ambiguity. On the tape the release is **nil, and
this session tripled the evidence**: across the **26** releases since 2015 that were not FOMC
meeting-eves, TLT closed 11/26 up at **−0.005%** (permutation p=**0.978**), IEF +0.006% (p=0.938),
^TNX +0.041% (p=0.935) — the sibling's n=8 null reproduced to three decimals from a *different date
source*, then extended to n=26. **2027-05-03 is not an FOMC eve** (the Fed's own published 2027
calendar has April 27–28, then June 8–9). What makes this edition worth reading anyway is that it
is the calendar's best candidate to be the **first debt-limit-live borrowing estimate**: the $41.1T
ceiling is reached around **May 2027** on the current third-party consensus, and this session's own
debt-to-the-penny arithmetic (headroom **$1.0054T** on 2026-09-04) brackets the same window. That
regime is where every large revision in the record came from — and it *still* does not trade: the
**14** limit-live releases closed TLT **−0.286%**, p=**0.241**, the largest effect anywhere in this
study and not significant.

## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** is 2027-05-03 the right date, what will the May-2027 borrowing-estimate
release actually contain, does the release move the tape, and does anything about *this* edition
change the sibling's stand-aside — around 2027-05-03?

**One-line verdict:** the date is right but the reason given for it was wrong, the release-day null
survives tripling the sample and holds even in the debt-limit regime this edition will probably sit
in, so the honest call is stand aside on the price and read one footnote.

### Method

Sourced primary research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md) (macro-print
mode), plus three first-party measurements built this session:

1. **Date audit.** `api.fiscaldata.treasury.gov/.../auctions_query`, fetched direct 2026-09-09
   (HTTP 200), filtered to 30-Year Bond new issues in February/May/August/November. Its
   `announcemt_date` field **is** the quarterly refunding announcement — 48 cycles, 2014-11 → 2026-08.
2. **Release-day study.** Yahoo **dividend-adjusted** daily closes via
   `scripts/research/market-data.mjs` → `bars()` (instrument caches busted first per the
   cache-discipline rule). Instruments **TLT · IEF · ^TNX · SPY · QQQ · ^VIX**, window
   2015-01-01 → 2026-09-09. Event set: **47** borrowing-estimate releases, each derived as the
   announcement Wednesday **minus two days**. Significance by two-sided permutation test (200,000
   iterations) against random same-size draws from the same session pool. Placebo columns T−2 … T+2.
3. **Regime splits.** FOMC meeting-eve from the Fed's own calendars
   (`federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm` for 2021–2027 plus `fomchistorical<year>.htm`
   for 2015–2020, all fetched direct 2026-09-09, HTTP 200) — 103 scheduled two-day decision dates.
   Debt-limit regime by two independent flags: a mechanical one from
   `fiscaldata` debt-to-the-penny (3,080 daily observations) and a statute-dated episode list.

**Everything the sibling could not fetch, this session fetched.** home.treasury.gov, fiscaldata and
federalreserve.gov all returned 200 to plain `curl`. One source was blocked and is recorded rather
than substituted: `bipartisanpolicy.org` returned **403** (Cloudflare interstitial) on 2026-09-09, so
its debt-limit dating is carried at search-snippet grade below, not as a fetched primary;
`crfb.org` returned 200 and is quoted directly.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The date is right; the rule given for it is wrong — SUPPORTED, and this is the session's first
correction.** The two proposals that named this event derived 2027-05-03 from *"Treasury holds its
quarterly refunding on the FIRST WEDNESDAY of February, May, August and November."* Measured against
all 48 cycles in the auction API, that rule **misses 10**:

| Weekday of the 1st | n | Announcement | "First Wednesday" agrees? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sunday | 7 | the 4th | yes |
| Monday | 7 | the 3rd | yes |
| Tuesday | 6 | the 2nd | yes |
| Wednesday | 7 | the 1st | yes |
| **Thursday** | **6** | **the last day of the PRIOR month** (e.g. 2024-01-31 for February 2024) | **no** |
| **Friday** | **7** | the 6th in May (2015, 2020, 2026); **the prior month's 30th** in Feb/Aug/Nov (2019-02, 2019-11, 2024-11, 2025-08) | **no, 4 of 7** |
| Saturday | 8 | the 5th | yes |

The rule that fits **48 of 48**: the announcement is the single Wednesday inside
**[the 30th of the prior month … the 6th of the refunding month]**. That window contains exactly one
Wednesday *unless the 1st is a Friday*, when it contains two and the tie has broken by month — May
takes the later, February/August/November the earlier. **May 2027 opens on a Saturday**, the
unambiguous 8-for-8 class, so the announcement is **2027-05-05** and the estimates release is the
Monday two sessions earlier, **2027-05-03**. The Monday-minus-two pairing is **15 for 15** against the
borrowing-estimate dates the [sibling ledger](treasury-borrowing-estimates-2026-11-02.md) read off
Treasury's own press releases, and home.treasury.gov restates it for the live cycle (*"(The next
release is scheduled for November 2, 2026)"* / *"(The next release is scheduled for November 4 ,
2026)"*, re-fetched direct 2026-09-09, HTTP 200, 73,518 bytes). **The entry stays `estimate`**: that
page publishes one cycle ahead, so no Treasury 2027 calendar exists, and a rule-derived date is never
self-confirmed here. The canonical `treasury-borrowing-estimates-2027-05-03.json` is written in this
PR with the corrected derivation; the two proposals that named it become inert.

### What the conditions support

Nothing directional. No house playbook is macro-keyed (S1/S2/E1/S3/S4 + G1 are symbol/earnings-keyed).
Two disciplines travel out of this session. Inherited and now confirmed at three times the sample:
**an event's reaction function must be computed against its collision set, not its date list** — the
entire apparent reaction to this release is the FOMC's, seen 48 hours early, and it replicates at
n=21. New: **audit a derived date against the record before building on it, because a plausible rule
is not a measured one.** "First Wednesday of February, May, August and November" is what every
description of the refunding calendar says, it is what both proposals cited, and it is wrong in a
fifth of cycles. It happened to be right for May 2027. It will not be right for February 2028 (the
1st is a Tuesday — fine) or for whichever future cycle opens on a Thursday, and any lane deriving a
refunding-week date from it will be a week off without noticing.

### Honest limits

- **The whole date rests on an inferred rule.** Treasury publishes one cycle ahead; 48 for 48 is a
  strong fit, not a schedule. The `estimate` label is doing real work and the entry keeps it.
- **The pre-2023 release dates are derived, not read.** The Monday-minus-two pairing is 15/15 against
  the sibling's primary reads, all of which are 2023 or later; the 32 earlier dates in the sample are
  announcement-minus-two and were not individually checked against a press release.
- **The 15:00 ET timing is still the worst signal-to-noise window on this calendar.** T+0
  close-to-close contains six and a half hours of unrelated trading before the news and one after it.
  Tripling n narrows the interval but does not fix the dilution: the measurement supports "no *large*
  release-day effect," never "no effect." **Intraday (15:00→16:00) data would settle it and was not
  used** — that remains the honest next step, unchanged from the sibling.
- **n=26 clean is better than n=8, not decisive.** With TLT's daily σ near 0.9% over the window, 26
  observations have roughly 80% power against a mean effect near ±0.5%. p=0.978 means not
  distinguishable, not zero.
- **The debt-limit split conflates two things at both ends.** The statutory episode list is
  search-sourced (not fetched primary), and the mechanical proxy has a known false-positive mode:
  low 60-day debt growth also happens in **April tax season**, which is why it flags 2016-05-02,
  2024-04-29 and 2026-05-04 — none of them limit-live. Both flags agreeing is reassurance, not proof.
- **The May-2027 debt-limit timing is third-party and partly unfetchable.** bipartisanpolicy.org
  returned 403 today; the "reached around May 2027 / X-date February 2028" framing is carried at
  search-snippet grade. CRFB's fetched page says only *"mid-to-late 2027"* for the raise, and this
  session's own extrapolation runs **earlier** (February–April 2027) because total debt outstanding
  grows lumpily and Treasury draws the cash balance down as a limit approaches. Treat the whole
  window as an order of magnitude, not a date.
- **The corridor looks thin and is not.** Only four tracked events sat within ±5 days of 2027-05-03
  before this PR, against thirteen for the sibling — but that is a sparse 2027 calendar, not a quiet
  week. This PR proposes three of the missing ones (ISM-mfg and SLOOS same-day, payrolls D+4); ADP,
  ISM Services and JOLTS are the same first-week cluster and remain untracked in 2027.
- **The impact tier stays `low`** and the measurement supports it at three times the sibling's
  sample. Nothing in legs 2, 4 or 6 argues for raising it.
- **No price-attribution forward test is registered.** Two same-day releases and payrolls at D+4 mean
  a directional test on 2027-05-03 could not attribute its own result. Three of the four tests below
  are scored from documents; the fourth (leg 7) is explicitly one observation on a hypothesis this
  ledger declines to trade.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (`estimate`-dated — and an estimate widens caution, it never licenses an entry).** Stand
aside on 2027-05-03 as a price event. The refusal is measured on **47** releases since 2015, and it
is stronger than the sibling's: on the **26** that were not FOMC meeting-eves, TLT closed 11/26 up at
**−0.005%** (permutation p=0.978), IEF +0.006% (p=0.938) and ^TNX +0.041% (p=0.935); 44 of 47 release
days sit at or below the 90th percentile of TLT's own move distribution, median 49th. The one regime
that could plausibly differ — a live debt limit, which this edition will probably sit in — measures
at TLT **−0.286%** (p=0.241, n=14) on statutory episodes and **−0.329%** (p=0.165, n=15) on a
mechanical proxy: the largest effect in the study and still an ordinary session. **Read the document
for two things:** whether Treasury attaches its debt-limit footnote to the end-June/end-September
2027 cash-balance assumptions, and the first **Jul–Sep 2027** borrowing estimate. Base case (**this
ledger's own forecast — no dealer consensus exists for a Monday quantity release; dealers publish
estimates for the Wednesday's *sizes*)**: a debt-limit-live edition carrying the footnote, with a
revision to Apr–Jun 2027 that may be large and will not matter. **Attribute no 2027-05-03 move to
this release**: ISM Manufacturing prints at 10:00 ET and SLOOS at 14:00 ET on the same Monday (both
proposed in this PR), the FOMC decided 04-28, refunding lands 05-05 and payrolls 05-07. **The
leading indicator is 2026-11-02**, not this date — that edition publishes the first Jan–Mar 2027
number and is the earliest place the debt-limit language can appear.

**Kill switches:**

- **Treasury's schedule page naming any date other than 2027-05-03** once the February-2027 cycle
  publishes (expected ~2027-02-01) → leg 1's 48-for-48 rule failed at its first out-of-sample test
  and every rule-derived refunding date on this calendar is re-derived, not patched.
- **The 2026-11-02 or 2027-02-01 edition attaching debt-limit language to its cash-balance
  assumption** → leg 5's timing arrived a quarter or two early; this ledger's "first limit-live
  edition" framing is wrong and the May-2027 edition is a follow-on, not a first.
- **A clean-subset release day producing a TLT move above the 90th percentile of its own
  distribution** → the nil finding in leg 2 breaks out-of-sample and the reaction function needs
  re-argument rather than a patch.
- **Intraday (15:00→16:00) measurement showing a real release-window effect** → the dilution limit
  bites, the daily-bar null is revealed as underpowered rather than informative, and this ledger's
  method is what was wrong.
- **The debt limit being raised or suspended before 2027-05-03** → leg 5's premise is void, this
  becomes an ordinary quiet edition, and the only remaining reason to read it is the Jul–Sep 2027
  number.
- **SPY closing 2027-05-03 up more than +0.5%** → leg 7's drift is live rather than a p≈0.08
  artifact, and the ISM-vs-Treasury ownership question needs settling before the next
  first-business-day release rather than after.
