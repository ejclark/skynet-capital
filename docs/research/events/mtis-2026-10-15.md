## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This id existed only as a proposal from the `mwts-benchmark-revision-2026-10-26` lane, which argued
that Census names **two** MTIS editions as carrying its AIES restatement and that this is the retail
one. Its sibling — the wholesale edition, `mtis-2026-11-17` — was researched on 2026-09-06 and found
to sit in the **loudest** MTIS nowcast subclass in the archive. **So: do the proposal's claims hold,
and is this edition the same finding one month early, or something else?**

**One-line verdict:** every claim survives and the edition is the sibling's mirror, not its twin — it
sits in the archive's **quietest** identifiable MTIS subclass on the same two classifiers, and the
restatement that names it has a measured precedent that also came in quiet.

### Method

Macro-print mode per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md) — `symbols: []`, so
neither `earnings-cycle.mjs` nor `intraday-edges.mjs` applies and the cache-busting rule has no
target. Ten inputs, all fetched direct on 2026-09-06; nothing is carried from a sibling ledger except

near 0.001. The retail restatement is a different animal — the

Census's own archived vintages and found **all 398** overlapping months moved, median **−2.098%**,
roughly **15×** the wholesale figure. The obvious inference is that this print's ratio is unreadable.

It is wrong, and the reason is a weight coincidence nobody had checked. At June-2026 levels from
`timeseries1.xlsx`, retail is **30.38%** of total business inventories ($832.6B of $2,740.2B) and
**31.50%** of total business sales ($665.1B of $2,111.3B). The ratio is inventories ÷ sales, so a
restatement that scales both retail legs by the same factor very nearly cancels:

| Retail restatement | Inventories leg only | Sales leg only | **Both legs proportionally** |
|---|---|---|---|
| −0.5% | −0.0020 (prints 0.00) | +0.0020 (prints 0.00) | **+0.0001 (prints 0.00)** |
| −1.0% | −0.0039 (**prints −0.01**) | +0.0041 (prints 0.00) | **+0.0001 (prints 0.00)** |
| −2.0% | −0.0079 (**prints −0.01**) | +0.0082 (**prints +0.01**) | **+0.0003 (prints 0.00)** |

(Base: 2,740,239 ÷ 2,111,298 = 1.2979, published as 1.30.)

**Size is the wrong question; asymmetry is the whole question.** A 2% proportional restatement is
invisible at two decimals. A **1pp difference** between what happens to retail inventories and what
happens to retail sales moves the printed digit. That is a sharper, cheaper test than the one the
sibling declined, and it is registered as `-2`. The honest limit: this measures **sensitivity**, not
the realised asymmetry — Census's retail *inventories* workbook `mrtsinv92-present.xlsx` **404s**
(recorded in `probe-ref.blocked`), reproducing the retail lane's own blocked source, so the −2.098%
figure describes the *sales* series and the inventories side has no archived vintage this session
could reach.

### Leg 7 — a retail basis change makes an MTIS vintage loud · **REFUTED**, on the only precedent that exists

Census's own footnote on `timeseries1.xlsx` names one: *"Prior to the benchmark report released in
April 2025, the monthly retail estimates that were released in the Manufacturing and Trade
Inventories and Sales estimates included nonemployers."* That is a change to the *definition* of the
retail leg of this exact aggregate, carried by the **2025-04-16** MTIS (February 2025 data). Its
GDPNow vintage moved the change-in-inventory-investment contribution by **−0.0147pp** — against an
all-MTIS median of **0.0529pp** and a month-2 class median of 0.0227pp. **A basis change of the whole
retail history moved the nowcast less than an ordinary MTIS edition did.**

n=1, and the two changes are not the same size: the April-2025 change removed a class of
establishments, the AIES restatement re-levels the surviving history. But it is the only observation
of this kind that exists, it points the same way as the class, and it is the reason `-1` is registered
at Medium rather than hedged into meaninglessness.

### Primary content read — what the last published edition actually says

CB26-132 (released 2026-08-14, June 2026 data): combined distributive trade sales and manufacturers'
shipments **$2,111.3B**, down **1.1%** m/m (±0.2) and up **10.0%** y/y (±0.3); inventories
**$2,740.2B**, *virtually unchanged* m/m — the 90% confidence interval includes zero — and up **3.0%**
y/y (±0.5); **total business inventories/sales ratio 1.30**, against **1.39** in June 2025.

Sales growing 10.0% y/y against inventories at 3.0% is a destocking regime. Census's own 414-month
workbook places 1.30 at the **24.4th percentile** of readings strictly below it since 1992 — **28.3rd**
counting the 16 months that also printed 1.30. The obvious read is that the slide continues into
August. Three measurements say otherwise, and they are read at a **two-month** horizon here (June →
August) rather than the siblings' three:

- **It has already stopped.** The ratio troughed at **1.28** in May-2026 and ticked back to 1.30.
- **Conditioning subtracts.** In a comparable regime (12-month ratio change ≤ −0.08; today's is
  **−0.090**; **n=25** episodes), the ratio two months later is at or below its level then **64.0%**
  of the time (strictly below only 28.0%; nine of the twenty-five are exact ties), median change
  **+0.000** — against **67.5%** unconditionally across all 412 two-month windows. The conditional is
  *worse* than the unconditional, the same direction the siblings found at three months (44.0%).
- **The seasonals are neutral.** The adjusted July→August ratio change has a median of **0.000**
  across 34 years (38.2% down, 38.2% flat, 23.5% up).
