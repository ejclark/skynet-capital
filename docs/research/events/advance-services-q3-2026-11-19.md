## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The proposal that created this event argued one thing: seven Census release calendars contain exactly
three series that ever carry a `Suspended` row, and the Advance Services Report's Q3 2025 slot is one of
them — so 2026-11-19, the first Q3 edition scheduled after the only Q3 this series has ever lost, is
"the cleanest live test available in 2026" of the rule that **Census cancels previews and keeps full
reports**. **Is that rule true, is this print the right place to test it, what does the print actually
contain, and does anything downstream read it?**

**One-line verdict:** the rule is true and Census publishes the proof itself in a single table, so this
print is a *worse* test than the proposal thought and a **more important release** than it thought —
because Census separately names this exact edition as the AIES benchmark carrier for services, and
because the advance number, once printed, has essentially never been revised.

### Method

Macro-print mode per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md) — `symbols: []`, so neither
`earnings-cycle.mjs` nor `intraday-edges.mjs` applies and the cache-busting rule has no target. Nine
inputs, all fetched direct 2026-09-06:

1. **Seven Census release calendars** — `calendar-listview.html` (2026, HTTP 200, 91,396 bytes, 179 rows)
   plus `calendar-listview-2020/2021/2022/2023/2024/2025.html`, each parsed row-wise into
   `(release date, reference quarter, release code)` → **28 scheduled Advance Services rows and 28
   Quarterly Services Survey rows, 2020-02-19 → 2026-12-10**. (The 2019 page redirects to a table-less
   landing page — the same degradation the `wholesale-trade-2026-11-09` ledger recorded.)
2. **`census.gov/services/release_schedule.html`** (HTTP 200, 48,030 bytes) — the QSS program's own
   advance/full pairing table, 2025 Q3 → 2026 Q3.
3. **`census.gov/services/index.html`** (HTTP 200, 507,910 bytes) — the Announcements block, read
   word-for-word.
4. **`census.gov/services/qss/qss-current.pdf`** (HTTP 200, 611,965 bytes) — the live release, **CB26-139**,
   Q2 2026 advance.
5. **31 Wayback captures of that same PDF** (CDX API queried 2019→2026, `id_` raw fetches, 38 distinct
   200-status captures enumerated, 34 retrieved) → **12 complete advance→full pairs** on the Selected
   Services Total headline, plus 8 prior-quarter revision observations.
6. **`GDPNowcastDataReleaseDates.xlsx`** (Atlanta Fed) — `PostedUpdates`, **82 dated rows 2025-12-23 →
   2026-12-23**.
7. **`GDPTrackingModelDataAndForecasts.xlsx`** — `ContribArchives`, **1,871 vintages 2014-05-01 →
   2026-07-28**, with the `Data releases` free-text column joined to input 1 on release date.
8. **FRED `SP500`** — **2,514 daily closes 2016-09-06 → 2026-09-04**, restricted to **1,425 sessions since

9. **FRED `VIXCLS`** — daily closes through **2026-09-03**.

**Three source notes, recorded rather than papered over.** (a) **Yahoo returned HTTP 429 on every

that boundary. (b) The Atlanta Fed URL that eight sibling ledgers cite —
`/-/media/documents/cqer/researchcq/gdpnow/…` — now returns a **404 HTML page** under an `.xlsx`
filename, which a naive fetcher would parse as an empty workbook rather than a failure. The live path is
`/-/media/Project/Atlanta/FRBA/Documents/cqer/researchcq/gdpnow/…`, harvested from the GDPNow landing
page; logged in `probe-ref.blocked` and worth carrying forward. (c) VIX is quoted at **14.32

2026-09-04 from a source not available to this session, and this ledger does not adopt a reading it did

### Leg 1 — the report is scheduled for 2026-11-19 · **SUPPORTED**, on two independent Census primaries

The general economic-indicators calendar lists `A202611191000`, reference `A202603`, 10:00 a.m. The QSS
program's *own* release-schedule page independently names **"Thursday, November 19, 2026"** in its "2026
Quarter 3" advance column, under the standing note *"All releases occur at 10 am."* Neither derives from
the other; the program page is maintained by the Service Indicator Branch and the indicators calendar by
the Economic Indicators Division, and they carry different date formats and different companion columns.

Status promoted **`estimate` → `confirmed`**, prefix **`EST:` → `CENSUS:`**, on the precedent this
calendar already carries (`housing-starts-2026-12-17`, `m3-full-report-2026-12-03`,
`durable-goods-2026-11-25`). One honest qualification, because this series is the awkward case for the
promotion rule: **`confirmed` here is a claim about the scheduled date, not a guarantee of
publication.** This series carries a `Suspended` row twelve months back. Leg 2 measures exactly what
that risk costs, and it is not what the label implies.

### Leg 2 — "Census cancels previews and keeps full reports" · **SUPPORTED**, and Census prints the proof

The proposal inferred this rule from three series carrying `Suspended` rows. It does not need inferring.
`services/release_schedule.html` states it in adjacent columns of one row:

| Period covered | Advance Quarterly Services Report | Quarterly Services Report |
|---|---|---|
| **2025 Quarter 3** | **Suspended** | **Thursday, December 11, 2025** |
| 2025 Quarter 4 | Friday, February 20, 2026 | Thursday, March 12, 2026 |
| 2026 Quarter 1 | Thursday, May 21, 2026 | Thursday, June 11, 2026 |
| 2026 Quarter 2 | Thursday, August 20, 2026 | Wednesday, September 9, 2026 |
| **2026 Quarter 3** | **Thursday, November 19, 2026** | **Thursday, December 10, 2026** |

And the full report kept its date. **CB25-162, "QUARTERLY SELECTED SERVICES ESTIMATES, THIRD QUARTER
2025," released 10:00 a.m. EST Thursday, December 11, 2025** — Selected Services Total **$6,119.4
billion, +2.4% (±0.4)** from Q2 2025, **+7.2%** year-over-year. Not delayed, not merged, not abbreviated.
The funding lapse cost this series a **21-day preview and zero data**.

Two refinements the proposal's three-series framing hides:

- **The three suspended series are not one class.** The Advance Economic Indicators Report lost **five**
  editions (Sep/Oct/Nov 2025 and, after a delayed December print on 2026-02-19, Jan/Feb 2026 as well),
  not resuming normal cadence until 2026-04-29. Preliminary Steel Imports lost three. **This series lost
  exactly one** and resumed at its very next scheduled slot, **CB26-34 on 2026-02-20**. Grouping them as
  "the series that carry `Suspended` rows" is true and not very informative; the useful number is how
  many editions each lost, and this one is the least damaged of the three.
- **The suspension is legible in the release text itself.** Every full report's Selected Services Total
  paragraph normally ends with a sentence about the advance estimate. **CB25-162 has no such sentence** —
  because there was no advance estimate to revise from. The absence is the lapse's fingerprint in the
  primary source, and it is how a future close-out can detect a repeat without reading a calendar.

**Where this leaves the proposal's stated purpose.** As a *test* of the rule, 11-19 is now largely
redundant: the rule was tested in Q3 2025 and Census published both halves of the result. What 11-19
still tests is narrower and worth keeping — whether the series' recovery holds through a *second*
funding cliff, which it will not face this quarter (Leg 5).

### Leg 3 — the "Census data-entry oddity" · **REFUTED**

The proposal flagged the 2026-11-19 row's reference code `A202603` as *"a Census data-entry oddity noted,
not corrected here"*, reading `03` as a March marker where a Q3 marker belongs. It is neither odd nor a
March marker. Quarterly Census series encode reference as `A<yyyy><qq>`, and the form is consistent
across **all 28 scheduled Advance Services rows 2020-2026** without exception: `A202004` = Q4 2020 (posted
2021-02-19), `A202101` = Q1 2021, `A202103` = Q3 2021, `A202504` = Q4 2025 (posted 2026-02-20), `A202601`
= Q1 2026, `A202602` = Q2 2026, **`A202603` = Q3 2026**. The suspended row reads `A202503` = Q3 2025,
correctly. The 28 Quarterly Services Survey rows follow the same scheme. Nothing is miskeyed; the code is
simply not a month.

Worth stating plainly because the proposal's note would otherwise propagate: a sibling reading this
calendar for a *monthly* series would find `A202609`-style codes and reasonably assume the last two
digits are always a month. For quarterly series they are a quarter, and the two are indistinguishable
without knowing the series' frequency.

### Leg 4 — what this print actually contains · **the AIES benchmark, named by Census for this edition**

`census.gov/services/index.html`, Announcements block, verbatim:

> **Upcoming Benchmarking** — Effective with the November 2026 advance release for the third quarter of
> 2026, we anticipate that not seasonally adjusted quarterly services estimates will be revised to
> reflect historical corrections and results of the 2023 and 2024 Annual Integrated Economic Survey. The
> seasonally adjusted Selected Services Total will be revised to reflect historical corrections, results
> of the 2023 and 2024 Annual Integrated Economic Survey, and new seasonal factors.

This is the services twin of the wholesale restatement the `wholesale-trade-2026-11-09` ledger built its
whole "read 10-28, not 11-09" call around — with one structural difference that matters. **Wholesale got
a standalone restatement event** (`mwts-benchmark-revision-2026-10-26`) and then a regular print two days
later. **Services gets no standalone event at all**: the benchmark is carried *inside* this advance
release. There is nothing else on the calendar to point at. Which means the proposal's framing had the
importance right and the reason wrong — 11-19 matters, but as a benchmark carrier, not as a resumption
test.

It also sharpens the confirmed/estimate reading in a way worth writing down: the announcement's verb is
**"we anticipate"**, and it is Census hedging its own benchmark content, not its date. The date has two
primaries; the *content* is the estimate here.

Two channels, both measured shut.

**GDPNow — zero, not "no vintage on 11-19."** The proposal noted the Atlanta Fed schedules no posting on

### Leg 6 — what the print adds, and what the full report takes back · **MIXED, and the asymmetry is the finding**

Harvested from 34 Wayback captures of the release PDF, parsed to the Selected Services Total block only

**But the advance report is itself the bigger reviser.** Every advance release restates the *prior*
quarter's change. Eight observations: not revised (Q2 2019), 1.4→1.6 (Q1 2022, **0.20pp**), 1.5→1.2 (Q2
2023, **0.30pp**), 0.5→0.7 (Q3 2023, **0.20pp**), 2.3→2.1 (Q1 2024, **0.20pp**), 1.5→1.6 (Q2 2024,
**0.10pp**), not revised (Q3 2025), not revised (Q1 2026) — **median 0.15pp, max 0.30pp**.

**So the release does more revising than it will ever receive**, which is the inverse of the wholesale
series the sibling ledger studied. There, the advance report was the news and the full print a rounding
correction of a median 0.10pp. Here the advance is *both* the news and the correction, and the full
report three weeks later is the rounding correction — a smaller one. **MIXED** rather than SUPPORTED only
because the sample cannot speak to the benchmark: no pair in it was drawn across a benchmark
restatement, which is exactly what 11-19 is.

### Leg 7 — the session · **REFUTED as a tradable tell, and the corridor explains why**

Twenty-two of the 26 realized release dates fall inside the ≥2021 window. Median absolute close-to-close
move on the S&P 500: **0.339%** on release days against **0.561%** across all **1,425** sessions —
quieter, but the two-sided permutation test over 20,000 shuffles returns **p=0.157**. That is a null, and
it should be read as one: at n=22 the point estimate is not evidence.

The two loudest sessions in the sample are both other people's news — **2024-02-22 (+2.11%)**, the
session after NVDA's Q4 print, and **2025-02-21 (1.71%)**. Strip them and the remaining twenty are
unremarkable. Against that, **2026-11-19 is a Thursday**, and Thursdays since 2021 run a median
**0.618%** (n=286, p=0.096) — *louder* than the all-session baseline, so the weekday cuts against the
quiet reading rather than for it.

None of which will be attributable anyway. The corridor around 11-19 is dense with things that actually
move the tape: **retail sales (high impact), MTIS, industrial production and import/export prices all on
11-17**; **FOMC minutes, housing starts and the APEC leaders' meeting on 11-18**; **monthly opex on
11-20**. A 10:00 quarterly services preview with no nowcast channel is the least of what that week
contains.

### Leg 8 — the funding cliff · **the corridor asymmetry worth carrying forward**

`cr-expiry-2026-12-11` is the tracked funding cliff. Both halves of this quarter's services pair —
**advance 11-19, full 12-10** — land *before* it. That is the exact inverse of Q3 2025, when the advance
fell inside a lapse, and it is also the opposite exposure from `mtis-2026-12-16`, which the sibling lane
flagged as the December cycle's genuine lapse test at five days past the cliff.

So the schedule risk this ledger was created to study **is not live this quarter**. It becomes live for
Q4 2026, whose advance would fall in February 2027 — outside every calendar page currently published.

### Honest limits

- **One suspension is one observation.** Leg 2's rule rests on a single instance on this series. It is
  corroborated by two other series behaving the same way and by Census's own table, but "n=1 plus
  analogy" is what it is.
- **The tape metric changed under protest.** Yahoo and stooq were both unavailable (recorded above), so
  this ledger measures index close-to-close where siblings measure SPY session range. The p-value is
  honest for its own metric and is **not** comparable to theirs.
- **Twelve pairs is a small revision sample**, and Wayback capture density — not sampling design —
  chose which quarters are in it. Quarters with no capture between two releases are simply absent; the
  set is not adversarially selected but it is not random either.
- **Nothing here measures the benchmark.** The AIES restatement has no precedent in this sample, which
  is the whole reason both forward tests point at it.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stand aside on the event, and treat 2026-11-19 as a benchmark date to read rather than a print to
trade.** The date is **`confirmed`** on two Census primaries; the release carries Census's named AIES
restatement of the services series; and both of the channels through which a macro print could reach
this book are measured shut — **0 of 1,871** GDPNow vintages, **0** macro-keyed house playbooks,
`symbols: []`. The tape test returns a null at p=0.157 and the corridor would drown any signal regardless.

The proposal's own thesis survives in amended form: **Census cancels previews and keeps full reports** is
SUPPORTED, published by Census in one table, and measurably costless on this series — a 21-day preview
lost, zero data. What it therefore does *not* need is a live test on 11-19, and the ledger declines to
frame the print that way.

**Kill switches — what would move this stance:**

- A macro- or services-keyed playbook landing in `docs/plans/trade-playbooks.md` before **2026-11-01**;
  today there are none.
