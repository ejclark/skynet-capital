## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The calendar tracks this monthly series at 08-25, 09-29 and 10-27 and then stops. Is 2026-11-24 the
right date for the November edition; is there anything about the *November* print that its two
siblings' framings do not already cover; is the claim both siblings inherited — *this release does not
move the tape* — actually true when measured rather than anecdoted; and what, if anything, should a
paper book holding NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL AMZN do about it?

**One-line verdict:** the no-trade call survives and gets much stronger evidence — a measured null
across seven instruments at n=68 replaces two anecdotes — while three things genuinely distinguish
the November edition (a post-midterm panel whose historical precedents refute the rebound story, the
last consumer read before the December FOMC, and a pre-Black-Friday buying-plans slot), and one
side-finding breaks the publisher's stated cadence rule in a way that changes what the calendar
should file next.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is right for November and stays `estimate` — SUPPORTED, and the arithmetic is
   corroborated by three actual November releases.** The Conference Board's page was fetched today and
   states verbatim: **"The Conference Board publishes the Consumer Confidence Index® at 10 a.m. ET on
   the last Tuesday of every month."** Its only named next release is **"Tuesday, September 29th at 10
   AM ET"** — November is not named, so this is a rule-plus-arithmetic derivation, exactly the case the
   lane's no-self-confirm limit keeps at `estimate`. The arithmetic: November 2026's last Tuesday is
   **11-24** (11-30 is a Monday). Unlike the 10-27 sibling, this doc also *tested* the rule in the
   month that matters — **November 2025 released 2025-11-25**, **November 2022 released 2022-11-29**
   and **November 2018 released 2018-11-27**, each the last Tuesday of its month. Three for three in
   November.

2. **But the stated rule is false in December — REFUTED as a general rule, on a Conference Board
   primary, and this is the finding with a calendar consequence.** The Board's own
   **`CCI-Dec-2024`** press page, fetched today, is dated **December 23, 2024** with a stated cut-off
   of **December 16, 2024**. The last Tuesday of December 2024 was **December 31**. The December 2025
   edition is likewise reported at **2025-12-23** against a last Tuesday of **2025-12-30** (secondary,
   snippet fidelity). Both land on the Tuesday-or-Monday *before Christmas*, not in the
   Christmas-to-New-Year week. **Consequence, acted on in this PR:** a naive application of the
   publisher's rule would have filed the successor edition at **2026-12-29**, and the primary says that
   would be wrong; the proposal filed here is
   `proposals/consumer-confidence-2026-12-22.from-consumer-confidence-2026-11-24.json`, `estimate`,
   with the weakness of the derivation stated in its own `notes`. **What this does *not* do** is
   undermine leg 1 — the exception is a holiday-week exception, it is confined to December on the
   record available, and November has three-for-three compliance including 2025.

3. **"This print does not move the tape" — SUPPORTED, and for the first time in this series,
   measured.** Both siblings rested this on two anecdotes (July and August 2026 misses with muted
   closes). Because both the Conference Board and FHFA key on the last Tuesday, the 68 release days
   since 2021-01-26 are the **entire population** of Conference Board release days in that window, and
   they are testable directly. Against 1,359 baseline sessions:

   **Zero hits at p<0.05 in seven tests**, and all seven point the *same* way — release days are
   narrower, which is the wrong sign for a "the survey adds variance" story. The tails agree: SPY
   release-day p75 **1.436%** vs baseline **1.418%**, p90 **2.122%** vs **2.098%**. **The honest
   caveat, stated in full:** every one of these 68 days is also an FHFA HPI release day, so this is a
   *bundle*, not a clean read — the same confound the [`fhfa-hpi-2026-11-24`](fhfa-hpi-2026-11-24.md)

   leg reads SUPPORTED where FHFA's reads DECLINED. What this cannot rule out is two real effects
   cancelling, and nothing here is worth the session it would take to chase that.

4. **The post-midterm rebound intuition — REFUTED on both precedents that exist.** The November edition
   is the first Conference Board panel run essentially entirely after the **11-03** midterms, and the
   natural prior is a partisan-driven bounce. The record says otherwise. **November 2018** (election
   11-06): headline **137.9 → 135.7**, Present Situation 171.9 → 172.7, **Expectations 115.1 → 111.0**.
   **November 2022** (election 11-08): headline **102.2 → 100.2**, Present Situation 138.7 → 137.4,
   **Expectations 77.9 → 75.4**. Both fell; in both, Expectations fell *harder* than the headline. The
   mechanism is unsurprising once stated — the aggregate index is a weighted average over partisan
   groups whose reactions offset, so a composition change nets out at the level. n=2 is n=2 and this is
   registered as a forward test rather than claimed as a finding, but the direction of the available
   evidence is unambiguous and it is the opposite of the intuition.

5. **The partisan composition is where the information is — SUPPORTED as context, and deliberately not
   modelled.** Conference Board chief economist **Dana Peterson**, quoted 2026-08-29, said sentiment
   among Republicans and independents is *"starting to fade"* into the midterms — *"there's just a
   general shift of downward optimism"*, with consumers naming *"oil and gas prices, food, and also war
   and conflict."* That is the pre-election reading; November is the first post-election one, so the
   subcomponent split is the thing an assessment should look at. **What this doc refuses to do** is
   convert it into an election or seat model. The 10-27 sibling already discarded an aggregator's
   "confidence below 80 → incumbent-party losses" claim as unverified, and nothing found today changes
   that; a search result quoting party-level Present Situation figures "in September" is discarded on
   its face, since the September 2026 print has not happened.

6. **It is the last consumer read before the December FOMC — SUPPORTED, structurally.** On any of the
   December derivations in leg 2 (12-22 estimated, 12-29 naive), the December edition lands **after**
   the **2026-12-09** FOMC. The **11-28** communications blackout opens four days after this print, and
   the pre-FOMC **Beige Book publishes 11-25**, the morning after — compiled on an earlier district
   cut-off, so this survey does not feed it. The claim is therefore narrow and exact: 11-24 is the last
   Conference Board consumer datapoint to exist before the committee meets, not evidence that the
   committee reads it.

7. **It is a pre-Black-Friday print, and that is not the usual case — SUPPORTED by arithmetic.**
   2026-11-24 is the **Tuesday of Thanksgiving week** (Thanksgiving 2026-11-26, Black Friday 11-27).
   Across 2015–2026 the "last Tuesday of November" falls *before* Thanksgiving in only **5 of 12 years**
   (2015, 2019, 2020, 2024, 2026); in the other seven it lands in the week *after* the holiday weekend.
   So the six-month buying-plans and vacation-intentions lines publish **three days before** the
   weekend they describe rather than after it. **Honest deflation:** this makes the print a *prior* on
   holiday retail, and priors are reading aids. Sizing anything off a sentiment survey ahead of a
   consumer print is what the E1/S2 discipline forbids, and nothing here changes that.
