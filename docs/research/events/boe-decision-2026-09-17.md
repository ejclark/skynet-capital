## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This is the **third non-Fed central bank** this calendar tracks, and like the other two it was added
on **placement** — discovered 2026-09-09 in the [`fomc-2026-09-16`](fomc-2026-09-16.md) D-7
adjacency sweep, which noted only that the MPC *meeting* ends 09-16 and announces the next morning.
So the question is not "what will the BoE do with Bank Rate" (the strip has already answered) but:
**is there anything on this date that is not already priced, and does any of it reach a US-only,
equity-and-earnings-keyed book?**

**One-line verdict:** the rate is dead information, but this meeting is **not** the ECB's — it
carries a **separate, unpriced, once-a-year balance-sheet decision** (the APF gilt-reduction target
for Oct-2026 → Sep-2027) landing on the most stressed long end in the G7, and that decision — not
the rate — is what a pulse of this event should be reading.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The rate decision is not news — a hold is 93.1% priced, and the cut side is empty.**
   SUPPORTED, from a **fetched** strip rather than a press relay. centralbank.watch (BoE page,
   fetched today, data stamped **2026-09-08**): **hold 93.1% · hike 6.9% · cut 0.0%**, implied rate
   **3.77%** against a Bank Rate of **3.75%**. Bank Rate has stood at 3.75% since late 2025 and was
   maintained at the **30 July 2026** meeting. A decision ~93% priced carries no tradable surprise
   in either direction — the tradable content of an event is the distance between outcome and
   price, and here that distance is ~7%. This is the same finding the
   [`ecb-decision-2026-09-10`](ecb-decision-2026-09-10.md) ledger opened with, and it survives the
   same test.

2. **The unpriced content is a balance-sheet decision, and this is the finding.** SUPPORTED,
   **primary, from two independent BoE documents**. The APF gilt-sales **Market Notice of 19 June
   2026** (fetched today) states verbatim: *"The Bank intends to communicate any future sales
   schedules for Q4 2026 or beyond following the MPC's annual review … which is expected to be
   announced alongside the MPC meeting on 17 September 2026."* The **2026 Q2 APF Quarterly Report**
   (fetched today) records the standing mandate: the MPC *"voted to reduce the stock of gilts held
   in the APF by a further £70 billion over the 12-month period from October 2025 to September
   2026"*, comprising *"both maturing gilts and sales"*, with five Q2 sale operations reducing
   holdings by **£6.1bn** and the stock at **£521.8bn** on **30 June 2026**, *"valued at initial
   purchase price."* So on 09-17 the MPC sets a **new annual gilt-supply number** — and unlike the
   rate, there is no futures market pricing it to 93%.

3. **The consensus for that number is £50bn, down from £70bn — and the precedent says the Bank
   slows rather than surprises.** SUPPORTED, press-cited on the expectation, primary-adjacent on the
   precedent. Market participants' median expectation for the 12 months to September 2027 is
   **£50bn**, *"unchanged from June's forecast"*. The precedent is the September 2025 decision
   itself: the MPC voted **7–2** to slow from **£100bn** to **£70bn**, *"broadly in line with a
   median forecast of £67.5 billion in a Reuters poll"* — i.e. the Bank has once already delivered
   the survey's number rather than a shock, and it has now slowed in two consecutive annual
   reviews. Registered as **FT-boe-decision-2026-09-17-1**.

4. **The Bank has already stopped selling the part of the curve that is stressed — which is the
   tell.** SUPPORTED, primary. The June market notice's Q3 2026 schedule is **five operations,
   £3.375bn total**: 20 Jul medium £600m · 27 Jul short £725m · 10 Aug medium £600m · 17 Aug short
   £725m · 14 Sep short £725m — **zero long-dated sales for the entire quarter**. The standing
   policy behind that is on the record from the prior review: for the year from Q4 2025 the Bank
   *"will aim to sell fewer long maturity sector gilts than gilts at other maturities, to better
   reflect demand conditions."* So the interesting question on 09-17 is not only the headline
   target but whether the **skew persists** — a slowdown that quietly re-opens long sales would be
   tighter than the headline reads, and vice versa. This is the qualifier the "one number" signal
   above names.

5. **The tape it lands on is the worst UK long end in 28 years, and the fiscal side is pushing the
   same way.** SUPPORTED, press-cited levels with dates. The **30-year gilt reached 5.89%** in
   early September 2026, *"the highest level since March 1998"*; on **2026-09-08** the UK sold
   **£4.25bn** of the 5.375% 2056 at **5.8168%**, *"the highest at any gilt auction or syndication
   since the UK Debt Management Office was established in 1998"*. Coverage ties the move to two
   things this calendar already tracks — the **US–Iran energy shock** (a country structurally
   dependent on imported energy) and the **synchronised global long-end move** the
   [`treasury-30y-bond-2026-09-10`](treasury-30y-bond-2026-09-10.md) sweep established. The supply
   side is moving too: the **DMO cut long conventional gilt issuance to £23bn for 2026-27, ~9% of
   total issuance**, with CEO Jessica Pulay citing *"declining structural demand for long-dated
   gilts from domestic pension funds"*, and the same coverage says the DMO is *"exploring whether to
   absorb long-dated gilts from the Bank of England's balance sheet."* Two institutions are
   therefore retreating from the same maturity bucket at the same time.

6. **The Bank's own estimate of what QT does to yields went UP this year, which raises the stakes on
   the number.** SUPPORTED, attributed. Deputy Governor **Dave Ramsden**, at the **July 2026**
   Monetary Policy Report press conference, put QT's effect on **10-year gilt yields at 20–30bp**,
   revised **up** from a prior **15–25bp**; against ~350bp of total gilt-yield rise since QT began
   in February 2022, that is roughly **6–9%** of the move. Read plainly: the Bank has just told the
   market its balance-sheet decision matters *more* than it previously said, one meeting before it
   has to make the next one. That is why leg 2's decision is the payload and not a footnote.

7. **The rate side's live question is the vote split, and the domestic data argue the hawks stay
   at three.** MIXED — the direction is supported, the magnitude is genuinely two-sided. The **30
   July 2026** minutes (fetched today): **6–3** to hold at 3.75%, with **Megan Greene, Catherine L
   Mann and Huw Pill** each preferring **+25bp to 4.00%**, arguing that *"setting policy as if there
   are stronger second-round effects and course correcting if needed, would prove to be less costly
   than vice versa."* The majority's premise is stated just as plainly — *"There is little evidence
   so far to suggest such effects"*, with underlying disinflation *"supported by a loose labour
   market and slowing wage growth"* — while the risk assessment is hawkish: *"risks to the inflation
   outlook are tilted to the upside."* **What has happened since runs mostly the majority's way.**
   July CPI: headline **2.9%** y/y (up from a 15-month low of **2.6%**), but **core held at 2.6%**
   and **services fell 3.6% → 3.4%**, with the jump *"almost entirely from energy"* — housing and
   household services **4.1%** on a **14.7%** gas rise after an Ofgem cap change. Labour (ONS,
   fetched today, Apr–Jun): regular pay **+3.5%**, total pay **+4.1%**, unemployment **4.9%**,
   vacancies **707,000** and falling. That is an energy-driven headline sitting on a decelerating
   domestic core — structurally the same shape as the euro-area print the ECB ledger tested, and the
   shape that historically keeps a hawkish minority a minority. **The counterweight, kept on the
   record:** the dissenting bloc has grown at three consecutive meetings, and two switchers make it
   5–4. Registered as **FT-boe-decision-2026-09-17-2**.

8. **September has no forecast round — which is exactly why the QT decision is the payload.**
   SUPPORTED, primary. The BoE dates page marks **February, April, July and November** as the
   Monetary Policy Report months for 2026; **September is not one**. So there are no new staff
   forecasts, no fan charts and no press conference to move the path — the meeting publishes a
   summary, minutes, a vote, and the annual QT review. An event with no forecast round and a
   93%-priced rate has precisely one unpriced document in it, and leg 2 named it.

9. **The path, not the meeting, is where a disagreement is available — and the strip is bolder than
   the data.** MIXED, and this is the ledger's one genuinely contestable position. centralbank.watch
   (fetched, 09-08 data) prices **54.2% higher at 2026-11-05** (implied **3.89%**) and **94.6%
   higher by 2026-12-17** (implied **4.11%**) — about **37bp, ~1.5 hikes, across three meetings**,
   with the September meeting contributing almost none of it. Against that: services and core are
   decelerating, regular pay is 3.5% and slowing, unemployment is **up 0.2pp on the year**, vacancies
   are falling, and the **28 October Budget** — Chancellor John Healey's first, with an OBR forecast
   the same day — is previewed across the UK press as tax-raising, i.e. **fiscal tightening arriving
   before the November MPR meeting**. A committee that gets a contractionary Budget five weeks before
   its forecast round has a reason to deliver less than 1.5 hikes. **The honest other side:** the
   energy shock is live and un-forecastable, the November meeting is the first with a full forecast
   round, and a strip at 94.6% is not usually wrong by accident. Registered at **medium**, not high,
   as **FT-boe-decision-2026-09-17-3** — a call that at most **one** hike lands by 12-17, not a call
   of none.

10. **No tracked symbol carries a sterling channel — and no play is proposed.** SUPPORTED, and this
    is where the honesty sits rather than a hedge. `symbols: []`. The house playbooks (S1/S2/E1/S3/S4

### Honest limits

across the whole year and redemptions arriving lumpily, and the June notice itself flags that *"it
may be necessary for the Bank to make some adjustment to meet the target."* That is a plausible
explanation, **not a verified one** — no September redemption schedule was fetched, and the £488bn
