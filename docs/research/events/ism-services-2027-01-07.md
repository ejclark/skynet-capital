## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This is the sixth ISM Services ledger. Its five siblings have measured the release's reaction function
to nil at daily resolution ([11-04](ism-services-2026-11-04.md)), at hourly resolution
([12-03](ism-services-2026-12-03.md)), and out of sample on the year's biggest beat
([02-03](ism-services-2027-02-03.md)) — while continuing to carry forward **one** positive quantitative
claim: that release days are more likely than baseline to be large-magnitude bond days (`^TNX` 34.8%,
TLT 30.3% clearing own-year p78 against a 22% construction baseline). The 02-03 sibling killed the
*month-slot* story and explicitly told the chain to keep this one "at its services size."

So the question this session asked is not "what will the December-2026 print be." It is: **is the
surviving claim an ISM fact at all, or an artifact of the comparator?** A release day is, by
construction, always the third or fourth trading day of a month. The chain has been comparing it to
*every day of the year*. Nobody has compared it to **the days sitting next to it**.

**One-line verdict.** It is an artifact. Against the correct comparator the release-day bond effect
disappears in all six instruments, `^TNX` is larger two sessions *before* the release than on it, 2026
rejects the pooled rate 0-for-9 out of sample, and what is actually real is an **early-month** effect
that has nothing to do with this survey. What is left worth tracking about 2027-01-07 is structural:
it is the emptiest corridor on this calendar, which makes it the cleanest observation available.

### Honest limits

**The placebo is a comparison of calendar sets and cannot establish causation in either direction** —
it shows the release day is not distinguishable from its neighbours, which refutes an attribution
rather than proving an absence; a real effect smaller than this test's power would be invisible, and at
n=132 vs 528 that power is modest (the `^TNX` +7.0pt gap is genuinely unresolved at p = 0.12, not shown
to be zero). **My pooled numbers do not exactly replicate the sibling's** — this session measures
`^TNX` 37.1% / TLT 31.1% / QQQ 26.5% / SPY 25.8% where [02-03](ism-services-2027-02-03.md) reported
34.8 / 30.3 / 25.8 / 23.5 on the same window and the same n=132, a 0.8–2.3pt gap that is almost
certainly percentile-index convention (this session ranks with `floor(0.78·(n−1))`; the sibling's
convention is unstated) and is **not reconciled here**. That gap does not touch the conclusion, since
the placebo, the control set and the baseline are all computed inside this one run. **The first-Friday
leg's label is unconfirmed** — as a BLS Employment-Situation proxy it matched 4 of 10 months on the
schedule read today, and it is reported as a calendar set for that reason. **The December-2026
Employment Situation date is not published at all** — bls.gov's schedule page ends at 2026-12-04
(Nov-2026 data), and its 2027 paths returned HTTP 404 — so whether 2027-01-07 sits one session or six
before the jobs report is unknown, and no `jobs-2027-01-08` is proposed here for exactly the reason the
[02-01 sibling](ism-manufacturing-2027-02-01.md) gave: this calendar's jobs entries carry a live `BLS:`
