## At a glance

**CLOSED 2026-09-10 — the stand-aside was right, and the event was bigger than assessed.** Read
[`## Outcome`](#outcome) first: the promised schedule published *on* 9/9 and made the long-end
maximum a **floor** ("= or > $4 billion"), setting the first operation at **$6B — 3× the old cap,
not 2×**. That operation ran 9/10 and accepted **$5.187B**, passing FT-1's $3.5B bar and breaking a
25-of-25 cap-fill streak on the way. It did not help: the 30Y (**5.37%**) and 20Y (**5.39%**) posted
their highest par closes of 2026 that same afternoon, firing kill switch 3. The pre-event call
below is preserved as written.

**TL;DR.** Treasury's long-end buyback ceiling doubles from $2B to **at least $4B** per operation
(10-20Y and 20-30Y sectors), effective 2026-09-09 through 2026-11-04 — a fiscal-side attempt to
steady long-duration yields, announced 2026-08-19 by Secretary Bessent, and **confirmed** off the
treasury.gov primary (sb0607, plain curl HTTP 200, 9/2). It is not a rate cut and not QE (Treasury
swaps long-term for short-term debt; no new bank reserves) — and its own first market test faded
inside a day: the 10Y fell ~6bps on announcement, fully round-tripped by 8/20, and by 9/1 sat
~**4.77%**, *above* its pre-announcement level, with the 30Y ~**5.27%** after a 19-year high.
**Two facts keep 9/9 a non-event on its own date.** The promised updated buyback schedule is
**still unpublished** — re-fetched 9/8, 20 days on, byte-identical, masthead still "For Publication
August 5, 2026", every long-end row still capped at $2B — so no published number yet confirms the
step-up. And the schedule lists **no long-end operation on 9/9 itself**: 9/9 is an eligibility date,
and the first test of the $4B floor is the 10-20Y operation on **9/10**, whose preliminary
eligible-CUSIP list posts 9/9 at 11:00 ET. **Updated 9/8, and it cuts both ways.** The increase is
**more real in execution than assumed** — Treasury's own operations data shows the $2B cap has been
*binding*, not demand: 10-20Y filled its cap **25 of 25** times since May-2024 and 20-30Y **24 of
26**, on offers never below **$6.4B**. But it is also **smaller than it sounds**: every other
nominal sector (1Mo-2Y, 2-3Y, 3-5Y, 5-7Y, 7-10Y) *already* ran a $4B cap, so 9/9 lifts the long end
to parity rather than creating a new tier — and the seven doubled operations through 11/4 add
**~$14B** of buying against **$61B** of 10Y+30Y supply auctioned this week alone, with coupon sizes
unchanged from June. Nothing here is a trade: read 9/9 as context for the Sep 1→16 rate-path
corridor, not as its own catalyst.

## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** what does the September 2026 increase in Treasury long-end buyback
operation size mechanically do to Treasury supply/demand dynamics, what is the stated policy
rationale, how has the market reacted to buyback-size changes this cycle (including the 8/19
announcement itself), and which of our tracked names (NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL AMZN)
carry meaningful rate-duration sensitivity worth flagging — around 2026-09-09?

**One-line verdict:** a real but modest fiscal-side liquidity tool — Treasury doubling its long-end
buyback ceiling to steady a yield curve under deficit/issuance/inflation pressure — whose own first
market test (the 8/19 announcement) faded within a day, so the honest read is a secondary,
context-only input to the Sep 1→16 rate-path corridor, not a catalyst in its own right; the house
answer is guard-shaped, and the sharper watch item is whether Bessent escalates the program (size,
sectors, or TGA funding) beyond what's dated here.

**Conviction legs, tested:**

1. **The mechanism and date are right — SUPPORTED (with a gated-primary caveat).** Treasury's own
   press release (home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0607, announced 2026-08-19) increases the
   maximum size of nominal long-end liquidity-support buyback operations from $2 billion to **at
   least $4 billion per operation**, covering the **10-20Y and 20-30Y** coupon sectors, with the
   larger operations running **2026-09-09 through 2026-11-04**. The direct treasury.gov fetch timed
   out during this research, so this leg rests on convergent, independently-sourced press citing the
   release directly (CNBC, Bloomberg, Axios, Quartz, all 2026-08-19) rather than the primary
   document itself — the same gated-primary pattern flagged on the ISM sibling docs, a real limit,
   not an unconfirmed fact.

2. **The stated rationale is yield-curve stabilization amid a deficit-driven long-end selloff, not a
   rate cut or QE — SUPPORTED on mechanism, contested on framing.** Bessent's own framing: the
   program is meant to "steady the bond market" and push down long-term yields (CNBC, Bloomberg,
   both 8/19), following a selloff in longer-dated debt that began in June 2026 on intensified
   deficit concerns, above-target inflation, and corporate debt issuance competing with Treasurys for
   investor demand. Multiple independent analyses (dlacalle.com, Seeking Alpha) agree the mechanism
   is **not QE** — Treasury swaps long-term for short-term debt without creating new bank reserves,
   closer to an "Operation Twist" than balance-sheet expansion. But critics (cited across CFR,
   Forbes, and wire coverage) frame it as a debt-monetization risk and a step toward "fiscal
   dominance" — Treasury directly influencing financial conditions in a role traditionally the Fed's
   — plus a structural concern that Treasury bills already run ~22% of marketable debt outstanding,
   above the Treasury Borrowing Advisory Committee's own recommended 15-20% range. Both readings are
   live in the coverage; this doc treats the mechanism claim as settled and the political-economy
   framing as a live, unresolved debate to watch, not a fact to adjudicate.

3. **The market's own first reaction to this program faded fast — SUPPORTED, and the load-bearing
   finding for the stance.** On the 8/19 announcement, the 10-year yield fell from ~4.68% to as low
   as ~4.63% (a roughly -6bps intraday move, closing near 4.647%) — but by the next session (8/20)
   yields had **fully round-tripped and moved higher**, up more than 5bps to ~4.704%, as the market
   "digested the move as well as the longer-term structural problems facing the fixed income market"
   (CNBC, 8/20, "Treasury bond buybacks ease long-term yields, but analysts see limited relief").
   Analysts quoted there frame the buyback tool as unlikely on its own to change the long-end yield
   *trajectory*, at most muting near-term pressure — a genuinely weak, fast-fading first data point
   for how much this specific program moves the tape.

4. **Historical operation-size precedent reinforces the "limited, not decisive" read — SUPPORTED.**
   Even the largest-ever single buyback operation to date (~$10 billion, June 2025) drew bids more
   than double its size (~$22.87 billion offered) without resolving the deeper structural issue
   analysts cite: scarcity of usable collateral, specifically on-the-run Treasuries, in dollar funding
   markets. Treasury's own stated posture is that it continues to evaluate further nominal-coupon and
   FRN auction-size changes based on structural demand trends — an open-ended, reactive policy, not a
   one-time fix.

5. **Funding mechanics and an escalation path exist but are not yet dated — MIXED (a real, undated
   risk).** Bessent has separately floated funding buybacks by tapping into the roughly **$1 trillion
   Treasury General Account** (CNBC, 8/24) and has said the program could expand beyond the $4B/op
   ceiling "if needed" (CNBC, 8/20) — genuine escalation paths that would be a materially bigger
   fiscal lever than what's confirmed and dated here. Neither has a firm date or size as of this
   research; flagged as a live watch item rather than proposed as a new calendar entry (no date to
   anchor it to).

6. **Tracked-name sensitivity — ranked, with mechanism.** `symbols: []` — market-wide, and the
   ranking runs through the same rate-duration channel every macro print in this window feeds. Most
   sensitive: **CRWV** (debt-financed, capital-intensive datacenter build — any durable long-end
   yield relief eases both its discount rate and its actual cost of capital, though the fast-faded
   8/19 reaction argues against sizing on this alone). Then the high-multiple semis **NVDA / AVGO /
   MRVL** (AI-beta, longest-duration cash flows). Middle: **MSFT / GOOG / META** (mega-cap duration,
   fortress balance sheets mute the financing channel). Least direct: **AAPL / AMZN** (rate-path
   exposure only here). No REIT or regional-bank name is on the tracked list, so the sector most
   textbook-sensitive to this specific tool (rate-sensitive financials/REITs) isn't one we carry
   direct exposure to — noted for completeness, not actioned.

**Stance (as of 2026-09-02: mechanism, sizes, sectors and dates now read verbatim off the

rate-sensitive position keyed to this program alone, given its own first market test (the 8/19
announcement) fully faded within a day. Base case (**estimate-labeled**: analyst consensus already

yield pressure modestly but do not reverse the underlying deficit/issuance/inflation-driven selloff

guard-shaped. **2026-09-02 update, and it cuts the base case's way:** with the 10Y at ~4.77% (9/1)

relief has reversed a full week before the larger operations run — the "structural supply problem,
not a liquidity problem" read is now the tape's read too, not just the analysts'.

**2026-09-08 update — the mechanism is better understood, and the stance is unchanged because the
two new facts cancel.** Both come off Treasury's own operations data (`fiscaldata.treasury.gov`
`buybacks_operations`), read directly for the first time in this ledger rather than through press
narration. *Upward:* the $2B ceiling has been the **binding constraint, not demand** — the 10-20Y
bucket accepted the full cap in **25 of 25** operations since 2024-05 and 20-30Y in **24 of 26**, on
offers whose minimum was **$6.4B** and median **$19.7B**. Doubling a cap that binds every time is a
real change in executed purchases, not a symbolic ceiling. *Downward, and larger:* the increase
lifts the long end to **parity with a size the rest of the nominal curve already had** — 1Mo-2Y,
2-3Y, 3-5Y, 5-7Y and 7-10Y all ran $4B caps before sb0607 existed, and only the two long-end
buckets sat at $2B — so this is the removal of a discount, not an extraordinary intervention. And
the totals are small: **+$2B per operation × 7 operations** in the 09-09→11-04 window ≈ **+$14B**
over eight weeks, against **$61B** of 10Y+30Y issuance in this single week, at reopening sizes
(**$39B**/**$22B**) **identical to June's** — which independently corroborates Bessent's stated
"auctions will continue as usual despite the expanded buyback program" (Fox Business). The program
adds to demand; it subtracts nothing from supply. **Neither fact is directional**, and the base
case stands: modest, technical, context for the corridor — never a catalyst. Registered as
FT-treasury-buyback-increase-2026-09-09-1 (execution) and -2 (the base case's dated guard).

### The first enlarged operation — FT-1 passes, and reveals where the real constraint sits

`buybacks_operations`, `operation_date = 2026-09-10`, 10Y-20Y nominal, 1:40–2:00pm ET, settling
9/11: **$10,489,000,000 offered · $5,187,000,000 accepted** across **23 of 40** eligible issues.

  binding constraint, not demand, so doubling it converts directly into accepted par* — is
  supported on its first and only observation.

  roughly **$5.2B**, above the announced floor and below the set maximum. That is the single most

  maximum high enough for demand to bind before the ceiling did.
