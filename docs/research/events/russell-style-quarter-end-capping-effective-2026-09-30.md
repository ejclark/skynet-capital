### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The dates are owner-published and the standing rule reproduces them — SUPPORTED, and the check is this
   session's own.** The FAQ's schedule table gives the four 2026 cut-offs and effective dates verbatim. The
   guide gives the *rule* — reference date **T-3**, effective the **last business day** of the quarter. Running
   the rule against the 2026 calendar:

   | Quarter | Last business day | T-3 by the rule | Owner's published cut-off |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Q1 | Tue 31 Mar | **26 Mar** | 26 March 2026 |
   | Q2 | Tue 30 Jun | **25 Jun** | 25 June 2026 |
   | **Q3** | **Wed 30 Sep** | **25 Sep** | **25 September 2026** |
   | Q4 | Thu 31 Dec | **28 Dec** | 28 December 2026 |

   Four for four. The December row is the one that earns the table: T-3 from Thursday 31 December has to step
   over **Christmas Day**, and the naive calendar count gives 28 December only if you count *trading* days. The
   owner's published date agrees. What remains `estimate` is the **prefix**, not the fact.

2. **HEADLINE — the proposal's scope claim is REFUTED, and it is refuted in the direction that makes the event
   bigger.** Effective at the open of **2025-03-24**, FTSE Russell applied capping to the **standard** Russell US

   name. So "a capped style product" is not an exotic sleeve — it is the default. The iShares Trust Form 497

3. **The single-name leg is dead by a wide margin — SUPPORTED.** The trigger is a **24%** capped weight for any
   one company. **NVDA is the largest weight anywhere in the family at 16.38%** (IWY, 2026-09-04) and **16.02%**

   to gain ~47% relative to its own index inside a quarter. Nothing in this family is a single-name story.

4. **Five of eight funds cannot breach at all — SUPPORTED, and it is structural rather than a this-quarter
   reading.** The aggregate leg counts only companies **above 4.8%**. IWP's largest holding is **3.09%**, IWS's

   against a 48% trigger. That is a property of a mid- or small-cap universe, not of this quarter's prices.
   IWD (16.40%) and IWX (22.79%) have non-empty cohorts and are still nowhere near.

5. **HEADLINE — the quarter-end check is a backstop that its own predecessor defuses, measured at roughly
   sevenfold — SUPPORTED, and it is the reason the call is a stand-aside rather than a watch.** The two reviews
   are not independent. The **quarterly** capping review (cut-off **2026-09-09**, effective at the **09-21**
   open) resets the over-4.5% cohort to **45%** under the RIC 22.5/45 scheme. Only *after* that reset does the
   quarter-end test strike, on the **09-25** close. Since IWY's cohort is **46.79%** — above 45% — the 09-09
   review **will** recap it. Feeding both starting points through five years of real prices (fixed cohort
   proportions, rolling 12-session windows, cohort return measured against the fund's own return):

   | Starting cohort | Needed vs index, 12 sessions | Windows clearing it | Base rate |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | **45.00%** (post-reset — the live case) | **+6.67%** | **50 / 1,243** | **4.02%** |
   | 46.79% (if the reset did not happen) | +2.59% | 360 / 1,243 | 28.96% |

   The reset is worth a **7.2×** reduction in breach probability. The equivalent figure for IWF, starting from
   its uncapped **44.62%** and needing **+7.57%**, is **28 / 1,243 = 2.25%**. Note what this says about the
   mechanism generally: **the quarter-end thresholds (24% / 4.8% / 48%) are deliberately looser than the
   quarterly ones (22.5% / 4.5% / 45%)**, so the test exists to catch drift *between* reviews and is designed to
   be rarely met. It is doing exactly what it was built to do.

6. **The recap arithmetic is company-level and iterative, and both details change the numbers — SUPPORTED.** The
   guide settles two things a careless read gets wrong. **Company level:** *"All lines of the same company are
   combined as part of the capping process."* Alphabet's two lines therefore count once, at **10.64%** in IWF —
   and since GOOG standalone is **4.76%**, just under the 4.8% line, the security-level reading returns
   **39.86%** against the correct **44.62%**, understating the cohort by nearly 5pp and turning a near-live leg
   into an apparently dead one. **Iterative:** Step 1 caps any company over y%, redistributes, *"repeat[s] until
   no company weight exceeds y%"*, then Step 2 selects the top group by a z% percentile (45% for RIC 22.5/45).
   That is **not** the live S&P Select Sector rule, which the sibling ledger established scales the whole
   over-4.8% group down to 45% *by relative proportions*. Two superficially similar rules, two different
   arithmetics.

7. **The proposal's correlation claim is wrong on timing — REFUTED, though the pairing survives in weaker
   form.** The proposal said both families recap *"into the SAME OPEN"*. They do not:

   | | Reference date | Effective | Trackers trade |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | **Russell US Style** | 2026-09-25 close (T-3) | **open of 2026-09-30** | **2026-09-29 close** |
   | **S&P Select Sector** | 2026-09-29 close (2nd-last bd) | **after the 2026-09-30 close** | **2026-09-30 close** |

   An index change effective *at the open* is traded at the **prior** close, which is the convention Russell's
   own reconstitution follows. So the two flows are **one session apart**, struck on reference dates **two
   sessions apart**, over different universes (mega-cap growth vs GICS sectors) and different aggregate
   thresholds (48% vs 50%). The proposal's underlying intuition — that both key off the same handful of mega-cap
   weights, so they tend to fire together or not at all — is directionally reasonable and untested here; what
   is wrong is the "one session, two families" framing that made it sound concentrated. It is a quarter-end
   *corridor*, and the corridor was already crowded.

### Honest limits

**The base rate is a proxy, and its cohort is frozen.** The 4.02%/28.96% figures hold today's five cohort
members at today's proportions across five years of prices; the real cohort's membership changed over that
window (AVGO's weight, and NVDA's, are recent). It measures *whether a mega-cap growth cohort of roughly this
shape can move this far in 12 sessions*, which is the right question, but it is not a backtest of the actual
index rule. **The weights are vendor ETF holdings, not index weights**, dated 2026-08-13 to 2026-08-27 and
drifted forward on daily closes — an ETF's holdings track its index with a lag and a sampling difference, so
every cohort figure here carries perhaps a few tenths of a point of error. On IWY's 46.79% against a 45% cap
that error does not change the conclusion; on a reading nearer a threshold it would. **The 12-session window is
an approximation** of the 09-09 strike to the 09-25 test; the exact session count depends on when the recapped
weights are struck versus applied, which the guide does not spell out to the day. **The cohort is a step
function** (leg 6's AVGO note), so a smooth-drift base rate understates the variance in both directions.
**Nothing here observed a quarter-end capping event actually firing** — no index notice for one was found this
session, and the T-3 gap gives only three sessions in which such a notice would appear, so "the test rarely
fires" is inference from arithmetic and thresholds, not from a count of past firings. That is the largest gap
in this sheet. **The date is `estimate`** and every trading-adjacent statement above carries that label; the
trigger is conditional and no primary can pre-confirm it fires; `symbols` is empty by design and no house
playbook is index-flow-keyed.
