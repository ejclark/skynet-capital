## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Six blackout ledgers precede this one, and the family has been arguing with itself about a single
session. [September 2026](fomc-blackout-start-2026-09-05.md) called a no-short-vol window because
the decision variable printed inside the gate; [October](fomc-blackout-start-2026-10-17.md) refused
that rule because October's prints landed *before* the gate;
[November](fomc-blackout-start-2026-11-28.md) named the **overhang** — the gate outlives the decision
by one session; [January](fomc-blackout-start-2027-01-16.md) turned it into a sizing rule;
[March](fomc-blackout-start-2027-03-06.md) refused January's rule and called the boundary inert; and
[May](fomc-blackout-start-2027-05-29.md) reversed March with the widest overhang measurement in the
sample (June: 1.145% vs 0.413%).

So the question is narrow and adversarial: **this gate's calendar is unusual — does that unusualness
license anything, and does the overhang premium the family keeps re-deriving survive being measured
on a gate shaped like this one?**

**One-line verdict.** The unusualness is real and it is the *reason* the premium fails. September
2027's meeting is the only second-Tuesday September in 21 years, which puts the first post-gate
session on **quad-witching**; in the six realized September gates with that same geometry the
overhang runs **1.010×** its own September baseline and loses to the next session **4 of 6**,
against **1.361×** and 10-of-13 wins in the thirteen that do not. Every other framing this gate
invites — Labor Day inside, payrolls on the deadline, the gate lifting into expiry — measures
**inert**. Date `estimate`; no directional call.
