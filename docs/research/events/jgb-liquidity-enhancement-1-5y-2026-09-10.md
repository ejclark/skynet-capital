## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This event was discovered twice on 2026-09-09, by two sibling adjacency sweeps that filed it as competing
proposals (`proposals/jgb-liquidity-enhancement-1-5y-2026-09-10.from-jgb-liquidity-enhancement-1-5y-2026-11-20.json`
and `…from-jgb-liquidity-enhancement-5-11y-2026-09-25.json`). Both named it the **scoring instrument** for
predictions their own ledgers had registered. Neither could ask the question this ledger has to ask,
because it only exists at D-1 with the terms published:

**is tomorrow's print actually comparable to the prints the family calibrated on?**

**One-line verdict: more comparable than any print in the family's history, and the statistic the family
would read it with is less comparable than any.** The menu is a genuine controlled repeat of 2026-07-16 —
four of five sectors identical down to the yen, same ¥700bn size — which is exactly the control the "the
pocket died in July" claim has never had. But the *pocket itself* has inverted its own composition over
the window it is quoted across, and the offering-size mechanism the 1-5y sibling proposed for its decay
is refuted by the sibling's own subsample: the decay ran to completion at a **constant** ¥700bn.

happened while size did not move.

**Why this is MIXED and not a clean replacement.** Menu composition and calendar time are collinear at
**+0.996** across these five prints — the ≥1% share rises monotonically because coupons rise
monotonically — so "the statistic drifted" and "the preference faded" fit the same six points and this
ledger cannot separate them at n = 5. **And composition drift alone cannot produce the observed sign
flip:** dilution of a fixed preference drives the pocket toward **zero**, never through it. July's
**−0.878** is therefore not arithmetic; something behavioural is in there too. The honest conclusion is
narrower than "the pocket is an artefact" and wider than the sibling's: **the size mechanism is dead, the
statistic is contaminated, and at least part of the decay is real.** What to do about it is leg 5.

**5. The fix is already in the family's own table: quote the vintage split — SUPPORTED, and it costs

inherits it — and only observes that it implies the coupon proxy must eventually break, and that the
composition table above is that break arriving.

### Honest limits on this close-out

- **The status stays `estimate`.** MOF published the result, but `market-events-data.ts`'s
  confirmed-prefix taxonomy still has no slot for a non-US sovereign debt office, and this lane may
  not self-confirm. The label constrains nothing here — nothing was ever keyed to this date.
- **n = 6 is still small.** Every era statistic in this document, including the two bands that broke,
  rests on six prints. The correlation collapse in §1 is itself an n = 6 number and should not be
  read as "there is no relationship" — only as "the published −0.978 was not robust."
- **One print is one observation.** FT-2, FT-3 and FT-4 are single passes, not promotions; the
  register's own rule is 2–3 prints before any of them is cited as established.
- **Eligible stock is face issued, not float** — unchanged from the initial research, and the
  understatement of 10-Year scarcity from BoJ holdings still runs in the direction that would make
  the 10-Year's 0.47× look *worse* than the true scarcity-adjusted figure.
- **No causal claim is made about why the 2-Year dominated.** A nine-day-old bond taking 39% of a
  reopening auction is consistent with several stories (a squeeze in the newest 2-Year, a
  repo-specialness effect this family has never measured, a single large participant). This document
  records what was taken, not why.
