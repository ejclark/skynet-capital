## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This event exists because the [`10-15 coupon-announcement`](treasury-coupon-announcement-2026-10-15.md)
initial research (2026-09-05) filed it as *"the free intermediate falsifier"* for the published-guidance
chain — four numbers off the same `sb0590` Oct-26 table row, printing two weeks before the announcement
that reads its own 20-Year from that row. That framing is correct and it is also, on its own, a
non-question: if the whole document is "58 / 39 / 22 will print, as already written," it restates a
prediction three sibling ledgers have already made in three different months.

So the question this session actually asked was: **what is in this announcement that is not already
spoken for — and is any of it a number the published guidance does not cover at all?**

**One-line verdict:** the coupon half turns out to be *more* determined than the calendar assumed — six
fields are predictable today, not three, because the two reopening CUSIPs follow mechanically from a
cycle that has never deviated — while the **bill** rows sharing this same announcement are the one part
of it that `sb0590` deliberately leaves open, that Treasury is visibly moving right now, and that no
ledger in this calendar has ever examined.

### Method

Rates mode per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md). `symbols: []`, so no symbol-keyed
instrument applies — `earnings-cycle.mjs` and `intraday-edges.mjs` have no target and the mandated cache

- **The guidance** — `home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0590` (2026-08-05 quarterly refunding

- **The live feed** — `treasurydirect.gov` `TA_WS/securities/upcoming`, HTTP 200, **43,843 bytes**, 14

- **The tape** — Treasury's own daily par yield-curve CSV (2026 full-year file, HTTP 200) and Yahoo

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The announcement publishes SIX predictable coupon fields, not three — SUPPORTED, and this is the
first of two findings.** The coordinate-reconstructed schedule puts exactly three non-bill rows on
Thursday, October 01, 2026:

| Security | Announce | Auction | Settle | Reopening? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `3-Year NOTE` | Thursday, October 01, 2026 | Tuesday, October 06, 2026 | Thursday, October 15, 2026 | **No** — no `R` marker |
| `10-Year NOTE R` | Thursday, October 01, 2026 | Wednesday, October 07, 2026 | Thursday, October 15, 2026 | **Yes** |
| `30-Year BOND R` | Thursday, October 01, 2026 | Thursday, October 08, 2026 | Thursday, October 15, 2026 | **Yes** |

The sizes are the three the calendar entry already carried. What it did not carry is *which securities*.
Treasury's quarterly pattern is a new issue in the refunding month reopened in the two following months,
and it does not deviate: measured from `auctions_query`, **every one of the 18 completed 10-Year
new-issue cycles back to 2022-01 was reopened exactly twice, in the two following months** (e.g.
91282CQQ7 new 2026-05-12 → reopened 06-10 and 07-08; 91282CPZ8 new 2026-02-11 → 03-11 and 04-08), and
**all 15 completed 30-Year cycles back to 2022-11** likewise. The August 2026 cycle is one reopening in:
**91282CRF0** sold 2026-08-12 at $42B and reopens 2026-09-09 at $39B; **912810UW6** sold 2026-08-13 at
$25B and reopens 2026-09-10 at $22B — both CUSIPs confirmed live on treasurydirect's upcoming feed this
session. So the 10-07 and 10-08 auctions are the **second and final** reopenings of those two securities,
after which 91282CRF0 is a **$120B** security (42+39+39) and 912810UW6 a **$69B** one (25+22+22). The
3-Year is the exception and is named as such: it is a *fresh CUSIP every month* (`reopen=No` on all 57
rows pulled), so its identifier is the one field of this announcement genuinely unknowable in advance.

**2. The coupon sizes are pre-published and the base rates agree with the document — SUPPORTED, and
therefore uninformative.** `sb0590`'s anticipated-size table, re-read verbatim across the headers
`2-Year · 3-Year · 5-Year · 7-Year · 10-Year · 20-Year · 30-Year · FRN`:

```
May-26  69 58 70 44 42 16 25 28
Jun-26  69 58 70 44 39 13 22 28
Jul-26  69 58 70 44 39 13 22 30
Aug-26  69 58 70 44 42 16 25 28
Sep-26  69 58 70 44 39 13 22 28
Oct-26  69 58 70 44 39 13 22 30
```

**Oct-26: 3-Year 58 · 10-Year 39 · 30-Year 22.** Independently confirming the 10-15 sibling's correction
that the final column reads **30** and not 28 — the column is FRN, and the third month of each refunding
quarter carries the FRN new issue. The revealed run-rates agree with all three:

| Leg | Current size | Unbroken since | Completed auctions at that size | What came before |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3-Year (new issue, monthly) | **$58B** | 2024-04-09 | **29** | a nine-month **+$2B/month ramp**: $40B (2023-07) → $56B (2024-03) |
| 10-Year (reopening) | **$39B** | 2024-03-12 | **20** | $37B (2023-12, 2024-01) |
| 30-Year (reopening) | **$22B** | 2024-03-13 | **20** | $21B (2023-12, 2024-01) |

Two things are worth pulling out of that table rather than leaving implicit. First, the **3-Year is the
longest-frozen leg in the block** at 29 straight auctions and ~2.5 years — longer than either reopening
leg, and it is also the block's **largest** number. Second, the ramp column shows what a *changing* grid
actually looks like from the outside: the 3-Year did not jump, it stepped +$2B every single month for
nine months. That is the shape a real issuance turn would take at 11-04, and it is a useful antidote to
imagining the kill switch as a single dramatic print.

**3. Three BILL rows share this announcement, and they are where the information is — SUPPORTED, and
this is the finding.** The same schedule rows put `13-Week BILL` (auction 10-05), `26-Week BILL`
(auction 10-05) and `6-Week BILL` (auction 10-06) on Thursday, October 01, 2026, alongside the coupons.
`sb0590` says in terms what that half is for: *"Treasury plans to address any seasonal or unexpected
variations in borrowing needs over the next quarter through changes in regular bill auction sizes and/or
CMBs."* Bills are the flexible lever by design; coupons are the rigid one. **No ledger in this calendar
has ever recorded a bill size** — a grep across `docs/research/` returns nothing — so this is a channel
the whole rates section has been blind to while writing eleven documents about the rigid half.

It is not idle right now. From `auctions_query`, weekly auction sizes in $B:

**5. The one channel that could break the coupon grid before 11-04 is a SOMA decision, and even that
cannot reach this announcement — SUPPORTED.** `sb0590`'s nominal-coupon paragraph, verbatim: *"Treasury

several quarters."* SOMA is named as an input, which makes the **2026-09-16 FOMC** the one dated event

signal. The *rate* decision has no path at all — issuance is set by borrowing needs, not by the funds

([`fomc-2026-09-16`](fomc-2026-09-16.md), 2026-09-02: CME ~66% hike vs Kalshi ~41–48%, the widest
cross-venue gap that ledger has recorded). And a balance-sheet change would transmit through Treasury's
*next* quarterly refunding decision on **2026-11-04**, not through an announcement 15 days later that
executes a table published 2026-08-05. So the honest statement is: watch 09-16 for a SOMA line, expect
it to matter at 11-04, and do not expect it to move 10-01.

### What the conditions support

Nothing directional — the standard answer for a `symbols: []`, `estimate`-dated rates event. What the
conditions do support is three things.

**Retire the coupon question entirely, and retire it for the securities too, not just the sizes.** Any
rates ledger in the October corridor can now write down six fields rather than three: 3Y $58B on a new
CUSIP, 10Y $39B on **91282CRF0**, 30Y $22B on **912810UW6**, settling 10-15. The CUSIP half is new to
this calendar and costs nothing to carry.

**Move the attention to the bill side, where the calendar has never looked.** Leg 3 is not a refinement
of the coupon story; it is a different question with a different answer. Treasury is actively cutting
$55B a week of short bills into the tax date while pinning the benchmarks, and the benchmarks have
stepped up at every quarter turn in the observed window — including at the equivalent announcement in
early October 2025. That is the one number in this release that is genuinely unknown on 2026-09-05, and
it is the one this doc registers a test on.

**Sequence the dated checks, and take the free one first.** The order is: **09-08/09-09/09-10** (the
September block — same structure, same two CUSIPs, one month early, at zero cost) → **09-16** FOMC, read
for a SOMA line only → **09-30** funding deadline, already resolved → **10-01** (this event) →
**10-06/10-07/10-08** (the auctions, now tracked) → **10-15** settlement and the sibling announcement →
**10-16** dealer agenda, measured at 0/1 as an early warning, read for one thing only → **11-02**
borrowing estimates → **11-04** refunding, where the grid itself can change and where
[`FT-39`](../forward-tests.md) lives.

### Honest limits

**No Treasury press release for the 2026-10-01 announcement exists yet** — it has not happened; every
prediction here is a document read or a base-rate extrapolation, not an observation. **The two CUSIPs
are inferences, not fetched fields** — treasurydirect's upcoming feed ends at 2026-09-17, so 91282CRF0
and 912810UW6 for October rest on an 18/18 and 15/15 cycle record and nothing else; that is why they are
registered as a test rather than stated as fact. **`sb0590`'s nominal-coupon table is explicitly
*anticipated* sizes** and its verbs are "anticipates" and "plans to", not a guarantee. **The bill-cut
mechanism is an inference** — the September-15 tax date is the obvious explanation for a −$55B short-bill
paydown, but no Treasury statement attributing it was fetched, and a different cause (a cash-balance
target, a debt-limit constraint) would produce the same tape. **The debt-limit position was NOT
established this session**, which matters because the only benchmark step-*down* in the observed window
(Feb 2025) was a debt-ceiling episode; the bill forward test is therefore exposed to a constraint this
doc did not measure, and that is named as its own kill switch. **TIPS rows were excluded by inspection**
of the size/CUSIP pattern rather than by the `inflation_index_security` field the 09-10 sibling
recommends — the exclusions are unambiguous at these sizes ($9B/$8B at 30Y, $19-21B at 10Y against
$22-42B nominals), but the method is weaker than the sibling's. **Base rates sit inside one policy
regime** and one guidance document with a dated expiry (2026-11-04). **Leg 5's agenda record and leg 8's
funding resolution are inherited** from sibling ledgers, cited rather than reproduced. **The ~11:00 ET
announcement time is unsourced convention**, inherited from the sibling chain; the calendar entry
discloses it.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `estimate`; three-row coupon composition and three-row bill composition both
primary-sourced from Treasury's own tentative schedule; all three coupon sizes primary-sourced from
`sb0590`; all base rates re-derived from `auctions_query`, everything fetched 2026-09-05).** This is a
**scheduled nil on its coupon half and a live seasonal decision on its bill half.** Expect **3Y $58B ·
10Y reopening $39B on CUSIP 91282CRF0 · 30Y reopening $22B on CUSIP 912810UW6**, announced ~11:00 ET,
all settling 2026-10-15. No position is or should be taken on it. The doc's durable outputs are four:
(a) **the CUSIP layer** — the two reopening securities are predictable today, which no ledger in this
calendar had noticed; (b) **the bill channel** — three bill rows share this announcement, `sb0590` names
bills as the adjustment lever, Treasury is pulling it hard right now (−$55B/week of short bills), and no
document here has ever recorded a bill size; (c) **the seasonal step** — the 13-/26-week benchmarks have
stepped up at every quarter turn since mid-2025 and 10-01 is the next one; (d) **the three untracked
auctions**, now filed.
