### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is correct, and it now rests on two independent derivations rather than a three-link chain —
   SUPPORTED.** The proposal called this date's status *"a rule-derived preliminary date that itself rests
   on a rule-derived implementation date"* — three links deep. It does not have to be. Measuring the
   owner's own published schedules gives a second offset that skips the preliminary date entirely:

   | Year | Prelim (Fri) | **Lock-down (Mon)** | Impl (Fri) | prelim→LD | **LD→impl** |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | 2020 | 06-05 | **06-15** | 06-26 | 10 | **11** |
   | 2021 | 06-04 | **06-14** | 06-25 | 10 | **11** |
   | 2022 | 06-03 | **06-13** | 06-24 | 10 | **11** |
   | 2023 | 05-19 | **06-05** | 06-23 | 17 | **18** |
   | 2024 | 05-24 | **06-10** | 06-28 | 17 | **18** |
   | 2025 | 05-23 | **06-09** | 06-27 | 17 | **18** |
   | 2026 | 05-22 | **06-08** | 06-26 | 17 | **18** |

   Current regime: **prelim + 17 = LD** (4/4) and **impl − 18 = LD** (4/4). For 2027, 2027-05-21 + 17 =
   **2027-06-07**, and 2027-06-25 − 18 = **2027-06-07**. Two derivations, one answer, and the second needs
   only the implementation date. All seven lock-down dates are **Mondays** and all seven are the Monday
   after the query period ends — 2020's release states its query end explicitly (*"query period runs
   through June 12"*) against a **June 15** lock-down, which is that convention observed rather than
   inferred.

2. **The proposal's Monday rule is regime-specific and fails on 2020–2022 — SUPPORTED, and it is the
   correction this leg exists to make.** The proposal wrote the rule as *"two weeks after the preliminary
   announcement … lock-down on the following Monday"* and confirmed it against 2023–2026. That is true and
   incomplete. Applied cold to 2021 (prelim 06-04) it returns Monday **06-21**; the owner published
   **06-14**. The old regime ran a **one-week** query period, so the offsets were prelim+10 / impl−11. What
   is actually stable across all seven years is the **Monday-after-query-end** convention; what changed in
   2023 is the query period's *length* — one week to two — arriving alongside the announcement window's
   change from three weeks to five, which the sibling's leg 2 already documented. Same class of correction,
   one link further down the chain.

3. **The owner's 2019 schedule names no lock-down date, so the sample is seven years and not eight —
   SUPPORTED (a bounding result).** The 2019 press release (dateline 2019-03-01) lists rank day 05-10,
   preliminary lists 06-07, updates 06-14 & 06-21, final 06-28, markets open 07-01 — and **no lock-down
   bullet at all**. The lock-down date first appears in the owner's published schedule in **2020**. Rather
   than back-fill 2019 from an offset the owner had not yet published, it is excluded, exactly as the
   sibling excluded 2017–2018 announcement dates. Every number below is n=7.

4. **§6.4.1 says what lock-down mechanically *does*, and no sibling in this chain had read it —
   SUPPORTED, and it is the strongest argument for tracking the date.** Verbatim from the extracted
   ground rules:

   > "For merger and spin-off transactions that are effective between rank day and the business day
   > immediately before the index lock down takes effect ahead of the reconstitution, the market
   > capitalizations of the impacted securities are recalculated and membership is reevaluated as of the
   > effective date of the corporate action. For corporate events that occur during the reconstitution
   > lock down period (which take effect from the open on the first day of the lock-down period onwards),
   > market capitalizations and memberships will not be reevaluated."

   Three things follow. The freeze bites **from the open** of 2027-06-07, so the date is a session
   boundary and not an end-of-day one. It is a **hard cutoff for corporate-action-driven membership
   revision** — an M&A close on 06-04 can still move June membership; one on 06-08 cannot. And Appendix F's
   softer definition (*"considered final, and any additional changes are made in exceptional circumstances
   only"*) is the discretionary layer sitting on top of a mechanical one.

5. **What freezes is membership, not weights — SUPPORTED, and it resolves an apparent contradiction in the
   owner's own schedule.** Every published schedule keeps posting updated lists *after* lock-down: 2026
   locked down 06-08 and still posted updates on **12 and 18 June**; 2023 locked down 06-05 and posted on
   **9 and 16 June**; 2020 locked down 06-15 and posted on **19 June**. Read "final" as "nothing more is
   published" and that is incoherent. The owner's 2024 release states the resolution — the lock-down
   leaves *"any further changes to shares and free float isolated to changes associated with corporate
   matters"* — and §6.4.1 agrees: **memberships** are not reevaluated; shares and float still move on
   corporate actions. So the announcement's two payloads separate here. The *names* are final on
   2027-06-07; the *weights* are not final until implementation.

### Honest limits

inherited from the sibling and undiminished. Adds and deletes are two-sided flow that largely cancels at
index level, so legs 6, 8 and 10 are a **floor** on any single-name effect. **n=7, and n=3/n=4 per

COVID-tape year** and it is the single exception in leg 10 and the largest positive in leg 6; it is kept

narrower than the sibling's** — the tests here were specified from the sibling's design before the data

confirmed against seven published instances across both regimes).** Treat 2027-06-07 as a **certain,
mechanically meaningful, entirely untradeable boundary** — and treat **2026-11-30 as the near-term thing
