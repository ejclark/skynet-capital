## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Stand aside — and stop expecting the February edition to revise the number the January
edition prints.** The proposal that created this event asked for `01-12 → 02-09` to be read as a pair
for the first-ever EIA **2028** electricity-consumption forecast. Both halves were checked against
EIA's own archived reports, and they resolve in opposite directions. **The structural half is
stronger than proposed and is now verified:** the January edition carries **no "Notable forecast
changes" table at all** — absent in `archives/jan25.pdf` and `archives/jan26.pdf`, present in all
nine other editions sampled — because a newly-added out-year has no previous forecast to diff
against. So **2027-02-09 is the first document in which EIA itself states which direction the 2028

## Initial research

**The question.** The proposal that created this event asserted one thing and asked for another: that
2027-02-09 is the *first revision* of the 2028 forecast lines 2027-01-12 prints, and that
`01-12 → 02-09` should therefore be read as a two-document pair for national electricity consumption.
Is the first revision actually a distinct kind of document, and does the pair-read produce a number
worth waiting a month for?

**One-line verdict:** yes to the first and no to the second — February *is* structurally unique
(it is the only edition that publishes a diff of a first print, because January publishes no diff at
all), and the diff it publishes is **~0.2%**, which means the January edition is the number and this
one ratifies it.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md)
(macro-print mode; the house instruments are equity/earnings-keyed and cover no commodity or
publication). Primaries fetched direct **2026-09-09**:
`eia.gov/outlooks/steo/release_schedule.php` (HTTP 200, **54,906 bytes** — byte-identical to both
sibling lanes' pulls), `eia.gov/outlooks/steo/` (HTTP 200), `eia.gov/about/announcement.php`
(HTTP 200), `eia.gov/outlooks/aeo/` (HTTP 200),
`federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm` (HTTP 200, "Last Update: August 19, 2026") and
**twenty-four EIA archive PDFs** — every edition from `sep24.pdf` to `aug26.pdf` — text layers
decompressed in-session. **All price statistics were measured by me this session**, not quoted:
Yahoo daily bars (`range=2y`) bucketed to `America/New_York` for CL=F, BZ=F, NG=F, XLE, USO, ^VIX,
NVDA, MRVL, AVGO, CRWV, MSFT, SPY. In-repo primaries used rather than re-derived:
[`eia-steo-2027-01-12`](eia-steo-2027-01-12.md), [`eia-steo-2026-12-08`](eia-steo-2026-12-08.md),
[`cr-expiry-2026-12-11`](cr-expiry-2026-12-11.md), and the sole prior proposal
`eia-steo-2027-02-09.from-eia-steo-2027-01-12.json`.

**A method note that is itself a finding, recorded because it retires a sibling limitation.** Every
edition's *Notable forecast changes* header names **both** its own release date and the previous
edition's ("Current forecast: February 10, 2026; previous forecast: January 13, 2026"). That makes the
archive a **self-certifying date chain**, each date corroborated twice. Reading it end to end supplies
every 2025 release date from an EIA primary — and the only dates that arrive *solely* via the next
edition's "previous forecast" field are the **January** ones, which is exactly the structural gap
this ledger is about.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is primary-sourced and doubly derived — SUPPORTED.** The raw table lists
   **02/09/2027** for the February edition, Notes cell **blank**, release window **12:00–12:15 pm

   footnote "If a Federal holiday falls on the Monday before the normal release date the release is
   delayed until Wednesday" — reproduces it, and the page's *own published worked example is a
   February edition* ("since the first Thursday of February 2019 was February 7 … the February
   edition was released on Tuesday, February 12"). **2027-02-04 is a Thursday**, the following
   Tuesday is **02-09**, and **2027-02-08 is a Monday that is not a Federal holiday** (Washington's
   Birthday 2027 is Monday **02-15**). Filed **estimate** on prefix grounds only.

2. **The January edition publishes no revision table, so February is the year's first — SUPPORTED,
   and this is the leg that upgrades the proposal's framing.** Checked across every edition from
   September 2024 to August 2026:
