## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Seven 2027 FOMC ledgers now exist, and between them they have built a monthly base-rate table, killed
a corridor rule, corrected a September mixture, and established what a no-SEP meeting acting on the
prior SEP's dots can and cannot do. This entry arrives with two things none of them had.

**First, it is the only `fomc-*` entry in the calendar named by its meeting's *day one* rather than
its decision day** — because the lane that proposed it,
[`consumer-confidence-2027-10-26`](consumer-confidence-2027-10-26.md), cared about day one: it is the
October 2027 Conference Board print date, the only zero-distance adjacency this calendar has ever had
for a CB print. That proposal explicitly declined to pick and left the naming to whoever adopted the
id. **Second, its month cell is the lowest in the family's own table — October, 0.594% — and the
sibling that found September's cell to be a mixture stopped there.**

So the question has two halves, and they turn out to be the same question asked at two scales.
**Is October's 0.594% cell a property of the October meeting, or an artifact of how the sample was
cut?** And **is FOMC day one — the session this entry is named after — a market event at all?**

**One-line verdict.** Both are negatives and both are the deliverable. **October's cell is a
bin-split artifact**: October and November are one meeting slot cut at a month boundary that falls
in the middle of an unbroken 16-day cluster, and the honest base rate is the pooled **0.778%**, not
the **0.594%** half that happens to contain 2027. And **day one is not a market event — it is
measurably quieter than an ordinary Tuesday** (0.506% vs 0.691%, P = 0.0131), with a 2%-plus tail
five times thinner. Both findings are refusals; neither implies a rate call, and none is made at
D-415.

3. **October's 0.594% cell is a BIN-SPLIT ARTIFACT, and this is the document's first deliverable.**
   SUPPORTED, and it corrects a sibling table without editing it.

   The FOMC does not meet every month. Its eight-meeting year runs Jan · Mar · Apr/May · Jun · Jul ·
   Sep · **Oct/Nov** · Dec, and the seventh slot — the autumn meeting between the September and
   December SEP meetings — lands on whichever side of the month boundary the calendar puts it. So
   the table's **October** and **November** cells are not two months' worth of meetings. They are
   **one slot, cut in two.**

   The arithmetic confirms it exactly: the autumn slot has **19 instances 2007–2025, one per year
   with no gaps**, and **8 + 11 = 19** — the October and November cells partition it and contain
   nothing else.

   | | n | mean \|move\| | median | ratio |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | Autumn slot, October bin | 8 | **0.594%** | 0.406% | 1.46 |
   | Autumn slot, November bin | 11 | **0.912%** | 0.653% | 1.40 |
   | **Autumn slot, pooled** | **19** | **0.778%** | **0.646%** | **1.20** |
   | All other decision days | 137 | 0.945% | 0.652% | 1.45 |
   | All decision days 2007–2026 | 156 | 0.925% | 0.650% | 1.42 |

   **The split is not a real division.** Permutation on the Oct-vs-Nov difference (0.318 points):
   **P = 0.334**. And the pooled slot is not distinguishable from the rest of the calendar either
   (0.167 points, **P = 0.475**), nor is the October cell itself against the other 148 decision days
   (**P = 0.306**). Nothing here is significant — which is exactly why quoting **0.594%** as if it
   were an October property is the error.

   **The strongest form of the argument is the raw dates, not the test.** Sorted, the nineteen
   autumn decision days run

   `10-24 · 10-28 · 10-29 · 10-29 · 10-29 · 10-30 · 10-30 · 10-31 · 11-01 · 11-01 · 11-02 · 11-02 ·
   11-02 · 11-03 · 11-03 · 11-04 · 11-05 · 11-07 · 11-08`

   — **sixteen calendar days end to end, with no gap at the month line**, and the median instance is
   **11-01**, one day past the boundary. The month boundary is not a feature of this distribution;
   it runs through its middle. A 2027 meeting on **10-27** and a 2023 meeting on **11-01** are five
   days apart and land in different cells.

   **Why this bites now rather than in general.** The slot is drifting earlier and has just crossed
   the boundary: **2021-11-03 · 2022-11-02 · 2023-11-01 · 2024-11-07 · 2025-10-29 · 2026-10-28 ·
   2027-10-27** — three consecutive Octobers after four consecutive Novembers, and 2027 is the
   **second-earliest autumn decision in the 21 years parsed**, behind only 2012-10-24. Every future
   instance that stays on this side of the line accretes to a cell whose current content is eight
   observations selected by nothing but a date. **Use the pooled 0.778%; there is no October
   meeting, only an autumn one.**

### Honest limits

- **Every statistic here is a decision-day or day-one close-to-close S&P move.** Nothing is measured
  on options, intraday paths, or any of this book's actual names — the channel from an FOMC session
  to a `symbols: []` event's P&L is unmodelled and stays that way.
- **The bin-split finding is a decomposition, not a test.** Its permutations are all *failures to
  reject* (P = 0.334, 0.475, 0.306). It says the October cell is not distinguishable from the slot
  it belongs to, not that a real October effect has been excluded — n = 8 against n = 11 can miss a
  moderate one.
- **The day-one finding is a modern-era regularity with a reversing crisis-era sample**, and its
  significance rests on one weekday-controlled look after an uncontrolled one at P = 0.088. It is
  registered as a forward test rather than asserted.
- **The venue is a prediction market, not a rate curve.** Kalshi ladders at D-415 carry hundreds of
  contracts per leg; the `27OCT` book's own tail legs quote 5-cent spreads on prices near 0.10. Leg
  6's point is precisely that such a book supports a *level* read at best and no path read at all.
- **`fomccalendars.htm` disclaims its own 2027 dates.** Everything dated here inherits that, and the
  promotion trigger is the **2027-09-14/15** meeting, not a better fetch.
