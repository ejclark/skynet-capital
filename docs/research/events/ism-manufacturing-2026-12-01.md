## Initial research

### The question, plainly

What should we expect from the November 2026 ISM Manufacturing PMI on 2026-12-01, is the date right,
and — the question three siblings have now handed forward — **does this release actually move
anything, measured outside the single year everyone has measured it in**? And given that it opens
the tightest nine-day macro corridor on this calendar from *inside* an FOMC communications blackout,
what should a paper-trading book holding long-duration tech (NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL
AMZN) do about it?

**One-line verdict:** the release has **no equity reaction at all** across eleven years, and a
**real but purely directionless magnitude effect in long bonds** that survives every confound this
session could test — month turn, dividend, payroll collision — yet is **absent in 2026**, is never
measured against option pricing, and lands on 12-01 inside a blackout eight days from a dot plot,
which is the one date on the calendar where the volatility it would tempt you to buy is provably
somebody else's.

### Honest limits

The date is `estimate` and is structurally unpromotable from this lane: ISM's calendar is SSO-gated
(leg 1, re-verified today), so an `ISM:` confirmed prefix needs a credentialed fetch. **Leg 2 is the
one to read sceptically, and its biggest problem is named in its own text** — the effect does not
appear in 2026 (2/8), so a reader is entitled to ask whether an 11-year result that skips the current
regime describes anything live. **The attribution is unresolved.** The first business day at 10:00 ET
is not ISM's alone: Census Construction Spending and the S&P Global US Manufacturing final PMI share
the morning, and this study cannot separate them — census.gov's C30 release schedule was fetched
directly this session (HTTP 200) and its "PRESS RELEASE DATES FOR 2025 & 2026" table does not extend
to December 2026, and it lists a **January 21, 2026** release that is *not* a first business day, so
the same-slot cadence could not even be confirmed, let alone controlled for. **No calendar entry is
proposed on that basis** — an uncorroborated rule-derived date would be worse than the honest gap.
Further: the percentile technique measures magnitude within a year and is blind to whether the move
was *caused* by the release; T+2 also runs elevated (TLT 31.4%, ^TNX 37.3%), which is where ISM
Services lands on the third business day and sits in unresolved tension with **FT-37**'s 2026-only
services null; no multiple-comparison correction is applied across the four instruments in leg 2
(TLT's p is small enough to survive one, SPY's non-result is unaffected); leg 3's Šidák correction
assumes twelve independent monthly tests, which they are not, so **0.081 is itself approximate**. The
funding status (leg 5) rests on the calendar's own 2026-09-01 sourcing with the presidential signature
unverified. The blackout window (leg 4) is **carried** from a sibling's `confirmed` FED fetch, not
re-fetched here. And the whole document assumes ISM publishes on schedule.
