## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **This print was put on the calendar to be the other suspect at 02:00 ET on 18 November,
and to carry the half of the UK energy story its CPI twin cannot. It carries neither.** ONS's own
methodology, fetched today, says **"All index numbers exclude Value Added Tax (VAT)"** — so the
electricity VAT zero-rating three sibling ledgers are watching is invisible here **by measurement
rule**, not by scope. And PPI's energy prices are a DESNZ panel of **"industrial and commercial
end-users"**, data ONS says **"differ from the data provided for the purposes of the Consumer Prices
Index"**, while Ofgem states **"You are not protected by the price cap if you… have a business energy
contract."** Wholesale reaches this index; the cap and the VAT cut do not. What the index *is* is a
lagged restatement of one observable price: the QMI says firms report **"transactions that occur on
or near to the first of each month"**, so the October index is a **1-October snapshot published 48
days later**, and crude oil (weight **6.9%**) explained roughly **three-quarters** of the input
index's monthly move in both June and July. That produces the one call worth making — **the
published disinflation story is about to reverse.** Input PPI fell 7.4% → 4.9% on collapsing crude;
first-week Brent in sterling then ran **+12.0% in August** and **+16.3% month-to-date in September**,
with Brent at **$100.69** today against a June average of **$84.62**. Against a flat October-2025
base, the 18 November print should show input inflation near **7%** (band 6.0–8.0%), not a third
consecutive fall. **None of it is ours to trade** — `symbols: []`, and a print that is a
seven-week-old snapshot of a price on the screen cannot surprise anyone. Date is **estimate** on a
taxonomy gap, not on the evidence.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This entry arrived by **proposal**. The [`uk-cpi-2026-11-18`](uk-cpi-2026-11-18.md) lane filed
`proposals/uk-ppi-2026-11-18.from-uk-cpi-2026-11-18.json` on 2026-09-09, arguing this print earns a
slot because it is "the only tracked release that publishes in the SAME 07:00 London minute as a UK
CPI print", and that its content is the missing half of the energy story: "business supplies keep
their VAT treatment, so PPI input prices see the wholesale and cap moves WITHOUT the consumer VAT
cut." That proposal was read in full before anything here was written, per
[`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md), and the canonical
`src/domain/market-events/uk-ppi-2026-11-18.json` was written in this PR from it.

So the question is: **does the proposed mechanism survive contact with the publisher's own
methodology, and if not, what is this print actually made of?**

**One-line verdict:** the placement finding survives and generalises — the same-minute pairing is
**structural, every month, not a coincidence on 18 November** — but the mechanism is **refuted
twice**: PPI excludes VAT entirely and its energy panel sits outside Ofgem's cap. What the index
actually is is a **one-day snapshot of Brent in sterling, published 48 days later**, and on that
reading the print's headline number is already visible today: input producer inflation is set to
**re-accelerate to roughly 7%**, not continue falling.

### Leg 3 — "PPI sees the cap and the wholesale moves without the consumer VAT cut." **REFUTED, on two independent grounds.**

This is the proposal's mechanism, and neither half of it holds.

**VAT is not in this index at all.** ONS's *Producer Price Indices QMI* (fetched, last revised
22 October 2025) states, verbatim:

> **"All index numbers exclude Value Added Tax (VAT)."**

So SI 2026/987 — the GB domestic electricity zero-rating whose 0.098pp of CPI three sibling ledgers
are tracking — cannot appear in PPI in either direction. Not "appears differently because business
supplies keep their 5% treatment"; **absent by construction**. The asymmetry the proposal described
would require PPI to carry VAT on one side. It carries it on neither.

**The Ofgem cap does not reach this index either.** The July 2026 bulletin's Section 7 says of the
energy inputs:

> "The gas and electricity data are an average price for the month from **a panel of suppliers
> relating to the supply of gas and electricity to industrial and commercial end-users**. These data
> **differ from the data provided for the purposes of the Consumer Prices Index (CPI)**… compiled by
> the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero (DESNZ)."

And Ofgem's own consumer page states who the cap covers:

> "You are **not** protected by the price cap if you… **have a business energy contract**."

The cap is domestic — it is set under the *Domestic* Gas and Electricity (Tariff Cap) Act. PPI's
energy panel is explicitly non-domestic. **Wholesale reaches this index; the cap does not.**

**And the exposure is small anyway.** The "Fuel" product group — electricity, gas and coal, per the
bulletin's own CPA 2.1 note — carries **2.5%** of the input basket (Table 2). The entire UK domestic
energy narrative that dominates the CPI sibling touches, at most, 2.5% of this index through a
different price series, uncapped, VAT-free.

*What survives of the proposal:* the placement, which is the part worth keeping, plus a weaker true
statement — 1 October is the day industrial contracts commonly reset, and it is also the day this
index prices (Leg 4). But that is a contract-cycle coincidence, not the cap or the tax.

### Leg 4 — "The print is a lagged restatement of an observable price." **SUPPORTED, and this is the stand-aside argument.**

Two facts from the publisher, one measurement from the tape.

**Fact one: it is a one-day snapshot.** The QMI, verbatim:

> "All businesses included in the survey are asked to provide the price data for **transactions that
> occur on or near to the first of each month**. Therefore, price changes that occur part of the way
> through the month will always be reflected in the following month's index."

The October 2026 index is therefore priced around **1 October 2026** and published **18 November
2026** — a **48-day** lag.

**Fact two: crude dominates the monthly move.** Crude petroleum, natural gas and metal ores carry
**6.9%** of the input basket (Table 2). Applying that weight to the reported crude moves:

| Month | Crude group m/m (ONS) | × 6.9% weight | Input index m/m (ONS) | Share explained |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| June 2026 | **−20.9%** | −1.44pp | **−1.9%** | **~76%** |
| July 2026 | **−18.0%** | −1.24pp | **−1.7%** | **~73%** |

**Measurement: the first-week Brent-in-sterling series reproduces it.** Computed from Yahoo daily
`BZ=F` × `GBPUSD=X`, fetched today, averaging only the first seven calendar days of each month —
the window the QMI describes:

| Month | Brent wk1, GBP, m/m | Brent full-month, GBP, m/m | ONS crude group m/m |
|---|---|---|---|
| June 2026 | −9.8% | −17.8% | **−20.9%** |
| July 2026 | **−23.6%** | −0.5% | **−18.0%** |
| August 2026 | **+12.0%** | +3.0% | *not yet published (2026-09-16)* |
| September 2026 (MTD) | **+16.3%** | +10.0% | *not yet published (2026-10-21)* |

July is the decisive row. Full-month Brent was flat (−0.5%) while ONS printed crude **−18.0%**; the
first-week measure printed **−23.6%**. A full-month model of this index is simply the wrong object.
The two measures bracket the outturn in June, which is what the QMI's own hedge — first-week actuals
"supplemented with the refining businesses' estimate… for the remainder" on "a (varying) mix of
future and spot prices" — predicts.

**The consequence, stated plainly:** a print that is ~three-quarters a lagged, published-in-advance
function of Brent in sterling contains almost no information the tape does not already hold. That is
a stronger reason to stand aside than "we own no UK instrument", and it is the reason this ledger
does not treat the release as an event risk at all.

### Leg 5 — "The published disinflation is the trend." **REFUTED. It reverses, and the base is flat enough to say so.**

The last two bulletins read as disinflation: input **7.4% → 4.9%**, output **3.5% → 3.1%**, both
driven down by crude. The Brent path since has round-tripped. Monthly averages (fetched):
**Mar $99.60 → May $104.09 → Jun $84.62 → Jul $84.51 → Aug $88.08 → Sep $96.81 MTD**, with spot at
**$100.69** on 2026-09-09 — **+19%** off the June average.

The base makes the arithmetic easy. From Table 1 (index, 2015=100):

| | Aug-2025 | Sep-2025 | Oct-2025 | Jul-2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Input (GHIP) | 156.8 (**0.0%** m/m) | 156.8 (**0.0%**) | **156.6** (−0.1%) | 164.5 (+4.9% y/y) |
| Output (GB7S) | 144.3 (+0.2%) | 144.4 (+0.1%) | **144.5** (0.0%) | 148.4 (+3.1% y/y) |

With the year-ago path essentially flat, **the October annual rate moves roughly one-for-one with
the August–October 2026 monthly path.** Building that path from first-week Brent in GBP at the 6.9%
crude weight, and holding the other nine groups at their recent trend:

- **August 2026:** crude +12.0% × 6.9% = **+0.83pp** → input index ≈ **+0.9% m/m** → **~5.8% y/y**
- **September 2026:** crude +16.3% × 6.9% = **+1.12pp** → ≈ **+1.2% m/m** → **~7.1% y/y**
- **October 2026:** depends entirely on Brent on 1 October. Flat near $100 → **~7.5% y/y**;
  a fall back to ~$85 → **~6.2%**; a push to ~$110 → **~8.2%**

**Base case: input 6.0–8.0% y/y, centred near 7%.** For output, refined petroleum (weight 4.5%)
passed through roughly a third of crude's move in each of the last two months (crude −20.9% → coke
and refined petroleum −7.0%; crude −18.0% → −2.9%), and the other groups have run ~+0.27%/month
ex-oil. That gives **output 3.3–4.2%, centred near 3.8%**, up from 3.1%.

**The economically interesting part is the gap.** Input minus output was **5.4pp in May, 3.9pp in
June, 1.8pp in July** — a fast narrowing as the oil shock unwound. On this base case it re-widens to
roughly **3.2pp**: a renewed imported cost shock that UK manufacturers pass through only partially.
That is the honest read of the print, and it is testable (**FT-uk-ppi-2026-11-18-3**).

**Named driver, from the publisher itself:** the bulletin states "the data published this month
continue to be affected by **the conflict in the Middle East**", attributing plastics, PVC and resin
price rises to it directly. The March 2026 Brent step (+43.5% m/m on the monthly average) is the same
event. This is a geopolitical print wearing a statistical costume.

### Leg 6 — "It is a reliable statistic to key anything on." **REFUTED — and the specifics matter more than the caveat.**

Four separate quality facts, each from the publisher, each fetched today:

1. **Not accredited.** The QMI's own output panel reads **"Statistical designation: official
   statistics"** — not *accredited* official statistics. The bulletin says ONS is "continuing to
   improve the quality of these statistics to address the findings and requirements set out in the
   Office for Statistics Regulation's *Spotlight on Quality: PPI publication*" and that "once the
   developments are complete, we will begin the process for **seeking reaccreditation**."
2. **An eight-month outage.** The bulletin footnotes, verbatim: *"Publications from February to
   August 2025 were cancelled."* The publisher's own release listing confirms it — it jumps from
   **"January 2025 — Released on 19 February 2025"** straight to **"September 2025 including
   services, July to September 2025 — Released on 22 October 2025"**. Seven consecutive monthly
   bulletins are missing. *Consequence for this print:* October 2026 is only the **second** monthly
   annual rate since the resumption whose year-ago base month was itself published contemporaneously
   (September 2025 on 22 Oct 2025; October 2025 on 19 Nov 2025). Every print before September 2026
   compared against a retrospectively-filled base.
3. **The machine-readable channel is dead.** `GHIP` and `GB7S` fetched from ons.gov.uk today return
   `releaseDate 2025-02-19`, **"Next release: 26 March 2025"**, and a last observation of **2025
   JAN** — 19 months stale, while the bulletin tables publish monthly. Anything automating this
   print must parse the bulletin, not the series API. This is recorded because a future pulse of
   this event will otherwise rediscover it the hard way.
4. **Revisions are routine and material.** "PPI figures for the latest 12 months are subject to
   revisions"; June and July 2026 are both flagged **provisional**; the July bulletin revises June's
   input annual rate to 7.4% and its m/m to −1.9%. Worse for a first-print reaction, the oil and
   energy figures are partly **the suppliers' own forecast**: the first estimate is "based on the
   average price for the first part of the month… **supplemented with the suppliers' estimate** of
   the volume and value for the remainder", revised "after one to two months".

None of this changes the direction of Leg 5 — a +19% move in the dominant input is not a revisions
artefact — but it does mean no threshold in this ledger should be read to a tenth.

### Honest limits

- **Two of the three months in the base case have not printed.** August (2026-09-16) and September
  (2026-10-21) are forecast from Brent, not observed. The first is seven days away, which is why
  it is registered as a test rather than asserted.
- **September's first-week Brent is four trading days, not seven.** The +16.3% is measured from
  1–9 September and is the thinnest number in this document.
- **The crude group is not pure crude.** "Crude petroleum and natural gas; metal ores" bundles gas
  and metal ores with oil, and the bulletin notes silver and precious-metal strength elsewhere.
  Applying a Brent move to the whole 6.9% is an approximation that probably *overstates* the oil
  sensitivity slightly; the direction is not in doubt, the decimal is.
- **The first-week/full-month bracket is fitted on two observations.** June and July 2026 are the
  only months where a published crude m/m can be compared against both measures, because the
  bulletin only tabulates two months of product-group detail. Two points establish that the
  full-month measure is wrong for July; they do not establish a coefficient.
- **The ex-oil output drift (~+0.27%/month) is a two-month average**, taken from the same two
  months. It is the weakest input to the output band.
- **No consensus to be surprised against.** No published Reuters/Bloomberg consensus for the October
  2026 UK PPI print was found today, so the base case is this ledger's arithmetic — the same gap
  every UK sibling has recorded.
- **The VAT and cap refutations are about PPI's *measured* prices, not about firms' cash costs.** A
  UK manufacturer's electricity bill is a real cost and can affect its output prices; the point is
  narrower and exact — the 1 October domestic zero-rating and the Ofgem cap do not enter *this
  index's* energy quotes, so neither can be read off it.
- **The taxonomy gap, now at nine entries.** ONS (twice over), HM Treasury, Ofgem, MOF Japan,
  S&P Global, NYSE/SIFMA, legislation.gov.uk and HMRC have all been filed `estimate` purely because

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date: estimate on a taxonomy gap; the underlying release is on the publisher's calendar).**
**Stand aside at every horizon.** There is no instrument in this book with a channel to a UK
producer-price print, and — the stronger half — the print carries almost no information that is not
already on the screen. Its operational value is four rules and a base case, none of which is a trade:

1. **This print cannot show the VAT cut, and it never could.** ONS: *"All index numbers exclude Value
   Added Tax (VAT)."* The proposal that created this entry said PPI would show the wholesale and cap
   moves without the consumer VAT cut; it shows **neither tax**, and its energy panel is
   industrial/commercial supply that Ofgem's cap explicitly excludes. Any attribution of a PPI move
   to SI 2026/987 is wrong twice.
2. **It is a 1-October snapshot published 18 November.** ONS: prices are collected for *"transactions
   that occur on or near to the first of each month."* Crude carries 6.9% and explained ~73–76% of
   the last two monthly moves. Forecast it with **first-week Brent in sterling**, not the monthly
   average — in July the two disagreed by 23 points and the first-week measure was right.
3. **The disinflation story reverses.** Input 6.0–8.0% y/y on 18 November (centred ~7%, up from

   an imported cost shock passed through only partially. Named driver, per the publisher itself: the
   Middle East conflict.
4. **The same-minute pairing is a monthly rule.** UK PPI and UK CPI publish together at 07:00 London
   on 09-16, 10-21, 11-18 and 12-16 — all four verified from ONS release pages. Every UK CPI date in
   this calendar is a two-release 02:00 ET slot, and 11-18 is the goods-only one (services ride the
   September and December reference months).
