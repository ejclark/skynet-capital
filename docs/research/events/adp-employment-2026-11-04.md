## Initial research

the market treat it, and which of our tracked names (NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL AMZN)
carry meaningful sensitivity — given that the generic "what is ADP" work is already banked across

precedent in the record an ADP surprise on that morning is not merely diluted but **invisible** — so
the house answer is a specific, dated **attribution refusal**, not a forecast: read it as the only
October read that will exist, do not attribute any 11-04 tape move to it, and let the **11-06** BLS
print carry the labor question exactly as the two sibling editions concluded.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The date is right on two independent cadence rules, and its promotion path is dated —
SUPPORTED (as an estimate).** FRED's release calendar **rid=194**, re-fetched direct today in
full-year view, lists twelve 2026 ADP editions and names **"Wednesday November 04, 2026"** at
**07:15 CT (= 08:15 ET)**. 11-04 satisfies *both* rules the series has followed this year: it is the
first Wednesday of November, and it is two sessions before the **BLS-confirmed 11-06** payrolls
Friday. The status stays **`estimate`** because a FRED forward calendar is a projection and ADP has
not itself named it — `mediacenter.adp.com` re-checked today carries nothing newer than the 09-02
August release, and no November advisory. What is new here is that the **promotion path is now
dated and mechanical**: ADP names its next release inside the body of the current one, verified on
two separate editions (the 2026-08 release named 09-30 verbatim; the **2025-10** release states
*"The November 2025 ADP National Employment Report will be released on December 3, 2025 at 8:15
a.m. ET"*). A second, older channel exists too — a standalone advisory, e.g. **2020-10-28**,
*"October 2020 ADP National Employment Report®… to be Released on November 4, 2020"*. So the
expected primary confirmation for this event arrives on **2026-09-30**, five weeks before the event.

**2. There is no October 2026 ADP release at all, which makes this the only ADP read on the October
labor market — SUPPORTED, from the calendar itself.** FRED's full-year rid=194 list runs
**… Aug 5 · Sep 2 · Sep 30 · Nov 4 · Dec 2**. Every other 2026 edition is a first Wednesday; the
**09-30** edition is the single anomaly, pulled off the first Wednesday of October (10-07) to stay
two sessions ahead of the **10-02** payrolls print. The arithmetic consequence is a **35-day gap**
between ADP releases — the longest of the year — and an **empty October**. Two practical readings.
First, this print is the *only* ADP measurement of October that will ever be published, which raises
its informational value relative to an ordinary edition. Second, and cutting the other way, its
month-over-month comparison spans a 35-day publication gap against a September figure that was
itself produced by a re-benchmark, so **the delta is the least trustworthy part of the release** and
the level is the part worth reading.

**3. This edition is ordinary with respect to the QCEW re-benchmark — and that is now MEASURED on
the one available prior cycle rather than asserted. SUPPORTED, n=1.** The
[09-30 doc](adp-employment-2026-09-30.md) established that ADP's annual preliminary re-benchmark
lands in the September edition (2025: a **−43,000** headline reduction and a prior month restated
**+54k → −3k**), with the full benchmark following in the next February report. The question this
doc has to answer is what the *next* edition looked like — and it is checkable. ADP's
**October-2025** report, published **2025-11-05**, the direct analogue of this event: headline
**+42,000**, **no** mention of benchmark, rebenchmark or QCEW anywhere in the release, and September
revised **−32,000 → −29,000** — a **3k** adjustment, i.e. an entirely ordinary prior-month revision
one month after a 57k re-benchmark swing. So the calendar note's "ordinary by construction" claim
survives contact with the one cycle that can test it. It is n=1 and stated at that weight; the
*This month* signal is written to catch the failure case.

**4. The load-bearing fact: 11-04 is the morning after the midterms, and on the one same-structure
precedent an ADP surprise of any size is invisible — SUPPORTED, n=1, and the error runs against the
print mattering.** [`midterm-elections-2026-11-03`](midterm-elections-2026-11-03.md) is `estimate`
(date-certain, outcome-unknown) and sits the session before. The only prior instance of a *national*
US election night immediately followed by an ADP release is **2020-11-04** — the same calendar date,
with the presidential result still undetermined at the open. That morning ADP printed **+365,000**
against a **600,000** Dow Jones consensus (CNBC, 2020-11-04): a **235k / 39% shortfall**, an order of
magnitude larger in proportional terms than any 2026 ADP surprise (August's was **−9k to −10k**
against 47–48k). CNBC's own contemporaneous wording is that stock futures were **"broadly unchanged

### Honest limits

- **Both load-bearing precedents are n=1.** 2020-11-04 (leg 4) and 2025-11-05 (legs 3 and 5) are
  single observations, and every conclusion drawn from them is stated at that weight.
- **2020 was a presidential election with a contested, undetermined result; 2026-11-03 is a
  midterm.** The analogy is *structural* — a national election the night before, result pending at
  the open — not equivalent in magnitude. A midterm's overnight repricing is ordinarily smaller, so
  the 2020 session's +2.20% should be read as an upper bound on the drowning effect, not a forecast
  of it. The [midterms ledger](midterm-elections-2026-11-03.md) measures the tape's own pricing of
  election day at **~1.4%**, with **November VIX futures 19.7 against 14.51 spot** (08-29) — a
  ~5.2-point election bump that is real but far short of 2020's regime.
- **The 2025-11-05 ceiling is an unconditional daily close, not an event-window measurement.** It
  does not isolate ADP from everything else that traded that day; it is an upper bound on the whole
  session, which is the direction that makes it usable as a ceiling.
- **The date is `estimate`, and so is most of its corridor.** This event, the refunding announcement,
  ISM Services and the midterms are all `estimate`-labeled; only jobs 11-06 is `confirmed`. Estimates
  widen caution and never license action, and a corridor built mostly of estimates is a weaker
  scaffold than its density suggests.
- **Leg 3 rests on a single post-re-benchmark edition** and on the assumption that ADP repeats the
  two-step cadence it described for the 2025→2026 cycle. `adpresearch.com` returned **404** on the
  methodology fetch in the 09-30 session and was not re-attempted here, so the "annual every
  September" framing still has no methodology-page source.
- **No price instruments were run** — macro-print mode carries none. Index, VIX and yield readings
  are Yahoo daily closes; last complete 2026 close is **2026-09-02**, and the 09-03 session
  (Challenger, claims, **Waller 08:30 ET**, ISM Services 10:00) had not resolved when this was
  written.
- **The reaction function is genuinely unknown at D-62** (leg 9) and this doc does not supply one.
  That is a limit, not a hedge: the last two editions that did supply one were wrong.
