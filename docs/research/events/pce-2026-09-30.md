## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **This is the print where the Fed's preferred inflation gauge gets rewritten, and the
rewrite lands on the one number the October FOMC will actually see.** On **2026-09-30 at 08:30 ET**
BEA publishes August PCE *and* the 2026 annual update of the National Economic Accounts in the same
release — new deflators for portfolio management, legal services and computer software, restated back
to **2021Q1**. The sourced consensus is that this mechanically subtracts **roughly 10–30bp from core
y/y with nothing in the real economy changing** (JPMorgan ≈−10bp, Goldman ≈−20bp, UBS's component
decomposition −20 to −30bp, Fortune's framing "up to 0.3pp"). The operational consequence is blunt:
**the core y/y printed on 09-30 is not comparable to July's published 3.3%** — both the current month
and its year-ago base move — so a "cooling" headline that morning is arithmetic until proven
otherwise. Three things make it matter more than an ordinary revision. It publishes **after** the
**09-16** FOMC and **before** the **10-28** one, so it is the *only* PCE the October committee sees.

2. **The annual update lands *in this release*, not merely on this date — SUPPORTED, BEA primary.**
   BEA's SCB preview states the update of the National Economic Accounts begins **"on September 30,

   "improve the deflation methodology … to better reflect the timing and quantity of services
   consumed"; **legal services** — "a BEA composite price index derived using detailed PPIs … will
   replace the consumer price index (CPI) for legal services beginning in 2024"; **computer software
   and accessories** — "a BEA-composite price index derived using the CPI for computer software and
   accessories; the PPI for game software publishing; and the PPI for hosting, ASP … and other IT
   infrastructure provisioning services will replace the CPI." **BEA states the method and says
   nothing about the level effect** — checked, not assumed.

3. **Comparability breaks *at* this print, and that is this doc's load-bearing finding — SUPPORTED by

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date confirmed, BEA primary fetched 2026-08-29; flipped from `estimate` in this PR).** Treat
**2026-09-30 08:30 ET** as a **high-impact release whose headline number is not comparable to the one
it replaces**. No directional macro position now or planned; `symbols: []` and no house playbook is
macro-keyed. **The operative content is a reading rule, pre-committed before the print exists:** any
core-PCE figure published on or after 09-30 is reported as containing roughly **10–30bp of
annual-update deflator change** (JPMorgan ≈−10bp / Goldman ≈−20bp / UBS decomposition −20 to −30bp /
Fortune "up to 0.3pp" — all pre-publication estimates, none measured) before it is read as
disinflation; and symmetrically, a core print that **fails to fall** despite the revision is hotter
than face value. **No base-case level is stated** — at D-32 there is no consensus, whisper or nowcast
for August data, and this doc will not invent one.

Two structural facts carry more weight than the level. **This is the only PCE the 10-28 FOMC sees**
(09-16 precedes it; 10-29 publishes the morning after the October statement), at a meeting with **no
SEP**, under a chair who **abandoned forward guidance on 08-28**. And **it survives a funding lapse**
that would delete **jobs 10-02** and **CPI 10-14** — so in that branch it is the **last federal
inflation reading before the October decision**, and the one whose comparability is broken. On
transmission, this doc takes a narrower view than its sibling: **the revision's policy effect is
likely smaller than its arithmetic**, because Warsh has reportedly pre-discounted tenths ("left of the
decimal point," WSJ at second hand), argued hawkishly from **headline** 3.7%/4.1% on 08-28 — figures a
core-only revision does not move — and now has a published UBS credibility critique available if he
wants a reason to discount a cooler core reading.

**Kill switches:**

- **The 09-30 release lands with revised core y/y within ~5bp of the old series** — the paper-cooling
  premise fails, legs 3–5 collapse, and the reading rule is withdrawn the same morning.
- **The realized revision materially exceeds the range** (≥40bp on core y/y) — the opposite failure:
  every pre-09-30 inflation comparison in this repo's docs needs restating and UBS's credibility
  critique becomes the live story rather than a footnote.
- **BEA delays, narrows or splits the annual update off 2026-09-30** — the measurement break moves and
  this reverts to an ordinary August PCE print; watch BEA's schedule page at each pulse.
- **A CR is signed before 2026-09-30** — the blackout branch dies, jobs 10-02 and CPI 10-14 print
  normally, and leg 8's "last federal inflation read" framing loses most of its force.
- **A lapse actually begins 2026-10-01** — leg 8 goes live: re-read this print as the October
  committee's final inflation input and say so explicitly in the next row.
- **Warsh or a governor cites the *revised* core figure to justify a stance** — kills leg 6's
  "pre-discounted" read and makes the revision policy-operative; the single most informative thing
  that can happen to this doc.
- **The 09-16 FOMC hikes** — this print stops being an input to a pending decision and becomes the
  first read on whether the September hike was justified, which changes its character; reassess
  off-cycle.
- **BEA publishes the software composite's weights** before the release — leg 9's refusal to infer a
  sign becomes answerable, and the AI-capex read inside core PCE can be decomposed rather than caveated.
- **A published estimate of the revision's effect on Dallas Fed trimmed mean appears** — scores the
  sibling's leg 8 inference and tells us whether the gauge Warsh says he prefers moves at all.
