## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **This calendar's most-cited rates finding has a month where it runs backwards, and 2026-12-28
is in that month.** The [11-24 sibling](treasury-5y-note-2026-11-24.md) established that a 5-Year auction
cheapens **−1.69bp against the average of the 3Y and 7Y** on its own day (t = −9.84, n=43, 2023+), firing
in **34 of 43** in-regime auctions against **11%** of ordinary sessions. Cut that same series by calendar
month this session (290 auctions against **7,176** par-curve sessions, both pulled today): **December
prints +0.705bp (n=22, t = +1.71)** against **−0.526bp** for the other eleven months, and only **3 of 22**
Decembers clear −1.0bp against **97 of 268** elsewhere. Paired within-year — each December against its own
year's Jan–Nov mean, which removes the era confound — the gap is **+0.960bp (n=22, t = +2.71, permutation
p = 0.0096**, 15 of 22 positive); ex-2008 **+0.740bp, t = +2.55**. **The decisive test is that this is not
the far-from-FOMC cut in disguise, which matters because the sibling reads far-from-FOMC as the *stronger*
population and 12-28 is far on both sides** (D+19 from [`fomc-2026-12-09`](fomc-2026-12-09.md), D−30 from
[`fomc-2027-01-27`](fomc-2027-01-27.md)): inside the fully-parsed **1998–2019** window, far-from-FOMC
non-December auctions run **−0.279bp (n=154)** while far-from-FOMC **Decembers run +1.214bp (n=14,
t = +2.22)** — Welch **t = +2.64**. Second finding: the **when-issued window is one session** — announced
Christmas Eve, Christmas Day shut, auction Monday — which only **3 of 290** 5-Year auctions since 1998
have had and only one since 2010, and it is measured **not** to cost demand (pooled across tenors, n=9,

**doubled 2Y+5Y session does not dilute the curve measure** (2023+ **−1.600bp** with a same-day 2-Year
against **−1.712bp** alone) because the same-day 2-Year does not drag the 3-Year denominator leg. Date
`estimate`, `symbols: []`, nothing here is a trade.

## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** the [11-24 sibling](treasury-5y-note-2026-11-24.md) left this calendar with a
usable base rate — a 5-Year auction cheapens ~1.7bp against its neighbours on its own day, 79% of the time
in the current regime — and re-labelled it from *mechanical* to *regime*. This event is the same tenor,
the same size era, the same regime, and the most structurally unusual session the tenor sells on in the
published window: announced on Christmas Eve, sharing Monday 2026-12-28 with the 2-Year note, settling on
the last business day of the year. The cheap question is whether any of that changes the base rate. This
session asked it three ways and one of them came back loud.

**One-line verdict:** the base rate does not survive the calendar — **December is the one month in which
the 5-Year auction-day dip runs the other way**, the effect survives both an era control and the FOMC
control that would most plausibly have explained it away, and the two structural oddities everyone would
have flagged instead (the one-session when-issued window, the doubled 2Y+5Y session) are both measured
**null**.

**Method:** sourced research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md), rates mode — the
house instruments are symbol-keyed and this event has `symbols: []`, so no `earnings-cycle` /
`intraday-edges` run applies and no cache bust was required. Every figure below is computed this session
from primaries fetched today: Fiscal Data's `auctions_query` (**2,330** Note records, 1979-10-31 →
2026-09-09), the **29** daily par yield curve year-files 1998–2026 (**7,176** sessions, 1998-01-02 →
2026-09-08, the latest published close), the Tentative Auction Schedule PDF (HTTP 200, **17,195** bytes,

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The date and terms are right, re-read from the primary, and the entry stays `estimate` —
SUPPORTED.** The schedule carries the row verbatim: `5-Year  NOTE · Thursday, December 24, 2026 ·
Monday, December 28, 2026 · Thursday, December 31, 2026`, with no `R`. The flag column is populated
elsewhere in the same December block — `2-Year  FRN R`, `20-Year  BOND R` and `5-Year  TIPS R T`, all
announced 12-17 — so the blank is a signal rather than a missing field, and the TIPS row (auctioning
12-22) is a different security. The full block reads: **2-Year NOTE and 5-Year NOTE both Monday 12-28**
with the 13-week and 26-week bills, **7-Year NOTE Tuesday 12-29** with the 6-week bill, all six announced
Thursday 12-24 and all six settling Thursday 12-31. The same fetch carries `Holiday - Friday, December 25,
2026 - Christmas Day`. The entry stays **`estimate`** at D−110 on the two limits this lane records: a
tentative schedule is tentative, and the confirming primary is the 2026-12-24 announcement.

**2. The size is not published, and this doc will not assert one — SUPPORTED (as a negative).** `sb0590`
(2026-08-05), read direct today: the anticipated-size grid is headed `2-Year 3-Year 5-Year 7-Year 10-Year
20-Year 30-Year FRN` and runs `May-26 Jun-26 Jul-26 Aug-26 Sep-26 Oct-26` — six rows, **no November and no
December**. The 5-Year column reads **70** in every one of them. Two verbatim sentences bracket what that
is worth: Treasury "anticipates maintaining nominal coupon and FRN auction sizes for at least the next
several quarters," and in the same release "continues to evaluate potential future changes to nominal
coupon and FRN auction sizes." So **$70B is an expectation** — 28 consecutive $70B prints (2024-04-24 →
2026-08-26, zero deviations) plus flat guidance — and the published number arrives at the **2026-11-04**
refunding. Every band in this doc is computed on the $70B era and is void if December deviates.

**3. December is the month the effect runs backwards — SUPPORTED, and this is the finding.** Cutting all
**290** in-coverage 5-Year auctions by calendar month on the sibling's own measure (5Y CMT change minus
the average of the 3Y and 7Y changes, close-to-close):

| May | 28 | **−1.393bp** | −3.11 | 15/28 |

| November | 27 | **−1.407bp** | −2.89 | 15/27 |
| … | | | | |
| **December** | **22** | **+0.705bp** | **+1.71** | **3/22** |
| all other months | 268 | −0.526bp | −4.74 | 97/268 |

December is the only month with a positive mean at |t| > 1, and its hit rate against the −1.0bp threshold
the sibling ledgers grade on is **14%** — indistinguishable from the 11% base rate on ordinary
non-auction sessions. **Paired within-year**, which removes the era confound entirely because each

**15 of 22 years are positive.** The paired effect is *larger* than the raw one, which is what a real
effect does when the era confound runs against it — December auctions cluster in years whose January–

**4. It is not the far-from-FOMC cut in disguise — SUPPORTED, and this is the test that makes leg 3
usable.** Nearly every December 5-Year sells well clear of the mid-month FOMC, and the 11-24 sibling reads
far-from-FOMC as the **stronger** population (2023+ −2.00bp, t = −7.50, n=20 vs −1.41bp near). If that cut
were doing the work, December should be *deeper* than its year, not shallower. Tested inside the
**1998–2019** window, where the Fed's historical calendar parses completely (**174** decision dates,
8/year; 212 auctions, 44 near-FOMC at ±3 sessions):

| Cell | n | Mean relative | t |
|---|---|---|---|
| far from FOMC · non-December | 154 | −0.279bp | −1.89 |
| far from FOMC · **December** | 14 | **+1.214bp** | **+2.22** |
| near FOMC · non-December | 42 | −0.464bp | −1.56 |
| near FOMC · December | 2 | −0.250bp | −0.14 |

The December-inside-far difference is **+1.494bp, Welch t = +2.64**. So the two cuts are orthogonal and
point opposite ways, and 2026-12-28 sitting **D+19** from `fomc-2026-12-09` and **D−30** from
`fomc-2027-01-27` does not license the deeper prior. This is the single reason the This-quarter call is
written as a differential rather than as the sibling's −1.0bp threshold.

**5. The when-issued window is one session — the shortest this tenor sells on in the modern era — and it
is measured not to cost demand. SUPPORTED as a null.** Counted on Treasury's own published-session
calendar (a session is a day the par curve publishes), the 12-24 announcement is followed by the Christmas
Day closure, the weekend, and then the auction: **exactly one session separates them**. Across the 290
in-coverage 5-Year auctions the announcement-to-auction gap distribution is 1 session ×3 · 2 ×42 · 3 ×93 ·
4 ×131 · 5 ×21, and the three one-session cases are **2005-09-07**, **2005-10-12** and **2020-12-28** —
only the last is post-2010. That n is too small to test alone, so it was pooled across the whole
2Y/3Y/5Y/7Y/10Y coupon population (**1,132** auctions with both a bid-to-cover and a parseable
announcement date, 1999-10-27 → 2026-09-08), each auction's bid-to-cover z-scored against the trailing 20
auctions of its own tenor:

| Window | n | Mean b/c z | t |
|---|---|---|---|
| **one session** | **9** | **−0.765** | **−1.34** |
| 2 sessions | 252 | −0.025 | −0.34 |
| 3 sessions | 345 | +0.037 | +0.58 |
| 4 sessions | 258 | +0.092 | +1.24 |
| 5 sessions | 208 | −0.033 | −0.40 |
| all others | 1,073 | +0.027 | +0.74 |

**Not significant**, and the composition says why the mean is misleading: the nine split into two deep
negatives (2015-12-28 z −3.01 and 2016-03-28 z −3.08, both 2-Years), one 2002 oddity announced the same
day it auctioned (z −2.43), one strong positive (2010-12-27, z **+1.69**) and four ordinary prints —
including the direct twin, **2020-12-28's 5-Year at z −0.42**. Dealer takedown agrees: one-session z

the story and it is not; it is recorded because a session grading the print on 12-28 will otherwise
discover the compressed window and over-read it.

**6. The doubled 2Y+5Y session does not dilute the curve measure, and the mechanical channel that would
have made it do so is refuted directly — SUPPORTED.** 2026-12-28 is the schedule's only doubled coupon
day. The worry is mechanical rather than behavioural: the measure subtracts the average of the 3Y and 7Y,
and if a same-session 2-Year auction dragged the **3-Year** leg down in sympathy, the denominator would
fall with the numerator and the measured 5-Year dip would shrink toward zero. Both halves were tested.

| Cut | n | Mean relative | t |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023+ · 5Y with a same-day 2-Year NOTE | 10 | **−1.600bp** | −4.82 |
| 2023+ · 5Y selling alone | 33 | **−1.712bp** | −8.48 |
| full record · with same-day 2-Year | 24 | −0.958bp | −2.79 |
| full record · alone | 266 | −0.385bp | −3.38 |

The two in-regime cells are indistinguishable, and **8 of the 10** doubled sessions cleared −1.0bp — the
same 79–80% as the population. The denominator channel is likewise absent: the **3Y-minus-7Y** spread
change on 2-Year auction days that carry no 3-Year sale runs **+0.211bp (t = +1.52, n=331)** on the full
record and **+0.186bp (t = +0.52, n=43)** in 2023+, against **+0.012** and **+0.044** on sessions with no
coupon auction at all — insignificant, and *positive*, which if anything would deepen the 5-Year measure
rather than shrink it. So the doubled session is safe to grade on the ordinary measure.

**7. Year-end settlement does not show up in demand at this tenor — SUPPORTED as a null, corroborating
the 7-Year sibling.** This sale settles **2026-12-31**, the last business day of the year, which is the
classic setup for the dealer-balance-sheet story. Fourteen 5-Year auctions since 1998 settled on their
year's last business day. Paired against each year's own January–November mean — the design the
[7-Year sibling](treasury-7y-note-2026-12-29.md) used to remove the secular decline in dealer share —
December dealer takedown runs **−0.164 percentage points (n=17, t = −0.08)**, with only **5 of 17**
Decembers above their year. Bid-to-cover leans the expected way but does not clear: **−0.058 (n=22,
t = −1.48)**, 13 of 22 negative. That is a second tenor reaching the 7-Year lane's conclusion on its own
data, which is how a folk belief actually dies.

**8. The direct precedent is a miss, and it is stated as one — SUPPORTED.** 2020-12-28 is a row-for-row
calendar twin: Thursday 12-24 announcement, 2-Year and 5-Year both Monday 12-28, 7-Year Tuesday 12-29,
settle Thursday 12-31, identical late-December weekdays. Its relative move was **+1.50bp** — a clean miss
on the −1.0bp threshold — on bid-to-cover **2.39**, dealer takedown **24.92%**, indirect 57.11%, at $59B.
It sits in the 2020-01 → 2021-09 window the 11-24 sibling measured at **+0.43bp**, i.e. before the current
regime, so it is a precedent for the *December* claim and not a test of the regime.

**9. The corridor is dense and the attribution problem runs one way — SUPPORTED.** Twenty-seven tracked
ids sit within five days of 2026-12-28. The session itself carries
[advance economic indicators](advance-economic-indicators-2026-12-28.md) and two foreign-market closures;
12-23 carries [PCE](pce-2026-12-23.md), [GDP-Q3-third](gdp-q3-2026-third-2026-12-23.md),
[durable goods](durable-goods-2026-12-23.md) and [new home sales](new-home-sales-2026-12-23.md) all pulled
forward by the holiday, and 12-30 carries the [FOMC minutes](fomc-minutes-2026-12-30.md). Against a 5-Year
auction whose measured *level* effect is zero, that means **any outright 5Y move on 12-28 belongs to the
tape and essentially none of it to five-year paper** — which is precisely why the forward test is written
on the relative measure.

**10. No new dated adjacency to propose, and the reasons are named — SUPPORTED.** The sweep found nothing
dated and untracked. Specifically: the **2-Year note on 12-28** is already proposed by two sibling lanes
(`treasury-2y-note-2026-12-28.from-treasury-7y-note-2026-12-29.json` and

**Honest limits.** **The month cut is one of twelve and it does not survive a multiple-comparison
correction — this is the objection and it is stated first.** Running the identical within-year paired test
on every month gives three at uncorrected p < 0.01: **August −0.871bp (t = −3.09, p = 0.005)**, **May
−0.670bp (t = −2.88, p = 0.004)** and **December +0.960bp (t = +2.71, p = 0.0096)**. Bonferroni across
twelve puts those at **0.059 / 0.052 / 0.116**, so **none survives**, and December is the *third*-largest
by |t|, not the largest. What keeps it in the call sheet rather than the bin is that it is the only one of
the three with an independent second test behind it (the FOMC interaction, leg 4, Welch t = +2.64 on a
held-out 1998–2019 window) — but a scorer should treat leg 3 as a hypothesis with one supporting
experiment, never as an established seasonal. The in-regime December sample is **n=3** and it points the
other way (mean −1.167bp, 2 of 3 clearing the threshold), which is why the forward test is registered as a
differential rather than as a directional claim. The FOMC classification uses ±3 sessions and a calendar
parse that is complete only for 1998–2019 — 2020–2026 decision dates parse with gaps, so leg 4 is
deliberately confined to the window where the input is whole, and it therefore says nothing about whether
