## Initial research

### The question, plainly

If the December 11 continuing resolution is the live funding cliff, what does a lapse beginning
**2026-12-12** actually cost — to the fifteen-odd dated events this calendar tracks in the same
quarter, and to a paper book holding long-duration tech (NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL
AMZN)? And is the December fight the same shape as the September one this calendar just watched
resolve?

**One-line verdict:** the equity channel is the same measured non-event it was in September, but the
*informational* channel inverts — this cliff lands **after** every input to the December FOMC and
**before** every check on it, on the very day the blackout lifts — and the cost of a lapse is far
more graduated than this calendar has been assuming, because BLS's own record shows lapse **length**,
not the lapse itself, decides whether a release is delayed or deleted.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date exists only if a bill nobody has verified as signed was signed — SUPPORTED, and it is
   why this stays `estimate`.** The House adopted the Senate-passed **H.R. 6500** (Continuing
   Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027) **370–48** on **2026-09-01** — R 193–19, D 176–29 — after
   the Senate's **90–6** on 08-08, funding agencies at FY2026 levels through **December 11, 2026**
   (Roll Call, fetched 2026-09-02). Every outlet checked today still describes the bill as awaiting
   signature; Fortune's headline says so verbatim. congress.gov and govtrack 403'd, and
   whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions lists nothing after **2026-08-28**. So the entry is `estimate`
   for a compound reason: the deadline's *date* is press-grade, and the instrument that creates it is
   **not verified as law**. Note for a later session: even an enacted public law would hit a schema
   gap — `market-events.ts` defines no `confirmed` source prefix for statute (the fifth instance,
   after a state utility commission, a state election authority, a federal court docket and the EIA).

2. **The administration's stated disposition is pro-signature, and it is now primary-sourced —
   SUPPORTED, with a boundary.** OMB's SAP of **2026-08-03** states: *"The Administration supports
   the Senate Amendment to H.R. 6500… President Trump strongly opposes another futile Government
   shutdown. Every Member of Congress should support passage of this CR… If this bill were presented
   to the President in its current form, his senior advisors would recommend that he sign it into
   law."* That is the strongest evidence this calendar holds on the signature question and it is a
   whitehouse.gov document, not a press summary. **The boundary matters:** it speaks to *this* CR,
   not to whatever December produces. Read as disposition, it is a mild argument against a December
   lapse; read as a commitment, it is nothing.

3. **The cliff's placement inverts September's finding — SUPPORTED, and it is the load-bearing leg.**
   Sequence the corridor: jobs **12-04** → FOMC + dot plot **12-09** → CPI **12-10** → **cliff
   12-11** → PPI **12-15** → import/export **12-17** → triple witching **12-18** → PCE **12-23**.
   Every federal input to the December decision prints **before** the cliff; every federal check on
   that decision prints **after** it. September's event was dangerous because a lapse would have
   deleted the payrolls and CPI feeding a **no-SEP** October meeting — a blinded *decision*. This one
   cannot do that. What it can do is blind the *audit*: the Fed would deliver a dot plot on 12-09 and
   the market would then lose, in order, its wholesale-inflation read (12-15), its traded-goods price
   read (12-17) and its preferred inflation gauge (12-23) — the exact three series
   [`pce-2026-12-23`](pce-2026-12-23.md) names as the source data its own deflators are built from.

4. **12-11 is also the day the communications blackout lifts — SUPPORTED, and nothing on this
   calendar had joined these two facts.** [`fomc-blackout-start-2026-11-28`](fomc-blackout-start-2026-11-28.md)
   establishes the gate runs **11-28 → 12-10**, so **Friday 12-11 is the first session in which any
   FOMC participant may speak** about a decision taken on 12-09 and a CPI printed on 12-10. It is
   also the session a lapse would begin. That doc's contribution was a one-session extension of the
   defined-risk window (12-09 → 12-11) on communications grounds alone; this leg says the extension
   is *doubly* earned, and the two reasons are independent.

5. **"BLS deletes rather than delays" is too strong, and BLS's own page refutes the flat version —
   REFUTED as stated, and this is the correction most worth banking.** From
   `bls.gov/bls/2025-lapse-revised-release-dates.htm`, fetched direct today:
   - **The 2026 lapse** (the Jan 30 – Feb 3 weekend gap) produced **five reschedules and zero
     cancellations** — JOLTS Feb 3 → Feb 5, Metro Area Feb 4 → Feb 6, Employment Situation Feb 6 →
     Feb 11, CPI and Real Earnings Feb 11 → Feb 13. A short lapse is a **two-to-five-day delay**.
   - **The 2025 lapse** (43 days) cancelled exactly the releases whose *collection or reference
     window* it swallowed: Employment Situation **October**, CPI **October**, Real Earnings
     **October**, JOLTS **September**, State JOLTS **September**, State and Metro employment
     **October**. Everything else was **delayed**, some heavily — September PPI Oct 16 → **Nov 25**,
     September import/export Oct 17 → **Dec 3**, September Employment Situation Oct 3 → **Nov 20**.
   - **Even a "cancelled" release is not always deleted data.** October 2025 PPI was cancelled as a
     news release, but its data was published with the November release on **2026-01-14**, and BLS
     records that October and November response rates were **"within the normal range"** with **no
     methodology modifications** — because PPI price-update requests can be sent late (October's went
     out Nov 19) and still be answered. **CPI is the opposite**: October 2025 CPS/CPI field
     collection never happened and cannot be done retroactively, which is why October 2025's
     unemployment rate will never exist.

   The honest rule this yields: **a lapse deletes the field-collected household surveys whose window
   it covers, and merely delays the establishment price surveys.** Both this calendar's `cr-expiry`
   note and the 09-30 sibling assert the blunt version; this leg replaces it.

6. **The 2025 drag ran far longer than the shutdown — SUPPORTED, and it is the underrated cost.** The
   same BLS table shows the 43-day lapse pushing releases out to **January and even March 2026**:
   December-2025 PPI to **2026-01-30**, Q4 ECI to **2026-02-10**, December import/export to
   **2026-02-10**, CFOI to **2026-02-19**, January-2026 PPI to **2026-02-27**, January import/export
   to **2026-03-05**. A lapse is not a gap, it is a **queue** — and the queue outlives the politics
   by roughly three months. Applied here, a long December lapse would still be distorting the release
   calendar in **Q1 2027**, past every dated event this calendar currently tracks.

7. **The exposure list is three tracked events, not one — SUPPORTED, and it corrects the calendar's
   own note.** `market-events.ts`'s `cr-expiry-2026-12-11` note says "the exposed tracked event is
   `pce-2026-12-23`." BLS's **December 2026** schedule, fetched direct today, shows two more, both
   already tracked and both federal: **PPI (November data) Tue 12-15, 08:30 ET** — three days after
   the cliff, and the *first* federal statistic in the blast radius — and **U.S. Import and Export
   Price Indexes (November data) Thu 12-17, 08:30 ET**. On leg 5's taxonomy both are
   delay-candidates rather than deletion-candidates, but [`ppi-2026-12-15`](ppi-2026-12-15.md) already
   records the worst measured slip on this calendar (**40 days**, Oct 16 → Nov 25, 2025), so "delay"
   is not "harmless." For **PCE**, the 2025 analogue is more precisely described than the sibling
   ledger has it: BEA cancelled the standalone **2025-12-19** November release and folded it into a
   **combined October+November release on 2026-01-22** — the release died, the data did not. BEA's
   true deletion that cycle was the **Q3 2025 GDP advance estimate**, cancelled outright.

8. **The December fight is a weaker aversion case than September's, on four counts — MIXED, and the
   doc puts no number on it.** September's aversion rested on two already-passed bills, a 90–6 Senate
   margin and an explicit shared purpose. December has none of those. (a) **Full-year approps are
   barely started** — House **3 of 12** passed the chamber, Senate **0 of 12** even released for

   appointees grant-approval power, and the restriction on Border Patrol funding transfers — both

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (both date and event `estimate`; the bill creating this date is not verified as signed as of
2026-09-02).** Take **no position, no hedge and no sizing change** off this event, and take no view on
whether a December lapse happens — the negotiation does not exist yet and this doc has no instrument
that could price it. The stance's content is **structural, not directional**, and it is three claims:

1. **A lapse here cannot blind the December FOMC decision** — jobs 12-04, the 12-09 dots and CPI
   12-10 all print before the cliff. It can only blind the *check* on that decision: PPI 12-15,
   import/export 12-17, PCE 12-23. That is a materially smaller harm than the 09-30 sibling's branch,
   and this event should be read as `high` impact for the queue effect (leg 6), not for a blinded Fed.
2. **The cost of a lapse scales with its length, not its occurrence** (leg 5, BLS primary). A
   weekend-shaped lapse costs 2–5 days of delay and nothing else. Only a lapse long enough to swallow
   a household-survey collection window deletes data permanently, and the first such window after
   12-12 belongs to releases published in **January 2027** — which this calendar cannot yet track.
3. **12-11 carries two independent reasons to be defined-risk** — the cliff, and the first session in
   which the Fed may speak after the 12-09 dots and the 12-10 CPI.

**Kill switches:**

- **H.R. 6500 verified signed** (a public-law number, a whitehouse.gov action, or a fetchable
  congress.gov record) — this date becomes real and the entry's `estimate` label narrows from
  "unverified bill + conditional event" to "conditional event" alone. Until then the live fiscal
  event is **2026-09-30**, not this one.
- **H.R. 6500 not signed by 2026-09-30** — this entry is moot; the September cliff fires and every
  premise here is replaced by that ledger's.
- **Full-year FY2027 appropriations, or a new CR expiring after 2026-12-23, enacted before 12-11** —
  this event retires, and PPI 12-15 / import-export 12-17 / PCE 12-23 lose their existence risk.
- **BLS moves PPI 12-15 or import/export 12-17 off their current dates** — leg 3's corridor and leg
  7's exposure list both shift; re-derive rather than carry.
- **A lapse actually begins 2026-12-12** — switch [`ppi-2026-12-15`](ppi-2026-12-15.md),
  [`import-export-prices-2026-12-17`](import-export-prices-2026-12-17.md) and
  [`pce-2026-12-23`](pce-2026-12-23.md) from "the data prints" to "the data queues," and start
  measuring the lapse's *length* against leg 5's threshold rather than reacting to its start.
- **A lapse deletes an establishment price survey outright** (a PPI or import/export release
  cancelled *with* its data, not folded forward) — leg 5's delete-vs-delay mechanism breaks and the
  blunt "BLS deletes" claim this doc refuted turns out to have been right.
- **The S&P moves >2% on a December shutdown headline** — the inherited 0.0% base rate breaks
  in-sample and the equity channel becomes worth measuring instead of dismissing.
- **`cr-expiry-2026-12-11` is retired or this entry is** — leg 10's duplicate is resolved and
  whichever ledger survives inherits the other's findings.
