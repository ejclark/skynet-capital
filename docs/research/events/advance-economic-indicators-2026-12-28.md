## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Does the November-2026 Advance Economic Indicators Report exist on 2026-12-28 given that it lands 17 days past the `cr-expiry-2026-12-11` funding cliff, what would it tell us if it does, and does any of it license an action for a book holding NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL AMZN?

**One-line verdict:** it is scheduled, it would carry the full composition punch of its series — and it is the least *readable* edition of the year regardless of the funding outcome, because the Atlanta Fed has already filed it as a non-posting internal update; meanwhile the funding premise this entry was filed under is measurably the wrong one, since a lapse takes this report's lead time and not its data.

### Method

### Leg 2 — the December edition carries the full composition punch · **SUPPORTED**

The sibling `advance-economic-indicators-2026-10-28` ledger measured the series as a whole: AEIR-driven vintages move GDPNow's composition 12.2× more than ordinary ones while barely moving the headline. The open question this ledger has to answer is whether a *late-December* edition — mid-window for Q4 rather than the quarter's final vintage — is a weaker instance. It is not:

| Vintage class | n | Median churn | Median \|Δ net exports\| | Median \|Δ headline\| | Churn ÷ headline |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Late-December AEIR** | **7** | **0.586pp** | **0.322pp** | **0.149pp** | **3.9×** |
| All AEIR vintages | 79 | 0.586pp | 0.265pp | 0.366pp | 1.6× |
| Every other vintage | 1,743 | 0.048pp | 0.002pp | 0.086pp | 0.6× |

Churn = |Δequipment| + |Δnet exports| + |Δinventories| in contribution points, versus the immediately preceding vintage of the same quarter. The December subsample is 2016-12-29, 2017-12-28, 2019-12-30, 2020-12-30, 2021-12-29, 2022-12-27 and 2023-12-28 — every late-December AEIR that produced a posted vintage in the archive. Its churn is identical to the population's and its net-exports move is *larger*, while its headline move is **less than half**. So the December edition is not a weaker AEIR; it is a **more extreme one on the axis that makes an AEIR interesting** — the ratio of what it rewrites to what it says.

The directional skew survives independently too: **6 of these 7 revised the nowcast down** (the outlier is 2019-12-30, +0.149pp), consistent with the 20-of-26 asymmetry the 10-28 ledger registered on final vintages. n=7 is too small to register as its own forward test and it is not being sized on; it is corroboration of a claim already on the record, nothing more.

### Leg 3 — 2026-12-28 produces a posted GDPNow vintage · **REFUTED**

The Atlanta Fed publishes two schedules in one workbook, and the distinction is defined in its own note text on `PostedUpdates`: BEA light-vehicle sales "may be incorporated in these posting dates. **When they occur on non-posting dates, they will be treated as an internal update.**" `PostedUpdates` is where the model publishes; `InternalUpdates` is where it ingests without publishing.

| 2026-10-28 | `PostedUpdates` | **Final nowcast of 2026:Q3 GDP growth:  Advance Economic Indicators** |

`PostedUpdates`' last row of the year is 2026-12-23 (GDP Q3 3rd estimate, personal income and outlays, NIPA detail, M3-1, new-home sales). This is the leg that closes the tradeability question for this edition, and it closes it harder than `symbols: []` does.

### Leg 4 — a suspended edition is unrecoverable information · **REFUTED**

This entry was filed on the premise that a funding lapse *deletes* this series, and that "a deleted edition is not recoverable information, unlike a late one." Parsing all 179 rows of Census's own as-published calendar shows what actually happened across the 2025–26 lapse:

| Reference month | AEIR | Full Intl Trade | Adv durable goods | Wholesale inventories |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oct 2025 | — | Jan 8, 2026 (69d) | — | Jan 8, 2026 |
| Nov 2025 | — | Jan 29, 2026 (60d) | Jan 26, 2026 | Jan 29, 2026 |
| Dec 2025 | **Feb 19, 2026 (50d)** | **Feb 19, 2026 (50d)** | Feb 18, 2026 | Feb 24, 2026 |
| Jan 2026 | **`Suspended`** (was Feb 27) | Mar 12, 2026 (40d) | Mar 13, 2026 | Mar 19, 2026 |
| Feb 2026 | **`Suspended`** (was Mar 27) | Apr 2, 2026 (33d) | Apr 7, 2026 | Apr 9, 2026 |
| Mar 2026 | Apr 29, 2026 (29d) | May 5, 2026 (35d) | Apr 29, 2026 | May 8, 2026 |

Three things fall out, and none of them is "information was lost."

**Every underlying number published.** For both suspended reference months the full International Trade report, the advance durable-goods report and wholesale inventories all came out. The advance report is a *preview* of those; when the backlog meant the definitive version would arrive first or nearly so, Census cancelled the preview and kept the report.

**The one edition that "merely slipped" lost its entire product.** December-2025 data published on **2026-02-19 under release code `A202602190830` — the identical date and identical code as the full International Trade report for the same month.** An advance report published simultaneously with the report it advances has a lead time of zero. Measured across the eight clean 2026 reference months, the AEIR normally precedes the full report by **6–11 days, median 7**. So the delayed edition and the deleted editions converge on the same outcome: the lapse took the seven days, not the data.

**Census sacrifices previews, not reports.** The only other series carrying `Suspended` rows anywhere on the calendar is *Preliminary U.S. Imports for Consumption of Steel Products* (Dec-2025 and Jan-2026 reference months) — also a preliminary product. That is the mechanism, measured rather than assumed, and it predicts what a 2026-12-12 lapse would do to this edition: suspend it, publish the November trade data in the full report roughly a week to a month later than it otherwise would have, and re-sync.

**And the queue drains predictably.** The full trade report's lag from reference-month end ran 69 → 60 → 50 → 40 → 33 days over five reference months — roughly 9–10 days recovered per month, back to the normal 33–38 day band by the February-2026 reference month, with the AEIR itself back on schedule (29 days) for March-2026 data. A December lapse's cost is that decay curve, not a permanent hole.

Graded REFUTED because the entry's own stated reason for existing does not survive the measurement. The entry still earns its row — but for Leg 3's reason, not this one.

### Leg 5 — 12-28 is a quiet session where an 08:30 print might be readable · **REFUTED**, and the failure mode is the opposite one

2026-12-25 is a Friday, so 2026-12-28 is the Monday first session after Christmas. Across 2006–2025, each session's true range and volume normalised to its own year's median:

| SPY | n | Median range ratio | Median volume ratio | Sessions below 1.0× |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First session after Christmas | 20 | **0.534** | **0.583** | 17 / 20 |
| All Dec 26–31 sessions | 82 | 0.700 | 0.725 | — |
| All sessions | 5,031 | 1.000 | 1.000 | — |

QQQ reproduces the shape (0.747 range / 0.553 volume, 17 of 20 below 1.0). Half the range on 55–60% of the volume is not a session in which a macro print gets priced; it is a session in which the desk that would price it is not at the desk.

The honest reading is not "safe, therefore ignorable." The distribution's single outlier is **2018-12-26, at 5.72× SPY / 4.26× QQQ** — the rebound from the Christmas-Eve crash. Thin books do not damp shocks, they amplify them. A funding lapse beginning 2026-12-12 would put its third week over exactly this tape. The stand-aside on 12-28 is therefore a stand-aside on the *session*, not only on the print, and it holds in both directions.

### Honest limits

- **`InternalUpdates` is a schedule, not a promise.** The Atlanta Fed's own note says the schedule is "subject to change," and a workbook re-issue could move 12-28 to `PostedUpdates`. That is why Leg 3 is registered as a forward test rather than asserted as settled.
- **n = 7 on the December subsample.** It reproduces the population medians closely and its down-skew matches an independently measured 20-of-26, but one anomalous December moves it. Nothing is sized on it, and it is deliberately not registered as its own test.
- **The 2025–26 lapse is one lapse.** Leg 4 generalises from a single episode with n=2 suspended editions. The mechanism it proposes — previews cancelled, reports kept — is consistent across the two suspended series on the calendar, and that is the whole of the evidence.
- **The funding branch is carried, not measured.** No independent bill-level primary was obtained this session; congress.gov requires an API key this lane does not hold. Everything about the cliff comes from `cr-expiry-2026-12-11` and its sources.
- **The session measurement is about seasonality, not about this print.** It says the post-Christmas Monday is thin. It does not say the AEIR is what would or would not move it.
- **`symbols: []` remains the binding constraint.** Even were every leg above twice as strong, this event has no instrument attached and no macro-keyed house playbook to attach one to.
