## Initial research

### The question

[`tic-quarterly-external-debt-2027-06-30`](tic-quarterly-external-debt-2027-06-30.md) censused this
series' whole archive and left this event behind as a checkpoint: *"the first of three chances before
2027-06-30 to watch a NON-June edition publish and revise nothing."* That framing makes this print a
control observation and nothing else. Two questions follow. Is that all it is? And if the interesting
edition really is June, is there anything about **which quarter** an ordinary edition publishes that
matters?

### One-line verdict

It is not all it is: the ordinary edition delivers the **most heavily revised as-of quarter in the
archive**, and the mechanism behind that concentration is **not** the end-June securities survey —
which is a second, independent refutation of the assumption the sibling killed on different evidence.

### Method

Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md) (macro-print mode —
no symbol instruments exist for this kind; `symbols: []`). Primaries fetched **direct** this session
(2026-09-09, plain curl, HTTP 200): Treasury's TIC release-dates table (**111,182 bytes**) including
its full REVISIONS-and-RELEASE-DATES note block, and Treasury's *U.S. Gross External Debt* statistics
page (**174,128 bytes**), whose per-as-of-quarter index names every current and archived vintage.
**All 135** archived Table A vintages were re-fetched from `ticdata.treasury.gov` (as-of 2009Q1
through 2026Q1) — the sibling's corpus, pulled again rather than inherited — plus Treasury's daily
par-yield-curve CSVs for **2011-2026** (3,922 sessions) and CBOE's `VIX_History.csv`. Every statistic
below was **recomputed from those files**; nothing is carried over from the sibling except where a
number is explicitly quoted as theirs. Each vintage's publication date is read from its own footnote
(`1/ Source: Department of the Treasury, <date>`, its `revision of <date>` variant, or the pre-2015
`Revised <date>` / bare trailing date), never from the index page's labels. Last-business-day
arithmetic was recomputed from `datetime` with 5 U.S.C. 6103 holidays constructed from the statute's
rules. Sector totals are taken from the main table only (the text is truncated at *Memorandum items*
first — the memorandum block repeats the sector labels, and not doing this silently returns the
securities memo line instead of the sector); Deposit-taking corporations is derived as the residual
`total − government − central bank − other sectors − intercompany`, which reproduces the parsed value
exactly on all **24** vintages where both are readable. `probe-ref.blocked` is empty: no cited source
failed.

### Leg 1 — the date · **SUPPORTED**, and promoted `estimate` → `confirmed`

Treasury's 2026 table reads `September | 16 | 16 | 30` across the monthly, quarterly-nonfinancial and
gross-external-debt columns, under *"All data releases occur at 4 p.m. Washington, D.C. time"*. The
note block states the rule in its own words:

> *"Quarterly U.S. Gross External Debt. The time of release is normally at 4:00 p.m. in Washington
> D.C. The release dates are the last business day of each quarter (March, June, September, and
> December)."*

The same page adds, in the asterisk under the table, a detail worth carrying: *"For quarterly data on
U.S. gross external debt there is no accompanying press release."* **2026-09-30 is a Wednesday and
the last business day of September 2026.**

The empirical corroboration is where this leg improves on the sibling's, because sorting the archive
by **release month** rather than pooling it changes the answer. Across the **64 distinct publication
stamps** the corpus yields (126 of 135 vintages carry a stamp; nine carry none):

| Release month | n | at offset +0 | the exceptions |
|---|---|---|---|
| March | 15 | **15** | — |
| **September** | **15** | **15** | **none, ever** |
| June | 20 | 16 | +1, +2, +2 (see below), +2 |
| December | 14 | 10 | −1, −1, **+16**, **+31** |
| **All** | **64** | **56** | |

Two corrections to the pooled reading fall out of this:

1. **Three of June's four "slips" are not slips.** They are stamped **2012-06-30** (a Saturday),
   **2013-06-30** (a Sunday) and **2024-06-30** (a Sunday) — dates on which nothing publishes. They
   are nominal month-end stamps applied to a release that actually went out on the preceding business
   day. Recognising them, June is **19 of 20**, and its only genuine delay is **2025-07-02** at +2.
2. **All genuine lateness beyond two days is December's**, and both instances are appropriations
   events: **2019-01-31** at **+31** (the 35-day funding lapse, 2018-12-22 → 2019-01-25) and
   **2026-01-16** at **+16**, flagged *"16 (delayed)"* on Treasury's own release-dates page. The two
   **−1** stamps (2021-12-30, 2024-12-30) are early publications, also December.

**September has never been late and has never been early.** That is the cleanest sub-series in the
record, and it is this event's month.

Promotion follows the sibling precedents ([`tic-quarterly-external-debt-2027-06-30`](tic-quarterly-external-debt-2027-06-30.md),
[`tic-annual-survey-prelim-2027-02-26`](tic-annual-survey-prelim-2027-02-26.md),
[`mts-october-2026-11-12`](mts-october-2026-11-12.md)): one named Treasury primary plus independent
mechanical corroboration. The `estimate` ground the proposal cited was this lane's **no-self-confirm**
limit on an event discovered in-sweep — the entry was created by the 2027-06-30 initial research, not
researched. That is cleared here by this event's own session re-fetching the page. As on every
sibling, `confirmed` describes **the published schedule and nothing else**: the release-dates page
carries the standing notice that a date *"will be revised after offices reopen"* if Washington D.C.
offices are closed, and the 2019 exception above is exactly that notice firing.

**Where the live schedule risk actually is.** The funding deadline that shares this date is
**already resolved** — `government-funding-deadline-2026-09-30` records H.R. 6500 enacted

release. It can touch the next one. `cr-expiry-2026-12-11` falls **twenty days** before the
**2026-12-31** edition, which is the month that owns both of this series' real delays. That edition
was **not in the calendar at all**; this session proposes it (see the sweep below).

### Leg 3 — the as-of quarter is the finding · **SUPPORTED**, and the obvious mechanism is **REFUTED**

The sibling asked *when* revisions happen and answered June. It did not ask *what* gets revised. Every
June edition restates the four quarters of the previous calendar year at once, so each edition
supplies one observation per as-of quarter, and fourteen editions give a balanced panel.

**Q2 is the hardest-revised quarter, and it is not close:**

| As-of quarter | n | mean \|revision\| (% of level) | median | largest-of-its-edition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | 14 | 0.693% | 0.491% | 2 |
| **Q2** | **14** | **1.563%** | **1.436%** | **7** |
| Q3 | 12 | 1.372% | 0.690% | 3 |
| Q4 | 12 | 0.848% | 0.564% | 2 |

- **Q2 is the largest revision of its edition in 7 of 14 June editions.** Against a 1-in-4 null the
  upper-tail binomial probability is **p=0.038**.
- **The magnitude gap survives a permutation test on medians**: Q2 **1.436%** against **0.521%** for
  the pooled other three, **p=0.011** (200,000 shuffles). On means it is **1.563%** vs **0.957%**,
  **p=0.088** — the mean test is weaker precisely because Q3 carries the **2024Q3** data event
  (**7.29%** of level, a **+$1,880.6bn** restatement of a bad first vintage), which is one anomaly
  rather than a property of Q3. The median comparison is the honest one.
- **The direction skews down**: **9 of 14** Q2 revisions are negative, median **−0.299%**, mean
  **−$169bn**. That is a tendency, not a finding — the binomial on 9/14 is **p≈0.21** and it is
  recorded here unregistered.

**Why this should have been the survey, and why it is not.** Treasury's note says the June revisions
*"include the results of the Survey of Foreign Holdings of U.S. Securities at end-June of the previous
year."* The survey's reference date is **end-June** — the exact close of Q2. If the survey drove
anything, its fingerprint would be a Q2 concentration in the one component it can move: Table A's
*Debt Securities: By Sector* memorandum. That is a sharper test than the sibling's pooled regression,
because it looks for the survey's **timing signature** rather than its correlation, and it is the test
that could have rescued the hypothesis.

It fails, twice:

| | Q1 | **Q2** | Q3 | Q4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mean \|Δ debt securities\| | $28.8bn | **$36.2bn** | $64.6bn | $47.0bn |
| median \|Δ debt securities\| | $14.4bn | **$21.6bn** | $51.3bn | $11.9bn |
| median \|Δ securities\| ÷ \|Δ headline\| | 0.271 | **0.121** | 0.252 | 0.199 |

Q2 versus the pooled other three on |Δ debt securities| gives a permutation **p=0.667** — no
concentration at all — and the securities **share** of the Q2 revision is the **lowest of the four
quarters**. The quarter that moves most is the quarter where the securities component explains least.

**Where the mass actually is.** Decomposing every revision by sector (residual-derived deposit-taking
corporations; see Method):

| Sector | Q1 | **Q2** | Q3 | Q4 | Q2 vs rest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| General Government | $39.9bn | $41.5bn | $26.5bn | $23.5bn | p=0.230 |
| Central Bank | $0.0bn | $0.0bn | $0.0bn | $0.0bn | — |
| Deposit-taking corporations | $16.7bn | $39.2bn | $39.9bn | $13.6bn | p=0.052 |
| **Other Sectors** | $104.8bn | **$335.6bn** | $298.4bn | $114.0bn | **p=0.105** |
| DI intercompany lending | $41.4bn | $37.3bn | $31.6bn | $31.2bn | p=0.419 |

**Other Sectors carries the mass** — mean |Δ| **$335.6bn** in Q2 against **$167.8bn** in the other
three quarters, an order of magnitude larger than any other sector's gap, on a permutation p of
**0.105**. Deposit-taking corporations has the tighter p (**0.052**) on a magnitude **eight times
smaller**, which is a real but minor effect. **General Government** — where the survey's restatement
of foreign Treasury holdings would land — is **$41.5bn vs $30.7bn, p=0.230**: flat.

So the honest statement, and it is narrower and more specific than the sibling's: **the June edition's
size is set by non-bank loans, deposits and trade credit, and its Q2 concentration is set by the same
thing.** The survey is folded in, is not the driver of the size (the sibling's leg 3), and is not the
driver of the timing either (this leg). Two different tests, two refutations, one conclusion.

**What that means for this print.** The number published on 2026-09-30 is the first vintage of an
as-of Q2 quarter. On the archive's own distribution it is the vintage most likely to be materially
restated — median **1.436%** of level, ≈ **$435bn** on a $30.3tn base — nine months later, on
**2027-06-30**, by a mechanism that has nothing to do with the securities survey whose reference date
it shares. That is the whole reason this "control observation" deserved a ledger.

### Leg 4 — what the print will actually say · **SUPPORTED**, and a number is registered

The 2026-09-30 release publishes the position **as-of 2026-06-30** (one-quarter lag). The last
published first vintage is **$30.196961tn** as-of 2026-03-31, released 2026-06-30, read from
`debta2026q1.html`.

**The estimator choice is forced by a seasonality the sibling did not need to measure.** At a
four-quarter horizon YoY is obviously right; at one quarter the natural move is QoQ, and QoQ is
seasonal:

| As-of quarter | n | mean QoQ | sd | median |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | 17 | **+1.83%** | 1.33 | +1.86% |
| **Q2** | 17 | **+0.81%** | 1.51 | +0.66% |
| Q3 | 16 | +0.99% | 1.55 | +1.28% |
| Q4 | 16 | +1.27% | 1.76 | +0.69% |

Q1 against Q2 is a **1.02pp** gap on a permutation **p=0.023**. A pooled QoQ estimator applied to a Q2
print is therefore biased high by about a point. **YoY anchored to the same quarter a year earlier
nets the seasonal out by construction**, which is the reason the sibling's estimator is the right one
here too — and this session measures the reason rather than assuming it.

Clean YoY of first vintages, **2023Q1–2026Q1**, excluding the two observations that are the same
anomaly seen twice (2024Q3's bad first vintage at **+0.58%**, and 2025Q3's comparison against that
depressed base at **+12.91%**): **n=11, mean +6.00%, sd 0.97pp, range +4.27% to +7.47%, no trend.**

```
base (as-of 2025Q2 first vintage, published 2025-09-30) = $28.604291tn
forecast = $28.604tn × 1.0600 = $30.32tn
±2σ on the clean sample      = $29.77tn – $30.87tn
registered band, widened for estimator choice = $29.7tn – $31.0tn
```

**Two cross-checks.** A Q2-only QoQ estimator on the post-2021 sub-sample (**n=5, +1.67%**) gives
**$30.70tn** — inside the band, near its upper half. And against the seventeen Q2 QoQ observations in
the record, the registered band corresponds to **−1.65% to +2.66%**, which **2 of 17** historical Q2
prints would have breached (2011Q2 at −3.69%, 2021Q2 at +3.82%). ~88% historical coverage, stated
rather than implied.

**The composition to expect.** As-of 2026Q1 the position splits **General Government $10.584tn**,
**Central Bank $1.099tn**, **Deposit-taking corporations $4.458tn**, **Other Sectors $12.246tn**,
**DI intercompany lending $1.810tn**, memorandum *Debt Securities: By Sector* **$14.438tn**.

### Honest limits

- **Nine vintages carry no publication stamp** (`deb2a2014q3`, `deb2a2017q4`, `deb2a2019q3`,

  They are excluded from every statistic rather than dated by inference, which is why this census
  counts 52 revisions where the sibling counts 66. The month attribution is 100% June in both.
- **The Q2-concentration panel is n=14 per quarter**, one observation per June edition. p=0.038 and
  p=0.011 are honest for that n and are not large-sample results; a single future edition can move
  them materially. That is what **FT-…-3** is for.
- **Leg 3 identifies a sector, not a cause.** Treasury publishes no revision-source attribution, so
  "Other Sectors carries the Q2 concentration" is a decomposition of *where* the restatement lands,
  not evidence of *why* firms' reported loans and deposits move most at a June reference date. The
  measurement that would settle it needs the underlying BC/BQ banking-form panel at two vintages and
  is not attempted here.
- **The sector decomposition rests on a parse.** The memorandum block repeats every sector label, and
  a naive regex silently returns the securities memo line instead of the sector total. The truncation
  fix is validated by the residual identity reproducing the parsed deposit-taking figure exactly on

- **n=14 on the September tape** is the entire published history of this edition and is still small.

- **The band's coverage is 15 of 17, not 19 of 20.** ±2σ on n=11 is a normal-theory interval on a

  otherwise.
