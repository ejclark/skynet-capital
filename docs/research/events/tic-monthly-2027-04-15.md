### Leg 2 — "the clean-axis test" · **REFUTED**, and the refutation needs no new data

The proposal's stated job for this event, verbatim:

> *"it is the CLEAN-AXIS test for FT-tic-monthly-2027-01-19-3's revision-edition effect… April is a
> Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct REVISION edition under Treasury's note (c)… and unlike every prior candidate it is NOT
> entangled with the disputed Feb/Mar bucket."*

The second sentence is true. The first does not follow from it, and the January sibling's own leg 3 already
contains the counter-example. It prints the test **twice**:

| Reading | Revision-edition mean | Ordinary mean | n | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Feb/Mar **excluded** (registered) | **$5.01bn** | **$1.67bn** | 6 vs 10 | **<0.001** |
| Feb/Mar **left in** | **$5.01bn** | **$4.46bn** | 6 vs 13 | **0.78** |

**The treatment mean is identical across both readings, and it could not be otherwise.** Note (c) makes the
revision editions **January, April, July and October**. February and March are therefore *never* in the
treatment class, so excluding Feb/Mar vintages can only remove **control**-class observations. That is
exactly what the table shows: the revision cell is unmoved at $5.01bn, the ordinary cell falls from $4.46bn
to $1.67bn, and the p-value's flip from 0.78 to <0.001 is **entirely a control-side effect**.

**Consequence.** A seventh revision-edition observation lands on the axis whose estimate was never in
dispute. It cannot discriminate between the two readings, because both readings agree about it. The event's
proposed job is **not performable by this event**, and calling it "the clean-axis test" inverts which axis
is dirty.

**What settles it instead — and it is already registered, so this ledger declines to duplicate it.** The
question is whether the three Feb/Mar outliers (2024-02: **+$515.0bn** and **−$745.9bn** on closed 2021/2022
totals; 2025-03: **$127.9bn**; 2026-03: **$68.5bn**) are a distinct documented mechanism or just the panel's
right tail. Answering it requires **ordinary**-class observations. `FT-tic-monthly-2027-03-18-2` already
watches the Jun/Sep/Dec editions against the $4.46bn Feb/Mar-inclusive mean, which is the correct axis. This
session's contribution is to propose a **purer** control than those: `tic-monthly-2027-05-18` is neither a
note (c) revision month, nor a quarterly-cut month (the quarterly columns are filled in Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec, so
the March sibling's three observations each carry an extra cut as a confound), nor a Feb/Mar vintage.

**What April CAN uniquely answer, and it is registered.** The January sibling's panel is **missing an
April** — its own limits section records that *"the 2025-04 press release returned HTTP 200 with no notice
PDF attached, costing the panel one April revision edition."* And it states, on n=2, that *"the big
restatement lands in October, not January."* So the treatment class may be **month-heterogeneous**, and
April's position inside it is unmeasured. That is an April-specific question, and it matters more than the
exclusion dispute: if April restates like an ordinary edition, "revision edition" is not one thing and the
whole treatment class needs re-cutting. Registered two-sided as **FT-tic-monthly-2027-04-15-3**.

### Leg 3 — the pairing claim is inverted · **REFUTED**, and it is the third misread of this table

The proposal's second claim:

> *"It is also the edition standing eleven business days (fifteen calendar days) before the FINAL annual
> survey (2027-04-30…), which is the pairing note (d) describes for the post-2011 regime — so the survey's
> benchmark reaching the monthly series through a revision edition is observable here rather than argued."*

**Two errors, one sentence.**

**(i) The sequence runs the wrong way.** 2027-04-15 stands **before** 2027-04-30. A survey published fifteen
days *after* a monthly release cannot have fed that release. The first revision edition *after* the final
survey is **2027-07-16**. The survey this edition could plausibly carry is the **preliminary** one
(2027-02-26, already tracked as `tic-annual-survey-prelim-2027-02-26`), **48 days earlier**.

**(ii) The attribution is to the wrong regime.** Note (d), verbatim from the page this session fetched:

> *"Beginning December 2011, the data are based on the monthly Form SLT data, and revised as described in
> (c) above. **Before September 2011**, revised data back to the previous June was usually released in
> **March** to include preliminary data from the annual survey… The **May** release usually included the
> final results from that survey."*

The March/May pairing is explicitly the **pre-September-2011** convention — the same sentence
[`tic-monthly-2027-02-18`](tic-monthly-2027-02-18.md) used to *retire* the annual-survey mechanism. Note (d)
describes no monthly/survey pairing at all for the post-2011 regime; it routes revisions through note (c)'s
Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct schedule. So "the pairing note (d) describes for the post-2011 regime" names something the
note does not contain.

**And mapping the old convention forward makes the point cleanly rather than only negatively.** Under the
pre-2011 pattern the prelim survey fed the *next* release and the final survey fed a later one. Mapped onto
the current revision calendar: prelim **2027-02-26** → next revision edition **2027-04-15**; final
**2027-04-30** → next revision edition **2027-07-16**. April is the **preliminary** survey's analogue, never
the final's. If the survey-feed mechanism had survived — and note (d) says it did not — this edition is
where the *preliminary* benchmark would show, and 2027-07-16 is where the final one would.

**Named as a pattern, because it is now three for three.** The February sibling caught its own proposal
treating the preliminary survey column as part of the monthly row. The March sibling caught its own proposal
treating the gross-external-debt column as a third cut of the same release, and wrote that a future session
*"should read the column header before the row."* This one read the column header correctly and then got
the **order** of two separate releases backwards. The failure mode has generalised: it is not
column-reading, it is **treating anything printed in this table's April row as belonging to the April
monthly release**. A fourth session should check the *date* as well as the column.
