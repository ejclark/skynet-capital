## Initial research

**The question.** What lands on 2026-10-15, does it touch any name in this book, and does the "GPFG holds ~1.5% of all listed equities so an ethics change is a change to its participation in every name we track" framing the entry was filed under survive contact with the primaries?

**One-line verdict:** it is an advisory committee's NOU, delivered into a process whose market-facing mechanism has *already been suspended* since 2025-11-07, whose one measurable US large-cap precedent moved a stock by a fifth-percentile-quiet −0.33%, and whose own mandate pushes toward excluding **fewer** large technology companies — so: **stand aside, and hold the entry as calendar context rather than as a dated risk.**

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md) (geopolitical mode). Three primaries carry the document, all fetched direct on 2026-09-06 and all HTTP 200: the committee's own site **nettsteder.regjeringen.no/uerspu/** (front page, `/mandat/`, `/medlemmer/`, `/se-innspill/`), **nbim.no**'s observation-and-exclusion page, and **norges-bank.no**'s letter of 2025-08-18 to the Ministry. ### Leg 1 — what is the body, and who chairs it? → **MIXED (and the calendar entry was wrong)**

The committee is **"Utvalg som skal vurdere det etiske rammeverket for Statens pensjonsfond utland"**, set up by the government on **7. november 2025**, mandated to "vurdere og foreslå eventuelle endringer i det etiske rammeverket og relevante deler av den ansvarlige forvaltningen av pensjonsfondet."

The calendar entry recorded its chair as **Aslak Skancke**. That is a misreading of the site: Skancke is listed as its **ansvarlig redaktør** (responsible editor, contact `postmottak@etikkradet.no` — the Council on Ethics secretariat), a website role that appears in the footer of every page. The `/medlemmer/` page names the actual **utvalgsleder as Svein Gjedrem** — former Norges Bank governor and former permanent secretary of the Ministry of Finance — with eight members: Annie Bersagel, Alexander Wright Cappelen, Marius Emberland, Gunhild Hoogensen Gjørv, Hanne Eggen Røislien, Arthur Sletteberg, Ulf Sverdrup and **Karen Helene Ulltveit-Moe**. This PR corrects the entry.

Two of those names matter for how this calendar reads the chain. **Ulltveit-Moe also chairs the Ekspertrådet**, the standing council behind `norway-gpfg-bond-expert-group-2027-01-25` — the two GPFG nodes this calendar tracks share a member. **Sverdrup** chaired NOU 2022:12 *Fondet i en brytningstid*, which the mandate quotes. **MIXED** because the entry's substance (dated, primary-sourced, part of the GPFG governance chain) holds while its named chair did not.

### Leg 2 — is the exclusion mechanism live between now and the report? → **REFUTED. It is switched off.**

This is the finding that reframes the entry, and it is primary on both sides.

The mandate states it plainly (nettsteder.regjeringen.no/uerspu/mandat/): the guidelines "er i lys av Stortingets vedtak 4. november 2025 opphevet og erstattet av midlertidige retningslinjer i påvente av at det etiske rammeverket for fondet gjennomgås." Under the interim regime the Council on Ethics keeps monitoring but "skal ikke sende tilrådinger om observasjon og utelukkelse til Norges Bank i denne perioden" — it may only inform the Bank of companies for possible ownership engagement. Both bodies were told by letter of **7. november 2025**.

NBIM states the other half (nbim.no, fetched direct): "Under the temporary ethical guidelines, Norges Bank shall not decide on observation or exclusion, **but may revoke previous decisions on observation and exclusion**."

**And the revocations are actually happening.** The same page records its list "last updated on 11 August 2026," in which four names were **removed** — Evergreen Marine Corp Taiwan, Korea Line Corp, Pan Ocean Co Ltd and Bombardier Inc — the first two exclusions revoked and the last two observations ended during H1 2026. So for the whole window between now and any new framework, the mechanism runs **one way, inward**. The list as parsed this session holds **183 exclusion rows and 18 observation rows** (the mandate's own count is "180 selskaper utelukket"; the small gap is a parse of a live page against a text written months earlier, and is stated rather than reconciled), of which **76** are the coal criterion and **23** the war-and-conflict criterion.

The practical consequence for this book: between 2026-09-06 and the adoption of a new framework, **no company can be newly excluded from the GPFG**. The channel the entry was filed to watch does not exist right now.

### Leg 3 — what does a GPFG exclusion actually do to a US large-cap? → **REFUTED as a tradeable event**

The exclusion list carries exactly one clean US large-cap case, and it is recent: **Caterpillar Inc.**, conduct-based, "serious violations of individuals' rights in situations of war or conflict," publishing date **25.08.2025** — part of a six-name batch with five Israeli banks (Bank Hapoalim, Bank Leumi, FIBI Holdings, First International Bank of Israel, Mizrahi Tefahot).

The **−0.33%** publication day ranks at the **20th percentile** — quieter than four days in five of the preceding quarter. The 5-session **−3.4%** is left **unattributed**: a single −3.65% session on 2025-08-29 supplies nearly all of it, and this session found no basis to pin that on a Norwegian exclusion four days earlier rather than on the macro tape. Everything past 20 sessions runs the other way, and CAT has since roughly doubled while still excluded.

**Why the null is structural, not luck.** Norges Bank's letter of **18 August 2025** states the practice verbatim: the Executive Board "decided on 8 August 2025 to exclude six companies," and "the names of these companies, and the recommendations from the Council on Ethics, will, in line with usual practice, **be published once the divestment is completed**." Decision **2025-08-08** → publication **2025-08-25**. The selling is finished, silently, before the market learns which name it was. The same lag is visible on the live list, which describes decisions taken "as at November 2025" yet carries publishing dates as late as **26.02.2026**. An announcement that post-dates its own flow cannot be an entry signal — and this is a *general* property of the mechanism, not a property of Caterpillar.

**Honest limit: n = 1.** The five Israeli banks in that batch are not comparable US large-caps, and no other US mega-cap has been excluded on a conduct criterion in the window this repo can measure. One clean observation supports "the announcement is not tradeable"; it cannot support a distributional claim.

### Leg 4 — does the report threaten any name in this book? → **REFUTED, and the mandate argues the opposite direction**

The book's tracked names across this calendar are **AAPL, AMZN, MSFT, META, AVGO, MRVL, CRWV**. None appears on the exclusion or observation list (an "Alphabet" string match on the page is the phrase "alphabetical overview," not a row). The abstract channel is real — the mandate itself puts the fund at "om lag 1,5 prosent av nærmere 8 500 selskaper i opp mot 70 land," so it holds all of them — but the mandate's framing points at **narrowing** the criteria, not widening them:

- Verbatim on the dilemma: "Store teknologiselskaper som leverer produkter som er avgjørende for en lang rekke sivile formål, som dataprogrammer og tjenester de fleste bruker hver dag, leverer også produkter som brukes i våpensystemer. Det kan gjøre det mer krevende å trekke en klar grense for de atferdsbaserte kriteriene."
- Verbatim on the consequence: "Dersom fondet ikke kan være investert i verdens største selskaper, blir det vanskeligere å forbli et globalt indeksfond. Det vil gjøre bytteforholdet mellom forventet avkastning og risiko dårligere."
- The Storting's own **2025-11-04** vedtak asked the government to keep the fund "et bredt diversifisert, globalt fond."
- The committee is asked to consider capping the scope of exclusions, giving ownership engagement more weight, distinguishing purpose-built from general-purpose goods, weighing what **share** of a company's business is implicated, and whether a company being domiciled in a Norwegian security partner should count.

Every one of those levers, if pulled, makes a mega-cap technology exclusion **less** likely, not more. The naive read of this event — "an ethics review is a divestment risk for our names" — is the wrong sign.

### Leg 5 — can anything be in force inside 2026 or H1 2027? → **REFUTED**

The mandate closes: "Utvalget skal følge **utredningsinstruksen**" and "skal levere sin **utredning** innen 15. oktober 2026." That makes the deliverable an NOU, which goes to public consultation before the Ministry takes a position.

The closest precedent is the previous review of this exact framework, dated end to end:

- Mestad-utvalget appointed by royal resolution **2019-04-05**.
- **NOU 2020: 7 *Verdier og ansvar*** delivered to the Ministry **2020-06-15**.
- Høring deadline **2020-10-20** — **~4 months** after delivery.
- **Meld. St. 24 (2020–2021)** presented **2021-04-09** — **~10 months** after delivery.
- Guidelines actually revised during **2021**, per the mandate's own account: "Retningslinjene er senere blitt justert, senest i 2021 med utgangspunkt i Mestad-utvalgets vurderinger i NOU 2020: 7."

Applied from **2026-10-15**: a høring closing around **February 2027**, and a white paper in **spring 2027** only if the Ministry moves *faster than it did after Mestad* (5–6 months versus 10). The realistic centre is the **spring 2028** paper, with revised guidelines in force after that. The one force pushing the other way is that the interim suspension is itself live and politically uncomfortable — Norway's finance minister was publicly discussing the transparency dilemma as recently as **2026-05-12** — but pressure is not a date, and this document does not convert it into one.

### Leg 6 — what residual risk is real? → **UNRESOLVED, and recorded as such**

Not exclusion flow: **the publication regime itself**. The mandate records verbatim that GPFG's exclusion decisions and the Council's recommendations "er blitt fulgt av mange andre investorer og fått bred internasjonal oppmerksomhet," and it explicitly asks the committee to "vurdere om offentliggjøringen av beslutninger og tilrådinger bør endres." A recommendation to publish less would remove a public signal that other investors demonstrably follow — a change to market *information*, not to GPFG's own flow.

This session could not size that channel, and the direction is genuinely two-sided (less publication also means less coordinated selling by followers). It is left open with **no trigger attached** rather than converted into a plausible-sounding falsifier.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (event is `estimate`-dated; no date-keyed action licensed, and none proposed): stand aside through the report, and hold this entry as calendar context rather than as a dated risk.** Four findings carry it. The **mechanism is already suspended** — following the Storting's vedtak of **2025-11-04** and the Ministry's letter of **2025-11-07**, no company can be newly excluded from the GPFG until a new framework is adopted, and Norges Bank may only *revoke* (four names came off the list on **2026-08-11**). The **base rate is null and structural** — Caterpillar's exclusion published **2025-08-25** produced a **20th-percentile** day (−0.33% excess vs SPY) and +23.8% excess over the next 60 sessions, because Norges Bank publishes a name only "once the divestment is completed" (letter of **2025-08-18**), so the flow is finished before the print. The **mandate points at narrowing** — it warns that excluding the world's largest companies would break the fund's global-index character and singles out large technology companies' dual-use software as the hardest line to draw, so the sign of any effect on this book's names is *inward*, not outward. And the **clock is long** — the Mestad precedent runs delivery **2020-06-15** → høring **2020-10-20** → **Meld. St. 24 (2020–2021)** on **2021-04-09** → guidelines revised in 2021, so from 2026-10-15 nothing is in force before H2 2027 at the very earliest and realistically not before 2028. The residual risk is the **publication regime**, and it is left explicitly undated and two-sided rather than given a manufactured trigger.

**Kill switches — any one of these and this stance is re-opened, not merely annotated:**

1. **Norges Bank publishes a *new* observation or exclusion** (not a revocation) while the interim guidelines are in force. Leg 2 — "the mechanism is switched off" — fails outright, and this entry becomes a live risk rather than context.
2. **Norway's Ministry of Finance reinstates or replaces the observation/exclusion guidelines before 2026-10-15**, i.e. acts ahead of its own commissioned report. Legs 2 and 5 both fail at once.
3. **The 2026-10-15 delivery moves, is pre-released, or is cancelled** (primary-sourced). The entry's date is its only content.
4. **Revised `retningslinjer for observasjon og utelukkelse` are in force before 2027-06-30** — faster than every dated precedent in Leg 5, which would mean the interim suspension bought speed the Mestad clock says is not available.
5. **Any name currently on the exclusion list, or any tracked name credibly reported as a candidate, shows a same-session or next-session |excess vs SPY| above its own trailing-60 p90 attributable to a GPFG ethics decision.** Leg 3's "the announcement is not the trade" claim dies on that observation.
6. **The committee's report recommends a *quantitative expansion* of the behaviour criteria to general-purpose technology suppliers** — the opposite of the direction Leg 4 reads out of the mandate — which would make the spring-2027/2028 white paper a live fork for this book's largest names rather than a formality.
