## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Five 2027 FOMC ledgers already exist, and between them they have named this meeting three times
without researching it. [`fomc-2027-01-27`](fomc-2027-01-27.md) established the roster;
[`fomc-2027-03-17`](fomc-2027-03-17.md) deployed a corridor rule around triple witching;

dots can and cannot do; [`fomc-2027-06-09`](fomc-2027-06-09.md) found that 2027's June meeting is
eight days earlier than its 2026 counterpart where every other meeting is one day earlier, withdrew
the March corridor rule, and tabulated a monthly decision-day base rate in which **July is an
unremarkable middle cell at 0.70%**. And [`consumer-confidence-2027-07-27`](consumer-confidence-2027-07-27.md),
which proposed this entry, established that the July Consumer Confidence print lands on **FOMC day
one** — mechanically, every year — and that the measured Fed effect lives on the *decision* day.

So this document's question is narrow and inherited: **the June sibling's eight-day anomaly has a
downstream half nobody followed, and its monthly table has a cell nobody opened. Does either one
carry anything?**

**One-line verdict.** Both do, and the second one is the deliverable: **July's 0.70% mean describes
no July that has ever happened.** Seven of thirteen July decision days moved under 0.15%, five moved
past 1.0%, one landed in between — the most bimodal month in the record, significant threshold-free
at p = 0.001, and *not* explained by whether the Fed changed rates. The calendar half is exact and
structural: 2027 and 2021 are the same calendar year, and **2027-07-28 is the only 2027 meeting
sitting between two seven-day-displaced neighbours**, which leaves it flanked by 49 days on each
side — the midpoint of the year's longest decision-free span. No rate call is made at D-325 and none
is implied.
