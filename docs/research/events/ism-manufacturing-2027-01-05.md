## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This is the sixth ISM Manufacturing ledger and it was created two days ago by the [services
sibling](ism-services-2027-01-07.md)'s adjacency sweep, which discovered that this calendar had
recorded ISM's manufacturing cadence **without its January exception**. That sibling also did
something larger: it ran a placebo the whole ISM chain had never run, concluded the release-day
bond-magnitude effect was a comparator artifact, and — in this event's own `notes` field — extended
that verdict to manufacturing sight-unseen: *"the manufacturing chain's own numbers were computed
against the same wrong comparator."*

So the question is not "what will the December-2026 print be." It is: **does the manufacturing
release survive the placebo the services release failed — and if it does, is there any way to tell a
release effect from a calendar effect?**

**One-line verdict.** It survives, and then it dies of something better. Against a *decontaminated*
early-month control the manufacturing release-day bond effect is nominally real (TLT p = 0.038,
`^TNX` p = 0.009) where services' is not — but January moves the release off the 1st trading day
onto the 2nd, and when it moves it **leaves the effect behind**. What is real is the trading-day
slot, not the survey, and this specific date is the only clean out-of-sample test of that anywhere
on the calendar.

### Honest limits

**Every January cell in leg 4 is n=11 sessions (n=22 pooled across two bond instruments)**, and
p = 0.505 / p = 1.000 mean "no evidence", never "no effect" — a release effect the size of the pooled
gap would be invisible at that power, and the argument rests on two independent contrasts agreeing in
direction rather than on either one's significance. **The natural experiment is still an observational
comparison of calendar sets** and cannot establish causation in either direction. **The clean control
is my construct, not a standard** — excluding both ISM days from the early-month pool is the right fix
for the contamination in leg 2, but it also removes the two highest cells from the comparator, which
mechanically widens every gap measured against it; the pre- and post-decontamination figures are both
reported for exactly that reason. **My pooled figures do not exactly replicate either sibling's** —
this session measures TLT 37.9% / SPY 25.0% on n=132 where the [12-01
sibling](ism-manufacturing-2026-12-01.md) reported 40.7% / 23.7% on a stated n=118 and the [02-01
sibling](ism-manufacturing-2027-02-01.md) re-derived 37.9% / 24.2% on n=132; this run agrees with 02-01
exactly and the n=118 discrepancy is still unreconciled. **The percentile technique measures magnitude
within a year and is blind to direction and to causation.** **Implied volatility is measured nowhere in
this document**, so nothing here speaks to realized-versus-implied — the only comparison that could
ever make a magnitude finding tradeable. **Leg 6's mechanism is deliberately unnamed**; three plausible
candidates are listed and none is sourced. **Leg 1's validation rests on five datelines, not twelve** —
seven derived January dates carry no primary evidence, and one of the five (2023-01-04) is a Cision
syndication datestamp rather than an ISM dateline. **Leg 12's geometry is a statement about the
checked-in calendar and a projected NYSE calendar**, not the world: this event, `fomc-2027-01-27` and
`fomc-blackout-start-2027-01-16` are all `estimate`-dated, and "six tracked events within ±5 days"
measures this repo's coverage, not the world's. **The first-trading-day 10:00 ET slot is shared** with
Census Construction Spending and the S&P Global US manufacturing final PMI, an attribution problem the
12-01 sibling recorded and this session did not re-attempt — and it is a *second* reason a TD1 effect
need not be ISM's. **The whole document assumes ISM publishes on schedule**, and that a 2026-12-01
report exists to name this date.
