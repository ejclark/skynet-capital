## Initial research

**The question.** The December FOMC is the year's last SEP/dot-plot meeting, landing the afternoon
before the November CPI ([`cpi-2026-12-10`](../../../src/domain/market-events.ts) in the calendar).
What will the Committee do, what is the market priced for, and how does the tape actually react to
this Committee's surprises — so that when the window approaches, the stance is about *reaction*, not
guessing the print?

**One-line verdict.** Base case is a **hold at 3.50–3.75%** (date confirmed; pricing is an
estimate), but the live tail is a **hike, not a cut** — under Chair Warsh the dots flipped hawkish in
June, and every 2026 surprise in that direction has been sold hard by long-duration tech, which is
our entire tracked roster.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md) macro-print
mode: primary sources (federalreserve.gov, kansascityfed.org, bls.gov) over aggregators, each with a
check date; consensus from CME FedWatch-derived snapshots and prediction markets, cross-checked and
labeled estimates; reaction function reconstructed from the last three 2026 macro prints (June FOMC,
July FOMC, July-CPI release) with same-day index/yield moves.

### Conviction legs tested

**Leg 1 — the date and the SEP are real.** **SUPPORTED.** Re-checked the primary this session:
federalreserve.gov's 2026 FOMC calendar lists December 8–9 with the projections asterisk (SEP + dot
plot), statement 14:00 ET Dec 9 (checked 2026-08-17, matching the calendar entry's 2026-08-15
check). This is the fourth and final SEP meeting of 2026 (Mar, Jun, Sep, Dec).

**Leg 2 — consensus is a December hold.** **MIXED.** The FedWatch-derived snapshot (aggregator,
checked 2026-08-17) prices ~78% no-change through December, ~15% for a 25bp cut, ~5% for a hike —
but Polymarket prices a **67.5% chance of at least one 2026 hike** (early-Aug snapshot), and
J.P. Morgan's house call is a **December hike** on reaccelerating inflation. The June SEP itself
leans hawkish: median 2026 dot moved **3.4% → 3.8%**, nine of 18 participants penciling in at least
one hike, only one projecting a cut. Read: hold is the modal path, but the meeting-level aggregator
and the year-level prediction market disagree enough that "consensus" is genuinely unsettled — that
spread *is* the whisper, and it points hawkish. All pricing figures are estimates as of 2026-08-17.

**Leg 3 — the reaction function is asymmetric-hawkish.** **SUPPORTED.** The last three prints:
- **2026-06-17 FOMC** (hold, dots flip hawkish, Warsh's first meeting): Nasdaq **−1.34%**, S&P
  −1.21%, 2y yield +16bp — the dot plot, not the decision, drove the move (CNBC/TheStreet,
  2026-06-17).
- **2026-07-29 FOMC** (hold, **three** hawkish dissents — Hammack, Kashkari, Logan — first
  three-way directional dissent since 2016): S&P −0.6%, Dow −1.6%, 10y +5bp, 30y +9bp
  (CNBC/federalreserve.gov statement, 2026-07-29).
- **2026-08-12 CPI** (July data, in-line: headline +0.1% m/m / 3.4% y/y, core +0.2%): futures up,
  yields down across the curve, September hike odds cut to ~42% (CNBC/BLS, 2026-08-12).

Pattern: hawkish signal → sold, in-line/cool inflation → relief. There is no 2026 observation of a
dovish *surprise* to calibrate against — the upside case is inferred, not measured.

**Leg 4 — "a December cut is the base case."** **REFUTED.** One dot out of 18 projected a 2026 cut
in June; cut odds sit ~15% on the aggregator snapshot (estimate, 2026-08-17); the July statement
held with the dissent risk pointing the other way. Anyone still framing December as a cut meeting is
trading a stale H1 narrative.

**Leg 5 — sensitivity ranking of the tracked names.** Directionally supported by mechanism and by
the June/July tape (high-multiple tech led both drawdowns):
1. **CRWV** — most sensitive: debt-financed AI-datacenter buildout makes cost of capital a direct
   P&L line, on top of the longest-duration multiple in the roster.
2. **NVDA / AVGO / MRVL** — high-beta long-duration semis; also second-order exposed via customers'
   financing costs for AI capex.
3. **MSFT / GOOG / META / AMZN** — cash-rich, less rate-sensitive as businesses, but they *are* the
   index, so a hawkish-dot day hits them mechanically (Nasdaq −1.34% on 6/17 was mostly them).
4. **AAPL** — least AI-capex levered; still moves with the index on macro days.
