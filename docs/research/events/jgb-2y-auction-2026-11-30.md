## Initial research

### The question, plainly

`jgb-2y-auction-2026-10-29` proposed this event on 2026-09-09 for one stated reason: it is *"the matched
control"* for that ledger's headline finding — that a 2-Year auction clearing on **day one of a live BoJ
MPM** prints thinner cover than its peers (n = 3, exact permutation p = 0.029, filed MIXED). The proposal
is explicit about the design: *"A treatment that reads thin and a control that reads normal is evidence;
both reading thin is a regime and kills the hypothesis."*

So the question this ledger has to answer is not "what will this auction do." It is: **is this event
actually the control it was created to be, and what will it be able to tell anyone?**

**One-line verdict: it is a real control and a nearly powerless one, and the design as written would
false-kill a live hypothesis one time in three.** The honest deliverable is therefore a *reading rule with
its error rates attached*, not an adjudication. Along the way two inherited facts turned out to be wrong
in ways that matter more than the test does — the announcement date is a market holiday, and the offering
size has been a published plan number all along.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. It is not a month-matched control — REFUTED as labelled, and the label is the proposal's, not a
misreading.** The treatment (No.490) is an October auction; this is a November one. "Month-matched" in the
10-29 ledger means something specific and load-bearing — its **leg 4** killed a full-sample allotment
signal (p = 0.043) precisely by restricting to the same calendar months (p = 0.40), concluding *"it was
the month, not the meeting."* A November print does not do that job.

**2. But on cover, the month effect it would confound is not detectable — SUPPORTED, and it partly
rescues the design.** Permutation over the between-month sum of squares across all 12 months, 44 auctions,
200k draws: **p = 0.63**. November's own three prints run **3.357** (2.911 / 3.630 / 3.531) against
**3.801** for the other 41 — one-sided p = 0.087, which is a **post-hoc pick out of twelve months** and
therefore nothing. So the mislabel costs little *on cover*, the metric the day-one test actually uses; it
would have been fatal on allotment. The residual risk is real but small and it runs one way: November's
three prints are on the low side, which is exactly the direction that would manufacture a false "regime."

**3. The control's decision rule false-kills 34% of the time — SUPPORTED, and it is the finding.** Take
the 41 non-day-one auctions as the distribution of an ordinary print (mean **3.788**, sd **0.600**, range
2.814–5.542). The proposal's rule reads a thin control as "regime → hypothesis dead." But:

| Band | Share of the 41 non-day-one prints | What it actually means |
|---|---|---|
| cover > 3.788 | **41.5%** | ordinary |
| 3.540 – 3.788 | **24.4%** | ordinary |
| 3.164 – 3.540 | **22.0%** | **looks thin, is not — the false-kill band** |
| ≤ 3.164 (p10) | **12.2%** | genuinely unusual |

**14 of 41 (34.1%) already sit at or below the day-one mean of 3.540.** A perfectly ordinary November
auction has better than a one-in-three chance of triggering the kill by chance alone. A single
observation against sd 0.600 has roughly **26%** power against the claimed 0.93-cover-unit effect at
α = 0.05 one-sided; the rule is being asked to decide something one print cannot decide.

**4. And the regime it is controlling for is not visible in the data — SUPPORTED.** The control exists to
answer "is late-2026 just a thin tape?" On 44 prints there is nothing to find. Trend in cover: slope
**0.0015** per auction (**0.018/yr**), two-sided permutation **p = 0.83**. Recent windows against their
priors: last 3 **+0.034**, last 6 **+0.243**, last 9 **+0.056**, last 12 **−0.025**. The 2026 calendar
year runs **3.885** against **3.746** pre-2026 — *above*, not below. Cover in this series is a stationary
noisy quantity, so the alternative hypothesis the control is built to test is one nobody has evidence for.

**5. The multiplicity ceiling settles the whole line of work — SUPPORTED, and it is the most durable
thing here.** The day-one test is a permutation over the July/October cohort. Today that is C(7,3) = 35,
so **p = 0.029 is the smallest value obtainable**. After 2026-10-29 the cohort is n = 8 with 4 treated:

| Jul/Oct cohort | Combinations | Best attainable p | Bonferroni over the 6 comparisons already run |
|---|---|---|---|
| n = 7, 3 treated (today) | 35 | 0.0286 | 0.171 |
| **n = 8, 4 treated (after 10-29)** | **70** | **0.0143** | **0.086** |
| n = 9, 5 treated | 126 | 0.0079 | **0.048** |
| n = 10, 5 treated | 252 | 0.0040 | 0.024 |

**Even a perfect 10-29 result cannot clear 0.05 honestly.** The hypothesis needs a *ninth* July/October
auction — one more collision, roughly one a year — before the best possible outcome is significant. That
is 2027 at the earliest and realistically later. This is a multi-year test, and **this November auction
is not in the cohort at all**: it enters no permutation, changes no p-value, and adds n only to the
regime question that leg 4 just showed has no signal in it.

**6. The inherited announcement date is a Tokyo market holiday — SUPPORTED, and it is the one immediately
actionable correction.** Both the proposal and the 10-29 ledger say terms publish "about 2026-11-23."
**2026-11-23 is Labour Thanksgiving Day.** This calendar's own `jpx-market-closure-2026-11-23` carries
JPX's finalized *"Not Open"* row and records that JGB futures and interest-rate futures are ineligible for
holiday trading — Tokyo is dark group-wide. MOF's own November calendar independently agrees by scheduling
nothing on Nov 23 or Nov 24. Parsing the announcement lead for all 44:

| Lead | Count | Which auctions, and why |
|---|---|---|
| **D-7** | **41** | the ordinary case |
| D-8 | 2 | 2023-11-30 (D-7 = **Labour Thanksgiving**) · 2025-09-30 (D-7 = **Autumnal Equinox**) |
| D-10 | 1 | 2024-09-30 (D-7 = **Autumnal Equinox**; D-8 and D-9 were the weekend) |

**All three deviations have exactly one cause, and MOF moved earlier every time — never later.** So the
rule is not "D-7 in 41 of 44" but **44 of 44: the last JGB-market business day on or before D-7**. For
2026-11-30: D-7 = Mon **Nov 23** (holiday), D-8 = Sunday, D-9 = Saturday, D-10 = **Fri Nov 20** — which

### Honest limits

- **The auction has not happened and its terms are not published.** Everything here is prior. The
  announcement — **2026-11-20** on this ledger's own derivation — settles the issue number and the size;
  the result page on 11-30 settles the rest.
- **Leg 6's announcement-date claim is a mechanism inferred from 3 exceptions, not a published MOF rule.**
  MOF states no announcement-lead policy that this session found. The inference is strong (3 of 3
  deviations are holidays, all moving earlier, and 41 of 41 non-holiday cases are D-7) but it is a
  described convention. A D-6 publication on **2026-11-24** would be new behaviour, not a parse error.
- **Leg 2 is a null result at n = 44 and is not proof of no month effect.** "No detectable calendar-month
  effect on cover (p = 0.63)" with ~4 observations per month is weak power, not absence. It is enough to
  say the mislabel is unlikely to be *fatal*; it is not enough to say November is interchangeable with
  October.
- **The 34% false-kill rate is computed against the 41 non-day-one prints as if they were exchangeable.**
  They span four offering regimes and 3.7 years of policy. The regime tests in leg 4 are what justify
  pooling them, and those are themselves null results at modest power.
- **The multiplicity ceiling assumes the six comparisons the sibling reported are the whole search.** If
  more were run and not recorded, the Bonferroni column is optimistic. It is taken from the 10-29 ledger's
  own honest accounting.
- **The FY2026 plan is read from MOF's English "Highlights" PDF, not from the Japanese primary or the
  budget documents**, and the plan is a *plan*: it binds MOF's intent, not its hand. The fiscal-year
  bucketing is verified against one year's published total (FY2025 supplementary) — one check, exact, but
  one.
