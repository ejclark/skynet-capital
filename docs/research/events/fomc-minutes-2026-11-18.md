## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The minutes of the October 27–28 FOMC publish at 14:00 ET on **2026-11-18**, three weeks before the
December 8–9 SEP meeting and ten days before that meeting's communications blackout. Every ledger in
this calendar that has touched minutes has asserted *"read them, do not trade them"* without ever
measuring it. So the question is narrow and testable: **does the minutes channel actually move
anything under this chair, and if not, what is this release for?**

**One-line verdict:** the channel is measurably weak — two releases under Warsh moved the S&P
**−0.28%** and **+0.21%**, neither of them minutes-driven, while one chair speech repriced September
hike odds by ~23 points — so the refusal is now evidenced rather than assumed. The release's value is
**positional**: it is the last Committee-authored document before the December dots, in a November
whose Fed calendar contains no scheduled speeches at all. Date **confirmed**; no directional call.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is FED-primary twice over — flipped `estimate` → `confirmed`.** SUPPORTED. Two
   independent federalreserve.gov pages, both fetched today. The November 2026 calendar page lists
   **"FOMC Minutes Meeting of October 27-28" at 2:00 p.m. on November 18**. The FOMC calendars page
   states the convention verbatim — *"The minutes of regularly scheduled meetings are released three
   weeks after the date of the policy decision"* — and its own 2026 rows show the convention holding
   all year: Jan 27–28 → **Feb 18**, Mar 17–18 → **Apr 8**, Apr 28–29 → **May 20**, Jun 16–17 → **Jul
   8**, Jul 28–29 → **Aug 19**. Oct 28 + 21 days is **Wednesday 2026-11-18**. The published row and
   the published rule agree, so the derivation is the source's, not mine. The calendar entry's
   standing `EST:` note — filed estimate purely because the proposing sweep would have been
   self-confirming — is now closed by an independent session. `FED:` prefix, **confirmed**.

2. **Minutes are the weakest communication channel in this regime — and that is the headline.**
   SUPPORTED, with a named confound. Both 2026 releases under this chair, measured:
   - **2026-07-08** (June minutes — the first Warsh meeting; content was a near-even split, *"many
     participants"* seeing the appropriate rate within-or-below the range against *"many other
     participants"* seeing it above): S&P 500 **−0.28%** to **7,482.71**; the reaction to the release
     itself reported as minimal.
   - **2026-08-19** (July minutes — explicitly hawkish: *"Many participants assessed that policy
     tightening would likely be necessary if inflation did not decline"*, against a 9-3 vote with
     three dissents *for a hike*): S&P **+0.21%** to **7,707.98**, Dow **+0.22%**, Nasdaq **+0.16%**.
     The session's actual driver was Treasury announcing it would at least double debt buybacks ($2B
     → ≥$4B, effective 09-09 through 11-04); government bonds **stabilized** after the pre-release
     selloff.

   The contrast that makes it a finding: explicitly hawkish minutes on **08-19** left September hike
   odds at **~35–36%** through **08-27**; Warsh's Jackson Hole keynote on **08-28** took them to
   **~58–60%** in one session (CNBC/TheStreet/Forbes, 08-28–08-30; Kalshi 48%, Polymarket 49% on the
   same day — the spread across venues is itself a caution). **One chair speech ≈ 23 points; one
   hawkish minutes release ≈ 0.** *Confound, named:* the 08-19 buyback headline cuts in a
   **masking** direction, so this evidence shows the minutes did not *dominate* the tape — it cannot
   prove they did nothing. And n=2.

3. **Staleness is unusually high for this specific batch.** SUPPORTED. The three-week lag is
   structural, but this particular gap is the densest of the year on our own calendar: **midterm
   elections 11-03** (`estimate`), **jobs 11-06**, **CPI 11-10**, **PPI 11-13**, **import/export
   prices 11-17**. By 14:00 ET on **11-18** the Committee's October reasoning has been overwritten by
   a national election result and a complete inflation-plus-labour cycle. The document describes a
   Committee that had seen none of it — which is the ordinary case for minutes, arriving here in an
   unusually severe form.

4. **But it is the last Committee-authored document before the December dots, and November's Fed
   calendar is empty of voices.** SUPPORTED, with a snapshot caveat. federalreserve.gov's November
   2026 calendar, fetched today, lists **no speeches and no Board events at all** — only the minutes
   (11-18), the **Beige Book** (11-25), **SLOOS** (11-02) and routine statistical releases. The
   blackout for the Dec 8–9 meeting begins **2026-11-28**
   ([`fomc-blackout-start-2026-11-28`](fomc-blackout-start-2026-11-28.md), `estimate`). So on today's
   schedule the entire record between this release and the **12-09** statement is: these minutes, a
   Beige Book that is staff-and-district anecdote rather than Committee reasoning, and silence. That
   is why a weak channel still earns a calendar slot. *Caveat, identical to the blackout ledgers':*
   the Board adds speeches on short notice and regional presidents' appearances live on district
   sites, not this page — a snapshot, and the first thing the next pulse re-checks.

5. **What is worth reading in it: the vote split and the dissent language.** SUPPORTED as a reason to
   read, not to trade. The revealed pattern under this chair is a genuinely divided Committee: **July
   was 9-3** to hold at **3.50–3.75%**, with Hammack, Kashkari and Logan dissenting for a 25bp hike —
   the first time since **September 2016** that three dissented in a unified direction, and reported
   as the most dissents against a new chair since **1970**. The June minutes recorded **nine of 19**
   officials seeing at least one 2026 hike as appropriate against nine seeing unchanged-or-lower. With
   forward guidance abolished and the statement cut to **~130 words** from 300+, minutes are close to
   the only structured evidence of where the Committee actually sits. That is also *why* they don't
   move the tape: by publication the split is old news.

6. **The format itself is a live variable — the task forces report by year-end.** MIXED. Warsh
   launched five task forces reviewing Fed processes, communications among them, with changes
   expected **by year-end** (press-reported, checked 2026-08-31); the June minutes already ran **14
   pages**, *"somewhat shorter though not dramatically so"* than typical. The **11-18** release is the
   last minutes of 2026 and therefore the most likely candidate to be the first published under a
   revised convention. Two directions and I cannot pick between them: a condensed release narrows the
   last structured channel immediately before an airtight blackout — which *raises* the 12-09
   statement's news load, exactly as [`fomc-2026-10-28.md`](fomc-2026-10-28.md) already argues for its
   own meeting; an unchanged release says the overhaul landed elsewhere. Either way an observation,
   never a position.

7. **Our exposure is inherited, not direct, and the corridor is benign.** SUPPORTED. `symbols: []`.
   The transmission read carries from [`fomc-2026-10-28.md`](fomc-2026-10-28.md): on this chair a
   hawkish surprise prices into the **front end** (2y +8bp on 08-28) rather than into equity
   volatility (S&P −0.13% to −0.25%; VIX closed **14.43**, a 2026 low). A minutes release is a
   strictly weaker version of that same channel. The tracked corridor around **11-18** is
   correspondingly light: **PPI 11-13** (confirmed), **import/export prices 11-17** (confirmed,
   `low`), **monthly opex 11-20** (confirmed) — and opex, not the minutes, is the window's real
   positioning date.

8. **The branch that would change the reading — but not the call.** MIXED, and explicitly
   conditional. If either **09-16** or **10-28** hikes, these become the minutes of a *hiking*
   Committee, and the question they answer flips from "how split is the hold" to "one-and-done or a
   sequence" — precisely what the December dots will price, which raises their informational value
   materially. It would **not** make them tradable: legs 2 and 5 apply with more force after a hike
   has already been delivered and interpreted for three weeks. Flagged as the first thing to re-check
   after 09-16.

### Honest limits

The base rate is **n=2**, both under this chair — the relevant regime, but two observations. The
08-19 session is confounded by the Treasury buyback headline in a **masking** direction, which is the
weaker failure mode but is still a confound. The speech-versus-minutes comparison sets **one Jackson
Hole keynote** (an annual set piece, the loudest speech on the Fed's calendar) against two routine
releases; the asymmetry is real but this comparator overstates it, and I have no measured base rate
for an ordinary governor speech. Rate-odds figures are press-cited snapshots that drift daily and
disagree across venues (fed funds futures ~58%, Kalshi 48%, Polymarket 49% on 08-28). Leg 4 is a
calendar snapshot. Leg 6 is press-reported with no primary fetched. And the whole document rests on
two meetings — **09-16** and **10-28** — that have not happened; nothing here survives a regime
change at either without re-derivation.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date confirmed):** **read it, do not trade it.** The minutes channel is measurably weak
under this chair (S&P −0.28% on 2026-07-08, +0.21% on 2026-08-19, neither minutes-driven, against ~23
points of hike-odds repricing from a single chair speech on 2026-08-28), and this batch is unusually
stale — three weeks that contain a national election, CPI and PPI. What earns it a calendar slot is
**scarcity, not power**: it is the last Committee-authored document before the 12-08/09 SEP meeting,
in a November whose Board calendar lists no speeches and whose blackout starts **2026-11-28**
(`estimate`, so caution only). No directional call, no size; the positioning date in that window is
**opex 2026-11-20**.

**Kill switches:**

- **Date kill:** federalreserve.gov publishing a minutes date other than **2026-11-18** for the Oct
  27–28 meeting, or that meeting's dates moving. Either breaks leg 1 and returns this entry to
  `estimate`. Score by **2026-11-18**.
- **Channel kill:** the **2026-11-18** release moving December hike/cut odds **≥10pp** on the day, or
  an S&P 500 close-to-close move **≥1.5%** attributable to it. Legs 2 and 5 die together and minutes
  become a repricing event under this chair. Score by **2026-11-19** (registered as **FT-31**).
- **Silence kill:** any FOMC participant's on-record monetary-policy speech appearing on
  federalreserve.gov's calendar for **2026-11-18 → 11-27**. Leg 4's "last Committee voice" framing
  narrows to "one of two", and the scarcity argument weakens. Re-check every pulse.
- **Format kill:** the Fed announcing a change to minutes content or publication convention before
  **2026-11-18**. Leg 6 resolves and this document's informational claim needs re-argument, not a
  patch.
- **Regime kill:** a hike delivered on **2026-09-16** or **2026-10-28**. Leg 8's branch fires; the
  informational value gets re-derived at the next pulse (the tradability call does not move).
- **Relevance kill:** hike odds under ~20% with cut odds still 0% before **2026-11-18** — a Committee
  with nothing live to split over makes the vote-split read uninteresting, and this reverts to the
  routine technical it usually is.

**Forward test registered:** **FT-31** in [`forward-tests.md`](../forward-tests.md) — the channel-kill
above, stated as a prediction with its anchors, its void condition and a **2026-11-19** score-by date.
