## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The [11-12 sibling](treasury-coupon-announcement-2026-11-12.md) asked what is deducible once `sb0590`'s
table runs out. This event is seven days later and inherits that answer, so repeating the question would
be waste. Two things are genuinely different here, and they set the question:

1. **This announcement is much bigger than any of its siblings, and it is invisible.** Nobody writes
   about the month-end block; everybody writes about the refunding. Is that ranking right?
2. **Its legs sit in different columns of the same table.** The 11-12 legs (20Y, TIPS) live in columns
   that alternate with the refunding cycle. The 11-19 legs (2Y, 5Y, 7Y) live in columns that do not move
   at all. Does that make this event harder to call, or easier?

And one adjacent question the calendar has never asked: the whole block is **compressed into three
sessions by Thanksgiving**, with the 7-Year selling the day before the holiday. *Does that cost demand?*

**One-line verdict:** the ranking is wrong (this release sizes **1.46x** the refunding's nominal coupon
dollars), the columns make it **easier** (the note legs need no cycle read at all), and the compression
costs nothing measurable — **the only number worth waiting for is the 52-week bill**, and even that is
effectively set on 2026-10-22.

### Honest limits

**No Treasury document names any of this event's sizes**, and legs 3–5 are base rates and column

by leg 9's sequencing. **The null in leg 6 rests on n=6.** Six pre-Thanksgiving 7-Year auctions across
