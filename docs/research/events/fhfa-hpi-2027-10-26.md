## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This event existed only as `proposals/fhfa-hpi-2027-10-26.from-consumer-confidence-2027-10-26.json` —
the August-2027 monthly edition, proposed by the Conference Board lane. That proposal is unusually
honest about what it does *not* settle, and its third caution is the whole reason this ledger exists:

> **THE CB LANE MEASURED NOTHING ON THIS DATE AT INTRADAY RESOLUTION.** Its whole study is at
> daily-bar resolution and says nothing about the 9:00-to-10:30 ET window, which is the only place a
> same-morning interaction between these two releases could live. A lane taking this id inherits an
> open question, not a finding.

It is not one lane's gap. **Every FHFA ledger in this chain closes with a version of the same
sentence** — *"the remaining confound is the Conference Board at 10:00, so the 9:00–10:00 ET window is
the only clean design and no daily bar is."* Six ledgers, six deferrals, and the deferral had hardened
into a fact about the world.

**Is the clean window actually unreachable, or has nobody tried to reach it? And if it is reachable,
what does it say?**

**One-line verdict:** it is reachable — a 09:00–09:30 ET pre-market bar, thirty-four release mornings —
and it says the FHFA release morning is measurably **quieter** than a comparable morning, with **no
direction at all**, which is the wrong sign for anyone hoping to trade it.

### Leg 2 — **THE CLEAN WINDOW: it exists, it was built, and it reads QUIET** · **REFUTED** on any release effect

This is the leg the chain has been deferring since its first ledger. The design it wanted was a window
that contains the 9:00 FHFA release and excludes the 10:00 Conference Board print. Yahoo's hourly bars
with `includePrePost=true` are aligned on the hour before the open and on the half hour after it:

| Bar | Spans | What is in it |
|---|---|---|
| 08:00 | 08:00–09:00 ET | the 08:30 macro prints (durable goods, GDP, PCE…) |
| **09:00** | **09:00–09:30 ET** | **the FHFA release, and nothing else scheduled** |
| 09:30 | 09:30–10:30 ET | the cash open **and** the 10:00 Conference Board print |
| 10:30 | 10:30–11:30 ET | post-CB drift |

**730** sessions are served (2023-10-09 → 2026-09-04) and they contain **34** FHFA release mornings,
2023-10-31 → 2026-08-25.

| 09:00 bar metric (n=34 vs 694) | Release mornings | Other mornings | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| median \|return\| (bps) | **3.70** | 8.59 | **0.0196** |
| median range (bps) | **30.78** | 52.39 | **0.0316** |
| median **signed** return (bps) | −0.95 | 0.00 | 0.6550 |

**Both significant readings point the quiet way, and the directional one is dead.** The release morning
is a *narrower* pre-market than a comparable morning, and there is no tilt to trade. The p=0.0196 is
seed-stable — **0.0196 / 0.0192 / 0.0204 / 0.0185 / 0.0189** across five seeds — so it is not the
n=6-style artefact the August lane warned about.

**The adjacent bars agree, which is the tell.** The 09:30 bar carrying the open *and* the CB print is
also quieter (|return| **10.64 vs 20.31 bps, p=0.0278**; range **p=0.0256**; **volume p=0.0119**), the
08:00 bar carrying the 08:30 prints is null (**p=0.2160 / 0.2220**), and the 10:30 bar is null
(**p=0.6133**). A morning that is quiet across the pre-market *and* the first cash hour, on lower
volume, is describing a **day type** — not a release.

### Leg 3 — **THE HONEST LIMIT: the window removes the Conference Board, not the slot** · **SUPPORTED**

Leg 2's design does exactly what six ledgers asked for and no more. Inside the intraday window:

- **35** last Tuesdays, **34** of them FHFA release days.
- **0** release days that are not a last Tuesday.

So within this sample **FHFA release day ≡ last Tuesday of the month**. The clean window removes the
Conference Board confound the chain named; it cannot remove the calendar slot, and no window can —
only a pre-2021 sample can, and Yahoo's intraday history does not reach back to one.

Tightening the control accordingly:

| 09:00 bar, release mornings vs… | n | \|return\| p | range p |
|---|---|---|---|
| all other sessions | 34 vs 694 | **0.0196** | **0.0316** |
| other late-month (day ≥ 22) sessions | 34 vs 192 | 0.0845 | **0.0462** |
| other Tuesdays | 34 vs 118 | **0.0197** | **0.0413** |
| **other LATE-month Tuesdays** | **34 vs 13** | **0.1017** | 0.0753 |

**Against the tightest matched control the effect is a tendency, not a finding** — and it is
seed-stable at that level too (**0.1017 / 0.1032 / 0.1018 / 0.0976 / 0.1018**), so it is honestly
p≈0.10, not a coin that landed badly. **n=13 is the binding constraint**, and it is a constraint of the
data window, not of the design.

**And a second limit that no control fixes.** Yahoo serves **volume 0** on every one of the 34
pre-market bars — the endpoint does not report pre-market size. A 3.70 bps median move on unknown
depth is a **quote observation, not a fill**, and nothing in this leg should be read as a cost estimate.

### Leg 4 — **THE BOUNDARY, MEASURED: 30-minute resolution is the ceiling** · **SUPPORTED**

The chain's standing sentence was *"the 9:00–10:00 ET window is the only clean design"* with no
statement of what it would cost. Probed directly this session, same endpoint, same symbol:

| Interval | Request | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `1h` + `includePrePost` | `range=730d` | **HTTP 200**, 12,128 bars, 2023-10-09 → 2026-09-04 |
| `30m` | `range=730d` | **HTTP 422** — *"The requested range must be within the last 60 days"* |
| `5m` | `range=730d` | **HTTP 422** — same |

So the finest resolution obtainable **over a release-day sample** is the 30-minute pre-market bar the
hourly endpoint happens to emit, and the maximum sample is **34** mornings. Sixty days of 30m data
would contain **two** release days. **What would raise this ceiling is a data source, not a better
query** — that is now written down instead of re-derived by the seventh lane.

### Leg 5 — **THE SECOND METHOD UPGRADE: control on the regime, not only the calendar day** · **SUPPORTED**, and it changes a live reading

The 09-28 sibling upgraded this chain's control from month-level to **day ≥ 22**, on the ground that
FHFA's last-Tuesday rule puts every release in the final third of its month. October is the case where
that upgrade is **not enough**:

| October release days (n=5) vs… | VIX9D/VIX | p |
|---|---|---|
| all other sessions | 1.0485 vs 0.9282 | **0.0165** |
| **other late-month (day ≥ 22) sessions** | 1.0485 vs 0.9183 | **0.0148** |
| other October sessions | 1.0485 vs 0.9505 | 0.0862 |
| other **late-October** (day ≥ 22) sessions | 1.0485 vs 0.9736 | 0.4872 |

The day≥22 control leaves it standing at **0.0148**, seed-stable across five seeds
(**0.0165 / 0.0178 / 0.0157 / 0.0180 / 0.0170**). The month-and-late-month control kills it. Why the
pooled late-month control fails is the finding:

**All five October release days since 2021 sit inside an FOMC communications blackout** — 2021-10-26,
2022-10-25, 2023-10-31, 2024-10-29, 2025-10-28, **5 of 5** — and the blackout is a real
term-structure regime:

| Class (2021-01-01 → 2026-09-04) | n | VIX9D/VIX | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| **all blackout sessions vs all clear sessions** | 408 vs 1,049 | **0.9884 vs 0.9065** | **0.0000** |
| release days **in** blackout vs release days **out** | 22 vs 45 | 1.0138 vs 0.9179 | **0.0001** |
| **release days in blackout vs OTHER blackout sessions** | 22 vs 386 | 1.0138 vs 0.9874 | **0.2796** |
| release days out of blackout vs other clear sessions | 45 vs 1,004 | 0.9179 vs 0.9049 | 0.4533 |
| October release days vs other blackout sessions | 5 vs 403 | 1.0485 vs 0.9879 | 0.2171 |
| **October release days vs other LATE-OCTOBER blackout sessions** | **5 vs 24** | 1.0485 vs 1.0103 | **0.5579** |

**The p=0.0001 is the Fed's, and it is not FHFA's.** A day≥22 pool mixes blackout and clear sessions;
in a month where the release is *always* in blackout, that pooling leaves the regime's own signature
attributed to the release. Control on blackout membership and the release contributes nothing.

**Which ledgers this governs is deterministic, so it is a rule and not a caveat.** Across 2021–2027 the
last Tuesday falls inside a blackout in exactly four months:

| Month | last Tue in a blackout | last Tue **is** FOMC day one |
|---|---|---|
| **January** | **7 of 7** | **7 of 7** |
| **April** | **7 of 7** | 4 of 7 |
| **July** | **7 of 7** | **7 of 7** |
| **October** | **7 of 7** | 4 of 7 (2023, 2025, 2026, **2027**) |
| November | 1 of 7 | 0 of 7 |
| the other seven months | **0 of 7** | 0 of 7 |

That is **22 of 67** post-policy releases. **February and September are 0 of 7**, so the chain's two
earlier near-misses — February's seed-unstable 0.0445→0.0523 and September's late-month steepening —

### Leg 6 — **A SIBLING CLAIM NARROWED: day two is impossible, day one is month-specific** · **MIXED**

The 2026-10-27 ledger read the FOMC collision as structural: *"last-Tuesday × Tuesday–Wednesday means
day 1 is possible and day 2 is impossible."* The **impossibility** reproduces exactly — **0 of 67**
post-policy releases fall on an FOMC day two, from an independent parse. The **collision** does not
generalize the way that framing suggests:

- **January and July: 7 of 7.** The FOMC's late-January and late-July meetings begin on the last
  Tuesday every year in the panel. Arithmetic, matching the 01-26 sibling's "8 of 8 across 2021–2028".
- **April and October: 4 of 7.** October collides in **2023, 2025, 2026 and 2027**, and does not in
