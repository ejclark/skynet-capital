## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Stand aside on the release — and when it lands, read the electricity chapter, not the
oil chapter.** The STEO is a forecast publication, not a data release: there is no consensus to
surprise against, and the release window is measurably quiet. Built this session from Yahoo hourly
bars: across the eight 2026 STEO releases, the 12:00–13:00 ET hour had a median absolute WTI move
of **0.16%** against **0.27%** for all 503 noon hours in the sample — *below* an ordinary noon hour.
STEO **days** are violent (WTI full-day median |move| **2.77%** vs **1.47%** all days), but the
violence is not in the release hour: on the biggest one, 2026-03-10's **−11.94%** day, only
**−2.34%** happened between noon and 1pm. So the day is newsy for Hormuz reasons and the STEO rides
along. The oil chapter is a lagging chronicle besides — EIA's own January edition forecast Brent at
**$56/b for 2026**; August says **$87/b**, and **$78/b for 4Q26** against a BZ=F close of **95.42
on 2026-09-02** and a Morgan Stanley 4Q26 number of **$100**. The September edition will almost
certainly revise up, and that revision is already in the tape. What *is* worth your eyes is one
paragraph in the electricity chapter: the August STEO cut Texas 2027 electricity-load growth from
**14% to 6%** in a single month after Governor Abbott's **2026-08-03** order pausing every data
center in ERCOT's interconnection queue (~474 GW of requests, ~90% of them data centers). That is
the first *downward* revision to AI-driven load growth in a market
[`ai-energy-constraint.md`](../ai-energy-constraint.md) names as one of only two where the AI-power
windfall accrues. The event is **estimate**-dated and carries `symbols: []`, so no date-keyed action
is licensed regardless of any of the above.

## Initial research

**The question.** What will the September 2026 STEO say, does its release move anything, and does
any of it change how we sit in the tracked names (NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL AMZN)?

**One-line verdict:** a scheduled forecast publication whose release hour is *quieter* than an
ordinary noon hour, whose oil chapter chronicles a move the tape made months ago, and whose only
book-relevant content is a single electricity-chapter paragraph about Texas — so: stand aside,
read one page, trade nothing.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md)
(macro-print mode; the house instruments are equity/earnings-keyed and cover no commodity or
publication). Primaries fetched direct 2026-09-02: `eia.gov/outlooks/steo/` (landing page),
`eia.gov/outlooks/steo/release_schedule.php` (the 2026 date table and release-time rule),
`eia.gov/outlooks/steo/report/global_oil.php` (the August edition's oil chapter),
`eia.gov/pressroom/releases/press582.php` (the January edition's press release). **The base rate in
leg 2 was measured by me this session**, not quoted: Yahoo hourly and daily bars for CL=F, BZ=F,
USO and XLE, `range=2y`, bucketed to `America/New_York`. Trade press (BOE Report 2026-08-11, Utility
Dive, Fox News, Houston Public Media, Akin, Gibson Dunn, Holland & Knight) used for the electricity
chapter and the Texas directive, each claim dated and labelled.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is primary-sourced, and it deviates from EIA's own stated rule by one day —
   SUPPORTED, with a small flagged risk.** `release_schedule.php` (fetched 2026-09-02) lists
   **09/09/2026** for the September edition, and the August landing page says verbatim "Next Release
   Date: September 9, 2026" — two independent EIA primaries agreeing. But the *same* schedule page
   states the rule: "the Short-Term Energy Outlook (STEO) scheduling procedure calls for the release
   of the STEO on the first Tuesday following the first Thursday of each month." September 2026's
   first Thursday is the 3rd, so the rule yields **Tuesday 09-08**; the published table says
   **Wednesday 09-09**. The table is the operative primary and the rule is described as a
   *procedure*, not a guarantee — but the one-day gap is worth carrying as a live risk rather than
   smoothing over, because the whole calendar's date discipline rests on noticing exactly this kind
   of mismatch. Release time is stated: **12:00–12:15 pm ET**. Filed **estimate** on prefix grounds
   only (this calendar defines no `confirmed` prefix for the EIA), not on date doubt. Remaining 2026
   dates from the same table, for the calendar's use: **10/06**, **11/10** (Winter Fuels Outlook
   edition), **12/08**.

2. **The release window is measurably quiet — SUPPORTED, and this is the leg that kills the trade.**
   No base rate for a STEO release existed anywhere in this repo, so I built one. Yahoo hourly bars,
   bucketed to `America/New_York`, the 12:00–13:00 ET bar (the release window), across the eight
   2026 STEO release dates (01/13, 02/10, 03/10, 04/07, 05/12, 06/09, 07/07, 08/11) against every
   noon hour in the same two-year sample:

   | Symbol | Noon-hour median \|move\|, all days | Noon-hour p90, all days | Noon-hour median \|move\|, STEO days (n=8) |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | CL=F | 0.27% (n=503) | 0.82% | **0.16%** |
   | BZ=F | 0.27% (n=502) | 0.93% | **0.41%** |
   | USO | 0.27% (n=494) | 0.82% | **0.48%** |
   | XLE | 0.20% (n=494) | 0.48% | **0.22%** |

   WTI's release hour is *below* an ordinary noon hour; XLE's is indistinguishable from one. Three
   STEO days do show extreme noon hours (01/13 −0.93%, 03/10 −2.34%, 06/09 +1.35% on CL=F, all ≥90th
   percentile) — and the decomposition below says why that is not the STEO.

3. **STEO days are violent, but the violence is not in the release hour — SUPPORTED, and it is the
   cleanest finding here.** Same bars, full-day close-to-close:

   | Symbol | Full-day median \|move\|, all days | Full-day median \|move\|, STEO days (n=8) |
   |---|---|---|
   | CL=F | 1.47% | **2.77%** |
   | BZ=F | 1.35% | **2.74%** |

   Nearly 2× — yet the same days' release hours are at or below normal. The extreme case makes it
   plain: **2026-03-10** was CL=F **−11.94%** on the day and only **−2.34%** between noon and 1pm,
   so ~80% of the move happened outside the window, and it is the session the
   [OPEC+ ledger](opec-plus-meeting-2026-09-06.md) dates to Iran's early-March closure of the
   Strait. STEO days land in a war year and inherit its headlines; the publication rides along.
   **The honest conclusion is a null with a mechanism**, not merely "no effect found."

4. **The oil chapter is a lagging chronicle, not a forecast that leads price — SUPPORTED from EIA's
   own primaries.** The January 2026 STEO press release (eia.gov, 2026-01-13) forecast "the price of
   Brent crude oil down to average **$56 per barrel in 2026**, a 19% drop from 2025." The August
   2026 edition (`report/global_oil.php`, released 08-11) forecasts Brent at **$87** for 2026,
   "an average of **$78/b by 4Q26**", and "an average of **$69/b in 2027**" — a **+$31** revision to
   the same calendar-year average across seven editions, every dollar of it *after* the tape moved.
   It is already stale again: BZ=F closed **95.42 on 2026-09-02** (my own pull), **~22% above** the
   EIA's own 4Q26 number, while Morgan Stanley carries Brent **$90 3Q26 / $100 4Q26 / $95 1Q27 /
   $90 2Q27** against a prior flat $75 (EnergyNow, Aug 2026) and J.P. Morgan carries **$86 3Q26 /
   $80 4Q26**. So a September upward revision is close to mechanical — and a revision the market
   already made cannot be information. The chapter's genuinely useful content is its *physical*
   estimates, which this calendar already cites: Hormuz crude+liquids transits **4.9 mb/d in 2Q26
   vs 21.6 mb/d in 4Q25**, Mideast shut-ins **~5.5 mb/d in July**, global inventories falling
   **4.2 mb/d in 2Q26** and a forecast **3.8 mb/d in 3Q26**, with disruptions of ~**0.6 mb/d**
   persisting through end-2027 and production "generally return[ing] to pre-conflict status" in
   early 2027.

5. **The one page that touches our book is the electricity chapter, and it already delivered a shock
   this repo has not logged — SUPPORTED.** The August 2026 STEO states: "We expect electricity load
   in Texas will grow by **6% in 2027**, in contrast to our forecast of **14% growth** in the
   previous STEO," attributing the cut to the Texas governor's **2026-08-03** pause on new data
   center development (BOE Report, 2026-08-11, quoting the STEO; corroborated independently by a
   second write-up putting the ERCOT *generation* growth cut at **13% → 5%** over the same one-month
   gap). The underlying action is well-sourced across many outlets: Abbott's 08-03 letter to PUCT
   Chairman Gleeson and ERCOT CEO Vegas ordered a "comprehensive verification and audit of all data
   centers advancing through ERCOT's interconnection process" before any additional data center may
   proceed, concluding "Any data center project that fails to comply with the verification and audit
   process ... must be denied"; ERCOT is holding ~**474 GW** of interconnection requests, ~**90%**
   data centers, more than **5× ERCOT's record peak demand**, with ~**300** projects ≥75 MW inside
   the Batch Zero process. National demand still climbs — **4,195 bkWh (2025) → 4,268 (2026) →
   4,391 (2027)**, gas generation share flat at **40%** — so this is a *state-level* cut inside a
   still-rising national number, which is precisely why it is easy to miss. It matters here because
   [`ai-energy-constraint.md`](../ai-energy-constraint.md) names ERCOT and PJM as the only two
   deregulated markets where the AI-power windfall accrues to merchant generators, and its whole
   frontier map assumes load growth is the binding input. **This is the first dated, quantified,
   official downward revision to that assumption**, and the September edition is the next scoreboard
   for it.

6. **Transmission to our tracked names via crude is two-step, low-fidelity and blocked this cycle —
   SUPPORTED.** `symbols: []`. The only crude path to the book is crude → CPI energy / ISM
   prices-paid → Sep-16 hike odds → real yields → long-duration AI names. Our own registered
   **FT-12** holds that CPI reactions are **core**-keyed with energy-driven headline surprises
   discounted — and the August CPI collection window closed before this STEO exists, so its
   contents cannot reach the **2026-09-11** print at all. A weak channel, discounted by our own
   reaction-function hypothesis, arriving too late to matter, is not a trade.

7. **The day is crowded and the STEO is the smallest thing on it — SUPPORTED.** 2026-09-09 at
   12:00 ET sits one hour ahead of both the **10Y note reopening** (1:00 pm ET,
   [`treasury-10y-note-2026-09-09`](treasury-10y-note-2026-09-09.md), `high`) and **Apple's iPhone
   event** (10:00 am PT = 1:00 pm ET,
   [`aapl-iphone-18-launch-2026-09-09`](aapl-iphone-18-launch-2026-09-09.md), `medium`, AAPL-keyed),
   with the **long-end buyback size increase** taking effect the same day. Eleven tracked events sit
   inside ±5 days. Any 09-09 energy move after 1pm ET is attributable to the auction, not the STEO —
   a stated attribution rule, not a hedge.

**What the conditions support.** Nothing to enter. The deliverables are three cheap, checkable
things: (a) a **measured base rate** for STEO release windows, which did not exist in this repo and
which kills the "trade the release" idea with numbers rather than assertion; (b) a **re-pointing of
what to read** — the electricity chapter's Texas line, not the Brent line; and (c) one **dated
discovery**, the ERCOT Batch Zero audit filing target of **2026-12-10**, proposed to the calendar in
this PR as an `estimate`.

**Limits, honestly.**
- **The base rate is n=8, one calendar year, in a war-distorted sample.** Yahoo's hourly bars cap at
  ~730 days, so no pre-conflict STEO sample was reachable; there is no control for co-incident news
  beyond the full-day/noon-hour decomposition in leg 3; and no significance test was applied. The
  *direction* (release hour ≤ ordinary hour) is robust to all of that. A precise effect size is not,
  and nobody should size anything off it.
- **The electricity numbers are secondary, deliberately labelled.** The August STEO PDF is 7 MB and
  no text extractor was available in this session, and EIA's `report/electricity.php` served no
  content to the fetcher. The Texas figures therefore come from two independent write-ups quoting
  the STEO verbatim (BOE Report 2026-08-11; a second outlet on the ERCOT generation cut), which
  agree in direction and magnitude. The oil figures **are** primary (`report/global_oil.php`). The
  September edition's electricity numbers should be read from the primary before anything is built
  on them.
- **One source discarded.** A Yahoo Finance piece ("Oil Shock Lifts EIA Price Outlook as Hormuz
  Crisis Reshapes Forecast") quotes Brent forecasts of **$79/2026** and **$64/2027** against a prior
  **$58/$53** — the article carries no STEO edition and no date, and its figures match no 2026
  edition verified here. **Not used** for any level; the January-vs-August primaries carry the
  revision claim instead. This is the fourth stale/undated-source catch in this event family.
- **There is no consensus and no whisper for a STEO, structurally.** It is a forecast publication,
  not a survey-tracked data release, so the standard "reaction function to surprises in each
  direction" question is malformed here rather than merely unanswered. That absence is itself part
  of why the release hour is quiet.
- **No STEO-day base rate exists for our actual names.** The measurement covers CL=F, BZ=F, USO and
  XLE. Whether a STEO day means anything for CRWV was not measured, and the honest prior — given
  `symbols: []` and a two-step transmission — is that it does not.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (set 2026-09-02, D-7): stand aside on the release; read the electricity chapter, ignore
the oil chapter.** The 2026-09-09 STEO is **estimate**-dated, carries no tracked symbols, and its
release window is measurably quieter than an ordinary noon hour (WTI median |move| 0.16% vs 0.27%,
n=8 vs n=503). Its oil chapter reports revisions the tape already made — $56 → $87 for 2026 across
seven editions, and still ~22% below spot at 4Q26. Its one book-relevant page is the electricity
chapter's Texas line. No entry, no hedge, no pre-positioning is licensed by this event, and the
**estimate** label forbids date-keyed action independently of any of the above.

**Kill switches** — any of these fires and the stance gets re-argued, not tweaked:

1. **The release hour stops being quiet.** CL=F's 12:00–13:00 ET move on **2026-09-09** exceeds
   **±0.82%** (the measured p90 of ordinary noon hours) with the STEO identifiable as the cause →
   the base rate built here is wrong or regime-dependent, and leg 2 needs rebuilding on a longer
   sample before it is cited again. Check: Yahoo hourly CL=F bars, 2026-09-09.
2. **The date slips.** No September STEO published by **12:15 pm ET on 2026-09-09** → the schedule
   table was wrong, the rule-derived 09-08 was right, and every EIA date this calendar carries
   (10/06, 11/10, 12/08) needs re-derivation from the rule rather than the table. Check:
   eia.gov/outlooks/steo/, 2026-09-09.
3. **Texas reverses.** The **2026-09-09** edition restores Texas 2027 load growth toward July's
   **14%** → the Abbott pause is being read as temporary, the ERCOT leg of the AI-power constraint
   is un-cut, and leg 5's "first downward revision" framing dies. Check: the September STEO
   electricity chapter, primary.
4. **Texas gets worse and spreads.** The **2026-10-06** or **2026-12-08** edition cuts *national*
   2027 electricity consumption below **4,391 bkWh** citing data-center pauses beyond Texas → the
   AI-load-growth premise under [`ai-energy-constraint.md`](../ai-energy-constraint.md) starts
   eroding at the demand end. That is a book-level input, not an energy-sector footnote, and it
   would re-open the CRWV exposure question rather than adjust this ledger.
5. **The oil chapter stops lagging.** The September edition's 4Q26 Brent forecast lands at or above
   **$95** (i.e. at spot, not behind it) → EIA has moved from chronicling to leading, and the "no
   information content" call in leg 4 needs re-arguing. Check: `report/global_oil.php`, 2026-09-09.

**Forward tests registered** in [`forward-tests.md`](../forward-tests.md) this session: **FT-47**
(the release hour is a non-event) and **FT-48** (the oil chapter revises up but stays behind spot —
the "lagging chronicle" claim, stated so both halves can fail).
