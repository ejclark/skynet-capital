## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** what is the November 2026 CPI print likely to show, and how will the
market react — given that it lands at 08:30 ET on Dec 10, the morning **after** the Dec 8–9 FOMC
(SEP + dot plot) prints its decision?

**One-line verdict:** at D-115 no consensus for this print can exist yet; what is establishable is
the regime — a live rate-hike cycle driven by an Iran oil shock, where the market trades every CPI
print as a hike referendum and the calendar forces the Fed to decide the day *before* seeing this
number — so the print's job is to validate or embarrass a 20-hour-old FOMC decision, which is a
volatility amplifier, not a directional edge.

**Method:** sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md)
(macro-print branch) — primary sources over aggregators (the calendar seeding on 2026-08-15 caught
an aggregator carrying Dec 18 for this very release vs the real Dec 10). BLS schedule pages
re-fetched directly 2026-08-17; reaction function reconstructed from the last two actual releases
(Jul 14 and Aug 12, 2026) via contemporaneous coverage (CNBC, IG, Benzinga, FedWatch-derived
odds); forecast anchors from the Philadelphia Fed SPF (Q2 2026) and the June 2026 FOMC SEP.

**Leg 1 — the date. SUPPORTED (confirmed).** BLS release schedule
(bls.gov/schedule/news_release/cpi.htm, fetched 2026-08-17) lists "November 2026 | Dec. 10, 2026 |
08:30 AM". Matches the domain entry exactly. The aggregator that had Dec 18 remains wrong; the
primary stands.

**Leg 2 — the inflation trajectory into the print. MIXED.** Headline CPI peaked at 4.2% y/y in
May 2026, eased to 3.5% in June (m/m −0.4%, the largest monthly decline since April 2020, on an
energy give-back) and 3.4% in July (m/m +0.1%, in line with consensus); core eased 2.6% → 2.5%
(CNBC 2026-08-12; IG 2026-07-13). That is disinflation off a spike — but the spike's cause, the
Iran conflict, is unresolved: Brent touched $100 in late July on escalation (CNBC 2026-07-23) and
was still rising +6%/wk with tanker attacks as of mid-August (per the
[`nvda-2026-08-26-print.md`](nvda-2026-08-26-print.md) 2026-08-17 sweep). Forecast anchors, both
labeled **estimate**: SPF Q2 2026 sees 2026 Q4/Q4 headline ~3.5%, core ~2.9%; a naive model path
has Dec headline ~3.0–3.2%. Whether November data print cool or hot is genuinely open and mostly
an oil-path question.

**Leg 3 — the reaction function. SUPPORTED (from the last two releases).** The market currently
trades CPI as a referendum on Fed hikes, not on inflation per se:
- **Jun-data print (rel. 2026-07-14):** headline 3.5% vs 3.8% consensus, m/m −0.4% → SPY futures
  +1%, NDX futures +1.2%, yields sharply lower, September hike odds cut (Benzinga/IG, 2026-07-14).
- **Jul-data print (rel. 2026-08-12):** +0.1% m/m, 3.4% y/y in line, core 2.5% → futures up,
  yields lower across the curve, September hike odds cut to ~42% (CNBC, 2026-08-12).
- Between prints, the *non*-CPI channel showed the other direction's magnitude: Iran/oil
  escalation alone swung September hike odds from ~53% to ~82% in one July week (CNBC 2026-07-23,
  Yahoo Finance). Cool prints have been worth roughly +1% on NDX futures; the hot scenario has so
  far arrived via oil rather than a CPI surprise, and repriced the entire front end when it did.
  Asymmetry inference (labeled **estimate**): with hikes live, a hot CPI likely hurts
  long-duration tech more than an equally-sized cool CPI helps.

**Leg 4 — the sequencing hazard. SUPPORTED (structural, confirmed dates).** The Dec 8–9 FOMC
(SEP + dot plot; calendar entry `fomc-2026-12-09`, FED-sourced) decides **before** seeing Nov CPI.
Also in the same week: Nov employment report Fri Dec 4 (BLS empsit schedule, fetched 2026-08-17).
That makes Dec 4–10 a compound macro window — jobs → FOMC+dots → CPI on three of five consecutive
business days — and puts this print in the unusual position of scoring a decision already made.
A print that contradicts the day-old statement (e.g. hot CPI after a dovish hold, or vice versa)
has no offsetting Fed communication for weeks; the mechanical read is elevated CPI-day volatility
relative to a standalone release.

**Leg 5 — which tracked names carry the most sensitivity, and why. SUPPORTED (mechanism), sizes
are estimates.** All symbols here via the rates channel — the event is market-wide (`symbols: []`
in the calendar):

  the highest beta on the roster.
- **NVDA, AVGO, MRVL** — high-beta AI semis; the Jul 14 cool print's NDX +1.2% understates their
  typical amplification (semis routinely 1.5–2× NDX on rate repricings — **estimate**, not
  measured by our instruments).
- **MSFT, GOOG, META, AMZN, AAPL** — long-duration megacaps, the NDX itself; directionally with
  the index, cash-rich balance sheets damp the credit channel.

**What plays the conditions support:** only the no-alpha guards, applied with the compound window
