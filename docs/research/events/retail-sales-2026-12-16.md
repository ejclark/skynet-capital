### Leg 3 — the Cyber-Monday-in-November story is not in the adjusted data — MIXED, and it is the premise most likely to be reached for

2026's Thanksgiving is **Thursday 2026-11-26** (the fourth Thursday), so Black Friday is **11-27**
and Cyber Monday is **11-30** — *inside* the November reference month, where in both 2024 (Dec 2)
and 2025 (Dec 1) it fell into December. The obvious inference is that November-2026 gets a
composition lift its two predecessors did not. The obvious inference has the sign backwards.

MARTS is adjusted **"for seasonal variation and holiday and trading-day differences, but not for
price changes"** — Census's own standing caption, quoted from all three release PDFs. Calendar
placement is exactly what the second clause exists to remove. Grouping the last eight Novembers by
where Cyber Monday actually fell (Cyber Monday = Thanksgiving + 4):

| Year | Thanksgiving | Cyber Monday | Nov headline m/m SA | Nov nonstore m/m SA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2018 | Nov 22 | **Nov 26** | +0.49% | +4.58% |
| 2020 | Nov 26 | **Nov 30** | −0.96% | +0.83% |
| 2021 | Nov 25 | **Nov 29** | +1.02% | +0.94% |
| 2022 | Nov 24 | **Nov 28** | −1.38% | −2.09% |
| 2023 | Nov 23 | **Nov 27** | −0.12% | −0.03% |
| 2019 | Nov 28 | Dec 2 | +0.70% | +0.24% |
| 2024 | Nov 28 | Dec 2 | +0.50% | +0.60% |
| 2025 | Nov 27 | Dec 1 | +0.50% | +0.69% |
| **2026** | **Nov 26** | **Nov 30** | — | — |

**Cyber-Monday-in-November median headline: −0.12%. Cyber-Monday-in-December median: +0.50%.** The
group that "should" be lifted is the weaker one, and it is also enormously noisier (−1.38% to
+1.02% against a +0.50%/+0.70% cluster). The nonstore line — the one that would actually carry an
online shopping day — tells the same story: dispersed from −2.09% to +4.58% in the November group,
tight at +0.24%/+0.60%/+0.69% in the December group.

**Why this is MIXED and not REFUTED.** Three of the five November-group years are 2020, 2021 and
2022 — COVID, stimulus base effects and a 9% inflation peak. Drop them and the group is 2018
(+0.49%) and 2023 (−0.12%), median +0.19% against the December group's +0.50%: still weaker, but
n=2, and the gap is inside ordinary noise. So the honest grade splits. **The naive directional
claim — Cyber Monday inside November means a bigger November — finds no support in any cut of the
data.** The reverse claim, that it means a *smaller* November, is not established either. What is
established is the null: the adjustment absorbs the placement, and the residual is noise rather
than signal. That is the same shape as the 11-17 ledger's fall-Prime refutation and for the same
mechanical reason — recurring calendar structure is what seasonal and trading-day adjustment is
built to remove.

One asymmetry worth carrying, because it points the *other* way from the narrative: an earlier
Thanksgiving lengthens December, not November. 2026 gives **28 shopping days** between Thanksgiving
and Christmas Eve against 27 in 2025 and 26 in 2024 — the longest of the three — and every one of
the extra days falls in the **December** reference month, which this print does not measure.

### Leg 4 — the gasoline channel is the live, quantified distortion, and it is the one nobody is naming — SUPPORTED

This is the leg that can actually move the number, and it exists because of the clause at the end of
Census's own caption: **not adjusted for price changes**.

- **The weight is large.** Gasoline stations were **$59.879B of a $763.602B** retail total in the
  latest published month (July 2026) — **7.84%**.
- **The seasonal expects a decline.** Monthly-averaged EIA weekly regular gasoline, November vs
  October: **2017 +2.34% · 2018 −7.44% · 2019 −1.11% · 2020 −2.31% · 2021 +3.17% · 2022 −3.41% ·
  2023 −8.16% · 2024 −2.69% · 2025 −0.33%** — down in **7 of 9**, median **−2.31%**. Post-Labor-Day
  demand falls and the RVP winter-blend switchover lowers cost; the SA factors embed both.
- **The shock is live and dated.** Brent closed **90.49 (08-31) → 94.65 (09-01) → 95.63 (09-02) →
  95.52 (09-03) → 96.24 (09-04)**, touching **$99.38** intraday on 09-03 after Iran retaliated for
  US strikes by hitting Jordan, Bahrain and Kuwait ([`jobs-2026-09-04`](jobs-2026-09-04.md), same
  day). WTI ran 85.76 → 91.47 over the same four sessions. Regular gasoline was already **$4.071/gal**
  at the 2026-08-31 weekly, up from $3.777 in early July.

The arithmetic follows directly. Crude→pump pass-through runs roughly 2–3 weeks, so the shock
reaches retail pumps in mid-to-late September; whether it survives to the November reference month
is the open question, not whether it is large. If November pump prices merely hold flat instead of
falling their seasonal 2.31%, the gasoline line contributes about **2.31% × 7.84% ≈ +0.18pp** to the
headline m/m. If they rise 5%, the swing against seasonal is 7.3pp and the contribution is
**~+0.57pp** — larger than six of the eight November headline prints in leg 3's table, from price
alone, with zero demand content.

**The reading rule this forces, and it is the practical output of this document:** on 12-16 the
**control group** — retail sales excluding autos, gasoline, building materials and food services —
is the demand read, and the headline is contaminated. Coverage will lead with the headline.
Registered as **FT-retail-sales-2026-12-16-1**, deliberately keyed to the EIA gasoline series
rather than to the Census print, so the test scores even in the lapse branch where the print does
not exist.
