December — and both of the things that make it unusual measure to nothing.** Three coupon legs,

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Four siblings have established what a coupon announcement *is* in this calendar. The
[2026-11-19](treasury-coupon-announcement-2026-11-19.md) and
[2027-01-21](treasury-coupon-announcement-2027-01-21.md) ledgers did the month-end block; the
[2026-12-24 ledger](treasury-coupon-announcement-2026-12-24.md) found the series' first non-null
(session compression) and banked the method lesson that a leg-pooled t-statistic is inflated. None of
them researched a **mid-month** announcement, and this one is not even a normal mid-month
announcement. Three things are genuinely different:

1. **Its shape is the rarest in the record** — three coupon legs, three *different security types*,
   and every leg a reopening. *How rare, and does an all-reopening block behave differently?*
2. **The 5-Year TIPS gets a 3-business-day when-issued window against a modal 5.** The proposal that
   filed this event asked exactly this by name: *does a compressed announce-to-auction window change
   what the block clears at?*
3. **The 20-Year reopening auctions 2026-12-23** — later in December than any 20-Year on record.
   *Does a long-duration sale that late into the holiday book cost anything?*

**One-line verdict:** the shape is genuinely the rarest in 288 announcements and the 12-23 date is
genuinely unprecedented for the tenor — and **both of the demand hypotheses they suggest are nulls**,
one of which turns out to have been a **reopening confound** amplified by **five junk records in the
dataset**. The durable outputs are two method corrections and one large, robust effect (the reopening
premium); the sizes are the easiest part.
