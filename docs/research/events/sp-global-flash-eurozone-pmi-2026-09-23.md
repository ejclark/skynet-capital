## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This event exists because a sibling lane needed it to exist. The
[US flash ledger](sp-global-flash-us-pmi-2026-09-23.md) measured the US **09:45–10:00 ET** window as
loud across three release days, then wrote down what it could not rule out — that some of that
loudness is European flash-PMI risk tone still bleeding through the US open, since the European
block prints on the **same calendar date**, from the **same compiler**, on the **same reference
month**, hours earlier. It proposed this entry with the gap stated in the proposal itself:

> **NOT MEASURED:** no first-hand bar work was done on the European block; the confound is named,
> not sized, and sizing it is the job of whichever session picks this up.

So: **(1)** is the date and the 08:00 UTC slot right, on a primary rather than a derivation?
**(2)** Does this print leave *any* measurable footprint in US-hours instruments — the sizing the
sibling asked for? **(3)** If it does not, what is the mechanism, and does the `low` tier survive?

**One-line verdict:** the date and slot hold on **three primaries** including an actually-delivered
release; the European print's US-hours footprint is **measured and absent at base rate**, so the
sibling's confound is **small**; and the mechanism is structural — the 08:00 UTC aggregate is
**pre-announced by its own components** 30 and 45 minutes earlier, which is why `low` survives.
