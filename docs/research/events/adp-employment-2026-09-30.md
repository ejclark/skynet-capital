## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **This is not a labor print. It is ADP's annual re-benchmark print, and the headline
number that comes out of it should not be read as a signal about hiring.** Every September, ADP
recalibrates its whole series to the Bureau of Labor Statistics' Quarterly Census of Employment and
Wages — the tax-record census that is the closest thing to a full count of American jobs — and
restates recent months on the new basis. Last year that recalibration **subtracted 43,000 jobs from
the September headline outright and revised the prior month from +54,000 to −3,000**, a 57k swing
that had nothing to do with anyone hiring or firing. The 2026 setup is the same and the trigger has
already fired: BLS published the Q1-2026 QCEW alongside its own preliminary benchmark revision on
**2026-08-28** (private payrolls marked down **−178,000**), and ADP's August report, published five
days later on 09-02, contains **no** benchmark language at all — so the adjustment is still pending
and 09-30 is where it lands. The practical instruction is a refusal: **do not trade, and do not
update any view on the labor market from, the 09-30 ADP headline until the release's own benchmark
note has been read.** Two further reasons this specific print is unusually low-value. **The 2025
analogue only half-carries:** last September's ADP mattered enormously because a government shutdown
had deleted the official jobs data, and that branch is now **dead** — Trump signed the
"Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027" on **2026-09-02**, funding through **12-11**, so
BLS payrolls print normally on **10-02** and this release has no monopoly on anything. And **ADP
lands at 08:15 ET, fifteen minutes before PCE at 08:30 ET** — the Fed's own target inflation measure,
carrying BEA's annual update, on the single most crowded session this calendar tracks. Date is
**confirmed** (promoted this PR on ADP's own primary + FRED corroboration). Nothing here is a trade.

will the market treat it, and which of our tracked names (NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. September is ADP's annual preliminary re-benchmark month, and the adjustment is large —
SUPPORTED, with one measured instance.** ADP's own September-2025 release states that it "conducted
a preliminary rebenchmarking of the National Employment Report based on full-year 2024 results from
the Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages released by the Bureau of Labor Statistics," and that
this "resulted in a reduction of **43,000 jobs** in the September 2025 ADP National Employment
Report." The same release restates **August 2025 from +54,000 to −3,000** — a **57k** swing — and
adds a data-quality caveat verbatim: "The most recent release of the QCEW contained a
higher-than-normal number of missing or redacted values for establishment size by NAICS sector and
geography subgroups. This required the benchmark to be calculated at a coarser granularity than in
previous years." It also names the follow-on: "ADP's full-year benchmarking will take place in
February 2026 with the publication of the January 2026 National Employment Report" — and that did
happen (the January-2026 report, published 2026-02-04, carried the full annual revision, reweighted
to QCEW through March 2025). **So the cadence is: preliminary re-benchmark in the September report,
full benchmark in the February report.** Honest weight: the *magnitude* is **n=1** (2025 only); the
*pattern* rests on ADP's own two-step description plus secondary characterisation of it as annual.

**2. The 2026 trigger has already fired and the adjustment has not yet been applied — SUPPORTED.**
BLS published first-quarter-2026 QCEW data together with the preliminary estimate of the annual CES
benchmark revision at **10:00 ET on 2026-08-28** (bls.gov `cesprelbmk.htm`, and the figures banked in
the [`jobs-2026-09-04`](jobs-2026-09-04.md) D-5 row): total nonfarm **−79,000 (−0.1%)**, **total
private −178,000 (−0.1%)** through March 2026, with softness in retail trade, education/health,
manufacturing and business services. Total private is the ADP-relevant line. ADP's August report
published **five days later** on 09-02 and — checked verbatim on two independent fetches of the same
release — contains **no** occurrence of *QCEW*, *benchmark*, or *rebenchmark*, and revised July
**upward** (44,000 → 46,000). An upward prior-month revision is the opposite of what a downward
re-benchmark produces. **The inference: the 2026 preliminary re-benchmark was not in the August
report, and 09-30 is the next scheduled slot for it** — the same slot it occupied in 2025.

**3. A re-benchmarked headline is not a hiring signal — SUPPORTED, definitionally.** The QCEW is
built from quarterly tax filings employers submit to their states under unemployment-insurance law;
it is a near-census, and when it disagrees with a sample-based estimate, the census wins. A
recalibration therefore changes the *level* of the series and the *basis* of recent months without
any employer having hired or fired anyone. The consequence for a reader is concrete: on 09-30 the
printed number and the prior-month comparison will not be measuring the same thing as +38k (Aug) or
+46k (Jul) did, and any "ADP fell/rose by X versus last month" reasoning off it is arithmetic on
mismatched bases. This is why the call for this print is a refusal rather than a direction.

**4. The 2025 analogue's *other* half — the data blackout — is dead — SUPPORTED, and it removes this
print's main claim to importance.** September 2025's ADP was consequential because the federal
shutdown that began 2025-10-01 had suspended BLS releases, making ADP briefly the only private-payrolls
read available (CNBC's own headline, 2025-10-01, framed it as "coming amid shutdown data blackout").
Our own [`government-funding-deadline-2026-09-30`](government-funding-deadline-2026-09-30.md) doc
anticipated a rerun and wrote the mechanism down: in a lapse "expect the market to over-weight the
surviving *private* surveys (ISM 10-01, ADP)." **That branch closed while this event was being
filed.** The House adopted the Senate's CR **370–48 on 2026-09-01** (Roll Call, WaPo, NPR, The Hill,
all 2026-09-01; the Senate had passed it 90–6 in August), and the president signed the *Continuing
Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027* on **2026-09-02**, funding the government through
**2026-12-11** (Epoch Times, dateline 9/2/2026 updated 9/3/2026; corroborated by KTSM and iHeart
2026-09-03). So the 09-30 cliff is retired, **jobs 10-02 and CPI 10-14 print normally**, and the
`medium`-tier rationale recorded in this event's own calendar note — "a PRIVATE labor read landing ON
the FY2027 funding deadline, so in a lapse branch it survives where BLS deletes" — **no longer
holds**. The note is corrected in this PR. The tier stays `medium` on the remaining ground (a
pre-NFP private payrolls read), not on the blackout ground.

**5. No September-specific consensus exists, and the one number visible is a placeholder — MIXED (a
gap, correctly timed).** At D-27 no ADP-specific September forecast was found, which matches the
09-02 doc's measured finding that ADP previews firm only 1–2 days out (its own August consensus did
not surface until D-1). What *is* visible is a trap worth naming so the next session does not fall
into it: TradingEconomics' ADP calendar shows a next-release forecast of **38K** against a previous
of **38K** — identical to the prior print, i.e. a mechanical carry-forward, not a survey. The 09-02
close-out already banked the related lesson that an aggregator band is usable "as a *bracket*, not as
*the bar*"; a carry-forward is weaker still and is not usable at all. Expect a real band ~09-28.

**6. The reaction function was measured one month ago and does not support an asymmetry — SUPPORTED,
n=1.** The sibling doc installed a beat-side asymmetry twice (at D-3 on Warsh's 08-28 "quite stable
… full employment" framing, at D-1 on the Larak Island strike driving Brent to $90.91) and the tape
**refuted it verbatim by its own registered falsifier**: August printed **+38k**, below both July's
+44k and the 47–48k Dow Jones/Bloomberg consensus, and the session traded **dovishly** on four
independent channels — CME hike odds **68.2% → 60.2–64.2%**, a 20-month-high 10-year yield
(4.818% intraday) round-tripping to unchanged, equities up with **small caps leading** (Russell 2000
+1.13%), VIX **−1.14**. This doc therefore starts **symmetric** and declines to re-derive an
asymmetry that was just falsified. Two honest caveats carried from the close-out: attribution that
day was contaminated (NY Fed's Williams gave a dovish CNBC interview ~4 hours later; AVGO reported
AMC), and one session is one observation.

**7. The composition question September inherits — the August print was already extraordinarily
narrow, and September has a known seasonal shape — MIXED (a real setup, an unmeasured effect).** The
09-02 close-out's banked finding: education/health alone (**+45k**) exceeded the entire August
headline (+38k), so all other sectors netted **−7k**; the two largest drags were **manufacturing
−17k** and **professional/business −16k**; **large firms (500+) supplied +34k of the +38k while
medium firms (50–499) added exactly zero**; and one region, the Northeast (+38k), supplied the whole
national number. September 2025's own composition suggests two seasonal forces run *against* each
other in this month: education/health **+33k** "as schools reopened," and leisure/hospitality
**−19k** "as vacation season wound down" (ADP release, 2025-10-01). August 2026's leisure/hospitality
was **+16k**. If the seasonal pattern repeats, September's arithmetic is tight before any
re-benchmark is applied. Stated as a setup, not a forecast: these are seasonally-adjusted series and
one prior year is not a seasonal.

**8. Attribution on 09-30 is impossible by construction — SUPPORTED, from this calendar's own
entries.** ADP prints **08:15 ET**. Fifteen minutes later, at **08:30 ET**, BEA publishes both
**PCE / Personal Income & Outlays (Aug data) + the BEA annual update** (`high` impact,
BEA-confirmed) and the **third estimate of Q2 GDP** (`medium`, BEA-confirmed); Chicago PMI follows at
**09:45 ET**. The session before carries JOLTS, consumer confidence, MU's earnings print and day one
of CoreWeave's Fully Connected; the session after carries ISM Manufacturing and Apple's EU DMA terms;
BLS payrolls land 10-02. PCE is the Fed's target measure and Warsh pinned the hawkish case explicitly
on inflation — so on a morning where PCE lands fifteen minutes after ADP, PCE owns the tape. **This
print has the lowest signal-to-attribution ratio of any ADP release this calendar has tracked**, and
that alone is sufficient grounds for the stand-aside independent of every other leg.

**9. Tracked-name sensitivity — deliberately not ranked.** The 09-02 doc published a ranking (CRWV
most rate-path-sensitive, then NVDA/AVGO/MRVL, then MSFT/GOOG/META, least AAPL/AMZN) and the one
session where it was cleanly testable came out **inverted at both ends** — CRWV **−1.12%** and MRVL
**−1.86%** on a dovish session while NVDA **+3.21%** and META **+2.47%** led on idiosyncratic AI-capex
flow. That failed to confirm rather than refuted the ranking (n=1, with AVGO reporting the same day),
but repeating an unconfirmed ranking as though it were established would be the kind of narrative
persistence this lane exists to prevent. **No ranking is claimed here.** What survives is the
mechanism only: all nine trade as long-duration assets on the rate path, and this print is not a
rate-path event.

### Honest limits

- **The re-benchmark magnitude is n=1.** 2025's −43k headline effect and 57k prior-month swing are
  one observation. The *mechanism* is documented in ADP's own words; the *size* for 2026 is unknown
  and could be materially smaller — BLS's own 2026 preliminary benchmark (−79k nonfarm / −178k
  private) is far smaller than 2025's −911k and 2024's −598k, which argues for a smaller ADP
  adjustment too, though ADP benchmarks to QCEW directly rather than to the CES revision.
- **The "every September" framing is secondary-sourced.** ADP's own releases describe a two-step
  (preliminary in September, full in February) for the 2025→2026 cycle; the characterisation that
  this recurs annually comes from press summary, not from an ADP methodology page — `adpresearch.com`
  returned **404** on the direct methodology fetch this session. Recorded as a failure, not passed over.
- **Leg 2's inference is negative evidence.** "The August report contains no benchmark language,
  therefore the adjustment is pending" is a strong reading of an absence. It could instead mean ADP
  applied the 2026 QCEW silently, or has changed the cadence. The 09-30 release itself adjudicates,
  and the *This month* falsifier is written to catch exactly that.
- **A source conflict on the 08-28 benchmark direction is unresolved and is recorded, not
  averaged.** The sibling `jobs-2026-09-04` doc records the BLS preliminary benchmark as **−79,000**
  nonfarm / **−178,000** private (BLS primary, 08-28). Two aggregators seen this session (ZeroHedge,
  cryptobriefing) headline it as payrolls "revised **higher** for the first time since 2022." The
  sibling's primary-sourced figures govern here; the conflict is flagged so it is not rediscovered.
- **A year-conflated search result was discarded, not used.** A search for the September ADP outlook
  surfaced NBC coverage of private payrolls falling **32,000 in September** with a +45k consensus,
  presented without a year. That is the **2025** print (published 2025-10-01) — confirmed by its wage
  figures (job-changers 7.1% → 6.6%, stayers 4.5%), which do not match the 2026 series
  (job-changers 7.3%, stayers 4.4% in August 2026). It is used below only as a dated 2025 analogue.
- **No price instruments were run** — macro-print mode carries none. Index and volatility readings

- **Date is `confirmed` as of this PR** on ADP's own release plus FRED corroboration; ADP is not

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `confirmed`).** Stand aside on the 2026-09-30 08:15 ET ADP release, and treat its
headline as **non-informative about the labor market until its benchmark note is read**. This is a
firmer refusal than the 09-02 doc's guard-shaped stance, and it rests on a different reason: not
"ADP is a noisy predictor of BLS" (true, and still true) but "this specific edition is a restatement
of the series, published fifteen minutes before the Fed's target inflation measure, on the most
crowded session this calendar tracks." No directional macro position; no rate-sensitive paper entry
initiated off this print in either direction; any read carried into
[`jobs-2026-10-02.md`](jobs-2026-10-02.md) as context, explicitly flagged for benchmark distortion.
No base-case number is offered — offering one for a re-benchmark print would be false precision, and
the *This month* call is a refusal rather than a forecast for exactly that reason.

**Kill switches:**

- **The 09-30 release carries no QCEW/benchmark language and no outsized prior-month revision** —
  this doc's central premise is wrong; the print is an ordinary ADP report and the 09-02 doc's
  guard-shaped framing applies unchanged. Score it in the close-out either way.
- **ADP publishes a methodology note, or a September preview, quantifying the 2026 re-benchmark
  before 09-30** — closes the n=1 limit; re-anchor the "how large" question to the stated number
  rather than to 2025's −43k.
- **A published ADP-specific September consensus emerges (expected ~09-28)** — replaces the
  TradingEconomics carry-forward placeholder; note explicitly whether the forecasters have priced the
  re-benchmark, because in 2025 they had not (+45k consensus against a −32k print).
- **A federal funding disruption re-emerges before 09-30** — would revive the blackout branch leg 4
  declares dead. The CR runs to **2026-12-11**; the live cliff moved to
  [`cr-expiry-2026-12-11.md`](cr-expiry-2026-12-11.md), not here.
- **The 09-16 FOMC resolves the hike question decisively in either direction** — changes what any
  labor read is worth in the back half of September; reweight at the following pulse.
- **ADP moves or cancels the 09-30 date** — reverts the header to `estimate`. Nothing observed
  suggests it; ADP named the date in its own release and FRED carries it.

No forward test is registered in [`forward-tests.md`](../forward-tests.md): the stance is a refusal
with no directional content, and the *This month* call's falsifier is self-scoring at the release.
