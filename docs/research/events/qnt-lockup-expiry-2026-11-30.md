## Initial research

### The question
Does the 2026-11-30 QNT lock-up expiry put enough new supply on the market to be worth a position, a hedge, or a stand-aside, and on what date does it actually fire?

**Verdict: the date is firm, the potential supply is the largest bucket in the study but gated, and the price premise is absent — stand aside.** One clean 180-day period, no early-release trigger, so unlike CBRS (staged) and XE (shortened by a blackout) there is no date risk beyond a discretionary waiver. The size sits in the study's top bucket (>=7.6x), whose full-window CAR was -2.4% (t -2.2 on the run-in) — weaker evidence than the middle bucket, and the study itself calls size "no dial". The Q3 print on 11-05 is the larger catalyst in the lead-up and is not a lock-up signal.

### Leg 1 — the date: SUPPORTED
The 424B4 says the lock-up parties "restrict the sale of the shares of our Class A common stock and certain other securities held by them for a period of 180 days after the date of this prospectus" (dated 2026-06-03; filed 2026-06-05). 180 days after 06-03 is 2026-11-30 (a Monday); day 181 is Tuesday 2026-12-01, the lockup study's day 0. The only early path is that J.P. Morgan and Morgan Stanley "may... release the securities... in whole or in part at any time"; the underwriting section lists carve-outs (gifts, estate planning, affiliate transfers, sell-to-cover for taxes, 10b5-1 plans that sell nothing in the period) but no earnings, price or blackout trigger. The Q2 10-Q repeats "a period of 180 days after the date of the IPO". The Registration Rights Agreement adds a separate 180-day market standoff for its parties. EDGAR lists no 8-K amending any of this. One drafting point matters for timing: holders may privately notify the company of a demand and prepare a confidential submission during the period, so a registered sale can be staged to go public right after 11-30.

### Leg 2 — supply: MIXED (the biggest bucket, but an Up-C and gated)
Class A outstanding 36,893,857 on 2026-08-13 (10-Q cover) plus 2,369,528 shares issued to the Commerce Department on 2026-09-08 (8-K) = about 39.26M; Class B 226,414,285, each paired one-for-one with a Common Unit of Quantinuum Holdings redeemable for Class A. Economic total about 265.7M. The IPO sold 28.5M shares (28.0M plus 0.5M of the 4.2M over-allotment, 10-Q), **all primary — no selling shareholder** — so the free float is 28.5M (~$958M at $33.60) and about **237.2M** are locked or restricted: 8.3x the float, ~108 days of the 20-day average volume (2.19M) or ~123 days of the 60-day (1.93M). Most of it (226.4M) is units, not shares: to sell, a unitholder must redeem into Class A (at the company's election, newly issued shares or a cash payment from a contemporaneous offering) and then either sell under Rule 144 or have the company register the resale. As an affiliate the Honeywell group may sell in any three months the greater of 1% of Class A (~0.39M) or the four-week average weekly volume (~11M, my arithmetic from the 20-day average), about **4.6% of the 237M, or ~9% of Honeywell's own ~125M** (47.4% of the voting power at 2026-06-30 from the 10-Q, applied to 263.3M voting shares; my arithmetic). Larger sales need a registered offering under the Registration Rights Agreement; the company has filed none, and is not S-3 eligible until about June 2027 (12 months reporting; my reading of the form's rule). Honeywell is an operating-company strategic holder, not a fund with a fixed life; it has said nothing public about selling that I could find (a web search 2026-10-31 found no report of a sale plan). The Commerce shares are a separate, small block with a shelf due within three months of 2026-09-08 (by 12-08): an S-1 that may appear on EDGAR in the window without being a sponsor sale. The company holds $2.1B of cash (10-Q, 2026-06-30), so it has no need to sell stock itself.
