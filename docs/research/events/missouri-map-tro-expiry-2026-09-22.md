### Conviction legs, tested

1. **2026-09-22 is what the order produces — SUPPORTED, and this upgrades a computed date to a
   read one.** Doc. 35, verbatim: "*This order remains in effect for 14 days from the date of
   issuance. The Court will set a preliminary injunction hearing by separate order.*" Signed
   2026-09-08; 09-08 + 14 = **2026-09-22**, a Tuesday. The proposing lane's arithmetic survives its
   own primary-source check. **Status stays `estimate` regardless** — the confirmed-prefix taxonomy

   gap the [09-08 ledger](missouri-map-ballot-deadline-2026-09-08.md), the

   [midterm ledger](midterm-elections-2026-11-03.md) each recorded, and this lane may not
   self-confirm an event it discovered in-sweep.

2. **Fed. R. Civ. P. 65(b)(2) is not the mechanism the date came from — REFUTED as to the
   proposal's reasoning, while its answer stands.** The rule reads: "*The order expires at the time
   after entry — not to exceed 14 days — that the court sets, unless before that time the court, for
   good cause, extends it for a like period or the adverse party consents to a longer extension.*"
   But 65(b) is the **without-notice** track: 65(b)(1) governs a TRO issued "*without written or
   oral notice to the adverse party or its attorney.*" Doc. 35 forecloses that reading twice —
   "*All parties, including Intervenors People Not Politicians and Richard von Glahn, had notice and
   submitted briefing. Docs. 2, 12, 19, 20, 29, 30*" and "*the Court finds that a hearing is not
   necessary because all parties have participated fully through their briefing, and because all
   parties agree on the material facts.*" The docket corroborates: doc. 13 ordered every party and
   putative intervenor to brief by **noon 09-07**, doc. 32 granted intervention **before** the TRO
   issued, and memoranda arrived from both sides (docs. 29, 30). **So the 14 days is a discretionary
   term that happens to equal the rule's ceiling.** The distinction is not academic — a term a judge
   chose is one the same judge can re-choose or replace, without needing 65(b)(2)'s "good cause."

3. **The TRO issued on notice is the reason an appeal exists at all — SUPPORTED as to the parties'
   behaviour, MIXED as to whether it succeeds.** A TRO is ordinarily unappealable under 28 U.S.C.
   § 1292(a)(1); the recognised exception is a TRO with "*the trappings of a preliminary
   injunction*" — adversarial notice, an effective hearing, injunction-like practical effect
   (secondary sources only this session: a Baylor Law survey of appealable TROs and a practitioner
   treatment; **no Eighth Circuit case was read direct**, and this leg is graded MIXED for that
   reason). The intervenors acted on that theory within hours: **doc. 36**, "Emergency NOTICE OF
   APPEAL," entered 09-08 with the remark "*Document emailed to COA on 9/8/2026*"; **doc. 38**,
   emergency motion to stay doc. 35; and in the Eighth Circuit, **No. 26-2797** civil case docketed
   09-08 **19:25 CT**, originating-court documents filed **19:32**, appellee appearances **19:54**,
   and the **emergency motion for stay pending appeal 19:55**. Whether the court of appeals agrees
   it has jurisdiction is the open question — and it is the question that decides whether 09-22
   matters.

4. **Three mechanisms land before 09-22 and any one moots it — SUPPORTED, and it is the central
   finding.** (a) **The Eighth Circuit**: an emergency stay motion filed on the evening of 09-08 in
   an election case with a September operative deadline is not a matter that sits for two weeks.
   (b) **The district court's own PI ruling**: doc. 35 says the court "*will set a preliminary
   injunction hearing by separate order,*" and a granted preliminary injunction **replaces** a TRO
   rather than letting it expire — plaintiffs moved for both in a single motion (doc. 2, "MOTION for
   Preliminary Injunction and TRO"). (c) **UOCAVA, 2026-09-19** — three days *earlier*, tracked as
   [missouri-uocava-ballot-mailing-2026-09-19](missouri-uocava-ballot-mailing-2026-09-19.md), the
   date ballots are transmitted under whichever map is then operative. **09-22 is therefore a
   ceiling on a placeholder**: the date by which the current order stops operating *if nothing else
   happens first*, in a posture where several things are already scheduled to happen first.

5. **The merits reasoning is narrow and reliance-based, which is why it is fast — SUPPORTED.** From
   doc. 35 direct: likelihood of success found on the **Equal Protection** claim — "*In the August
   primaries, voters have already cast their ballots and candidates already ran using the HB 1 map …
   Replacing the HB 1 map after the primary would sort hundreds of thousands of voters into
   different districts*" — with the **Article I, Section 2** theory resting on *United States v.
   Classic*, 313 U.S. 299, 318 (1941): "*[w]here the state law has made the primary an integral part
   of the procedure of choice … the right of the elector to have his ballot counted at the primary,
   is likewise included in the right protected by Article I, Section 2.*" Imminence is pinned to
   **Mo. Rev. Stat. § 115.125(2)**, the eighth-Tuesday ballot-finalisation deadline — *the same
   09-08 date the [sibling event](missouri-map-ballot-deadline-2026-09-08.md) tracks*, which is why
   the two orders landed the same day.

6. **"Purcell in reverse" is the court's own phrase, held expressly — SUPPORTED.** Doc. 35: "*courts
   normally invoke the Purcell principle to caution against federal-court intervention … But Purcell
   does not cut against injunctive relief here. This case presents Purcell in reverse, but with no
   less weight … Issuing a TRO here would preserve, rather than disturb, the election rules that
   candidates, election officials, and voters have relied on.*" The proposing lane recorded this as
