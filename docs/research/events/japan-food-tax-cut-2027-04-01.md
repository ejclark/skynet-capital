### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The affected CPI weight is 2,152 per 10,000 — SUPPORTED, primary, and it closes the parent
   ledger's largest stated gap.** The Statistics Bureau publishes the 2025-base item list as an
   Excel workbook; its `List` sheet carries a `Weight` block with `Actual number` and `Per 10000`
   columns for Japan and for the Ku-area of Tokyo. Walked cell-by-cell, the Japan per-10,000
   weights are: **All items 10,000 · Food 2,754 · Alcoholic beverages 111 · Meals outside the home
   508** (= *Eating out* **491** + *School lunch* **17**) · **Newspapers 61 · Fresh food 379 · Food
   less fresh food 2,375 · All items less fresh food 9,621 · All items less fresh food and energy
   8,872.** Japan's reduced rate covers food and non-alcoholic beverages **excluding dining out**,
   and excludes alcohol. So the affected basket is **2,754 − 111 − 491 = 2,152**, i.e. **21.52%** of
   the CPI. School lunch (17) is retained as reduced-rate-eligible; treating it either way moves the
   drag by ~0.01pp and is immaterial. Cross-check on the published aggregate: **2,375 − 111 − 491 =
   1,773**, which is the affected basket less fresh food, and it reconciles exactly.

2. **The drag is −1.39pp headline and −1.19pp core — SUPPORTED, derived, and the core figure is the
   one that matters.** Cutting a tax-inclusive price from 108 to 101 is a **1.01/1.08 − 1 =
   −6.4815%** price change. At full pass-through:

   | Index | Affected weight | Share | Drag |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | All items (headline) | 2,152 / 10,000 | **21.52%** | **−1.395pp** |
   | All items less fresh food (**core — the BoJ's target**) | 1,773 / 9,621 | **18.43%** | **−1.194pp** |
   | All items less fresh food and energy (core-core) | 1,773 / 8,872 | **19.98%** | **−1.295pp** |

   **Core takes the smallest drag of the three**, because fresh food (379) is stripped from the
   denominator but sits wholly inside the cut. That is the non-obvious result, and it is the reason
   this leg matters: the parent ledger's 1.0–1.4pp band was stated against "year-on-year CPI"
   unqualified, and a reader would naturally reach for the top of it. The BoJ projects core. **The
   number to carry into the January Outlook is −1.19pp.**

3. **The method reproduces an independent house estimate, and that estimate is for a policy that was
   not approved — SUPPORTED, and it is a live correction to the number in circulation.** Nomura
   (January 2026; Kyohei Morita, Kengo Tanahashi, Uichiro Nozaki, Yuki Ito), verbatim: *"cutting the
   consumption tax rate on food to zero would lower CPI inflation by as much as 1.5%."* Running leg
   2's method at a **zero** rate gives 1.00/1.08 − 1 = −7.4074% on the same 21.52% weight =
   **−1.594pp** headline — Nomura's figure to within ~0.1pp, from an independently sourced weight.
   The convergence validates the method. It also exposes the trap: **the approved policy is 1%, not
   0%**, worth **87.5%** of the zero-rate effect. A "~1.5pp drag" applied to the enacted bill
   overstates it by roughly 0.2pp, and by ~0.35pp against core.

4. **The drag maps onto exactly one fiscal year — SUPPORTED, arithmetic, and it removes the
   board's easiest escape.** The cut runs two years from 2027-04-01, so it is in the price level
   from the **April 2027** CPI (released ~2027-05-22) through the **March 2029** print. A y/y rate
   is depressed only while the *comparison* straddles the change: **April 2027 → March 2028**, which
   is **FY2027 exactly**. FY2028 is unaffected (both sides cut). The **reversal** — a symmetric
   *boost* — is **April 2029 → March 2030 = FY2029 exactly**. Two consequences. **For the January
   Outlook:** FY2027 is precisely the year that report projects, and the drag cannot be spread
   across the projection horizon to soften it — it is all in one row. **For the tell:** the July-2026
   Outlook put FY2027 core at **+2.4%** and FY2028 at **+2.0%**; booking −1.19pp prints FY2027 at
   **~1.2%**, roughly **0.8pp below** FY2028. An inversion that size is not a judgement call.

5. **Japan has never cut this tax before, so there is no domestic precedent for how it is handled —
   MIXED, and it is why the footnote fork is genuinely open.** The consumption tax was introduced in
   1989 and has only ever risen (1997, 2014, 2019, when the 8% reduced rate for food was created).
   Multiple syndications describe this measure as the **first-ever cut**. The BoJ's standing Outlook
   convention strips *"the effects of consumption tax **hikes**"* — wording built for the only
   direction that had ever occurred. **Honest weakness, inherited and not closed here:** that
   footnote text is search-cited in the parent ledger and was **not extracted verbatim** by this
   session either; the Outlook PDFs remain the unread primary. What this leg adds is the reason the
   gap is not pedantry — there is no prior cut whose treatment could have settled the convention by
   practice.

6. **The passage risk is structurally smaller than the press framing — SUPPORTED on the seat
   arithmetic, this lane's own reading on the constitutional route.** The cabinet approved the bill
   on **2026-08-05** (Nikkei Asia; Japan Today, *"Cabinet OKs 2-year consumption tax cut on food to
   1% from next April"*), for submission to an extraordinary session. Langley Esquire's
   **2026-08-30** briefing frames the venue as *"whether a cut that leaves a ¥10 trillion hole can
   pass a chamber the coalition does not control"* — and it is true that the coalition does not hold
   the House of Councillors. But the **2026-02-08** general election returned **LDP 316** and
   **Nippon Ishin 36** of **465** (Nippon.com, verbatim: *"The LDP ended election day with 316 out
   of the 465 in the chamber—more than two thirds of the total. The addition of Ishin's 36 seats
   brings the coalition total to 352, a massive majority that allows the parties to override
   rejections of legislation in the House of Councillors."*). Two-thirds of 465 is **310** — the
   **LDP clears it alone**. A consumption-tax amendment is ordinary legislation, so **Article 59(2)**
   makes an upper-house rejection overridable. *The constitutional-route step is this lane's own
   reading and is labelled as such;* the seat counts are sourced. **Counterweights:** two-thirds is
   of members *present*, not of the full chamber; using the override on a flagship tax bill carries
   political cost the government may prefer to avoid; and the accompanying **funding** measures are
   a separate fight that concessions could reshape without touching the headline rate.

7. **Therefore the binding constraint is lead time, not votes — MIXED, and it is what the pulses
   should track.** Article 59(4) permits the lower house to deem a bill rejected only after the
   Councillors fail to act for **60 days**. Submitted in early October, that clock expires in early
   December — inside a typical extraordinary session, so the override path is available *within the
   session*. Slip, and enactment moves to the ordinary session convening in January, leaving perhaps
   eight weeks before 2027-04-01. Japan's 2019 introduction of the dual-rate system came with a long
   runway precisely because point-of-sale, invoicing and accounting systems must carry a **third**
   rate. **This is the mechanism by which "the cut is deferred" actually happens** — not a defeated
   vote, a calendar that runs out. Stated as MIXED because no source read today puts a required
   lead time on the retail systems change; the inference is this lane's.

### Honest limits

pass the full 7pp of tax through to shelf prices. Japan's 2014 hike is the usual evidence for high
pass-through on food, but that was an *increase*, and the incentive to absorb rather than pass on is
asymmetric between directions. This session found no study of Japanese pass-through on a
consumption-tax **cut**, because there has never been one (leg 5). A partial pass-through scales
every figure down proportionally, and that is precisely what
**FT-japan-food-tax-cut-2027-04-01-3** exists to measure. **The scope of the cut is taken from press
descriptions, not from the bill text** — this session did not retrieve the cabinet-approved bill, so
whether **newspapers** (weight 61) ride along with the food rate is unresolved; including them moves
headline to −1.43pp and core to −1.24pp, which is why the base case excludes them and states the
bound. **The BoJ Outlook footnote is still unread verbatim**, inherited from the parent ledger and
not closed here — the PDFs' text layers resisted extraction there and this session did not retry.
**The Article 59 route in legs 6 and 7 is this lane's own constitutional reading**, labelled as such;
