## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The short-auction ledger ([`uk-gilt-auction-short-2026-10-27`](uk-gilt-auction-short-2026-10-27.md)) filed this event as the better test of UK fiscal credibility than the short sale. The question: **does this auction tell anyone anything — about long-end gilt demand after the Budget, about the US — and what would a bad one look like?** One-line verdict: **a thin-data, low-information event; watch the Budget-day calendar, the 11-03 size and the cover/tail against 2.50x / 1.0bp, and trade nothing.**

### Method

Primary sources for every fact below, fetched direct 2026-10-26 as PDFs and decoded locally from their text layer: the DMO's calendar notice (28 Aug), consultation agenda (17 Aug), and result notices for the 2056 syndication (08 Sep), the 2056 tender (26 Mar), and the 4⅞% 2036 auctions (29 Sep, 20 Oct). ### 1. The date and the stock — SUPPORTED, and the proposal's source line was wrong

The 28 Aug notice's Table 1 reads *"Tuesday 10 November · 10.00am · 5⅜% Treasury Gilt 2056 · Tuesday 3 November"*; the 17 Aug agenda types it *"a long conventional gilt"* and plans **one** long conventional auction for the quarter (a programmatic long tender on 10-13 replaced a second). The proposal and the first-draft source said the gilt was not named in either notice; it is, in the 28 Aug table (the short ledger carries the same slip for its own 10-27 line, which the notice names as 4⅝% 2032 — its owner's next pulse can correct it). What is **not** announced is the size (11-03), and the calendar's own footnote says dates and gilts stay *"subject to demand and market conditions"*; a Budget-day remit revision is the one scheduled thing that can change this line (`FT-…-1`).

### 2. The stock — SUPPORTED: it is the house's most recently tested long benchmark

The 5⅜% Treasury Gilt 2056 (ISIN GB00BT7J0241, matures 31 Jan 2056; DMO notice 13 May 2025) was launched by syndication in May 2025. The DMO's own result notices give three dated reads: **03-26 tender** £300m, **3.84x**, tail **0.3bp**, average **5.517%**, amount in issue then £5.904bn; **09-08 syndication** £4.25bn re-opening at **£93.806 / 5.8168%**, 71% UK-placed, a "volatile market environment" per the DMO's own words; so the stock cleared **+30bp** in 5½ months. A £4.25bn tranche settled 09-09, so the 11-10 auction adds to a line that has just been topped up: size is the real unknown.

### 3. Demand at the long end through a 6% 30-year — SUPPORTED for tenders, UNKNOWN for an auction

Press-relayed: the 30-year gilt breached 6% on 10-01 and printed 6.03% on 10-07 (roic.ai headlines; not re-derived). Through that window the DMO's 2063 tender (10-13, £500m) took **3.98x** at 5.802% against 3.51x previously. **Tenders are small (£0.3–0.5bn), voluntary and dealer-heavy; an auction of several £bn is not.** The one long conventional auction result found is 4⅜% 2054 on 2025-07-01 (£2.25bn, **2.75x**, tail **0.3bp**; press-relayed, the DMO's own page not reachable). So **2.50x** is a floor set just under one observation, not a forecast, and the confidence is Low.

### 4. The medium end in the same weeks — SUPPORTED, and a correction to the neighbouring ledger

Direct from the DMO: 4⅞% 2036, £4.25bn, **09-29 3.34x, tail 0.5bp**, 5.383%; **10-20 3.61x, tail 0.2bp**, total bids £15.33bn, 5.418%. The short-auction ledger's "all seven conventional auctions had tails 0.1–0.4bp" omits 09-29, a 0.5bp tail inside its own sample window, so its 0.5bp kill switch is not outside the sample. That is its owner's to amend on its own pulse (rows append only); this ledger uses **1.0bp** for the long end, where tails run wider.

A gilt move that day cannot be pinned on the auction from the ETF alone; the result notice (cover, tail) is the clean read.
