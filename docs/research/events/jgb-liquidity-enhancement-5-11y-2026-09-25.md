## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This entry was proposed on 2026-09-09 by the
[`jgb-liquidity-enhancement-5-11y-2026-10-22`](jgb-liquidity-enhancement-5-11y-2026-10-22.md) adjacency
sweep, on one job: it is the **last print of this instrument before 2026-10-22** and therefore **the first
chance to score** that ledger's headline — a coupon preference in what bidders take, filed with a kill
switch requiring *two consecutive negative* coupon-weighted prints after 2026-08-26's first negative.

So the question this ledger had to answer first was not "what will 09-25 do." It was:

**is the coupon reading identified at all, and does the proposed kill switch have the power to kill it?**

**One-line verdict:** **no, and no.** The sector-level ordering the family reported is a real
*description* of this zone, but at the **issue level** — 3,255 issue-auction observations across these 32
auctions, with standard errors block-bootstrapped **by auction** — the coupon coefficient's 95% interval
contains zero in **every** specification, **including with no controls at all**. The only coefficient that
clears zero is **log float**, and it is negative. The **1–5y zone**, 16 prints the family had never read,
**refutes the monotone ordering outright**. And the "August broke ranks" print that motivated this entry
was never a coupon reversal: the take went to the **longest-remaining** 20-year paper in the band, which
is the same thing bidders do in the prints the coupon story counts as *confirmations*.

### Method

Sourced research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md), rates mode — no price
instruments, `symbols: []`. Every number below is computed this session from a primary fetched direct on
**2026-09-09** unless labelled otherwise. Nothing is inherited from the sibling ledgers; where a number
they reported is repeated here it was recomputed first, and the two places they disagree are stated.

- **MOF auction calendars** — **35** monthly slates, `2401e`–`2611e`, all HTTP 200, parsed row by row for
  the zone label, the date and the announcement-link state. This produced **70 Liquidity Enhancement rows**
  across the three zones.
- **MOF auction announcements** — **48** pages (`announcement/auct<date>e.htm`), all HTTP 200: all 32
  mid-zone auctions and all 16 completed 1–5y auctions, parsed for the **offering amount** and the
  **eligible issue-number ranges** of every security.
- **MOF result pages and their "Issues Re-opened" companions** — **96** pages
  (`eresul/eresul<date>.htm` + `eresul<date>a.htm`), all HTTP 200, for those same 48 auctions, parsed cell
  by cell into bids, accepted, both spreads, and **every reopened issue number and amount**.
- **MOF Auction Results for JGBs workbook** (`past_auction_results/Auction_Results_for_JGBs.xls`, 492,032
  bytes) — the 2-, 5-, 10-, 20-, 30- and 40-year sheets read for **issue number → maturity date → nominal
  coupon → cumulative issued amount**, summing every reopening auction of each issue.
- **MOF JGB par yield curve** — `jgbs/reference/interest_rate/data/jgbcm_all.csv` (1,176,385 bytes) merged
  with the current-month `jgbs/reference/interest_rate/jgbcm.csv`, **13,296 sessions from 1974-09-24
  through 2026-09-08**.
- **Yahoo Finance** for VIX. Labelled secondary wherever cited.

The panel is the new instrument. One row per **(auction, eligible issue)** — 3,255 rows here and 1,448 in
the 1–5y zone, **4,703** in total across **48** auctions. Each row carries the issue's share of that
auction's eligible stock, its share of what the auction actually took, its coupon, its remaining maturity
at the auction date, and its float. Because the dependent variable is a *share difference within an
auction*, auction-level effects are differenced out by construction; the standard errors are then
**block-bootstrapped by auction** (2,000 replications, resampling whole auctions), because 3,255
observations coming from 32 auctions are nowhere near 3,255 independent draws — and that single correction
is what changes the answer.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The coupon preference is not identified in this zone — REFUTED, and this is the finding.** Dependent
variable: each issue's overweight ratio (share of take ÷ share of eligible stock). Auction-clustered 95%
intervals from 2,000 block-bootstrap replications:

| Specification (5–11y, n=3,255 rows / 32 auctions) | coupon | remaining maturity | log float |
|---|---|---|---|
| coupon alone | **+0.205 [−0.112, +0.585]** | — | — |
| + remaining maturity + log float | −0.021 [−0.447, +0.420] | −0.019 [−0.152, +0.117] | −0.241 [−0.510, +0.041] |
| + security dummies | +0.192 [−0.253, +0.685] | +0.050 [−0.119, +0.209] | **−0.687 [−1.265, −0.126]** |

**Coupon never clears zero — not even alone, before any control is applied.** The naive OLS t-statistic on
the univariate coefficient is +1.90; clustering by auction is what removes it, which is the correct
inference here and not a matter of taste. The one coefficient whose interval excludes zero is **log float,
negative**: a larger outstanding issue is taken *less* than its share. That is a **scarcity** reading, and
it is what a facility literally named "Liquidity Enhancement" is designed to serve — bidders come to it to
source paper that is hard to find elsewhere. Coupon correlates with scarcity because old, small-float
issues are also the high-coupon ones, which is the collinearity the sibling named as its own limit
(*"coupon and issue age are collinear… this ledger cannot say which of those three is doing the work"*).
The panel separates them, and coupon is the passenger.

**2. Out of sample the monotone ordering is refuted — REFUTED, on a zone the family had never read.** The
1–5y auction spans **five** securities against this zone's three, and their coupons span 25× — the widest
coupon spread available anywhere in the family at matched remaining maturity. All 16 completed prints,
auction-clustered:

| Security (1–5y zone) | Wtd-avg coupon | Share of eligible stock | **Overweight, all 16** | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10-Year No.348-363 | **0.100%** (lowest in zone) | 37.1% | **1.07×** | 0.77 – 1.36 |
| 5-Year No.154-185 | 0.713% | 34.6% | **0.49×** (most underweight) | 0.24 – 0.82 |
| 2-Year No.476-488 | 1.227% | 10.3% | 0.78× | 0.25 – 1.45 |
| 20-Year No.97-129 | 2.056% | 17.3% | **2.12×** | 1.63 – 2.71 |
| 30-Year No.1-5 | **2.507%** (highest in zone) | 0.6% | **0.61×** (n=8) | 0.00 – 1.81 |

A monotone coupon preference predicts the order 10Y < 5Y < 2Y < 20Y < 30Y. The observed order is
**5Y < 30Y < 2Y < 10Y < 20Y**. The lowest-coupon security is overweight, the highest-coupon security is
underweight, and 10-Year's interval (0.77–1.36) does not overlap 5-Year's (0.24–0.82) despite 5-Year
carrying **seven times** the coupon. The zone's *aggregate* coupon-weighted overweight **is** strongly
positive (**+0.296pp, CI +0.149 to +0.455, 15 of 16 prints**) — the aggregate statistic replicates and the
mechanism does not, which is exactly the signature of an unidentified summary. At the issue level that
aggregate is fully absorbed: coupon alone **+0.597 [+0.346, +0.859]**; adding remaining maturity, coupon
falls to **+0.228 [−0.204, +0.629]** while **remaining maturity is +0.672 [+0.186, +1.136]**.

**3. "August broke ranks" was a remaining-maturity take, not a coupon reversal — REFUTED as posed.** The
2026-08-26 print is the whole reason this entry exists. Its ¥401.2bn of 20-year takeup, issue by issue
against a ¥75.9tn 20-year eligible stock:

| Issue | Coupon | Remaining | Float | Taken | Overweight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No.136 | 1.6% | 5.57y | ¥1,095bn | ¥11.4bn | 1.97× |
| No.144 · 147 · 148 · 151 · 152 · 153 | 1.2–1.6% | **6.57 – 8.82y** | ¥1.1–3.3tn | ¥19.4bn total | **0.01× – 0.51×** |
| No.155 | 1.0% | 9.32y | ¥3,276bn | ¥84.5bn | **4.88×** |
| No.156 · 157 · 158 · 159 | 0.2–0.6% | 9.57 – 10.32y | ¥3.0tn each | ¥156.0bn | 1.22× – **5.55×** |
| No.160 | 0.7% | **10.57y** | ¥2,797bn | ¥113.0bn | **7.64×** |
| No.161 | 0.6% | **10.82y** | ¥2,529bn | ¥16.9bn | 1.26× |

The take is a step function in **remaining maturity**: everything from 6.57y to 8.82y was refused, and
everything from 9.32y to 10.82y — the longest 20-year paper the 5–11y band contains — was bought. Those
issues are also the lowest-coupon ones, **because inside a single sector an older issue is both
higher-coupon and shorter-dated**, which is precisely the caution the sibling wrote down. Reading the same
print through the coupon lens turns "bidders bought the long end of the band" into "the coupon preference
reversed." **They are the same event.**

**4. The proposed kill switch has almost no power — REFUTED.** The sibling registered "two consecutive
negative coupon-weighted prints" as the death of the reading. Recomputed here from the same 32 auctions:

| Window | n | Mean coupon overweight | Negative prints | P(two consecutive) under independence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Whole series 2024-01 → 2026-08 | 32 | **+0.158pp** (clustered CI +0.011 to +0.311) | **13 / 32 (40.6%)** | **≈ 16.5%** |
| Last 12 (the window the sibling quoted) | 12 | +0.448pp | 1 / 12 (8.3%) | ≈ 0.7% |

The same kill switch is a **one-in-six** coincidence or a **one-in-140** event depending only on which
window it is calibrated to — a **24× spread**, and nothing in the data adjudicates between the windows
because the 2025 break date was itself chosen after seeing the series (the sibling filed that as MIXED and
was right to). With the whole-series prior (mean +0.158pp, sd **0.451**), the probability that 2026-09-25
prints negative is roughly **36%**. A negative print on 09-25 would therefore be an ordinary draw, not
evidence of anything.

**5. What actually replicates across both zones — SUPPORTED, and it is less than the family thought.**
Setting coupon aside, no *single* variable survives in both zones either: **float** is the significant one
here (−0.687, CI −1.265 to −0.126) and does not clear zero in the 1–5y zone (−0.358, CI −1.036 to +0.213);
**remaining maturity** is the significant one there (+0.672, CI +0.186 to +1.136) and is flat here
(−0.019, CI −0.152 to +0.117). The honest summary is that takeup is dominated by **auction-specific
sourcing** — a bidder needing particular issues — around a weak standing tilt toward scarce and
long-in-band paper. The sector-level ordering survives only in part:

| 5–11y sector | Wtd-avg coupon | **Overweight, all 32** | 95% CI (auction-clustered) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10-Year No.364-382 | 0.854% | **0.64×** | **0.43 – 0.86** |
| 20-Year No.130-161 | 1.273% | **1.31×** | **1.11 – 1.51** |
| 30-Year No.6-26 | 2.255% | 1.64× | 1.10 – 2.23 |

**10Y genuinely below 20Y** — the intervals do not overlap, and that leg is real. **20Y versus 30Y is not
distinguishable**: the intervals overlap across almost their whole width, on a series whose 30-year values
range 0.00× to 7.84× (sd 1.73) against a ¥10.2tn denominator. The family's headline should be quoted as
*"10-year paper is taken light"*, which the data supports, and not as a monotone coupon ladder.

**6. The sibling's own controlled test is not controlled on float — MIXED, and it survives weakened.**
Re-measuring the two transferred blocks as of 2026-10-22 from the workbook:

| Transferred block | Issues | Total | **Mean float per issue** | Remaining maturity | Coupon |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20-Year No.161-177 | 17 | ¥43.5tn | **¥2,557bn** | 10.66 – 14.66y | 0.467% |
| 30-Year No.27-34 | 8 | ¥13.7tn | **¥1,719bn** | 10.91 – 14.41y | 2.265% |

Remaining maturity is controlled, as the sibling said. **Float is not** — the 30-year block's issues are
**1.49× scarcer** individually, the same direction as the take, so its "only the coupon differs" claim is
not true and the experiment does not separate coupon from scarcity either. Its qualitative point stands
(the block that got bought was the *smaller* one, which cuts against crowd-out); its identification does
not. Two measurement notes, both in the sibling's favour on the ratio and against it on the level: this
session computes the blocks at **¥43.5tn / ¥13.7tn** against its **¥5.7tn / ¥1.6tn**, because the workbook
lists each issue's multiple reopening auctions and they must be summed — verified directly on 10-Year

### Honest limits

- **This refutes an interpretation, not the observations.** Every number the sibling reported reproduces
  here (its 2026-08-26 coupon overweight −0.030pp against this session's −0.039pp; whole-series +0.181pp
  against +0.158pp; sector ordering 0.62/1.32/1.86× against 0.64/1.31/1.64×). The disagreement is entirely
  about what those numbers *identify*, and about the uncertainty attached to them.
- **Clustering by auction is a choice, and it is the one doing the work.** Naive OLS gives coupon a
  t-statistic of +1.90 univariate; the block bootstrap over 32 auctions gives an interval spanning zero.
  Clustering is correct — 3,255 rows come from 32 auctions and a single bidder's sourcing decision moves
  dozens of rows at once — but a reader who rejected it would reach the sibling's conclusion, and that
  should be stated rather than hidden.
- **Float is measured as face issued, not float.** It sums the workbook's auction-accepted amounts and
  ignores BoJ holdings, buy-backs and anything else removed from the tradeable stock. BoJ ownership is
  heaviest in 10-year paper, so true scarcity of 10-year issues is **understated** — which cuts against
  this ledger's own scarcity reading, not for it.
- **The 30-year sector here is a ¥10.2tn denominator with 0.00×–7.84× realisations.** No claim about it
  should be quoted without its interval.
- **Neither zone's surviving variable survives in the other.** Float clears zero here and not in the 1-5y
  zone; remaining maturity clears zero there and not here. The honest reading is a weak standing tilt plus
  large auction-specific sourcing, not a second mechanism substituted for the first.
- **The 1-5y comparison is out of sample but not independent.** Same issuer, same facility, overlapping
  bidder base; it is a replication attempt, not a fresh draw.
- **The regressions are linear in coupon and remaining maturity** and pool across a zone redefinition.
  A non-linear preference (only the very highest coupons, say) would not show up.
- **The auction result does not exist**, and neither does its size or its issue list. Everything here is
  prior.
- **VIX is Yahoo, i.e. secondary**, and is a US reading with no mechanical bearing on a JGB auction; it is
  carried because the probe-ref contract asks for it.
- **The date and the size are both `estimate`.** MOF is a primary and the date is not in doubt; the label
  reflects an unannounced size, an unpublished issue list, and the gap in `market-events-data.ts`'s prefix
  taxonomy, which has no slot for a non-US sovereign debt office. Estimates widen caution and license
  nothing.
