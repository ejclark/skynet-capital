## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Does federal funding lapse after 2026-12-11, is that meaningfully more or less likely than the
2026-09-30 cliff this calendar already researched, and — the part the calendar actually needs — what
does either branch do to the eleven tracked events between 12-01 and year-end and to the January data
this book will be reading in six weeks?

**One-line verdict:** the September cliff was averted by an incentive that **expires on 2026-11-03**,
the one modern precedent for exactly this configuration (a December lame-duck deadline after a
midterm that changes control) is a **35-day shutdown**, and so this doc refuses to inherit its
sibling's aversion base case — while holding the same stance for a different reason: the equity
channel is a measured non-event, and the exposure that matters is whether the **December household
survey gets collected at all**.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is real but the bill is unsigned, and `estimate` is doing two jobs — SUPPORTED.** The
   House adopted the Senate-passed CR **370-48 on 2026-09-01**, funding agencies through **December
   11** (Roll Call, UPI, NPR, Breaking Defense, Defense One, Washington Times, all 09-01; the Senate
   had passed **90-6 on 2026-08-08**). Two independent reasons keep this `estimate`: **congress.gov
   returned 403**, so there is no bill-level primary, and **the signature is unverified** —
   whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions, fetched 2026-09-02, lists nothing past 2026-08-28 and no bill
   signings at all. Additionally, this lane may not self-confirm an event, and the calendar defines no
   `confirmed` source prefix for an act of Congress. Note the label is carrying *date* doubt here (is
   there a Dec 11 window at all?), which is the ordinary case — unlike its 09-30 sibling, where the
   date was statutory and only the *occurrence* was open.

2. **The reason the September cliff was averted is dated, and the date is 2026-11-03 — SUPPORTED, and
   this is the load-bearing fact of the document.** Every source describes the September deal in the
   same terms: the House vote "avert[ed] shutdown before midterms" (UPI), "push[ed] next shutdown
   showdown past midterms" (Washington Times), "punt[ed] a potential shutdown fight until after the
   midterm elections". Vice Chair Murray's own statement names the mechanism from the other side —
   "the fight must continue... before the new deadline of December 11" (Government Executive,
   2026-08-03). The 09-30 ledger's leg 3 built its base case on precisely this: *"the reporting is
   explicit that its purpose was to fund the government past the November 2026 midterms."* **A base
   case resting on a pre-election incentive cannot be carried across the election.** December 11 is
   the date that incentive was spent to buy.

3. **The lame-duck pattern is documented and points one way — SUPPORTED on the pattern, thin on n.**
   Government Executive (2026-08-06) states the historical rule directly: when "one House or both
   change hands in the midterms, they wait until a new Congress is seated before any final spending
   deals," which raises shutdown risk during lame-duck periods. The mechanism is obvious once stated
   — an incoming majority has every reason to wait for January, and an outgoing one has little left
   to trade. The same piece supplies the configuration match: the **2018 shutdown began 2018-12-21,
   ran 35 days, and ended only after Democrats took House control in the November midterms and
   Pelosi was seated as Speaker.** It also records that until 2018 every shutdown for four decades
   occurred under divided government, and that the last three all occurred under *unified*
   government — so the "unified control prevents lapses" intuition is already dead in this era.
   **Honest limit on this leg, stated plainly:** it is a pattern claim with n in the low single
   digits and no significance test, and — like the sibling's leg 3 — this doc has no instrument that
   measures political incentives. It is reasoning, not measurement.

4. **The election makes the pattern live rather than hypothetical — SUPPORTED, but on model output.**
   Democrats need three seats for the House and current forecasts put them at roughly **73.4%**
   (Race to the WH / Silver Bulletin, 2026-09-02); Government Executive's 08-06 piece cited "better
   than an 80% chance." Both are model output, they disagree by seven points, and neither is a
   measurement — both are on the record as such. The Senate is the more competitive map (Republicans
   currently 53-47, favoured to hold), which produces **divided government as the modal outcome** —
   exactly the configuration leg 3's pattern is about. This calendar's own
   [`georgia-senate-runoff-2026-12-01`](georgia-senate-runoff-2026-12-01.md) research is worth
   reading against this: its ledger measured the Georgia runoff branch as **near-dead** (a two-name
   ballot clears 50% by arithmetic), so the "Senate control still unresolved on 12-01" framing that
   the calendar entry for *this* event carries is **weaker than that entry states** — Mississippi is
   the surviving branch and is Safe R at all three handicappers. Recorded as a correction to my own
   entry's note, not to the runoff ledger.

5. **The 2018 analogue must NOT be used for the market claim, and this leg exists to stop a future
   session from doing so — REFUTED as market evidence.** The structural parallel is tight enough to
   be a trap: in 2018 the Fed hiked with an SEP on **12-19**, and the shutdown began **12-21**; in
   2026 the FOMC decides with an SEP on **12-09** and this deadline lands **12-11**. Two days, both
   times. And Q4 2018 was the worst December for equities since the Great Depression. **The
   attribution is wrong.** The selloff began **2018-12-17** — before the hike and four days before
   the lapse — and the sources are explicit that the **19.8% decline "came ahead of the government
   shutdown"**, driven by the Fed's signal that increases would continue into 2019 against a
   slowing economy (CNBC 2018-12-28; Yahoo Finance compilation). The shutdown started after the
   drawdown, not before it. **The correct reading is the inverse of the tempting one:** 2018 shows a
   December FOMC repricing the market and a lapse arriving into the wreckage without adding much.

6. **The equity channel is still close to nil — SUPPORTED, inherited, not re-derived.** Across **22
   funding gaps since 1976** the median S&P return *during* a shutdown is **0.0%** (+3.1% since
   2010), and more than half of the prior 21 were positive. The widely-quoted **+18.9% median
   12-month return after 10-day-plus shutdowns is again NOT used** — the 09-30 ledger rejected it for
   want of an unconditional-drift control and a tiny n, and that rejection carries here verbatim.
   The defensible claim is the during-shutdown one only.

7. **The informational channel moves forward one reference month and lands on a knife-edge —
   SUPPORTED on mechanism, UNRESOLVED on the decisive detail, and this is the finding worth the
   session.** The 09-30 ledger established the permanence: in the 2025 lapse the October CPI was
   cancelled outright and **October CPS household data was never collected and cannot be collected
   retroactively** — October 2025's unemployment rate will never be known. Applying that mechanism
   forward requires one fact the sibling never needed. BLS's own documentation gives the CPS
   schedule: the **reference week is the calendar week containing the 12th**, and **interviewing
   begins the week immediately following, usually the week containing the 19th**. For December 2026
   unshifted, that is **reference week Sun 12-06 → Sat 12-12** and **interview week Sun 12-13 → Sat
   12-19** — the interview week sits **entirely inside** a lapse beginning 00:00 Saturday 12-12.
   **But BLS also documents that "the November and December reference weeks are sometimes moved one
   week earlier so that survey interviewers are not contacting households during major holiday
   periods."** Shifted, the reference week is Nov 29 → Dec 5 and interviewing runs Dec 6 → 12,
   **finishing before the lapse**. So the December-2026 household survey either survives intact or
   is deleted forever, and the deciding fact is a published BLS scheduling choice — checkable, and
   **not resolved in this session** (bls.gov has 403'd this lane repeatedly; no December-2026 CPS
   schedule was fetched). Named as the highest-value next check.
