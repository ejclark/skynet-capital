## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Stop reasoning about this from the historical "shutdowns are rare and markets ignore

passed the House floor**, while the Senate has not agreed a topline or moved a single bill (CRFB,

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Does federal funding lapse when the CR expires on 2026-12-11, and — the part this calendar actually
needs — what would that do to the dated events it tracks in December and January, and to a paper
book holding long-duration tech (NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL AMZN)?

**One-line verdict:** a punt into the new Congress is the base case, an outright shutdown is a
live tail that the 2026 record makes *much* less exotic than the historical prior implies, and the
exposure worth preparing for is informational and **bill-specific** — which of the twelve
appropriations bills lapses decides whether this calendar loses its December/January federal
prints, and the three bills that matter to it are the three least advanced.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is primary-sourced; the event is not law and not certain — SUPPORTED, and the
   labeling is subtler than either Dec 11 entry currently says.** Division A **Sec. 106** of
   H.R. 6500 makes appropriations available until whichever occurs first of (1) enactment of an
   appropriation for the activity, (2) enactment of the applicable FY2027 act without provision for
   it, or **(3) December 11, 2026**. That is the date, verbatim, from the bill text. Three
   qualifications keep it `estimate`: the bill **is not yet law** (below), the **lapse is
   conditional** on Congress failing to act, and — structurally — **this calendar has no
   `confirmed`-tier source prefix for a federal legislative primary**. Its prefix table tops out at
   agency schedules (`BLS:` `FED:` `TSY:` `BEA:` `CENSUS:` …); a GPO statute has nowhere to go. That
   is the **fifth** instance of the same schema gap, after a state utility commission, a state
   election authority, a federal court docket and the EIA. Recorded, not fixed from this lane.

2. **The signature could not be verified, and four independent checks agree on that without
   proving it — MIXED.** On **2026-09-02**: congress.gov returned **403** (Cloudflare interstitial);
   GovTrack's H.R. 6500 page was **last updated 2026-08-26** and still shows "Passed Senate with
   Changes (back to House)", i.e. it had not ingested the 09-01 vote; whitehouse.gov's
   briefings-and-statements listing showed **nothing past August 2026**; and the govinfo enrolled
   package `BILLS-119hr6500enr` returned **302** (unpublished) while the `eas` package served
   cleanly. All four are *consistent* with "passed both chambers, not yet enrolled or signed," and
   none of them is evidence of it — three are staleness and one is a block. **The honest statement
   is that this doc does not know.** It does not matter much for the call: signature changes the
   entry's status note, not the December question.

3. **Three lapses in fourteen months, and 2026's two were both partial — SUPPORTED, and this is the
   load-bearing correction.** The record: **2025-10-01 → 2025-11-12** (43 days, the then-record);
   **2026-01-31 → 2026-02-03** (4 days, roughly half of federal departments); **2026-02-14 →
   2026-04-30** (**76 days, DHS only**, and on **2026-03-27** the longest shutdown in US history).
   Both 2026 lapses came out of one dispute — immigration-enforcement reform after CBP agents killed
   **Alex Pretti on 2026-01-24** — with Senate Democrats stripping DHS out of the package to force
   negotiation over warrant rules, agent identification and use-of-force standards. **Two things
   this corrects in the 09-30 sibling ledger.** That doc characterized the January lapse as caused
   by "chamber scheduling, not disagreement" (a Conference Board alert's framing) — the cause was
   substantive, which makes its own counterweight *stronger* than it argued. And it did not have
   the **76-day DHS lapse at all**. Neither correction is edited into that append-only doc; both
   are banked here and both cut the same way: **the "shutdowns are rare" prior is dead in this
   regime.**

4. **The tape ignored all three, measured not quoted — SUPPORTED at the endpoint.** Own run,
   2026-09-02:

   | Lapse | Window | S&P 500 | VIX Δ | 10y Δ |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 2025 (43d, full) | 09-30 6688.46 → 11-12 6850.92 | **+2.43%** | +1.23 (peak 25.31) | −8bp |
   | Jan 2026 (4d, ~half) | 01-30 6939.03 → 02-03 6917.81 | **−0.31%** | +0.56 | +3bp |
   | Feb–Apr 2026 (76d, DHS) | 02-13 6836.17 → 04-30 7209.01 | **+5.45%** | −3.71 | +33bp |

   Three out-of-sample cases, all consistent with the ~0% median the 09-30 ledger leaned on. **This
   calendar can now stop citing press compilations for that claim.**

5. **…but the 76-day window contains the counter-mechanism, and it is dated — MIXED, and this is
   the most useful thing in the doc.** Endpoint-to-endpoint the DHS lapse was +5.45%. Inside it,
   the S&P fell **−7.20%** from 6836.17 (02-13) to **6343.72 on 2026-03-30**, with **VIX 31.05 on
   2026-03-27**. Those dates are not arbitrary: 03-23 is when ICE agents were sent to staff airport
   security after TSA callouts, and 03-27 is both the day the lapse became the longest in history
   and the day the President ordered TSA payroll resumed. **Honest counter-evidence against my own
   story, stated plainly:** the market bottomed **2026-03-30** and had rallied to 6967.38 by
   **04-14** — a full **month before the shutdown ended on 04-30**. A clean "shutdown caused the
   drawdown" reading cannot survive that; the recovery did not wait for resolution, and March 2026
   had other candidate drivers this doc did not isolate. What survives is narrower and still worth
   carrying: **the one lapse that reached an economically visible consumer service is also the one
   that coincided with a 7% drawdown and a 31 VIX.** That is the mechanism to watch in December, not
   a base rate to trade.

6. **The base case is a punt into the new Congress, not an omnibus and not a shutdown —
   SUPPORTED as reasoning, and it is reasoning, not measurement.** Appropriations reality (CRFB,
   **2026-08-12**): the House has cleared **12 of 12** bills through committee but only **3 of 12**
   on the floor — MilCon-VA, National Security-State, and Agriculture — and the **Senate has agreed
   no topline and moved none**. Fourteen weeks separate 09-02 from 12-11, nearly all of them
   campaign recess, and what remains is a lame duck. The historical pattern (GovExec, **2026-08-06**)
   is that when a chamber changes hands, Congress waits for the new one before finalizing spending;
   polling gives Democrats **>80%** for the House. **The mechanism and the incentive both point at
   another stopgap into January or February**, with the 120th Congress seated **2027-01-03**. Same
   honest limit the 09-30 sibling put on its equivalent leg: this is a political-incentive argument
   and this doc owns no instrument that measures political incentives.

7. **The tail has a named 2018 analogue and it is a December one — SUPPORTED.** GovExec's piece
   makes the comparison directly: a lame-duck December deadline under a chamber about to flip is
   precisely the 2018 setup, which produced the **35-day 2018-12-22 → 2019-01-25** shutdown. Add
   the 2026 fault line: **Homeland Security is not among the three FY2027 bills that have passed the
   House floor**, so the exact bill that caused two shutdowns this year arrives unresolved at the
   December package. That is the shape of the tail — not a total shutdown, a **DHS-shaped** one.

8. **What a lapse actually costs this calendar is bill-specific, and that is a refinement over the
   09-30 read — SUPPORTED.** The sibling ledger's finding stands: BLS **deletes** rather than
   delays (October 2025 CPI cancelled outright; October 2025 household-survey data never collected
   and uncollectable — Richmond Fed "Phantom Figures"). But 2026's partial lapses add the missing
   half: **which agency goes dark depends on which bill lapses.** BLS is funded in
   **Labor-HHS-Education**; BEA and Census in **Commerce-Justice-Science**. Neither has passed the
   House floor. Applied to the corridor: **jobs-2026-12-04 and cpi-2026-12-10 print before the cliff
   and are safe on every branch**; the exposed tracked event is **[`pce-2026-12-23`](pce-2026-12-23.md)**
   — BEA, twelve days after the cliff, and its own research already records that the **2025 analogue
   of that exact release was cancelled** and folded into a combined print on 2026-01-22 — plus every
   January release. A **DHS-only** lapse, the 2026 mode, would cost this calendar **nothing at all**.

9. **The cliff is out of the blackout, not inside it, and it lands on a loaded session —
   SUPPORTED, and it corrects the entry's own filed note.** The `cr-expiry-2026-12-11` entry's
   `notes` state that the cliff sits "inside the blackout gate … so no FOMC participant may respond
   to a lapse in the session it would begin." **That is wrong**, on this calendar's own primary: the
   Fed's blackout policy (PDF re-fetched 2026-08-31 by the blackout ledger) ends the gate at
   **23:59 ET Thursday 2026-12-10**. So **12-11 is the first day Fed officials may speak in two
   weeks** — and per that same ledger it is already the day the November CPI response was expected,
   because CPI prints 08:30 on 12-10 with nobody permitted to react. December 11 therefore carries
   **the first post-blackout Fed commentary, on a CPI the dot plot never saw, on the day funding
   expires.** Also worth stating because it changes the mechanics: appropriations run *through*
   Dec 11, so a **lapse begins Saturday 2026-12-12** — a weekend start, the same structural setup
   as the January 2026 lapse, with the House able to reconvene Monday 12-14.

10. **Tracked-name exposure is indirect and thin — SUPPORTED, inherited.** `symbols: []`; none of
    the nine tracked names has a federal-revenue channel material to its thesis. Transmission is
    entirely via leg 8 (a Fed reading a sparser dataset) and leg 5 (a risk-off episode, if the tail
    fires). Ranking carried unchanged from the 09-30 sibling and not re-derived: **CRWV** most
    exposed (debt-financed buildout, cost-of-capital sensitivity), then **NVDA / AVGO / MRVL**, then
    **MSFT / GOOG / META**, least **AAPL / AMZN**.

11. **The CR is a bundled expiration cliff, not just a funding one — SUPPORTED, primary-sourced,
    and new to this calendar.** Reading the bill text rather than coverage of it: Division B expires
    a stack of authorities on the same **December 11** date — the **Defense Production Act §717**,
    the **Cybersecurity Information Sharing Act of 2015** liability protections, the Federal
    Cybersecurity Enhancement Act, **USPTO fee-setting authority**, the Technology Modernization
    Fund, TSCA fee authority, USCIRF, Grain Standards and Food for Peace. Division C's **Surface
    Transportation Extension Act of 2026** ends the same day (Highway Trust Fund expenditure
    authority to **December 12**), and Division D expires dozens of VA health, housing and benefits
    authorities. Division A **Sec. 157** additionally freezes OMB's Uniform Guidance rewrite (NPRM
    published **2026-05-29**) only *through* 12-11. **Why this matters to the call and not just as
    trivia:** it raises the cost of letting the date pass for *both* parties, which supports leg 6's
    punt base case — and it means "nothing happens on 12-11" is not actually available. Something
    is enacted, or a long list of authorities lapses alongside the appropriations.

### Honest limits

**This doc puts no probability on a December lapse** and should not be read as implying one; leg 6
is an incentive argument and leg 7 is an analogy. **The 2026 shutdown chronology rests on an
encyclopedia** — Wikipedia's article, fetched via the MediaWiki API — not on a primary or even on
fetched contemporaneous reporting; it is dated, internally consistent and corroborated on the DHS
dispute by committee releases, but the 76-day figure and the 03-27 "longest in history" date are
**one source deep** and a future pulse should upgrade them. **Leg 5's attribution is explicitly not
established**: the March 2026 drawdown coincides with the lapse's peak-pain phase, but the recovery
began a month before resolution and this doc isolated no other candidate driver — treat it as a
hypothesis with a watch condition, never as a measured shutdown beta. **CRFB's appropriations table
is stale to 2026-08-12** and predates the House vote; the 3-of-12 floor count may have moved.
**congress.gov and CBO both 403'd**, so there is no committee-report or cost-estimate layer here,
and the signature question (leg 2) is unresolved rather than answered. Finally, the **duplicate
entry problem is not fixed by this PR** — see the stance — and until it is, any consumer of this
calendar double-counts the December cliff.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (event `estimate`, date now primary-sourced but the bill unenacted as of 2026-09-02).**
Take **no position, no hedge and no sizing change** off this event, and take **no action at all**
before **2026-11-03**. Base case (**Medium**, reasoning not measurement): the December 11 cliff is
cleared by **another stopgap into the 120th Congress**, because the appropriations work is barely
started, the remaining calendar is nearly all campaign recess, and a chamber expected to flip waits
for the new Congress. The tail this doc refuses to call small: **three lapses in fourteen months**,
an unresolved DHS bill that already caused two of them, and a lame-duck December that rhymes with
2018. **The stance's content is about the branch, not the odds:** on a lapse, the cost to this book
is informational and **bill-specific** — a DHS-only lapse costs this calendar nothing, while a
Labor-HHS or CJS lapse deletes **pce-2026-12-23** and the January releases on the 2025 precedent
that BLS and BEA *delete* rather than delay. The equity channel is a measured non-event at the
endpoint (+2.43% / −0.31% / +5.45%) and is explicitly **not** the reason to track this.

**A structural flag that belongs to Eric's lane, not this one:** this calendar currently carries
**two entries for the same event** — `cr-expiry-2026-12-11` and
`government-funding-deadline-2026-12-11`, identical date, identical `high`/`geopolitical`/`symbols:
[]` shape, both filed **2026-09-02** by two parallel adjacency sweeps that could not see each
other. Neither is wrong; they are duplicates. This PR deliberately **does not** retire either one:
the sibling's own initial-research session is running concurrently, and deleting the entry under it
would orphan its ledger. Recorded here so the dedupe is a deliberate decision by whoever lands
second, not a silent overwrite — and so a future reader knows the December cliff is single-counted
in reality even though it is double-counted in the table.

**No new calendar entry is proposed in this PR.** The one dated candidate the sweep surfaced is the
seating of the **120th Congress on 2027-01-03** (statutory, 20th Amendment §2), which is the target
date of the punt branch in leg 6. It is deliberately left un-proposed for the same reason the 09-30

`market-events-data.ts` on the day two of them collided is exactly how the duplicate above

**Kill switches:**

- **A full-year package, or all twelve FY2027 bills, enacted before 2026-12-11** — the event
  retires, legs 6–8 collapse to a footnote, and this doc closes out early.
- **Senate Appropriations agrees an FY2027 topline and marks up bills** — the first real evidence

- **A lapse begins 2026-12-12 that includes Labor-HHS or Commerce-Justice-Science** — the base case

- **A lapse begins 2026-12-12 limited to DHS (or any set excluding Labor-HHS and CJS)** — the base
  case is wrong but this calendar loses nothing; downgrade the event to a headline and stop
  spending sessions on it.
- **The S&P moves >2% on a December funding headline** — leg 4's three-for-three "the tape ignores
  it" breaks in sample, and the equity channel becomes worth measuring instead of dismissing.
- **A December lapse reaches a visible consumer-facing service (air travel, benefit payments)
  within its first two weeks** — leg 5's named mechanism fires; that is the only condition under
  which this event has ever coincided with a 7% drawdown and a 31 VIX.
- **The midterms return unified control of both chambers to the incumbent party** — leg 6's
  "flipped chamber waits for the new Congress" premise disappears and the December package becomes
  a live negotiation rather than a punt.
- **H.R. 6500 is not signed** — a pocket-veto or refusal path was never contemplated here; it would
  move the cliff to **2026-10-01** and hand this entire read back to the 09-30 sibling.
