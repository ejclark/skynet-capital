### Leg 2 — "The annual rate is a forecast." **REFUTED for most of it — and the direction is the opposite of the sibling's.**

The annual rate decomposes exactly:

> **index(Aug 26) / index(Aug 25) − 1** = **[ index(Jul 26) / index(Aug 25) − 1 ]** (the *carry*,
> already determined) **+ [ index(Aug 26) / index(Jul 26) − 1 ]** (the *one-month clear*, unknown)

From `J5EK`, fetched and recomputed here:

| Month | Index | Month | Index |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 Jun | 100.5 | 2026 Feb | 102.7 |
| **2025 Jul** | **102.1** | 2026 Mar | 103.2 |
| **2025 Aug** | **101.6** | 2026 Apr | 102.3 |
| 2025 Sep | 102.7 | 2026 May | 103.6 |
| 2025 Dec | 101.6 | 2026 Jun | **104.3** |
| 2026 Jan | 103.5 | **2026 Jul** | **103.8** |

**Carry = 103.8 / 101.6 − 1 = +2.17pp.** Fixed, subject only to revision (Leg 4).

**Here is the whole point of this ledger, and it is one subtraction.** The base **falls** from July
2025's **102.1** to August 2025's **101.6** — a **−0.49%** step. Because the annual rate divides by
that base, a completely **flat** month in 2026 prints **+0.49pp** higher than July's did. July's
published annual rate was **+1.6%**; an unchanged index of 103.8 gives **+2.17%**.

**The clear** is the remaining question, and it is close to a random walk. Across the **30**
single-month clears since January 2024 the mean is **+0.12%** with a standard deviation of **1.08pp**
(range **−1.86%** to **+2.43%**). Unconditionally:

- Centre: 2.17 + 0.12 = **+2.28% y/y**
- ±1 standard deviation: **+1.20% to +3.36%**

The thresholds that matter, computed against the 101.6 base:

| Annual rate | Needs an August index of | Which is a month of | Months since 2024 that cleared it |
|---|---|---|---|
| +1.0% | 102.62 | −1.14% | 22 / 30 |
| **+1.6%** (July's own rate) | 103.23 | **−0.55%** | **21 / 30 (70%)** |
| **+1.7%** (the test threshold) | 103.33 | **−0.46%** | **19 / 30 (63%)** |
| +2.0% (the street) | 103.63 | −0.16% | 18 / 30 (60%) |
| +3.0% | 104.65 | +0.82% | 10 / 30 |

That **63%** is why **FT-uk-retail-sales-2026-09-18-1** is registered at **medium** and not high
confidence: the centre of the band sits well clear of +1.7%, but the *threshold* probability is what a
binary test pays out on, and it is a long way from a sure thing.

**The symmetry with the sibling is exact and is worth stating as a system property.** The
[10-23](uk-retail-sales-2026-10-23.md) ledger registered its FT-1 at the **same +1.7% threshold** on

fell. Two consecutive bulletins, one series, opposite directions, one mechanism — if both score

### Leg 3 — "August was weak, so the print will be weak." **REFUTED as stated — the two claims are not the same claim, and the street agrees with the arithmetic.**

**The till, cooling.** BRC-KPMG's August 2026 monitor (four weeks to **29 August**; trade-press
secondary today, matching the sibling's primary read): total UK retail sales **+0.7%** y/y against
**3.1%** in August 2025 and a 12-month average of **1.6%**; food **+2.6%** (against +4.7%); non-food
**−0.8%** (against +1.8%). The cause given is rising household costs plus early-summer heat having
pulled seasonal purchases forward.

**The survey, warming.** GfK/NIQ's August barometer (NIQ primary, published **2026-08-21**, fieldwork
**30 July – 12 August**, 2,000 respondents): headline **−14**, up three points, *"its highest level
for two years"*; the major-purchase index **−7**, up five, the strongest since **December 2021**;
personal finances for the next twelve months **+4**, up three; the backward-looking past-twelve-months
measure down slightly to **−6**.

**The street, with the arithmetic.** TradingEconomics (**secondary aggregator, labelled**) shows a
consensus of **+2.0%** for this print against a "forecast" of 1.6%, and records July as **actual
1.6%, consensus 2.2%, previous 3.8%**. Two things there are worth more than the consensus itself:
the **+2.0%** sits **inside** this ledger's band and **above** July, so the street is on the same side
of the base step; and its "previous 3.8%" reproduces the June annual rate this document computes from
`J5EK` (**+3.78%**), which is a small but real check that the aggregator is reading the right series.
This is a better position than the 10-23 sibling had — that ledger found **no** published consensus
and had only its own arithmetic on the table.

So the three instruments do not actually conflict; they measure different things. The till measures
**year-on-year value across a member panel**. The barometer measures **stated intent**. The ONS
number is a **ratio of a seasonally-adjusted volume index to a base that fell**. A weak month and an
accelerating annual rate are simultaneously available, and this print is the case where that happens.

**What this ledger does *not* claim:** that the consumer improved. Nothing here supports that. The
call is about the *printed number*, and the headline-risk rule in the signals list exists precisely so
the number is not mistaken for the thing.
