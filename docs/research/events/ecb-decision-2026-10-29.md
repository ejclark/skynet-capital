Governing Council meets on 10-29 with **no staff projections** (they run March / June / September /
December only) and **no October inflation print** — the ECB's statistical calendar puts the

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The September sibling ([`ecb-decision-2026-09-10`](ecb-decision-2026-09-10.md)) settled that a
euro-area policy decision has **no price channel into this book** and that the only live content in
the euro cycle is a **~50bp disagreement about the terminal rate**. That entry named this meeting as
*"the first venue where leg 2's 50bp gap gets tested."* So the question here is narrow and
falsifiable: **is 10-29 actually that venue — and if it is not, what is this date for?**

**One-line verdict:** it is **not** the venue. October is structurally the ECB's least-informed
meeting of the autumn — no staff projections, no October inflation print — and the market prices

cash hours** on the busiest attribution day of the quarter, which makes it a contaminant of the tape
rather than a reader of it.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **October is a no-projections meeting — the yardstick the September meeting has is absent
   here.** SUPPORTED, structural. Eurosystem/ECB staff macroeconomic projections are published
   four times a year, in **March, June, September and December**. The September sibling's whole
   scoreable claim (**FT-ecb-decision-2026-09-10-1**) is a single projection cell — 2028 HICP
   against June's 2.0% — and that instrument simply does not exist on 10-29. Whatever the
   projections say on 09-10 stands unrevised until **12-17**. This is not a soft read of ECB habit;
   it is the publication schedule.

2. **The Council will not even have a fresh inflation print.** SUPPORTED, ECB primary. The ECB's
   own statistical calendar (`press/calendars/statscal/ges/html/sthicp`, fetched 2026-09-05) lists
   the euro-area flash HICP for reference month **Sep-2026 at 02/10/2026 15:00 CET** with the full
   release **16/10/2026 12:00 CET**, and the flash for **Oct-2026 at 04/11/2026 15:00 CET** — i.e.
   **six days after the decision**. So on 10-29 the freshest inflation datum in the room is the
   September HICP, already thirteen days old at the full release and ~four weeks old as a reference
   month. Combine legs 1 and 2 and October is information-starved by construction: no new forecast,
   no new print.

3. **The market prices the next step for December, not October.** SUPPORTED, press-cited.
   Morningstar's own framing (via search summary; the article returned HTTP 403 to direct fetch) is
   *"almost a 50/50 chance of a further rate increase in December, bringing the deposit rate to
   2.75%"* — a December step, not an October one. The Irish Times' 2026-09-01 wrap of the Eurostat
   flash says the same in institutional terms: *"policymakers may skip the October meeting and focus
   on the next round of economic projections in December,"* alongside *"financial markets see two
   more rate hikes in the next year."* Both are consistent with the strip the September sibling
   recorded (~100% odds of **3.00%** by June 2027, as of 2026-09-04): two further hikes spread over
   twelve months is a **December-then-2027** path, not back-to-back autumn moves.

4. **The desk case against an October hike is explicit and dated.** SUPPORTED, named source. ING's
   Carsten Brzeski, published **2026-09-02**: *"We expect the ECB to hike interest rates by 25bp next
   week"* — and, in the same note, *"hiking rates further beyond next week would not make a lot of
   sense and could harm the eurozone economy,"* with the shock characterised as *"mainly
   energy-driven"* and the September move as an *"insurance"* or *"dovish rate hike."* Bloomberg's
   David Powell, quoted **2026-09-01**: the ECB is *"unlikely to tighten by as much as financial
   markets are currently pricing in,"* because *"the sharp rise in headline inflation contrasts with
   a drop in underlying measures of price increases."* This is the same supply-side reading the
   September sibling adopted, now with a meeting attached: the first place the "we are done" view
   gets expressed is a **hold on 10-29**.

5. **The most accessible October probability is cumulative — and is already being misread in
   public.** SUPPORTED, and this is the finding most worth banking. `centralbank.watch`
   (page last updated **2026-09-04**, fetched today) publishes a meeting grid reading, for hike:
   **Sep-10 88.6% · Oct-29 91.9% · Dec-17 96.4% · Feb-27 97.4% · … · Sep-2027 99.1%**, with its own
   note that these are *"cumulative"* — *"a later meeting's probability already includes moves priced
   in for earlier ones,"* so they *"should not be summed or compared independently across dates."*
   Read correctly, the Oct-29 cell is **P(at least one hike by 10-29)**; its **~3.3pp** increment over
   September is the probability the *first* hike arrives in October rather than September, and the
   table **cannot price a second hike at all**. Read carelessly it says the opposite — and a search
   engine summarising that exact page returned *"90.6% probability of a rate hike"* for the October
   meeting to this session, unprompted. That is the misread in the wild, on the first page anyone
   would land on. Any future pulse that quotes an October hike probability from a cumulative table is
   quoting a number that does not mean what it says.

6. **The clock moves an hour, and this calendar's own note was wrong about it.** SUPPORTED,
   arithmetic, verified in this session. EU summer time ends on the **last Sunday of October** =
   **2026-10-25**; US daylight time ends on the **first Sunday of November** = **2026-11-01**. In the
   week between, Frankfurt is on CET (UTC+1) while New York is still on EDT (UTC−4) — a five-hour
   gap, not the usual six. The ECB's standing **14:15 / 14:45 CET** slots therefore land at
   **09:15 / 09:45 ET** on 2026-10-29, against **08:15 / 08:45 ET** on 2026-09-10. Consequences,
   both material: the decision lands **45 minutes after** the 08:30 ET GDP-advance and PCE prints
   rather than 15 minutes before them, and the **press conference begins 15 minutes after the US
   cash open**, running through roughly the first 45 minutes of trade. The `market-events-data.ts`
   note for this event said *"an 08:15 ET euro presser precedes two US prints"* — wrong on both
   counts, and corrected in this PR. (Honest caveat, inherited from the September sibling: the
   14:15/14:45 CET slots are the ECB's convention and are **not** printed on the meeting-calendar
   page. The conversion is exact; the input is conventional.)

7. **The date sits inside a compound-risk window this calendar has already named — and the euro leg
   arrives last.** SUPPORTED, from our own entries. Within five days: consumer confidence and
   durable goods **10-27**, **FOMC 10-28**, **GDP Q3 advance** and **PCE** both **10-29** at 08:30

8. **No tracked symbol carries a euro channel — unchanged, and not re-argued.** SUPPORTED, inherited.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `estimate`):** **stand aside completely, and expect a hold.** The 2026-10-29 decision
is, on this evidence, the ECB's **skip** meeting: no staff projections (March/June/September/December
only), no October HICP until **2026-11-04**, a market pricing its next step for **December**
(~50/50 to 2.75%), and a named desk case — ING 09-02, Bloomberg 09-01 — that further tightening past
September is unwarranted against an energy-driven shock. This book holds nothing with a euro-rates
channel and none is proposed. The ledger's three substantive commitments are analytical, not
positional. First, it **calls a hold on 10-29** and registers it. Second, it **retires the cumulative
probability table as a source of October odds** — the public 91.9% is P(any hike by then), the
increment over September is ~3.3pp, and a quoted "October hike probability" from that table is a
misread this doc has now seen made in public. Third, it **corrects the clock**: 09:15 / 09:45 ET,
not 08:15 / 08:45, because the EU falls back 10-25 and the US does not until 11-01 — which puts the
press conference inside US cash hours and turns this event from an attribution *explanation* (its
role on 09-10) into an attribution *contaminant*. Estimates widen caution and license nothing.

**Kill switches:**

- **Skip kill (registered):** the ECB changing the deposit rate at the **2026-10-29** decision. The
  structural case in legs 1–4 loses and October is a live policy meeting after all. Registered as
  **FT-ecb-decision-2026-10-29-1**, score by **2026-10-30**.
- **Channel kill (registered):** any tracked name (NVDA/AVGO/MRVL/CRWV/AAPL/AMZN) moving **>2%** in
  the **09:45–10:30 ET** press-conference window on **2026-10-29** in a way the tape attributes to
  the ECB. Leg 8's "no price channel" claim would be false on its strongest test. Registered as
  **FT-ecb-decision-2026-10-29-2**, score by **2026-10-30**.
- **Guidance kill:** the **2026-09-10** presser pre-committing to a specific further path, or that
  meeting's staff projections putting **2028 HICP above 2.0%**. Either makes a back-to-back October
  move live, and legs 3–5 are re-derived rather than patched. Score by **2026-09-11**.
- **Pricing kill:** a per-meeting (non-cumulative) October probability appearing above ~40%, or an
  implied 10-29 deposit rate materially above the post-09-10 level. Leg 5's "priced as a skip" read
  dies on a real number, which is the outcome it would most welcome. Re-check every pulse.
- **Supply-side kill:** the ECB or Eurostat data turning the shock from energy into **wages** —
  services HICP re-accelerating above its August **3.0%**, or the ECB wage tracker moving above the
  July account's **2.6% (2026) / 2.7% (early 2027)**. The whole "insurance hike, then done" frame
  goes with it. Score at the **2026-10-16** September HICP full release.
- **Clock kill:** the ECB publishing a decision or press-conference time for 10-29 other than
  14:15 / 14:45 CET. Leg 6's 09:15 / 09:45 ET conversion moves with it and the contamination window
  is re-derived. Re-check every pulse.
- **Date kill:** the ECB moving the 10-28/29 meeting, or the meeting producing no decision. Breaks
  the header. Re-check every pulse.
