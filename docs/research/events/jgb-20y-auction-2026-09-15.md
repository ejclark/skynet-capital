## At a glance

**TL;DR.** Japan reopens ¥700bn of its 3.7% 2046 bond on **2026-09-15**, the same date the US reopens
its own 20-year and the day before the FOMC. **Nothing here is ours to trade, and the reason is
measured rather than assumed:** across **44** JGB 20Y auctions since January 2023, a soft one (tail
≥ 2bp) preceded a **+1.43bp** mean move in the US 20Y on the same date against **−0.10bp** for a firm
one — a **+1.53bp** difference with a **4.99bp** daily standard deviation, **t = +0.80**. The sign is
right, the size is noise. What the date *does* buy is **sequencing**: Japan's result publishes inside
the Tokyo session, **hours before** the US 20Y's 1:00pm ET stop, so it is a free same-day read on
whoever is willing to own twenty-year duration. Two primary-sourced findings reframe the event. **(1)
Japan's super-long market is supply-managed, not demand-recovered** — MOF has cut the 20Y from
¥1,000bn to ¥800bn to **¥700bn** (−30%) and the 40Y from ¥500bn to **¥300bn** (−40%) in twelve
months, which is why cover ratios *rose* (2.97 → 4.52 → **3.98**) while the 20Y yield hit
**3.864% on 09-02, its highest since 1996-07-18**. **(2) The FX-hedge gap that keeps Japanese money
home has halved.** A yen investor's domestic 20Y beat a hedged US 20Y by ~**242bp** in 2023 and by
only **+83 to +127bp** on 09-04 — the narrowest of the sample, and a BoJ hike on **09-18** narrows it
further. Date **estimate**; it widens caution and licenses nothing.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This entry reached the calendar sideways: it was proposed on 2026-09-08 during the
[`treasury-30y-bond-2026-09-10`](treasury-30y-bond-2026-09-10.md) adjacency sweep, which was testing
whether that ledger's "synchronized global long-end move" leg was narrative or calendar. It turned out
to be calendar — **Japan and the United States both sell 20-year duration on 2026-09-15**. So the
question this document has to answer is not "will the auction go well." It is:
**does a second sovereign selling the same duration on the same date change anything for a US
AI-equity book — and if it does not, what is the entry for?**

**One-line verdict:** the spillover is a **measured null** (n=44, t = +0.80), so the entry is an
**attribution and sequencing instrument**, not a signal — and the two things worth knowing about
Japan's long end are that its supply has been cut 30–40% (which manufactures the strong cover ratios)
and that the FX-hedge gap keeping Japanese money at home has halved in three years.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The date, size and identity of the bond — SUPPORTED, from three independent MOF primaries.**
The announcement dated **September 8, 2026** reads verbatim: auction date **September 15, 2026**,
issue date **September 16, 2026**, maturity date **June 20, 2046**, offering amount **"About 700
billion yen"**, with the footnote *"20-year JGBs to be issued in September will be a reopening issue
of the July 2026 issue."* The September calendar lists the 09-15 20-year independently. The workbook
identifies the July 2026 issue as **issue 197, 3.7% coupon, maturity 2046-06-20**, first sold
2026-07-14 and reopened once already on **2026-08-20** — so 09-15 is the **second** reopening, not
the first. The proposal that created this entry said "a reopening of the July 2026 issue" without
noticing the August leg; that is corrected here.

This is worth one sentence of process: at D-7 this auction's terms are **already fixed**, where the
US 20Y's are not (its size lands at the 09-10 11:00 ET announcement). MOF announces about a week
ahead; Treasury announces about six business days ahead. The two calendars are not symmetric.

**2. There is no measurable JGB-auction → US-long-end spillover — REFUTED as a tradeable signal,
MIXED as a directional prior.** This is the leg the entry exists to test, so it was tested rather
than argued. Method: every 20Y JGB auction from **2023-01-19 to 2026-08-20** (44 with a usable US
reading), tail measured as MOF's yield-at-lowest-accepted minus yield-at-average, matched to the
**same-date** change in the US 20Y CMT — same date, because Tokyo's session closes ~11 hours before
the US cash open, so a same-day US move is the first US session that could possibly react.

| Split | n | Mean US 20Y same-date change | Median |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soft JGB auction (tail ≥ 2.0bp) | 14 | **+1.43bp** | +2.00bp |
| Firm JGB auction (tail < 2.0bp) | 30 | **−0.10bp** | +0.00bp |
| Difference | — | **+1.53bp** (SE 1.91, **t = +0.80**) | — |

Bid-to-cover gives the same answer: low-cover auctions (< 3.1x, n=16) precede **+1.44bp**, high-cover
(n=28) **−0.21bp**, difference +1.65bp, **t = +0.93**. Correlation of tail against the US move is
**+0.174** (n=44, t = 1.15). The US 20Y's own daily standard deviation on those dates is **4.99bp**.

Read it honestly in both directions. The **sign is consistent** across two independent measures of a
weak auction, and it points the way theory says it should. But an effect of 1.5bp against 5bp of
daily noise, at t < 1 on n=44, is **not** something to size a position to — and most of these dates
carry US prints of their own, so even the sign is confounded. **Verdict: no tradeable spillover.**
Registered as a forward test rather than left as an assertion (`FT-jgb-20y-auction-2026-09-15-1`).

**3. Japan's super-long market is supply-managed, not demand-recovered — SUPPORTED, and it is the
finding that changes how the 09-15 result should be read.** The naive read of the tape is that
Japanese long-end demand recovered: cover ratios are strong while yields sit at generational highs.
The workbook says the size changed underneath.

| Tenor | Old size | First cut | Current size | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20-year | ¥1,000bn (through 2025-06) | ¥800bn (2025-07-10) | **¥700bn** (from 2026-04-14) | **−30%** |
| 40-year | ¥500bn (2025-05-28) | ¥400bn (2025-07-23) | **¥300bn** (from 2026-05-27) | **−40%** |

And the cover ratios move *with* the cuts rather than against them. 40-year cover, in order across
the two cuts: 2.21 · 2.13 · 2.60 · 2.59 · 2.76 · 2.54 · 2.70 · **2.82**. 20-year cover in 2026:
4.82 · 4.01 · 2.97 · 4.52 · **3.98**. A 4x cover on ¥700bn absorbs the same yen as a 2.8x cover on
the old ¥1,000bn. **So a strong 09-15 cover is not evidence that Japanese institutions want
twenty-year duration; it is evidence that MOF sized the auction to be covered.** That is the reading
this ledger will apply on the day, and it is the honest one for a market whose issuer has visibly
been managing the clearing price.

**4. The FX-hedge gap that keeps Japanese money at home has halved — SUPPORTED, and this is the real
channel between the two 09-15 auctions.** Japan is the largest foreign holder of US Treasuries, so the
mechanically correct question is not "do two auctions compete for the same money" but "what does a
yen investor earn on each, after hedging?" Computed from primaries, hedging approximated as the
short-rate differential (a rolling hedge; cross-currency basis excluded — see limits):

| Date | US 3M | US 20Y | JP 1Y | JGB 20Y | Hedged US 20Y | **Domestic advantage** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023-09-05 | 5.55 | 4.56 | −0.076 | 1.356 | −1.07 | **+242bp** |
| 2024-09-05 | 5.15 | 4.11 | 0.250 | 1.717 | −0.79 | **+251bp** |
| 2025-09-05 | 4.07 | 4.72 | 0.686 | 2.630 | 1.34 | **+129bp** |
| 2026-03-05 | 3.70 | 4.71 | 1.006 | 3.026 | 2.02 | **+101bp** |
| 2026-06-05 | 3.78 | 5.03 | 1.140 | 3.551 | 2.39 | **+116bp** |
| **2026-09-04** | **3.91** | **5.25** | **1.546** | **3.717** | **2.89** | **+83bp** |

On the shorter hedge leg the gap is wider — MOF's 3-month bill (#1404, auctioned 2026-09-04) cleared
at **1.1076%** average yield, giving a 2.80pp hedge cost, a hedged US 20Y of **2.45%** and a domestic
advantage of **+127bp**. So the honest current range is **+83 to +127bp depending on hedge tenor**,
and both readings agree on the direction of travel: the advantage is **the narrowest of the sample**
and has roughly **halved since 2024**.

The mechanism that closes it is worth stating precisely, because it is counter-intuitive: the gap
narrows when **Japanese short rates rise**, since the yen leg of the hedge is what a JPY investor
earns while hedging. A BoJ hike to 1.25% on 09-18 mechanically takes another ~25bp off the domestic
advantage, other things equal. That makes the BoJ meeting, not either auction, the thing that
actually moves the cross-border allocation — and it is why this ledger's quarter-horizon call points
at the gap rather than at auction results. Registered as `FT-jgb-20y-auction-2026-09-15-3`.

Three of those are dated superlatives, checked against the full 1974→2026 series: the **20Y at 3.864%

**6. The yen has done the repricing the auction has not — SUPPORTED (Yahoo, secondary).** USD/JPY:
**160.196 (09-01) → 158.923 → 155.660 (09-03) → 156.197 → 154.079 (09-08 live, ~10:09 ET)**. That is

so it does not settle the 09-15 question; it does establish that nothing was broken in Japanese

### What plays the conditions support

**None.** The house answer here is a refusal, and it is the correct one three times over: no tracked
symbol has yen-rates exposure, no playbook is rates-keyed, and the one channel that could have
justified an entry — a JGB auction result informing the US long end — was tested and returned a null
at t = 0.80. The event's value is entirely diagnostic: it stops a 09-15 long-end move being
automatically attributed to the US auction, and it hands the US 20Y sibling a free demand read that
lands before its own stop.

### Honest limits

- **The spillover test is n=44 and confounded.** Most JGB auction dates carry US prints of their own;
  the same-date US move is not cleanly attributable in either direction. A null on n=44 is "no
  evidence of an effect," not "evidence of no effect" — the test would not detect an effect below
  ~4bp at conventional power.
- **The hedge-cost calculation is an approximation.** It uses the short-rate differential and
  **excludes the cross-currency basis**, which has historically been negative for JPY and would make
  hedging more expensive for a yen investor buying USD assets — i.e. the true domestic advantage is
  probably *wider* than the +83 to +127bp shown, and the convergence conclusion is conservative. It
  also ignores credit, liquidity and capital-treatment differences that a real lifer weighs.
- **The auction result itself does not exist.** Everything about 09-15's demand is prior, drawn from
  five 2026 predecessors and a workbook that stops at 2026-08-13.
- **The precise result-publication clock is derived, not sourced.** MOF publishes each result on a
  same-dated page inside the Tokyo session; the exact minute was not found on a MOF primary this
  session, so the ledger claims only "during Tokyo hours, before the US open," which the session
  boundaries make unambiguous.
- **USD/JPY, VIX and Brent are Yahoo, i.e. secondary**, and the 09-08 readings are intraday rather
  than settles.
- **The date is `estimate`.** MOF is a primary source and the date is not in doubt; the label reflects
  a gap in `market-events-data.ts`'s prefix taxonomy, which has no slot for a non-US sovereign debt
  office. Estimates widen caution and license nothing.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (2026-09-08, D-7; date `estimate`).** **Stand aside, permanently, and use the event as an
instrument rather than a signal.** No entry, exit or hedge is keyed to the 2026-09-15 auction or to
the 09-15 session. The entry's two jobs are (1) **attribution** — a long-end move on 09-15 is not
automatically the US 20Y auction's, because a second sovereign sells the same duration that day — and
(2) **sequencing** — Japan's result is in hand before the US 1:00pm ET stop, so it is a free prior on
same-day twenty-year demand. Both jobs survive a null spillover; neither becomes a trade if the
spillover were real, because nothing on this calendar is exposed to yen rates.

Three reading rules attach to the day itself, and they exist to stop this ledger drawing the wrong
conclusion from a strong result:

- **A strong cover is not demand recovery.** MOF cut this auction 30% in twelve months. Judge the
  result against the ¥700bn it is, then restate it against the old ¥1,000bn before comparing to 2025.
- **A flattening curve is not a cheapening one.** 10s20s at 0.807 is the flattest of 2026; the 20Y is
  richening to the 10Y into its own supply, so "highest yield since 1996" overstates the concession.
- **Credit the BoJ, not the auction, for anything that moves the yen.** The decision is D+3 and the
  channel is owned by [`boj-decision-2026-09-18`](boj-decision-2026-09-18.md).

**Kill switches.**

1. **The no-price-channel premise breaks** — a tracked name (NVDA/AVGO/MRVL/CRWV) moves **>2%** on a
   session between **2026-09-08** and **2026-09-15** that the tape attributes to a JGB or yen
   headline. This document is rebuilt, not patched.
2. **The spillover null breaks** — the **2026-09-15** JGB tail is **≥ 5bp** and the US 20Y CMT rises
   **≥ 10bp** the same date. One 2-sigma observation in the predicted direction is not proof, but it
   is enough to re-run the test with the auction-day confounders enumerated
   (`FT-jgb-20y-auction-2026-09-15-1`).
3. **The supply-managed reading breaks** — the 09-15 reopening covers **below 3.0x** or tails **above
   3bp** despite the cut size, which would mean the cuts are no longer buying a clean clearing price
   and Japan's super-long demand is deteriorating faster than issuance can be trimmed
   (`FT-jgb-20y-auction-2026-09-15-2`).
4. **The yen goes disorderly** — **USD/JPY through 150.00** before **2026-09-15**. A −3.8% week is a
   repricing; a break of 150 into a live BoJ meeting is the carry channel the sibling ledger argues is
   dormant, and its dormancy is a premise here.
5. **The hedge-gap convergence stalls** — the domestic-minus-hedged advantage stands **at or above
   +83bp** on **2026-12-31** on the 1Y-hedge basis (`FT-jgb-20y-auction-2026-09-15-3`).
6. **MOF changes the terms** — any revision to the ¥700bn size or the 09-15 date before the auction.
   The announcement is dated 09-08 and MOF's calendar note allows changes "in light of changes in
   circumstances."

Three predictions carry score-by dates and are registered in
[`forward-tests/jgb-20y-auction-2026-09-15.md`](../forward-tests/jgb-20y-auction-2026-09-15.md).
