### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is not merely enumerated, it is *derived* — SUPPORTED, and this over-discharges the
   proposal's task (a).** The release-dates file gives both the *true response deadline* and the
   *news release date* for every survey since 1990Q2. Excluding weekends **and the Veterans Day
   federal holiday**, the deadline-to-release gap since 2012 is:

   | Business-day gap | 2 | **3** | 4 | 5 | 8 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | Quarters (n = 59) | 3 | **40** | 13 | 2 | 1 |

   **40 of 59 at exactly 3 business days; 53 of 59 at 3 or 4.** Both of the two largest outliers are
   the government-shutdown quarters the file itself footnotes (**2013Q4** at 5, **2019Q1** at 8).
   The deadline itself is the **second Tuesday** of the quarter's middle month in 8 of the 13
   non-shutdown Q4s since 2012, and a Monday of the same week in 3 more.

   Applied to Q4 2026: the second Tuesday of November 2026 is **Tue 2026-11-10**; three business
   days later, skipping **Wed 2026-11-11 (Veterans Day)**, is **Mon 2026-11-16** — Thu 12, Fri 13,
   Mon 16. That is the calendar row, reproduced from a rule rather than copied.

2. **The rule also disposes of a trap that would have produced a wrong date — the Q4 Monday is
   Veterans Day, not a different schedule.** Read naively, the release-date file says the SPF is a
   Friday release:

   | Quarter | Friday releases since 2012 | Modal weekday since 2005 |
   |---|---|---|
   | Q1 / Q2 / Q3 | **45 of 45 — zero exceptions** | Friday |
   | **Q4** | 4 of 15 | **Monday, 15 of 21** |

   A session extrapolating "the SPF always prints on a Friday" would have predicted **2026-11-13**
   and been wrong by one business day. There is no separate Q4 rule: the *business-day* gap is
   identical across all four quarters (leg 1's table pools them), and Veterans Day simply falls
   inside the Q4 window, converting a 3-calendar-day Friday turnaround into a 6-calendar-day Monday
   one. The mechanism is checkable rather than fitted — it predicts a Friday Q4 exactly when the
   holiday falls *on or before* the deadline (2012, 2019, 2024 all released Friday) and a Monday
   when it falls strictly inside the window. **2015 is a documented counterexample** (holiday inside
   the window, Friday release at a 2-business-day gap), so the rule is strong, not exceptionless —
   which is why it is registered as a forward test rather than asserted.

3. **The proposal's tier question — REFUTED. The SPF has never moved a tape, across four series and
   two decades.** Absolute close-to-close change on SPF news-release days versus all other days:

   | Series | Span | Release days | Mean \|Δ\| release | Mean \|Δ\| other | Ratio | Welch t |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | **T10YIE** (10-yr breakeven) | 2003–2026 | **95** | **0.0223** | 0.0230 | **0.97** | **−0.34** |
   | T5YIE (5-yr breakeven) | 2003–2026 | 95 | 0.0300 | 0.0297 | 1.01 | +0.09 |
   | VIXCLS | 1990–2026 | **145** | 1.0359 | 0.9999 | 1.04 | +0.36 |
   | DGS10 (10-yr note) | 1990–2026 | 145 | 0.0446 | 0.0431 | 1.04 | +0.53 |

   Four series, four nulls, no t-statistic even reaching 0.6. **The single series most directly
   comparable to what the SPF publishes — a 10-year inflation expectation — is the one that moves
   *less* on release days than on an average day.** The largest release-day breakeven move in the
   whole 95-day sample is **0.10** (2022-05-13, inside the inflation surge), against a full-sample
   daily maximum many times that.

   Narrowing to Q4 specifically, the release this document is about: **n = 23** November release
   days with breakeven data, mean |Δ| **0.0161**, **maximum 0.050** — **23 for 23** at or below five
   basis points.

   **The confound cuts the right way.** SPF release days sit in the second week of the quarter's
   middle month, historically the CPI week, so release days are drawn from a *data-heavier* part of
   the month than the average day. That biases the test **toward** finding excess volatility, and
   none was found. No release-hour test is run or claimed — this is close-to-close only, which is
   the honest limit of daily data.

4. **The mechanism behind the null is the survey's own arithmetic — the long-run number is
   effectively frozen.** From `Inflation.xlsx`, over the 95 quarters that overlap the breakeven
   series:

   | Series | n | sd | Mean quarterly \|Δ\| | Range |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | SPF **10-year** annual-average CPI | 95 | **0.157** | **0.062** | **2.03 – 2.95** |
   | SPF **1-year** CPI | 95 | 0.395 | 0.153 | 1.56 – 3.60 |
   | **Market 10-year breakeven** (T10YIE, daily) | 5,925 | **0.392** | — | **0.04 – 3.02** |

   The market's own 10-year inflation number carries **2.5× the standard deviation** across a range
   **3.2× as wide** as the survey's. And they are not tracking each other: correlation of **levels**
   is 0.503, but correlation of **quarterly changes** is **0.156** — near zero on n = 94. The SPF's
   long-run line is an *anchor*, not a signal; over the last forty quarters it has taken only 22
   distinct values in a 92-basis-point band. **A number that moves six basis points a quarter cannot
   surprise a market that moves the same concept by forty**, and that is the whole of leg 3's
   explanation.

   The 1-year line *does* move — 2.5× the long-run line's volatility. But that is precisely the
   content weekly sell-side consensus already supplies, continuously and days earlier, and the SPF's
   own deadline puts its near-term view **three business days stale** at publication by construction
   (leg 1).

5. **What the survey publishes — ESTABLISHED, and it is genuinely substantial, which is the honest
   other side of legs 3 and 4.** From the landing page and the Q3 2026 release read this session:
   the SPF is the **oldest quarterly survey of macroeconomic forecasts in the United States**, begun
   **1968** under the American Statistical Association and NBER, taken over by the Philadelphia Fed
   in **1990**. The Q3 2026 edition, released 2026-08-14 from **32 forecasters**, publishes median
   quarterly and annual paths for real GDP, unemployment and payrolls; headline and core CPI and PCE
   at quarterly, Q4/Q4 and 5- and 10-year horizons; mean **probability densities** over GDP,
   unemployment and core PCE ranges; the **Anxious Index** (probability of a real-GDP decline next
   quarter — **20.0%** for 2026Q4, down from 24.5%); and, in third-quarter editions, the panel's
   **natural rate of unemployment** (2026Q3: **4.25%**, from 50% of 24 answering, range 3.70–4.50).
   The 2026Q3 10-year headline CPI projection is **2.30%** (PCE **2.20%**).

   That is more genuine macro content than most rows on this calendar carry. It is also all
   *forecast*, none *data* — nothing in it is a first observation of the world, which is the reason
   legs 3 and 4 come out the way they do and not a contradiction of them.

6. **The corridor — and the one fact about 2026-11-16 that is actually unusual.** Querying

   **An empty date is the exact inverse of this event's proposer.** PIES releases 90 minutes after

   identifiable tape. The SPF has the cleanest possible one — and *still* shows no effect (leg 3).

   single most load-bearing sentence in this document.

   The second fact is the **derived deadline**. Tue **2026-11-10** is CPI day *and* the expiry date
   of the US-China tariff truce. Whether the Q4 panel's information set contains either turns on

   The proposal's framing that this is *"the last published professional consensus before the

### Honest limits

- **Close-to-close only; no release-hour test was run or claimed.** The SPF prints at 10:00 ET and
  daily FRED data cannot isolate a 10:00 window. A large intraday reaction that fully reverses by
  the close would be invisible to leg 3. The counter is leg 4: a series moving 6bp a quarter has
  little to react *to*, and the 23-for-23 November result at ≤5bp is hard to square with a
  meaningful intraday effect.
- **The Q4 2026 deadline of 2026-11-10 is inferred, not published.** The release-dates file stops
  at 2026Q3. The inference is leg 1's modal 3-business-day rule run backwards from a published
  release date, which is why the This-month call is `medium` and not `high`.
- **The file gives dates, never times.** So whether the panel's 11-10 cut precedes or follows the
  08:30 CPI print is genuinely unknown, and leg 6 states it as unknown rather than resolving it.
- **The construction rule has a live counterexample.** 2015Q4 put Veterans Day inside the window and
  still released on a Friday at a 2-business-day gap. The rule is **40 of 59** at exactly 3 business
  days, not exceptionless, and it is registered as a forward test for that reason.
- **The SPF release-day sample is not a clean event study.** Release days cluster in the CPI week, so
  they are not exchangeable with the average day. As leg 3 notes, that confound biases toward finding
  an effect, so the null survives it — but the correct read is "no *detectable excess*," not a
  proof of zero.
- **`Inflation.xlsx` is as-published today.** No vintage archive of the inflation file was found, so
  historical figures reflect any revisions the bank has since made. Score on as-published numbers.
- **The Q3 2026 release page carries a publisher erratum.** It states that an earlier PDF of the
  2026Q3 survey carried 2026Q1 historical values in the "Actual" columns of Tables One, Three and
  Four, corrected since, with *"no impact on the panelists' projections."* No figure quoted in this
  document comes from those tables.
- **One data path was blocked and was not substituted silently.** stooq served a JavaScript
  challenge; VIX comes from CBOE's own delayed feed, stated as such and recorded in
  `probe-ref.blocked`. FRED and every Philadelphia Fed endpoint were reachable this session — a
  difference from `philly-fed-price-inflation-expectations-q4-2026-11-25`, whose session met FRED
  connection failures, and worth noting so a future lane does not treat FRED as globally blocked.

## Stance & kill switches

**Permanent stand-aside on the `estimate`-dated 2026-11-16 print, and the tier question is closed at
`low` on measurement.** The proposal left open that a national forecaster consensus might warrant
`medium`. It does not: across **95** release days on the 10-year breakeven and **145** on VIX and
the 10-year note, SPF release days are indistinguishable from ordinary days (ratios 0.97–1.04, Welch
t from −0.34 to +0.53), and the November-only subsample is **23 for 23** at or below a 5bp breakeven
move. A future lane reaching for a re-tier on the "it's the oldest and most-cited US macro
consensus" argument should read leg 3 first — that argument is about *prestige*, and prestige is not
the variable this calendar tiers on.

**The stance is not "ignore the document."** Its value is **informational**: the Q4 edition is the
last *national* forecaster consensus before `fomc-blackout-start-2026-11-28`, it is the only tracked
event on its date, and its content is fixed by the derived **2026-11-10** deadline — which is CPI
day. Read the **near-term revision**; the 10-year line is an anchor that has moved 6bp a quarter for
twenty-four years and carries no information a breakeven does not already price.

**What would change the stance:**

- **Any single SPF release day showing a 10-year breakeven move beyond 0.10** — the largest in the
  95-day sample — which would mean leg 3's null was an artifact of a low-volatility era rather than
  a property of the print.
- **A tracked name acquiring a forecaster-survey channel**, or the Q4 print moving the 10-year CPI
  projection more than **0.15pp** off 2026Q3's **2.30** — a break larger than 1 sd of its entire
  24-year level distribution, which would retire leg 4's "frozen anchor" reading.
- **The Philadelphia Fed re-dating the Q4 release off 2026-11-16**, or publishing a 2026Q4 true
  deadline other than **2026-11-10**, either of which voids the construction rule and forces a
  re-derivation rather than a pulse.
- **A federal government shutdown beginning before 2026-11-10.** The publisher's own file documents
  four shutdown-delayed surveys and one shipped without jump-off values (leg 7); this is the only
  channel by which the date realistically moves.
