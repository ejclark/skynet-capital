## At a glance

**TL;DR.** Japan sells its 5-year on **2026-11-12** — an anticipated ¥2,500bn at maturity
**2031-09-20**, terms due **2026-11-05**. **Nothing here is ours to trade.** This entry exists as the
control arm for [`jgb-10y-auction-2026-11-05`](jgb-10y-auction-2026-11-05.md), and the control
worked: it **refutes three of that ledger's claims, including the one it called its own durable
finding.** The 3–5y bucket's **net supply to private hands rose +132.8%** (¥798.6bn → ¥1,859.0bn a
month) from a base sitting **¥1.3bn — 0.16% — from the 10-year's**, and yet the 5-year has printed
**zero tails at or beyond 2bp across 33 auctions since 2024** against the 10-year's 19.4%. **A
near-identical supply shock at a near-identical base produced opposite outcomes**, so net supply does
not explain auction quality. The 10-year's "only tenor where net supply rose" is **false as stated** —
it computed three of six buckets; the 2-year rose **+49.4%** too. And its headline, *"deterioration
splits at ten years of duration"*, is **a metric artifact**: a ≥2bp tail is a fixed *yield* threshold
across tenors whose tails scale with duration — **3.3× the 2-year's own pre-2024 p90, 1.25× the
30-year's**. Scale-free, deterioration is **universal and statistically indistinguishable**: 2Y
**32.3%** · 5Y **39.4%** · 10Y **32.3%** · 20Y **38.7%** · 30Y **25.8%** above each tenor's own p90,
every one z ≥ **+2.93**, widest gap **z = +1.16 (n.s.)**. On that metric the 5-year is the *worst*
tenor on the curve, not the cleanest. Date and size both **estimate**; they widen caution and license
nothing.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The [`jgb-10y-auction-2026-11-05`](jgb-10y-auction-2026-11-05.md) sweep proposed this entry on
2026-09-09 for one measured reason, and wrote the job description into the proposal itself:

> The 5-year is the sharpest control that exists for the 10-year: its bucket took nearly the same
> dose (−62.3% vs −64.8%), its issuance moved LESS than the 10-year's (±0.0% vs −3.7%), and it is
> auctioned seven days later in the same month — so a cross-sectional comparison between them
> separates "the 10-year is damaged because its bucket was tapered" from "the 10-year is damaged
> because it carries ten years of duration in a market that repriced from 0% to 2.9%."

So: **does the control discriminate between those two stories?**

**One-line verdict:** it discriminates by **refuting both**, and on the way it refutes the proposing
ledger's own "one durable finding." A matched pair on net supply — two tenors ¥1.3bn/month apart at
the start of the window, hit with shocks nine percentage points apart — produced opposite auction
outcomes, which kills the supply channel. And the outcome difference itself turns out to be an
artifact of a **fixed yield threshold applied across tenors of wildly different duration**: measured
scale-free, all five tenors deteriorated by statistically indistinguishable amounts, and the 5-year
is the worst of them.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The supply channel dies on a matched pair — REFUTED, and this is the whole reason the entry
exists.** The proposing ledger's leg 2 called net private absorption its durable finding. Netting
MOF's offering path against the BoJ's bucket purchases, per month:

| BoJ bucket | MOF supply 2024-01 | BoJ 2024-01 | **Net then** | MOF now | BoJ now | **Net now** | Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–3y (2Y auction) | 2,900 | 1,501.3 | 1,398.7 | 2,800 | 710.4 | 2,089.6 | **+49.4%** |
| **3–5y (5Y auction)** | **2,500** | **1,701.4** | **798.6** | **2,500** | **641.0** | **1,859.0** | **+132.8%** |
| **5–10y (10Y auction)** | 2,700 | 1,902.7 | **797.3** | 2,600 | 670.7 | **1,929.3** | **+142.0%** |
| 10–25y (20Y) | 1,000 | 450.6 | 549.4 | 700 | 200.8 | 499.2 | −9.1% |
| 25y+ (30Y + 40Y/2) | 1,250 | 150.7 | 1,099.3 | 750 | 150.4 | 599.6 | −45.5% |

**The 5-year and the 10-year start ¥1.3bn a month apart — 0.16% — and take shocks nine percentage
points apart.** That is as close to a matched pair as an observational rates calendar is ever going
to hand anybody. And the auction outcomes are opposite: **tail share ≥2bp since 2024 is 0.0% at the
5-year (33 auctions, max 1.5bp) against 19.4% at the 10-year (max 5.5bp).** If net supply into
private hands drove auction stress, these two could not diverge. Registered as
`FT-jgb-5y-auction-2026-11-12-1`.

**2. "The 10-year is the only tenor where net supply rose" — REFUTED as stated, because it was
computed over three of six buckets.** The sibling's table carries 5–10y, 10–25y and 25y+ only.
Extending it to the short end, **three tenors rose**: the 2-year **+49.4%**, the 5-year **+132.8%**
and the 10-year **+142.0%**. The correct statement is narrower and still interesting: *net private
absorption rose everywhere the BoJ cut and MOF did not, which is the entire 1y–10y region*; the
super-long fell because MOF cut issuance **−30% to −57%** there.

**3. The ≥2bp tail-share metric is duration-biased, and it manufactured the "deterioration splits at
ten years" result — REFUTED. This is the finding that reaches furthest.** A tail is quoted in **yield**;
a fixed yield threshold is not comparable across tenors whose tails scale with duration. Expressed
in each tenor's own pre-2024 distribution, 2bp is a wildly different event:

| Tenor | Own 2007–23 tail p90 | 2bp as a multiple of it | 2bp as a **price** tail | Share ≥2bp, 2024+ | **Share above own p90, 2024+** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2Y | 0.60bp | **3.33×** | 0.039 yen | 0.0% | **32.3%** (10/31) |
| **5Y** | **0.77bp** | **2.60×** | 0.094 yen | **0.0%** | **39.4%** (13/33) |
| 10Y | 1.10bp | 1.82× | 0.175 yen | 19.4% | **32.3%** (10/31) |
| 20Y | 1.50bp | 1.33× | 0.306 yen | 32.3% | **38.7%** (12/31) |
| 30Y | 1.60bp | **1.25×** | 0.394 yen | 19.4% | **25.8%** (8/31) |

**Every tenor deteriorated, by an amount that does not distinguish them.** Against the 10% a
stationary distribution implies, the z-scores run **+4.13 · +5.63 · +4.13 · +5.33 · +2.93** (exact
binomial tails 6.1e-04 to 8.3e-06). The widest gap on the board — the 5-year's 13/33 against the
30-year's 8/31 — is **z = +1.16, not significant at 5%**. On this metric **the 5-year is the worst
tenor on the curve and the 30-year the best**, the exact reverse of the absolute-threshold reading.
Registered as `FT-jgb-5y-auction-2026-11-12-4`.

**What this does and does not do to the sibling's leg 3.** It leaves the *refusal* standing and
removes the *replacement*. The dose story still cannot be supported — but neither can "duration",
because on a scale-free metric there is no split to explain. For completeness and not as a
statistic: the Spearman between dose depth and scale-free deterioration over the five tenors is
**−0.70** (deeper dose → more deterioration), i.e. **directionally the opposite of the inversion the
sibling reported**. At n=5 that is not evidence for the dose story; it is evidence that **n=31–33
auctions per tenor cannot discriminate between these stories at all**, and that the appearance of
discrimination came from the metric.

**4. Duration × volatility — the alternative the sibling named and explicitly did not test — TESTED,
and REFUTED as a proportional explanation.** From the par curve, daily first differences:

| Tenor | Daily sd 2007–23 | Daily sd 2024+ | **Vol ratio** | **Tail ratio** | tail ÷ (mod. dur × daily sd) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2Y | 1.077bp | 1.936bp | **1.80** | 1.75 | 0.145 |
| 5Y | 1.774 | 2.707 | **1.53** | 1.89 | 0.056 |
| 10Y | 1.961 | 3.039 | **1.55** | 2.35 | 0.042 |
| 20Y | 2.110 | 3.369 | **1.60** | 2.05 | 0.029 |
| 30Y | 2.264 | 3.466 | **1.53** | 1.75 | 0.019 |

**The volatility shock was almost uniform across the curve (1.53×–1.80×) and the tail response was
not (1.75×–2.35×), and the two are uncorrelated (−0.355, n=5).** The 2-year took the *largest* vol
shock and its ≥2bp share went to zero. In levels the vol-normalised tail falls monotonically with
duration by a factor of **7.8× from the 2-year to the 30-year**, so a constant "tails are duration ×
vol" rule is wrong in the other direction too. What survives is descriptive: **tails stepped up
roughly uniformly in each tenor's own units, and neither the taper's dose nor the volatility regime
explains the size of that step.** Registered as `FT-jgb-5y-auction-2026-11-12-5`.

**5. The 10-year's issue-rotation rule does not generalise to the 5-year — SUPPORTED, and it changes
what can honestly be predicted about 11-12.** The sibling established a near-deterministic 10-year
rotation (32 of 34 completed issues run exactly three auctions; **every** maturity date carries
exactly one issue) and used it to call 11-05 "issue 384's first reopening." The 5-year does not work
that way. Across the **103 auctions from 2018-01**:

| Property | 5-year | 10-year |
|---|---|---|
| Auctions per completed issue | **1: 24 · 2: 9 · 3: 19 · 4: 1** | 2: 1 · **3: 32** · 4: 1 |
| Maturity dates carrying >1 issue number | **9 carry two, 5 carry three** | **0** — all 35 carry one |
| Reopening vs new number, auction to auction | **51 reopen · 53 open a new number** | reopens by rule |
| New issues that kept the prior maturity date | **19 of 53** (a pure coupon re-set) | — |

MOF re-sets the 5-year's coupon by opening a **fresh issue number at the same maturity** rather than
reopening off-par — issue 186 (2026-07-09, **2.0%**) and issue 187 (2026-08-18 and 09-08, **2.2%**)
both mature **2031-06-20**, read straight off `eresul20260818.htm` and `eresul20260908.htm`. The best
single-threshold rule on the yield-minus-running-coupon gap (new issue when the gap reaches
**+0.062pp**) calls it correctly **74 of 104 times, 71.2%** — better than a coin flip and nowhere
near a rule. **So the issue number for 11-12 is deliberately not predicted here.**

**What is predictable is the maturity**, and it is as strong as anything the family has: the 5-year
matures on the **20th of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec in 103 of 103** auctions, and each auction quarter maps to
exactly **one** maturity date in **34 of 35** quarters — the sole exception being **2018Q1, the
window's own first**, the same shape as the 10-year's issue-350 exception. The recent chain runs
2025Q4 → 2030-09-20, 2026Q1 → 2030-12-20, 2026Q2 → 2031-03-20, 2026Q3 → 2031-06-20. **So 2026Q4, and
therefore 2026-11-12, carries maturity 2031-09-20**, opened by the 2026-10-14 auction. Registered as
`FT-jgb-5y-auction-2026-11-12-2`.

**7. The "±0.0% issuance since 2024" the proposal rested on is an endpoint identity over a round

### Honest limits

- **The matched pair is observational and endpoint-matched.** Two tenors ¥1.3bn/month apart at one
  date, hit with shocks nine points apart, is a strong natural comparison — it is not random
  assignment, and the paths between the endpoints differ (leg 7). It is enough to refuse the supply
  channel as *the* explanation; it does not identify what is.
- **The BoJ buckets are not tenor-pure, and this cuts against every leg that maps a bucket to an
  auction.** The 3–5y bucket absorbs seasoned 10-year and 20-year paper as well as new 5-years, and
  the 5–10y bucket the same. The sibling inherited this caveat and so does this ledger; neither has a
  way to decompose the purchases by original tenor.
- **The BoJ series is successful-purchase amounts, not offered amounts or holdings.** Every operation
  in the window cleared essentially in full, so the first two are interchangeable here; neither is
  the *stock* of 3–5y paper, which a pure stock-effect story would want.
- **2022–2023 is excluded from the operation series for cause** (YCC defence ran a different policy),
  inherited from the sibling and re-stated so a future session can undo it deliberately.
- **The scale-free metric in leg 3 has its own arbitrary choice.** "Above each tenor's own 2007–2023
  p90" is one normalisation; p95 was also computed (2Y 12.9% · 5Y 21.2% · 10Y 25.8% · 20Y 32.3% ·
  30Y 19.4%) and orders the tenors differently, which is itself the point — **no threshold on this
  data separates the stories**, and any single one that appears to is doing so by construction.
- **The Spearman in leg 3 and the correlation in leg 4 are both n=5.** Neither is reported as a
  statistic; they are reported so a future session does not compute them, find a sign it likes, and
  present it as a finding.
- **Modified durations in leg 4 are approximations** (2Y 1.95, 5Y 4.72, 10Y 8.75, 20Y 15.3, 30Y
  19.7), not computed per-issue from coupons and prices. They are used only for ordering and for the
  price-tail column, both of which are robust to a few percent.
- **The issue number for 11-12 is not predicted, and the maturity is derived rather than announced.**
  MOF has published no announcement for either the 10-14 or the 11-12 auction and prints no issue
  number for an unsettled row. The maturity inference rests on 103 of 103 and 34 of 35, but MOF's
  calendar reserves the right to change terms *"in light of changes in circumstances."*
- **The auction result time is derived, not sourced.** MOF's announcement pages carry no time field,
  which this session checked directly; the ~12:35 JST convention inherits the limit every sibling
  records.
- **The holiday adjustment in leg 6 rests on one instance.** Mountain Day explains the single
  eight-day lead of 2026, and the rule predicts 11-05 — but one exception is one exception, and the
  2026 sample is nine auctions.
- **The two most recent 5-year auctions are read off MOF result pages, not the workbook**, which
  stops at 2026-07-09. Parsed cell-by-cell against the pages' own labels; not yet reconciled against
  a later workbook release.
- **The hedge-gap numbers are approximations** — short-rate differential, cross-currency basis
  excluded, which has historically been negative for JPY and would widen the true domestic advantage
  at every tenor.
- **The "essentially domestic bid" reading in leg 10 is an observation, not a test.** No
  investor-category flow data was pulled; the hedge-gap ordering is consistent with it and does not
  establish it.
- **The auction result does not exist**, and neither does its size. Everything here is prior.
- **VIX and USD/JPY are Yahoo, i.e. secondary**, and both 09-09 readings are intraday. MOF's par
  curve and the US curve both run through **2026-09-08**.
- **The date and the size are both `estimate`.** MOF is a primary and the date is not in doubt; the
