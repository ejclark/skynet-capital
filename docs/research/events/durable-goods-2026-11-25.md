## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Is 2026-11-25 an anomalous slot for the October-2026 advance durable goods report, as the calendar
entry asserts; what kind of session is the Wednesday before Thanksgiving; and does anything about
either license an action for a book holding NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL AMZN?

**One-line verdict:** the slot is not anomalous — it is the series' standing Thanksgiving-week
address, 7 for 7 across sourced Census schedules — and the session is the **most compressed regular
session this calendar has measured**, roughly 41% narrower than normal on SPY, which sharpens the
refusal rather than creating an opportunity.

### Method

Macro-print mode per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md) — `symbols: []`, so
neither `earnings-cycle.mjs` nor `intraday-edges.mjs` applies and the cache-busting rule has no
target. Four inputs:

1. **Two Census primaries, fetched and parsed directly 2026-09-06** —
   `census.gov/manufacturing/m3/release_schedule.html` (53,177 bytes, the advance/full-report grid
   decoded row by row) and `census.gov/economic-indicators/calendar-listview.html` (91,396 bytes,
   179 table rows, which carries the 8:30 AM time, the reference month and the release id).
2. **Six archived copies of those same two Census pages**, recovered from the Internet Archive this
   session, to reconstruct **actual scheduled release dates** for the October reference month back to
   2019. Snapshots used: `20190906094211` and `20200712190650` (indicator calendar, 2019 and 2020
   schedules), `20221006160444` (2022), `20230108210941`, `20231208225612` and `20241128082737` (M3
   release schedule, 2023–2025). These are archived copies of the primary, not an aggregator.
3. **An original measurement, run for this doc.** Yahoo daily bars — SPY from 1993-01-29, QQQ from
   1999-03-10 — reduced to three per-session statistics (overnight **gap**, prior close → open, the
   window an 08:30 release lands in; intraday **range**, (high − low) / prior close; **close-to-close**),
   with every **pre-Thanksgiving Wednesday** ranked against its **own trailing 250 sessions**. The
   rolling baseline is deliberate: a 33-year sample spans four volatility regimes, so a fixed
   two-year baseline (the method the 10-27 and 09-25 ledgers used over shorter windows) would confound
   the effect with regime. **Null control:** every *other* Wednesday in the sample, scored identically.
4. **A holiday-carry measurement** — pre-Thanksgiving Wednesday close → the following Monday close,
   against every two-session close-to-close move in the SPY sample.

Baselines end at the **2026-09-04 close** (Friday; today is Sunday 2026-09-06, so no partial bar is
involved). The 2026 forecast-dispersion table, the semiconductor exclusion and the 2025-lapse slip
table are **carried** from the 09-25 and 10-27 ledgers, not re-fetched. Genre model:
[`nvda-aug-2026-print.md`](../nvda-aug-2026-print.md).

2. **"Pulled forward onto the Wednesday before Thanksgiving" is REFUTED — this is the series'
   standing slot, 7 for 7.** The entry note frames the date as a displacement. Census's own schedules,
   recovered from six archived snapshots plus today's live pages, say otherwise:

   | Reference month | Advance report scheduled | Weekday | Thanksgiving that year | Relationship |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | Oct 2019 | **2019-11-27** | Wed | 2019-11-28 | Wednesday before |
   | Oct 2020 | **2020-11-25** | Wed | 2020-11-26 | Wednesday before |
   | Oct 2021 | **2021-11-24** | Wed | 2021-11-25 | Wednesday before |
   | Oct 2022 | **2022-11-23** | Wed | 2022-11-24 | Wednesday before |
   | Oct 2023 | **2023-11-22** | Wed | 2023-11-23 | Wednesday before |

   | **Oct 2026** | **2026-11-25** | Wed | 2026-11-26 | Wednesday before |

   The mechanism is arithmetic, not coincidence: this series' steady-state publication lag is **~25–26
   days** after reference-month end (verifiable on the live 2026 grid: Jul→8/26, Aug→9/25, Sep→10/27,
   Oct→11/25, Nov→12/23), and Thanksgiving is the fourth Thursday, i.e. **Nov 22–28**. A ~25-day slot
   for October data therefore lands inside Thanksgiving week **every year**, and Census places it on
   the last full session. **One honest wrinkle:** the 2025 row is the *scheduled* date — the 2025
   funding lapse pushed October-2025 data to **2025-12-23**, and the 2025-11-26 slot ended up carrying
   the delayed **September**-2025 report instead. So a durable-goods release landed on that Wednesday
   in 2025 too; only the reference month differs.

### Honest limits

The **compression finding is a session-level statistic, not an event study** — it says what this
Wednesday does, not what the durable-goods release does to it, and the 6-date release subset
(**0.528×**, 6/6 below median, **p = 0.031**) is too small to separate the two. Compression may also
be partly **mechanical**: a session preceding a full closure has less time for positioning to
unwind, which would predict the same result with no information content at all; no test here
distinguishes those. **n = 32 (SPY) / 26 (QQQ)**, one observation per year, so the effective sample is
years, not sessions, and the p-values are single-hypothesis values against a null — no multiple-testing
correction is applied across the four statistics reported. **Leg 6 is disclosed as a likely false
positive** and no call rests on it. The **2025 row of leg 2's table is a scheduled date, not a
published one** (the lapse moved it). The archived Census snapshots are **copies of the primary**, and
the 2018-and-earlier October-reference dates were **not recoverable** — no snapshot exists before
2019-09. The **2026 forecast table and the semiconductor exclusion are carried**, inheriting the 09-25
ledger's single-source (mql5) caveat. **No October-data consensus exists at D-80** and, on the 2025–26
pattern, none will before release week — so this doc has no surprise benchmark and does not pretend to
one. Boeing's October-orders publication timing (~11-09) rests on one dated comparable and its mapping
to the headline is unmeasured. And the release itself disclaims measurable statistical significance:
M3 is not a probability sample, and Census computes no confidence interval for any figure quoted here.
