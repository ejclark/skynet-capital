## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **This is not "the smaller second leg." It is the day the whole 2022 fuel-duty cut ends, and
its size is not fixed at 2p — it depends on what happened in January.** SI 2026/164's articles 8–11
have effect only *"in the period beginning with 1st January 2027 and ending with 28th February 2027"*
(legislation.gov.uk, HTTP 200, 2026-09-09), and **nothing replaces them**. The Order is a
*percentage-deduction* instrument, not a rate-setting one — its own Table D reads *"Amount payable
before adjustment 0.5795 | Percentage deduction 3.45 | Amount payable after adjustment 0.5595"* — so
**57.95p is the untouched Hydrocarbon Oil Duties Act 1979 s.6(1A) statutory rate** and every "cut"
since 2022 is a rebate off it. On 1 March 2027 the last rebate lapses and the statutory rate applies
with nothing left to disapply it: **+2.00p if the January leg ran (55.95p → 57.95p), or +5.00p in one
day if January was deferred (52.95p → 57.95p, the entire cut unwinding at once)**. That makes
**2027-03-01 the load-bearing date whichever way January goes** — and it sharpens the sibling lane's
`FT-uk-fuel-duty-rise-2027-01-01-2`, which models the two legs as symmetric. **Two structural facts
measured here, not asserted.** First, the annual renewal cadence arrives **22 days too late**: every
order in the five-year chain came into force on **23 March** — 2022/365, 2023/329, 2024/300, 2025/228,
2026/164 — because s.2(2) of the 1979 Act anchors the one-year clock to the 2022 in-force date, so the
customary vehicle cannot reach 1 March and a deferral needs an **out-of-cycle** order. Second, the
timing would be **unprecedented**: DESNZ's own duty column across **1,214 weekly rows** shows the
tightest gap between any two duty changes in 23 years is **12 weeks**; a January-plus-March pair would
be **8 weeks**. **The fiscal leg the sibling could not fetch is computed here** from HMRC's clearance
volumes: FY2025-26 main-rate clearances of **45,723 Ml** make the 2p step worth **~£914m** a year, the
January 3p **~£1,372m**, the whole cut **~£2,286m**. **And "restoring the pre-2022 rate" is nominal
only** — 57.95p in March 2027 is **47.49p** in March-2022 money, and RPI-uprating from March 2011 would
have reached **104.46p**, so the restoration recovers **55%** of the real 2011 rate. **Size in the
print: ~0.04pp** on March-2027 headline CPI (~0.10pp on the 5p contingency), published **~2027-04-21**.
**Nothing here is ours.** `symbols: []`, no UK-listed name, no energy-, rates- or sterling-keyed
playbook. Date is **estimate**; it widens caution and licenses nothing.

- **Read this date as the terminal date of the whole 5p cut, not as a 2p step.** After 28 February 2027

- **The one date that can reach this is 2026-10-28**, and the reason is mechanical: the annual renewal

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Does the UK fuel duty rate reach 57.95p/l on 1 March 2027, how big is the step, and is any of it ours?
The id reached this lane as **one** proposal,
`from-uk-fuel-duty-rise-2027-01-01` (filed 2026-09-09), read in full before a line of this was written
— it is now shadowed by the canonical `src/domain/market-events/uk-fuel-duty-rise-2027-03-01.json`
written in this PR. **Its central framing is corrected here**, not carried forward: the proposal calls
this "the second and final leg of the 5p unwind… ~2.4p pump effect". The 2p figure is conditional, and
the condition is the whole story.

**One-line verdict: this is the date the 2022 cut ends — by +2p or by +5p depending on January — it is
the only one of the two legs the annual renewal machinery cannot reach, and none of it is tradeable
here.**

### Leg 1 — is this a 2p step? **REFUTED: its size is conditional, and the conditional case is 5p.**

This is the leg the proposal did not have, and it inverts its framing.

SI 2026/164 article 1(4), as amended by SI 2026/555 article 2(2)(c), gives articles 8–11 effect
*"in the period beginning with 1st January 2027 and ending with 28th February 2027."* **Nothing in
either Order operates after that date.** And the Order does not *set* a rate — every one of its
adjustments is expressed as a deduction from a pre-existing amount. Its Table D, verbatim:

| (A) Product | (B) Amount payable before adjustment (£/l) | (C) Percentage deduction | (D) Amount payable after adjustment (£/l) |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Unleaded petrol | 0.5795 | 3.45 | 0.5595 |
| (c) Heavy oil | 0.5795 | 3.45 | 0.5595 |

Column (B) is the **Hydrocarbon Oil Duties Act 1979 s.6(1A) statutory rate**. It has not moved. Every
"cut" since March 2022 is a rebate against it, and the Explanatory Note says so — *"The adjustments
made by this Order are all in the form of a deduction from the amount payable… of a specified
percentage not exceeding 10 per cent."*

So the three live paths through 1 March 2027 are:

| Path | Rate on 28 Feb 2027 | Step on 1 Mar 2027 | At the pump (incl. VAT) |
|---|---|---|---|
| January leg ran as legislated | 55.95p | **+2.00p** | +2.40p |
| January deferred by a further continuation of the 2022 Order | 52.95p | **+5.00p** | +6.00p |
| Both deferred by a *fresh* adjustment order with a new effect window | 52.95p | none | — |

Note what the third path costs: after 28 February there is no live provision to extend, so a deferral
of this step cannot be done by the two-word date substitution SI 2026/555 used ("31st August 2026" →
"31st December 2026"). It requires a **new adjustment order making a new deduction against the
statutory rate**. Legally that is the same class of act under the same power; the difference is
drafting and timing, not constitutional friction — stated plainly rather than overclaimed.

**A structural ceiling worth recording:** s.1(2) of the 1979 Act caps an adjustment at 10 per cent, and
the 5p cut is **8.63%** of 57.95p. This instrument cannot go much deeper than it already has.

### Leg 2 — can the annual renewal machinery reach 1 March? **REFUTED: it arrives 22 days late.**

Fetched from the statute book, four separate instruments, all HTTP 200 on 2026-09-09:

| Order | Made | Coming into force |
|---|---|---|
| S.I. 2022/365 | 23 March 2022, 1.23 p.m. | **23 March 2022**, 6.00 p.m. |
| S.I. 2023/329 | 14 March 2023 | **23 March 2023** |
| S.I. 2024/300 | 5 March 2024 | **23 March 2024** |
| S.I. 2025/228 | 26 February 2025 | **23 March 2025** |
| S.I. 2026/164 | 25 February 2026 | **23 March 2026** |

Five for five on the same calendar date, and the reason is in s.2(2) of the 1979 Act, quoted in SI
2026/164's own Explanatory Note: an order *"shall cease to be in force at the expiration of a period of
one year from the date on which it takes effect, unless continued in force by a further order."* The
clock is anchored to 23 March 2022 and has been re-anchored there every year since.

**1 March 2027 falls 22 days before that anniversary.** Every one of the five prior deferrals was
delivered by a vehicle that would arrive too late to stop this step. A deferral here has to be
out-of-cycle — which is not impossible (SI 2026/555 was made 21 May 2026, in force 15 June 2026, and
SI 2022/365 was made and in force the same afternoon), but it has never been the route used for the
annual freeze decision itself.

**And there is no second venue.** `uk-autumn-budget-2026-10-28` is the only scheduled UK fiscal event
in this calendar, 124 days before this step. A gov.uk search for a Spring Statement 2027 returned
HTTP 200 with **no matching announcement** on 2026-09-09.

### Leg 3 — what does the duty series say about a two-step? **REFUTED as unprecedented.**

Computed here from DESNZ's own duty column, **1,214 weekly rows, 2003-06-09 → 2026-09-07**:

| Finding | Value |
|---|---|
| Duty changes in the whole series | **11** — nine rises (2003-10-06 → 2011-01-03), two cuts (2011-03-28, 2022-03-28) |
| Last week UK fuel duty **rose** | **2011-01-03**, 58.19p → 58.95p |
| Longest unchanged run | **574 weeks at 57.95p**, 2011-03-28 → 2022-03-21 — **the exact rate this step restores**, and 3.4× the next-longest run |
| Tightest gap between two duty changes | **12 weeks** (2011-01-03 → 2011-03-28) |
| Proposed January→March 2027 gap | **8 weeks** — a third tighter than anything in 23 years |
| Month of every duty change in the series | Oct ×3, Dec ×2, Apr ×2, Mar ×2 (both the 28th), Sep ×1, Jan ×1 — **never February, never early March** |

Two readings, and only the second is honest. The weak one is "a rise is overdue." The strong one is
that **the series has no precedent for two duty changes eight weeks apart**, which is direct evidence
against the sibling lane's symmetry assumption and *for* a partial resolution — in either direction.

*(A discrepancy worth flagging for the next session: the sibling ledger records "ten" pre-2011
increases. Recomputed here from the same two CSVs, the count is **nine**: 2003-10-06, 2006-12-11,
2007-10-01, 2008-12-01, 2009-04-06, 2009-09-07, 2010-04-05, 2010-10-04, 2011-01-03. Ledger rows are
append-only, so the sibling's row stands as written; this is the correction, not an edit of it.)*

### Leg 4 — what does a deferral cost? **SUPPORTED at ~£914m/yr, computed here.**

The sibling ledger left this leg unquantified because obr.uk and CBP-10340 both returned 403. Those
403s reproduced here — but the number is recoverable from a primary that is *not* blocked. HMRC's
Hydrocarbon Oils Bulletin (`Oils_Tab_Jun_26.ods`, published 2026-07-31, fetched direct 2026-09-09)
carries clearance quantities by financial year:

| FY2025-26 (provisional) | Million litres |
|---|---|
| Total petrol | 18,407.9 |
| Total diesel | 27,315.5 |
| **Main-rate total** | **45,723.3** |

(Gas oil and fuel oil are excluded — they are rebated at different rates.) At that run rate:

| Step | Full-year Exchequer value |
|---|---|
| **This step (+2p)** | **~£914m** — ~£76m for March 2027 alone |
| January step (+3p) | ~£1,372m |
| The whole 5p cut | **~£2,286m/yr** |

This cuts *against* the deferral base rate rather than for it, and it is the reason the quarter call is
graded **low** and not medium: deferring both legs costs a Chancellor framed on fiscal discipline
roughly **£2.3bn a year**, and this leg alone is 40% of it. The base rate is 5-for-5; the price of a
sixth is now measured rather than assumed.

### Leg 5 — is "restoring pre-2022 rates" true? **MIXED: nominally exact, really about half.**

**Nominally it is exact, and checked row by row.** The gov.uk paper's *"Rates from 1 March 2027"*
column equals its *"Rates prior to 23 March 2022"* column for **every one of the sixteen fuel types**
listed — petrol 0.5795, diesel 0.5795, Avgas 0.3820, marked gas oil 0.1114, LPG 0.3161, aqua-methanol
0.0790, and the rest. This date, not 1 January, is the full restoration.

**In real terms it is not a restoration at all:**

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| CPI (ONS `D7BT`), Mar-2022 → Jul-2026 | 117.1 → 142.9, **+22.0%** |
| 57.95p on 2027-03-01, in March-2022 money | **47.49p** |
| RPI (ONS `CHAW`), Mar-2011 → Jul-2026 | 232.5 → 419.1, **+80.3%** |
| 57.95p of March 2011, RPI-uprated | **104.46p** — the restoration recovers **55%** |

The operational point is not rhetorical. The Budget-2025 plan resumes **RPI uprating in future years**
*after* this step, so 57.95p is the **floor from which uprating restarts**, not a destination. That is
what makes this date structurally more significant than its 2p headline: it unfreezes the rate.

### Leg 6 — how big is it in the print? **SUPPORTED at ~0.04pp (or ~0.10pp).**

2p of duty plus 20% VAT is **2.40p** at the pump — **+1.46%** on 164.40p petrol, **+1.29%** on 186.36p
diesel (DESNZ, w/c 2026-09-07). ONS's 2026 CPI weight for liquid fuels, vehicle fuels and lubricants
(series **A9FS**) is **27.1840 parts per 1000**, giving **0.035–0.040pp** on the March-2027 headline.
On the 5p contingency it is **0.088–0.099pp** — call it **~0.10pp**, 2.5× larger and the only version
of this event that could plausibly enter a BoE narrative.

It lands in the March 2027 collection, published **~2027-04-21**. Unlike January's — which stacks with
the January Ofgem cap step — **that print is clean**: `uk-electricity-vat-reversion-2027-04-01` falls
in the April collection, published ~2027-05-19. So March 2027 is the cleaner of the two for actually
observing a duty effect in CPI, if anyone wanted to.

### Honest limits

- **The OBR's own costing is still unfetched** (403 twice, with and without browser headers). The
  £914m/£2,286m figures here are computed from HMRC clearance volumes at a static run rate — they carry
  **no behavioural response, no volume decline, and no VAT interaction**, all of which the OBR would
  model. Treat them as an order of magnitude, not a costing.
- **The 22-day gap is a cadence constraint, not a legal barrier.** SI 2026/555 (made 21 May 2026) and
  SI 2022/365 (made and in force the same afternoon) both prove out-of-cycle orders happen. The finding
  is that the *routine* vehicle cannot reach this date — not that nothing can.
- **`n = 5` is a small base rate on a political decision**, and this ledger now argues both sides of it:
  five-for-five deferral against an unprecedented 8-week double step and a measured £2.3bn price. That
  genuine two-sidedness is why the quarter call is graded **low**, not medium — and a low-confidence
  call is a stand-aside, never a small position.
- **A discovered date that is *not* worth a calendar slot.** SI 2026/164's own s.2(2) life expires
  **2027-03-22**, one year after it took effect. By then its adjustments have already lapsed (28
  February), so nothing happens on that date — it is a legal no-op and is deliberately **not** proposed
  as an event. Recorded here so the next session does not re-discover it.
- **The step is `estimate`, and both halves of that label are doing work** — the rate schedule is
  taxonomy-blocked from `confirmed` (no prefix covers HM Treasury, HMRC or the statute book), *and* the
  outcome is genuinely open by the publisher's own admission. Nothing date-keyed may act on either.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (estimate).** **Stand aside at every horizon — permanently, not pending information.** There
is no instrument and no playbook; the informational content was published 2026-05-22 and its resolution
is a UK fiscal decision with no channel into this book. Separately, and as forecasts rather than
positions, this lane takes **two** views, and the second is the one that earns this event its own file:

1. **Weakly, take the under on 57.95p printing on 1 March 2027** — the 5-for-5 deferral base rate is
   real. Confidence **low**, and lower than the sibling's medium on the January leg, because the two
   arguments this ledger added both cut against it: the 23-March renewal vehicle cannot reach this date,
   and deferral now carries a measured ~£2.3bn/yr price rather than an unquantified one.
2. **Expect this leg to resolve DIFFERENTLY from the January leg** — against
   `FT-uk-fuel-duty-rise-2027-01-01-2`, which predicts they move together. The mechanism is the 22-day
   gap between 1 March and the 23-March anniversary, and the supporting statistic is that no two duty
   changes in 1,214 weeks have ever fallen 8 weeks apart. Confidence **low**; this is a coin-flip
   sharpened by structure, not a conviction.

Because both confidences are low and the instrument set is empty, this changes **no** sizing. A
forecast is not a position, and a low-confidence forecast is not a small one.

**Kill switches:**

1. **The cut ends on schedule** — the DESNZ weekly road fuel prices row for **w/c 2027-03-01** prints
   **57.95p**. The base rate breaks and every future UK duty ledger re-weights.
   (`FT-uk-fuel-duty-rise-2027-03-01-1`)
2. **The legs move together** — both w/c 2027-01-04 and w/c 2027-03-01 reach their scheduled rates, or
   neither does. The asymmetry thesis is wrong. (`FT-uk-fuel-duty-rise-2027-03-01-2`)
3. **A second venue appears** — HM Treasury announces a spring 2027 fiscal event dated **on or before
   2027-02-28**. The "one venue, and the annual vehicle arrives 22 days late" model is wrong.
4. **The conditional-size model is wrong** — the w/c 2027-03-01 row prints a rate that is neither
   57.95p, 55.95p nor 52.95p. A novel adjustment exists that Leg 1's three paths do not cover, and Leg 1
   is rebuilt. (`FT-uk-fuel-duty-rise-2027-03-01-3`)
5. **A price channel appears** — any tracked name (NVDA/AVGO/MRVL/CRWV) moves **>2%** on a session
   attributable to a UK fuel-duty headline before **2027-03-01**. The `symbols: []` premise is false and
   this document is rebuilt from scratch, not amended.
