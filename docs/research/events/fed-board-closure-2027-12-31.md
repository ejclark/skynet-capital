## Initial research

### The question, plainly

On Friday **2027-12-31** the Federal Reserve **Board of Governors** is closed while the NYSE holds a full
regular session, the OCC runs a quarter-end expiration, and the Reserve Banks operate. The
[`fomc-minutes-2027-12-29`](fomc-minutes-2027-12-29.md) adjacency sweep discovered the date on 2026-09-08
and proposed it as
[`proposals/fed-board-closure-2027-12-31.from-fomc-minutes-2027-12-29.json`](../../../src/domain/market-events/proposals/fed-board-closure-2027-12-31.from-fomc-minutes-2027-12-29.json),
with two claims attached: that the calendar *"needs to know that no Board publication can land that day,"*
and that this is *"the one 2027 date where they come apart,"* because *"every other Fed-Board closure this
calendar would ever carry coincides with a market closure."*

Both claims are testable and neither had been tested. So: **is the Board's desk actually dark in a way
that costs the market anything, and is the divergence actually as rare as the proposal said?** And behind
both, the question the row exists to answer for a member: **is there anything to do on 2027-12-31?**

**One-line verdict:** stand aside — the closure displaces exactly one low-impact weekly release (**H.8**)
and provably not the one that matters (**H.4.1**, whose slot is Thursday), the divergence is *commoner*
than the proposal claimed in one sense and correctly rare only under a narrower definition it did not
state, and the session itself is a measured null on every dimension checked.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date and the split are triple-sourced, and K.8 names 2027-12-31 outright.** SUPPORTED. The page
   header states the rule verbatim — *"For holidays falling on Saturday, Federal Reserve Banks and
   Branches will be open the preceding Friday; however, the Board of Governors will be closed"* — and its
   first footnote enumerates the affected dates: *"Saturday - the Federal Reserve Banks are open, but the
   Board of Governors is closed on July 3, 2026, June 18, 2027, December 24, 2027, December 31, 2027, and
   November 10, 2028."* Its 2028 column carries New Year's Day as `January 1*` against that footnote, and
   the page records a dated correction: *"On July 8, 2026, the first footnote was updated to reflect the
   observance date for New Year's Day from December 31, 2028, to December 31, 2027."* That is the Board
   having revisited **this** observance thirteen months before the fact — the strongest form of sourcing
   this class ever gets. Corroborated by 5 U.S.C. 6103(b) and OPM's own *"if a holiday falls on a Saturday
   … the preceding Friday will be treated as a holiday"* (152,589 bytes, fetched today), and 2028-01-01 is
   a Saturday by computation, not assumption.

2. **The NYSE's Saturday rule has a New-Year's carve-out, and the exchange's own price record proves the
   asymmetry.** SUPPORTED. The 2027 NYSE column closes the preceding Friday for a Saturday holiday
   everywhere it applies — Christmas reads *"Friday, December 24 (Christmas Day observed)"*, Juneteenth
   *"Friday, June 18 (…observed)"* — and then the 2028 New Year's cell is an em dash with the footnote
   *"Because the holiday falls on Saturday, January 1, 2028, no New Year's Day holiday is observed."* No
   early-close footnote attaches: the exchange's only December half day across 2026/2027/2028 is
   *"Thursday, December 24, 2026"*, so 2027-12-31 is a full 9:30–16:00 session. **The carve-out is not
   new and is visible in the tape**: in every Saturday-Christmas year in the VIX era — 1993, 1999, 2004,
   2010, 2021, which are the same years by construction, since Dec 25 and the following Jan 1 share a
   weekday — CBOE's `SPX_History.csv` carries **no Dec-24 bar** (5 of 5) and **does** carry a Dec-31 bar
   (4 of 5, the exception being leg 8's data gap). The exchange closes the Friday before a Saturday
   holiday except when that Friday is the last business day of the year.

3. **The proposal's uniqueness claim is REFUTED as written; the correct claim is narrower and it is a
   census, not an adjective.** REFUTED, and this is the correction. *"Every other Fed-Board closure this
   calendar would ever carry coincides with a market closure"* is false in the commonest case available:
   the Board is closed and the NYSE holds a full session on **every Columbus Day and every Veterans Day**,
   both of which K.8 lists and neither of which appears anywhere in the NYSE's ten-row table. This
   calendar already tracks three of them from the bond side —
   [`sifma-bond-market-closure-2026-10-12`](sifma-bond-market-closure-2026-10-12.md),
   [`-2026-11-11`](sifma-bond-market-closure-2026-11-11.md),
   [`-2027-11-11`](sifma-bond-market-closure-2027-11-11.md). What is genuinely rare is not
   *Board-closed-while-equities-open* but **board-only dark**: the Reserve Banks *at work* while the Board
   is shut, which K.8's header confines to Saturday holidays. Run across K.8's five footnoted Saturday
   cases against the NYSE table, exactly **two** qualify — 2026-07-03, 2027-06-18 and 2027-12-24 are all
   full NYSE closures, leaving **2027-12-31** and **2028-11-10** (Veterans Day 2028 falls on a Saturday;
   proposed in this PR as

### What the conditions support (date `estimate`)

**One corrected calendar rule, one measured refusal, four nulls, no direction, no size, no level.**

- **Calendar 2027-12-31 as a full trading session with a dark Board desk, and treat that as bookkeeping.**
  It is the last session of 2027, an OCC quarter-end expiration, and a 14:00 ET bond close — none of which
  the evidence here makes actionable.
- **Reuse the corrected rule, not the proposal's version.** *Board-only dark* requires a Saturday federal
  holiday (K.8's header) and an NYSE that trades that Friday; the census across 2026–2030 is two dates.

  Day and Veterans Day.
- **Probe a Board closure's cost by release slot, not by the closure.** The archive is keyed to release
  date and answers in two states; the answer is "Thursday releases are immune, Friday releases displace."
- **Do not read the year's final session as an opportunity in either direction.** It is quieter

Explicitly **not** claimed: that the Reserve Banks' open status is sourced from the Reserve Banks (see

### Honest limits

**The Reserve Banks' own page did not yield its holiday table.**
`frbservices.org/about/holiday-schedules` returned **HTTP 200 (94,776 bytes)** but renders its schedule
client-side, so the HTML carries no 2027 content; the "Reserve Banks open" leg therefore rests on K.8's
own header sentence — a Board primary asserting it — and not on the Banks' own calendar. Not recorded in
`probe-ref.blocked`, because nothing failed: the fetch succeeded and the page is simply a JavaScript
application. **The H.10 probe was uninformative**, not negative: `releases/h10/<date>/` 404s for every
date tried including ordinary ones, so the daily FX-rate release could not be tested by the same
instrument and is excluded rather than counted. **The Board's 2027 statistical-release calendar does not
exist yet** and was not requested, so leg 5's Thursday claim is a four-instance empirical regularity plus
a stated cadence, not a published 2027 schedule. **The archive probe is an availability test, not a
publication test** — a 404 is strong evidence a release was not issued that day but is formally evidence
about the archive. **Leg 8 is n = 6 with two pre-VIX rows**, and its 1999 member is missing from the data.
**Leg 6's year-final set is n = 19** and leg 7's extension uses SPX alone, so its VIX and residual columns
have no long-history counterpart. **The status is `estimate` for a taxonomy reason** and cannot be
promoted from a better fetch; three primaries already agree and a fourth would not change the label.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `estimate`):** **stand aside on all four horizons, and bank the corrected calendar rule
instead.** The date and the Board/market split are triple-sourced, with K.8 naming 2027-12-31 in its own
footnote and recording a **2026-07-08** correction to that very observance. The closure is real and its
market cost is **measured at nil**: probed on the Board's own release archive by release date, **H.4.1**
sits in a Thursday slot (control pair 200/404) and published on Thursday December 30 in all three prior
instances, while **H.8** — the one weekly Board release whose slot is this Friday — is **0 of 4** on
board-closure Dec 31s against **7 of 7** matched control year-end Fridays, displacing forward in 2010 and
2021 and backward in 2004. **2027-12-30 is a Thursday**, so the balance sheet lands as normal and the
closure's entire footprint is one week of bank-credit data on an adjacent day. The seeding proposal's
uniqueness claim is **refuted as written** — the Board is dark with equities trading every Columbus Day
and Veterans Day, three of which this calendar already carries — and replaced by a census: *board-only
dark*, the Reserve Banks at work while the Board is shut, is **two dates** in K.8's 2026–2030 window,
2027-12-31 and 2028-11-10. On the tape every dimension checked is a null (quarter-end sessions n = 76, all
P ≥ 0.17; year-final n = 19, all P ≥ 0.06), the one figure that looked alive — a 31.6% up-share on the
year's final session — **does not survive** extension to fifty years (44.0%, P = 0.201) and would not have
survived selection correction either, and the six prior instances of this exact configuration have a max
absolute move of **0.54%**. No directional call, no size; `estimate` widens caution and licenses nothing.
