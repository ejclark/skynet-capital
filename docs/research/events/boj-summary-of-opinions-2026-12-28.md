## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The [March 2027 sibling](boj-summary-of-opinions-2027-03-29.md) has already answered the generic
question. It parsed all **86** Summaries of Opinions ever published, established that the BoJ's is
the **fastest meeting account of the three majors** (median 10-day lag against the Fed's stated 21
and the ECB's stated ~28), and measured the release as a non-event on the S&P, the Nikkei and the
yen. None of that is re-litigated; this session **replicated** it on a different data source as a
check and moved on.

Two questions remain that are specific to **this** venue, and one of them the sibling explicitly
registered as an open kill it had no venue to test:

1. **The year-end question.** 2026-12-28 is a Monday in the thinnest liquidity week of the calendar
   year — a US session bracketed by a half day, a closure and year-end flow. Does that amplify the
   release (the intuitive reading) or is it just a quiet week?
2. **The acting question.** The sibling's *"Relevance kill (upward)"* says verbatim that *"an account
   of a meeting that acted is a different document from an account of a meeting that held — impact
   would be re-tiered and legs 2-7's pooled base rates re-cut on the acting subset."* It could not
   run that cut. Here it is live: the [parent 12-18 ledger](boj-decision-2026-12-18.md) calls a
   December hike **live, not modal**, so this may be the account of an acting meeting.

**One-line verdict: the year-end effect is real but belongs to the week and not the document
(1.08× a matched dead-week control against 0.78× an ordinary session), and the acting effect is real
but entirely Japanese — the Nikkei's acting-SoO median is 1.8× its holding median while the S&P's is
*lower* than its holding median.** The stance is stand aside, unchanged. What this session adds is
the two cuts, the matched control that makes the first one honest, the exact-analogue observation
(2025-12-29), and a second BoJ document in the corridor that publishes five days earlier.
