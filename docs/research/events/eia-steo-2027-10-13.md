## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Stand aside — and stop reading the calendar's own "Winter Fuels Outlook" label as a
delivery promise.** The November sibling correctly established that the Winter Fuels Outlook is an
**October** product, and that fix stands. This session tested the half of it nobody had checked: does
the winter product actually *land* on the October STEO's release date? **On EIA's own primaries, not
reliably.** The 2025–26 Winter Fuels Outlook article states in its own body that it *"was published
on October 15, 2025, and … based on price and weather forecasts from the **October 2025 edition**"* —
while EIA's live STEO Archives shows that October 2025 edition released **10/7/2025**. An **eight-day
gap**. In 2024 the two coincided (2024-10-08). **n=2, split 1–1**, so the honest reading of the
`Winter Fuels Outlook` string in EIA's Notes cell is *which edition the supplement is built from*,
not *when the winter numbers exist*. Second finding, purely mechanical: of the 24 rows in EIA's live
table, **10/13/2027 is the only holiday-shifted Wednesday EIA failed to annotate "(Wednesday)"** —
09/09/2026, 07/07/2027 and 09/08/2027 all carry the marker. The **date** survives that anyway,
because the rule-plus-footnote reproduces it exactly (first Thursday 10-07 → Tuesday 10-12 → Monday
10-11 is Columbus Day → Wednesday **10-13**) and the **Fed's own K.8 holiday page** independently puts
Columbus Day 2027 on **October 11**. The missing annotation is recorded as a source inconsistency, not
as date doubt. Structurally this edition publishes into **October expiration week** (two days before
[`opex-2027-10-15`](opex-2027-10-15.md), **confirmed**), on the Wednesday of a week whose Monday the
bond market is shut ([`sifma-bond-holiday-close-2027-10-11`](sifma-bond-holiday-close-2027-10-11.md))
— **the same holiday that moved this date is already an event on this calendar**. The one clean
observation of the winter product publishing *alone* (2025-10-15, itself an expiration-week Wednesday)
shows the **entire energy complex at or below its ordinary noon hour** — NG=F **0.10% vs 0.56%**, USO
**0.06% vs 0.27%**, CL=F **0.26% vs 0.28%** — while equities ran hot, which is the sharpest available
proof that the winter document is not what moves a tape. The event is **estimate**-dated with
`symbols: []` and sits **399 days out**; nothing here licenses any date-keyed action.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is the best-derived EIA date on this calendar — SUPPORTED, on three independent
   footings.** (a) The **table cell**: `release_schedule.php`, re-fetched 2026-09-08 at 54,906
   bytes — byte-identical to the November sibling's fetch, so nothing on the page has moved in a day
   — parses to `['October 2027', '10/13/2027', 'Winter Fuels Outlook']`. (b) The **rule plus its
   footnote**, both quoted verbatim from the same page: release "on the first Tuesday following the
   first Thursday of each month", and *"If a Federal holiday falls on the Monday before the normal
   release date the release is delayed until Wednesday."* The first Thursday of October 2027 is
   **10-07**; the following Tuesday is **10-12**; the Monday before is **10-11**. (c) The **holiday**,
   from a source with no EIA involvement at all: `federalreserve.gov/aboutthefed/k8.htm` (fetched
   direct 2026-09-08, HTTP 200, 82,205 bytes, page's own "Last Update: July 8, 2026") carries the row
   `['Columbus Day', 'October 12', 'October 11', 'October 9', 'October 8', 'October 14']` across
   2026–2030, so **Columbus Day 2027 is October 11** — which is also the second Monday of October
   2027 by arithmetic. Table, rule and holiday all land on **2027-10-13**. Filed **estimate** on
   prefix grounds only: this calendar defines no `confirmed` prefix for the EIA — the standing schema
   gap [`eia-steo-2026-09-09`](eia-steo-2026-09-09.md) named — and this lane never self-confirms an
   event it discovered in-sweep.

2. **The "Winter Fuels Outlook" label does not date the winter product — REFUTED as a delivery
   promise, and this is the finding this session exists for.** The chain of corrections behind this
   entry runs: the October sibling called **2026-11-10** the Winter Fuels edition (wrong);
   [`eia-steo-2026-11-10`](eia-steo-2026-11-10.md) corrected it on four EIA primaries — the winter
   product is an **October** product, Notes cell attached to 10/06/2026 and 10/13/2027 — and proposed
   *this* event so the corrected fact would live in calendar data. That correction is right and is
   not disturbed. What no session had checked is the implicit second half: that the October STEO's
   release date is therefore when the winter numbers exist. **EIA's own primaries say otherwise.**
   The 2025–26 article (`.../perspectives/2025/10-winterfuels/article.php`, fetched direct
   2026-09-08, HTTP 200, 139,441 bytes) is headed *"Winter Fuels Outlook 2025–26 / as published on
   October 15, 2025, unless otherwise noted"* and states in its body: *"The data values and analysis
   below were published on **October 15, 2025**, and were based on price and weather forecasts from
   the **October 2025 edition** of the [STEO]."* EIA's **live Archives** page (`outlook.php`, HTTP
   200, 173,992 bytes) lists that October 2025 edition as released **10/7/2025**, and the archived
   2025 schedule table carries `['October 2025', '10/07/2025', 'Winter Fuels Outlook']`. **Eight
   days.** The 2024–25 article's forecast-comparison tables are labelled *"Release date: October 8,
   2024"* — the same day as the October 2024 STEO, per the November sibling's own read. So the base
   rate is **n=2, split 1–1**, and the correct reading of the Notes string is *which edition the
   supplement is derived from*. This session amends the canonical entry's title accordingly. It is
   worth being explicit about what did **not** cause the 2025 gap: the October 2025 STEO **published
   on schedule** despite a Federal funding lapse that began 2025-10-01 (Archives: 10/7/2025), so the
   supplement's slip is not a shutdown artifact borrowed from the STEO — the shutdown reading was
   available and the primary does not support it.

   exactly what EIA's own rule and footnote produce, and 10-12 would require EIA to contradict its own

   carrying: 2027-10-13 also falls outside the same page's stated *"normally appear between the 6th
   and the 12th of the month"* window — one of only **two** dates in the live 24-month table that do,
   the other being 01/13/2026, and both are holiday artifacts.
