## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This event exists because the `new-home-sales-2026-11-25` lane's adjacency sweep proposed it, on a
coverage argument: this calendar tracked the **quantity** side of housing in six places — existing-home
sales, pending-home sales, housing starts, construction spending, new-home sales — and had **no
house-price entry at all**, while the proposer's own release publishes a median and an average price
every month with no independent read beside it.

**Is the release real and regular, does it reach the nowcast, does it reach a price, and is the number
it publishes worth reading on the day it lands?**

**One-line verdict:** it is real and *the most regular release on this calendar* — statutorily
immune to the lapses that delayed its Census siblings by 105 days — and it reaches neither the model
nor, so far as anything can be measured, a price; the thing to hold it for is that it keeps publishing
when nothing else does.

### Leg 2 — the schedule is the best on this calendar, and it is lapse-proof by statute · **SUPPORTED**

The 271-row archive gives an unbroken record.

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| Months in the monthly era (2008-03 → 2026-08) carrying a release | **222 of 222** |
| Releases since 2021-01-01 | **68**, all on the **last Tuesday** of their month — **68 of 68** |
| Weekday distribution since 2021 | Tuesday, 68 of 68 |
| Months with two releases (a lapse catch-up doubling up) | **0** |
| Q3 quarterly slot, 2008 → 2025 | every year in the **Nov 22–30** window, without exception |

The 2026-11-24 date is the **nineteenth consecutive** Q3 report in that window (2020-11-24,
2021-11-30, 2022-11-29, 2023-11-28, 2024-11-26, 2025-11-25 are the recent six), and the
**sixty-ninth consecutive** last-Tuesday release.

**Why it is unbroken is the finding, not the count.** FHFA does not receive an annual appropriation.
It funds itself by assessments on Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac and the eleven Federal Home Loan Banks, and
12 U.S.C. § 4516 says what those assessments legally are:

> **(f)(2) Not Government funds.** The amounts received by the Director from any assessment under
> this section shall not be construed to be Government or public funds or **appropriated money**.
>
> **(f)(3) No apportionment of funds.** Notwithstanding any other provision of law, the amounts
> received … shall **not be subject to apportionment** for the purpose of chapter 15 of title 31 or
> under any other authority.

Chapter 15 of title 31 is the apportionment machinery the Antideficiency Act runs on — the mechanism
by which an appropriated agency must stop work in a funding lapse. FHFA is outside it, and the
release record is the receipt:

| Event | FHFA HPI | The Census housing siblings |
|---|---|---|
| 2018-12-22 → 2019-01-25 shutdown (35 days) | **published 2018-12-27 and 2019-01-23, both inside it** | Census suspended economic-indicator releases |
| 2025 appropriations lapse | **published 2025-09-30, 10-28, 11-25, 12-30 and 2026-01-27, every one on the last Tuesday** | September-2025 new-home sales released **2026-01-13** — a **105-day** lag, with three dates carrying two reference months each to clear the backlog |

That asymmetry is the reason to hold this event. `cr-expiry-2026-12-11` is already tracked; in the
lapse branch of that fork, this series is one of the few housing reads still arriving, and the
`consumer-confidence-2026-10-27` ledger makes exactly the same argument for its private publisher.

**Two archive artifacts, recorded so the next lane does not re-derive them.** The report archive's
listing has **no January 2025 row** — but the document exists (`/document/d/hpi/fhfa-hpi-monthly-
january-2025`, HTTP 200, 1,169,929 bytes) and its own header reads *"Data through November 2024 —
Released on January 28, 2025"*, the last Tuesday of that month. The apparent gap is a **listing
omission, not a skipped release**, and the 222/222 figure above is corrected for it. Separately, the
archive carries one implausible weekday (**2016-11-13, a Sunday**), which is an archive metadata
error rather than a Sunday release; it sits outside every window used here.

### Leg 4 — the tape claim is **DECLINED**, and the confound is a cleaner one than the family has seen

This class is **not separable**, and the reason is a rule rather than an accident. The Conference
Board's own August-2026 release states: *"The Conference Board publishes the Consumer Confidence
Index® at 10 a.m. ET on the last Tuesday of every month."* FHFA HPI landed on the last Tuesday in
**68 of 68** releases since 2021. Therefore **every** FHFA HPI release day in the measured window is
also a Consumer Confidence day — a `medium`-impact print on this very calendar — and no daily bar can
attribute anything to FHFA. S&P CoreLogic Case-Shiller conventionally shares the same slot, which
would make it a third occupant, but its publisher returned **403** and that collision is stated here
as unverified rather than counted.

The measurement is still worth running, because a confounded **ceiling** is a real result: whatever
FHFA HPI contributes, it is contained inside a bundle that itself measures nothing.

| QQQ | 68 | 1.343% | 1.370% | 0.8044 |
| **ITB** | 68 | **1.905%** | 2.018% | 0.3482 |

1.738%** and ITB **3.281 · 1.596 · 1.024 · 2.335 · 4.494%** — a wide spread with no direction, on the
same days the Thanksgiving-week tape is thinning.

**How this differs from both siblings, and why the wording matters.** Construction spending was
*unmeasurable* (72 of 81 dates shared ISM Manufacturing's slot) and declined its claim. New-home sales
was *separable* (28 of 81 dates were Census-solo) and returned a measured null across eight
instruments. FHFA HPI is unmeasurable in the strongest form — **68 of 68**, by two publishers' stated
rules rather than by calendar luck — so the honest verdict is *declined*, and the number above is a
ceiling on a bundle, not a null on this print.

### Leg 5 — the number itself · **MIXED**: the revision is small, but so is the signal

FHFA publishes no margin of error on the HPI, so the Census-style "is this move distinguishable from
zero" test cannot be run. What it *does* publish, in every monthly table, is a **"(Previous Estimate)"**
row under each of the prior five months — its own one-step revision, in print. Extracting those from
21 reports gives **105 one-step revision observations**:

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Observations | **105** (21 reports × 5 months) |
| Revised at all | **37 of 105 (35.2%)** |
| \|revision\| median · mean · max | **0.00pp · 0.036pp · 0.20pp** |
| Revision size distribution | 0.0pp ×68 · 0.1pp ×36 · 0.2pp ×1 |
| \|current m/m\| median · mean · max | **0.30pp · 0.27pp · 0.70pp** |
| m/m printed as exactly **0.0** | **19 of 105** |
| \|revision\| ≥ \|the m/m it revises\| (non-zero m/m) | **9 of 86** |
| Sign flips (+ to − or − to +) | **0** |
| **Flips to or from exactly 0.0** | **15 of 105 (14.3%)** |

Two readings, and they pull against each other.

**The stable half.** The Census sibling's frame — *"the revision is bigger than the signal"* — does
**not** transfer, and neither does *"the revision reverses the direction more often than it confirms
it."* Zero of 105 observations flipped sign. A repeat-sales index built from closed Enterprise
mortgages simply does not move around the way a survey headline with a ±15.5% error bar does.

**The half that still bites.** The m/m is published to one decimal and its median absolute value is
**0.30pp**, against a maximum revision of 0.20pp — so the revision is small *relative to nothing much*.
Nineteen editions in 105 printed exactly `0.0`, and a 0.1pp revision moved the number to or from that
value **15 times**, which is precisely the granularity the headline sentence uses: *"FHFA's seasonally
adjusted monthly index for June remained unchanged from May"* is a claim a single 0.1pp restatement
overturns. FRED's `HPIPONM226S` agrees on the amplitude independently: over the last 60 months the
median \|m/m\| is **0.374%**, p75 **0.648%**, max **1.492%**, and **46 of 60** sat at or under 0.7%.

### Leg 6 — what 2026-11-24 will actually say, and how old it will be

**The two-month lag is the structural fact.** Every report states it in its own header — *"Data
through May 2026 — Released on July 28, 2026"*. The 11-24 print carries **September 2026** monthly data
and **2026Q3**. `new-home-sales-2026-11-25` reports **October** the next morning. **This calendar's
house-price read arrives one reference month behind the quantity read it is supposed to explain, and
one day ahead of it on the clock.**

**A naming trap worth banking.** From roughly December 2020 the report *titles* switched from naming
the data month to naming the release month: `U.S. House Price Index - July 2026` was released
2026-07-28 and contains **May** data. A lane joining the archive listing to a reference month on the
title alone will be off by two months on every row since 2020.

### Honest limits

- **The tape result is a ceiling, not a null, and the ledger says so everywhere it appears.** With
  68 of 68 release days confounded by Consumer Confidence, no design on daily bars can isolate this
  print. An effect confined to 9:00–9:15 would additionally be invisible in a daily bar even if the
  slot were clean. The claim made is "the bundle does not widen the session," and nothing narrower.
- **The Case-Shiller collision is asserted from convention, not from a source.** S&P returned 403,
  so the third occupant of the slot is named as unverified. If Case-Shiller does *not* share the
  slot, the confound is one print rather than two — which changes nothing about the decline.
- **The lapse-immunity argument is statutory plus two natural experiments, not a guarantee.** The
  statute is unambiguous and the 2018-19 and 2025 records are clean, but FHFA has never announced a
  policy of publishing through a lapse; it has simply always done so. A future Director could pause
  releases for reasons the statute does not compel, which is exactly what the `This quarter`
  falsifier watches for.
- **The revision series is 105 observations from 21 reports, not a designed sample.** Coverage is
  Dec-2024 → Aug-2026 with the reports the archive links; the same reference month appears in
  several reports as *different* one-step revisions, which is intended, but the set is determined by
  what was fetched rather than by sampling. It also measures only the **one-step** revision — FHFA
  re-estimates its whole index history at each release, and a multi-year restatement would not
  appear in a "(Previous Estimate)" row at all.
- **`USSTHPI` and the purchase-only index disagree at the margin.** FHFA's standing "positive every
  quarter since the start of 2012" line is about the purchase-only seasonally adjusted series; the
  all-transactions quarterly series on FRED shows **2 of 58** quarters negative (2012Q1 −1.62%,
  2012Q2 −0.31%), both at the boundary of the claimed window. The base rate quoted for `-1` uses the
  purchase-only proxy, which is the series the November print reports.
- **The quarterly proxy is built from monthly averages**, not from FHFA's published quarterly
  purchase-only index, which is estimated separately. It tracks closely but the two are not the same
  number, and no forward test here is registered against the proxy's exact value.
- **`symbols: []` is doing real work, again.** ITB, XHB, DHI, LEN, PHM, TOL and XLRE were measured
  because they are the obvious candidates for a house-price print, not because anything holds them.
  Even a clean, significant result would have no instrument to express it.
