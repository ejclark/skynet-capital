## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This id existed only as two proposals — `from-nar-metro-home-prices-2026-10-29` and
`from-pending-home-sales-2026-10-20` — filed the same day on the same coverage argument: the
calendar tracks six housing series and carried **zero** Case-Shiller entries, despite it being the
repeat-sales benchmark the others get read against. That argument is sound and this session writes
the canonical file. But coverage is not a finding, and one day earlier the
[fhfa-hpi-2026-10-27](fhfa-hpi-2026-10-27.md) lane had already taken 2026-10-27 apart in detail. So
the honest question is not "should we track Case-Shiller":

**What is true of THIS index that is not already true of the FHFA print published the same morning
— and is there any channel at all through which a 9:00 a.m. house-price release could reach a
price?**

**One-line verdict:** two things are true of it and both cut the same way — **it is
pre-chewed** (a three-month moving average at a two-month lag, forecastable from its own last value
to about a third of a typical monthly move) and **it has never had an attributable release day**
(all 68 since 2021 are shared with FHFA and Consumer Confidence, which this session proves by
reproducing the sibling's table exactly) — while the one genuinely new test its 9:00 a.m. stamp
permits, the pre-open gap, returns nothing on nine instruments.

### Leg 1 — the release exists on 2026-10-27 · **SUPPORTED**, and a sibling's doubt is **REFUTED**

> ET**.

October 2026's Tuesdays are the 6th, 13th, 20th and 27th. The two-month lag — the 2026-08-25 release
reports **June 2026** — puts **August 2026** data on that edition.

### Leg 3 — the 9:00 a.m. stamp buys one genuinely new test, and it returns nothing · **SUPPORTED**

Case-Shiller is the only one of the three last-Tuesday publishers with a **stated release time
before the open**: 9:00 ET, thirty minutes ahead of the bell. FHFA states no time at all (the
sibling says so explicitly) and the Conference Board publishes at 10:00, inside the session. So the
**overnight gap** is a channel available to this print and to neither of the others — the one place a
Case-Shiller-specific effect could show up without the other two contaminating it.

**Reading it honestly.** Nine tests, ~0.45 hits expected by chance, **zero** at p<0.05. The two

print could plausibly reach is flat, and the largest housing deviation (PHM, +0.082pp) sits at
p=0.4071.

**The honest alternative reading of the near-misses, stated rather than buried.** A calmer open on
the last Tuesday of the month is at least as consistent with **month-end positioning** as with
anything a house-price index does — the last Tuesday is inside the window where index rebalances and
month-end flows concentrate, and that story predicts exactly what the table shows: an effect in the
index products, not in the builders. This ledger prefers that explanation and registers the housing
form of the claim as `-3` rather than asserting either.

### Leg 4 — it is the most forecastable print on this calendar, by construction · **SUPPORTED** (the central finding)

The national index is a **three-month moving average** published at a **two-month lag**. So the
2026-10-27 edition averages **June, July and August** closings — two-thirds of which will already
have been published by then — and contains nothing signed after the **2026-09-16 FOMC**.

That is a qualitative claim everyone repeats. Measured against the obvious control, it is a large
quantitative one. Monthly m/m on seasonally-adjusted national series, 2015-01 → 2026-06 (n=138):

| | Case-Shiller national SA (`CSUSHPISA`) | FHFA purchase-only SA (`HPIPONM226S`) |
|---|---|---|
| lag-1 autocorrelation of m/m | **0.939** | 0.774 |
| **1-step persistence forecast MAE** | **0.119pp** | 0.218pp |
| 2-step persistence forecast MAE | **0.201pp** | 0.246pp |
| zero-forecast MAE (the null) | 0.544pp | 0.571pp |
| median \|m/m\|, last 60 months | 0.312pp | 0.374pp |

**Repeating last month's number forecasts the Case-Shiller print to 0.119pp — against a typical
monthly move of 0.312pp, and against 0.218pp for the same trick on FHFA.** The dumbest available
forecast removes **78%** of Case-Shiller's error and **62%** of FHFA's. That gap is the moving
average, and it is the reason a print this widely covered carries so little tradeable surprise: most
of what it reports has already been reported.

**Today sits at the 2-step distance** (the latest published month is June; July prints 09-29 and
August on 10-27), which is why the 09-29 edition is proposed in this PR rather than merely noted —
it roughly halves the uncertainty on this print, from 0.201pp to 0.119pp.

**And the two indices published that morning mostly agree, which removes the other candidate
surprise.** Same reference month, 2015+: m/m correlation **0.892**, sign agreement **127 of 138
(92.0%)**, median gap **0.122pp**. Since 2021 agreement loosens to **56 of 66 (84.8%)** — so roughly
**one morning in six** the two national house-price indices published within an hour of each other
print **opposite m/m signs** for the same month. That is a headline hazard worth knowing about, and
it is the one respect in which the morning is genuinely confusing; it is not an edge, because
nothing on this calendar holds a position either index could express.

The publisher's own framing is the sharpest line available on the state of housing, and it is worth

NSA y/y.

**Revisions: everything moves every month, by a rounding error — except the freshest month, which
moves by a fifth of a typical print.** Thirteen ALFRED vintages, 2025-08-26 → 2026-08-25, each
compared to its successor over a rolling two-year window:

- In **8 of 12** vintage transitions, **every** month in the window was revised. Across all 208
  revised observations the median magnitude is **0.2bp** and the p90 is **1.9bp** — statistically
  omnipresent, economically nil.
- The **newly-published month, one vintage later**, is a different story: median revision **~6bp**,
  with **−14.8bp** (2025-12) and **+9.7bp** (2026-05) inside twelve months. That is roughly **20% of
  a median monthly move (0.312pp = 31bp)** rewritten one month after it prints.

So the first print of any month is provisional at about a fifth of its own signal — which compounds
Leg 4 rather than offsetting it: the number is mostly known in advance **and** partly wrong on
arrival.
