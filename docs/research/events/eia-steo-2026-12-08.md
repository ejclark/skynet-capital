## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Stand aside — but not by inheriting the sibling ledgers' null, because this edition is
the one day their sample barely covers.** The October and November STEO ledgers concluded that a
STEO day is an ordinary day for our names, measured on the **eight 2026 editions**. This session
doubled that sample to **twenty** using EIA's own **2025** release-schedule table, and the extension
does two things. It **confirms the headline null and strengthens it** — full-day median |move|
across n=20: **NVDA 0.61% vs 1.64% on all days**, MRVL 1.25% vs 2.27%, AVGO 0.97% vs 1.61%, MSFT
0.46% vs 0.84%, SPY 0.25% vs 0.49% — and it **refutes two of the inherited numbers as artifacts of
the 2026 window**: the October ledger's "STEO days are ~2× more violent in crude" (CL=F full-day
median **2.77% on 2026 alone — reproduced here to the cent, so this is sample and not method — vs
1.15% in 2025 and 1.34% pooled against 1.46% on all days: no elevation at all**) and its "NG=F is
markedly calmer" (2.10% pooled vs 2.42%, not the 0.97% vs 2.45% the 2026 window showed). **The
finding that matters for 12-08 is structural, not statistical.** Per the Fed's own calendar,
**2026-12-08 is day one of the December 8–9 FOMC meeting — the SEP/dot-plot meeting** — and it sits
inside the **confirmed** blackout (2026-11-28 → 2026-12-10) that [`fomc-2026-12-09`](fomc-2026-12-09.md)
already carries. **Only 2 of the 20 STEO releases in two years landed on an FOMC day-1**, so the
inherited null was measured almost entirely off this corridor. Measured directly: FOMC day-1 roughly
**doubles crude** (CL=F 2.41% vs 1.46% full-day) while making the **12:00–13:00 ET hour the
quietest equity hour in the sample** (NVDA 0.14% vs 0.30%, SPY 0.08% vs 0.12%) — the pre-decision
freeze, which is exactly the hour the STEO publishes into. The one exact precedent, **2025-12-09**
(December edition, FOMC day-1, SEP meeting), reads near-silent: NVDA **0.02%**, MRVL **0.03%**, SPY
**0.06%**, CL=F **0.10%** in the release hour. **The publication risk also moves rather than
fires:** PL 119-103 §106 funds agencies through **2026-12-11**, so 12-08 publishes three days
*inside* the funded window; the exposed edition is **2027-01-12**, proposed to the calendar in this
PR. The event is **estimate**-dated with `symbols: []`, so no date-keyed action is licensed by any
of it.

## Initial research

**The question.** What will the December 2026 STEO say, does its release move anything, and does any
of it change how we sit in the tracked names (NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL AMZN)?

**One-line verdict:** the sibling ledgers' null survives a doubled sample and gets stronger — but
two of their crude/gas numbers do not, and this edition is the one they could not speak to, because
it is the only 2026 STEO that publishes into an FOMC day-1 freeze, three days before a funding cliff
it does not actually touch.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md)
(macro-print mode; the house instruments are equity/earnings-keyed and cover no commodity or
publication). Primaries fetched direct **2026-09-08**: `eia.gov/outlooks/steo/release_schedule.php`

   following the first Thursday of each month", with the footnote that *a Federal holiday on the

   the following Tuesday is **12-08**, and 12-07 is not a Federal holiday. The 2027 table on the

   **estimate** on prefix grounds only — this calendar defines no `confirmed` prefix for the EIA —

2. **2026-12-08 is FOMC day-1 of the SEP meeting, and the whole corridor follows from that —

   *"Meeting associated with a Summary of Economic Projections."* So the STEO publishes at noon on

**Limits, honestly.**

  its single biggest limitation — the same one both siblings recorded. Every content claim reads the
  **August** primary. Three more editions (09-09, 10-06, 11-10) land before this event, and the

- **The FOMC-day-1 STEO precedent is n=2**, and only one of the two (2025-12-09) is a true

- **The 2025 STEO dates come from an archived page, not a live one.** EIA's current
  `release_schedule.php` shows 2026–2027 only; the 2025 table is read from a `web.archive.org`
  snapshot of that same EIA URL dated 2025-12-01. It is EIA's own content, but it is archived, and
  every use of the n=20 sample inherits that qualification.
- **No significance test was applied to anything here**, the equity sample is ~500 trading days in a
