this session owns the promotion. (2) **What actually happens to a 10:00 ET macro print published on

both about *whether this release exists and can be read at all* — BLS's own lapse page shows this

**2. The 2025 lapse cancelled this exact slot — SUPPORTED, and upgraded from inference to a
primary.** Both sibling ledgers argued the lapse risk from the *absence* of a Sep-2025 row in the
schedule table — which is weak, because that table is a rolling window and an absent row can simply
be the window's edge. BLS publishes the answer directly. From
`bls.gov/bls/2025-lapse-revised-release-dates.htm` ("Revised news release dates following the 2025
and 2026 lapses in appropriations", last modified 2026-02-12, fetched 2026-09-05):

| Lapse | Reference period | Originally | Revised |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 | September 2025 | Tuesday, **November 4, 2025** | **"Canceled (see JOLTS note)"** |
| 2025 | October 2025 | Friday, December 2, 2025 | Tuesday, **December 9, 2025**, 10:00 AM ET |
| 2026 | December 2025 | Tuesday, February 3, 2026 | Thursday, **February 5, 2026**, 10:00 AM ET |

The combined release states the mechanism in its own words: *"Publication of the September 2025
JOLTS news release was canceled due to a lapse in appropriations. September JOLTS estimates reported
in this release include partial data that businesses self-reported electronically during the
shutdown and data collected in November following the shutdown"* (USDL-25-1555, the Dec 9 2025
release, fetched 2026-09-05). Three consequences, and the third is new:

- **The mapping is slot-for-slot.** Sep-2025 data was due **Tuesday Nov 4, 2025**; Sep-2026 data is
  due **Tuesday Nov 3, 2026**. Same reference month, same weekday, same ~5-week lag. If a
  2026-10-01 lapse runs long, this is the release that disappears, not a neighbour.
- **The data survives; the release does not.** `JTS000000000000000JOL` carries **2025-M09 = 7,169k**
  (BLS API, fetched 2026-09-05), alongside M10 7,170k and M08 6,919k — a complete series. The
  [09-29 sibling's](jolts-2026-09-29.md) shorthand that the print was "never published" is wrong in
  the way that matters: it was *cancelled as a release and folded forward*, with a stated data-quality
  caveat attached to that month.
- **The risk is graded by duration, which no sibling ledger states.** The short early-2026 lapse cost
  JOLTS **2 days**. The long 2025 lapse cost it **a release**. "Does a lapse happen" is the wrong
  question; "how long does it run" is the one that decides whether 11-03 exists.

**3. There is an exact election-day precedent, and it argues against instrumenting this bar —
SUPPORTED, measured, n=1.** The calendar entry predicted "no attributable footprint." The precedent
says something sharper and different. The September-2018-data JOLTS released **10:00 a.m. ET
Tuesday, November 6, 2018 (USDL-18-1785)** — midterm election day. The tape that session (Yahoo daily
bars, fetched 2026-09-05; percentile = |open→close| against that symbol's own trailing-60-session
distribution):

| Election day | SPY session / pctile | QQQ session / pctile | Overnight gap (SPY) | Next session (SPY) | VIX overnight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2018-11-06** (midterm, **JOLTS printed 10:00**) | **+0.659% / 80th** | +0.752% / 62nd | **+0.887%** | +1.243% | **−9.44%** |
| 2020-11-03 (presidential) | +0.701% / 62nd | +0.989% / 58th | +1.437% | +0.786% | +3.49% |
| 2022-11-08 (midterm) | +0.234% / 17th | +0.149% / 7th | −0.542% | −1.527% | −0.78% |
| 2024-11-05 (presidential) | +1.044% / 93rd | +0.943% / 75th | +2.168% | +0.312% | −21.62% |

Read honestly, in descending confidence. (a) **Election-day sessions are not reliably quiet** — the
two midterms split 80th and 17th percentile, so any claim that this print lands in a sleepy tape is
unsupported. (b) **The decisive move is overnight in every one of the four**: the gap exceeded the
day session in 2018, 2020 and 2024, and in 2022 it ran the opposite direction to it. (c) **Therefore
the release bar is contaminated, not silent** — on 2018-11-06 a genuinely soft-looking JOLTS headline
(openings reported *down* to 7.0M) coincided with a green 80th-percentile session, and no honest
procedure could separate the print from the count. That is a stronger reason to skip instrumenting
this bar than "nothing happens" would have been, and it is the opposite conclusion to the
[12-01 sibling's](jolts-2026-12-01.md) case, which rests on a *measured quiet* pairing.

**4. That same precedent is this series' sharpest revision warning — SUPPORTED.** The 2018-11-06
release headline read *"The number of job openings decreased to 7.0 million on the last business day
of September"* (USDL-18-1785). The current BLS vintage for the same month reads **7,411k, up from
August's 7,208k** (`JTS000000000000000JOL`, fetched 2026-09-05). The as-reported *decline* is a
*rise* of 203k in the final data — the **direction** flipped, not merely the magnitude. Any surprise
measured against a prior month on this series may be measured against a number that no longer
exists, and on the one election-day instance the headline itself did not survive.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `confirmed`, BLS primary, promoted this session — see leg 1).** Treat **2026-11-03
10:00 ET** as a medium-impact, second-tier labor-slack read that is **not measurable at the
release-bar scale**, because it publishes into a national election session whose only precedent
(2018-11-06, the sole prior election-day JOLTS) produced an **80th-percentile** SPY session with the
decisive move overnight. The distinction from the [12-01 sibling](jolts-2026-12-01.md) is
deliberate: that print is unreadable because its bar is *quiet*; this one is unreadable because its
bar is *contaminated*. No directional macro position; no rate-duration paper entries sized around it.
Base case for the data (`estimate`-labelled — no consensus exists, and this is trend extrapolation
from primaries, not a forecast): the low-hire/low-fire composition persists — hires and quits soft,
layoffs contained, openings roughly flat — with openings-per-unemployed near **1.0–1.05** and as
likely to cross on the unemployment denominator (printing 10-02) as on openings. Two structural
points hold and are not contingent: **(i)** the terminal meeting in this print's corridor
(`fomc-2026-12-09`) carries an SEP and dots, so this read is absorbed rather than priced raw — the
09-29 sibling's no-SEP upgrade is refuted for this instance; **(ii)** unlike 12-01, this print lands
**outside** any FOMC blackout, so official reinterpretation is possible in the 11-03 → 11-28 window.
One `estimate`-labelled conditional sits 34 days upstream: `government-funding-deadline-2026-09-30`.
BLS's own lapse page grades it — a **short** lapse costs this series ~**2 days**; a **long** one
cancelled this identical slot in 2025 and folded its data into a December release with a stated
quality caveat.

**Kill switches:**

- **A 2026-10-01 funding lapse occurs** — re-verify this date immediately and grade by *duration*
  against BLS's revised-dates page: ≲1 week implies a small slip (the Feb-2026 precedent, 2 days);
  a multi-week lapse implies cancellation and a fold-forward into the 12-01 release (the Nov-2025
  precedent, this exact slot). A CR or full-year appropriation enacted before **2026-09-30** retires
  this switch.
- **A 2026-11-03 SPY open→close move below the 40th percentile of its trailing-60-session
  distribution, with the overnight gap into 11-04 smaller than that session** — the election did not
  swamp the day, the contamination claim is dead, and this bar becomes readable after all.
  Registered as `FT-jolts-2026-11-03-1`.
- **The midterm result being known before the 11-03 close** (a decisive early call, or a result
  effectively settled beforehand) — removes the overnight-resolution structure the whole
  contamination argument rests on.
- **The 12-09 FOMC losing its projection round** — leg 5 is the whole of the "absorbed, not priced
  raw" claim; if the dots go, the 09-29 sibling's no-SEP framing becomes the right one for this print.
- **Openings-per-unemployed crossing ~1.0 in either direction** — decompose before reacting: July's
  rise to 1.05 came from the denominator, and September's denominator prints **2026-10-02**, inside
  the lapse-exposed window.
- **A published Sep-data consensus emerging** (expect late October) — resolves the largest honest
  limit; re-run the surprise framing against a number rather than trend extrapolation.
- **A bls.gov view placing this release anywhere other than 2026-11-03 at the next pulse** — the
  residual fetcher risk in leg 1; a persistent contradiction demotes the status back to `estimate`
  rather than being reasoned away.
