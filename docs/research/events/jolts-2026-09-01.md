## At a glance

**TL;DR.** July JOLTS lands **today, Tuesday 2026-09-01 at 10:00 ET** — a second-tier labor read that
is the Fed's cleanest gauge of *slack* (openings, quits, openings-per-unemployed), reporting the
**same July reference month** as the −23k jobs shock already on the tape. It shares its minute with
**ISM Manufacturing PMI** (high-impact — the louder of the two) and opens the pre-FOMC data week (→
ADP + Beige Book + AVGO 9/2 → ISM services + Challenger 9/3 → jobs 9/4 → blackout 9/5 → PPI 9/10 →
CPI 9/11 → FOMC 9/16). **What changed since D-2 is not labor — it is energy.** US strikes on Larak
Island (8/30) and Iranian retaliation on US bases in the UAE and Jordan (8/31) ended a month-long
pause; **Brent went back above $90**, the Sep-16 hike is now priced **62–66%** (CME FedWatch, 8/31)
vs 56–60% at D-2, and the 10y hit **4.76%**, its highest since Jan 2025. That *lowers* this print's
marginal weight: a hike being priced off an oil-driven inflation impulse is one a soft openings
number has little power to talk down. Consensus resolved wider and flatter than D-2 read it —
**~7.33–7.40M** across venues vs June's **7.359M** actual, i.e. roughly flat, not a re-firming. The
signal sits in the sub-components: **quits frozen at 2.0%** and **openings-per-unemployed already

**confirmed** (BLS primary, two schedule pages agree); every consensus figure is **estimate**-labeled
and widens caution only.

## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** what is July JOLTS likely to show, how does the market react to a JOLTS
surprise in each direction inside a live Fed *hike* debate, and does it change how we sit in the
tracked names (NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL AMZN) around 2026-09-01?

**One-line verdict:** a second-tier labor-slack read reporting the same July month as the -23k
payroll shock — likely to corroborate the cooling (dovish, hike-relief) rather than surprise, it
moves markets less than the jobs report but feeds the same hike-odds channel every pre-FOMC print
does; the house answer is guard-shaped, not directional (no macro playbook exists), and this print's
own muted reaction history argues against even the known-date-variance treatment CPI/FOMC get.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md)
(macro-print mode: no price instruments). Primary sources over aggregators — the BLS release
schedule (bls.gov/schedule/news_release/jolts.htm, fetched 2026-08-19) cross-checked against the
data file's `bls.gov/schedule/2026/09_sched_list.htm` citation; the July jobs figures from BLS
empsit + CNBC reaction narration, each claim dated. Reaction-function evidence is press narration,
not our own price instruments. All figures checked 2026-08-19.

**Conviction legs, tested:**

1. **The date and time are right — SUPPORTED.** The BLS JOLTS schedule
   (bls.gov/schedule/news_release/jolts.htm, fetched 2026-08-19) lists "July 2026 data:
   Sep. 01, 2026, 10:00 AM" ET — matching the calendar entry exactly, and consistent with the data
   file's `09_sched_list.htm` citation. Two BLS primary pages agree; the aggregator-staleness trap
   the CPI doc's seeding caught (a wrong CPI date) does not apply here. The BLS schedule also
   confirms the *next* JOLTS (August data) at Sep. 29 — the calendar's `jolts-2026-09-29` fast
   turnaround is real, not a typo.
2. **This is July data — a same-month cross-check on the -23k shock — SUPPORTED, and the reason it
   matters more than usual.** The July Employment Situation (BLS, released 2026-08-07) showed
   nonfarm payrolls **-23,000** vs a +80–83k Dow Jones consensus, unemployment edging to **4.1%** on
   falling participation, government jobs -53,000, and 12-month average hourly earnings slipping to
   **+3.2%** (lowest since May 2021) (CNBC, 2026-08-07). JOLTS reports the *same July reference
   month* from the demand side (openings, hires, quits) — so a soft openings/quits print corroborates
   the establishment-survey weakness, while a firm one would flag the -23k as possibly overstated
   (revision risk the household/establishment divergence already invites).
3. **The market trades weak labor as hike relief in this regime — SUPPORTED (one clean data
   point), with a non-linear caveat.** The single cleanest recent labor-print reaction: the 8/7 jobs
   miss sent yields down and equities *up* — bad news bought as hike relief — with September hike
   odds cut from **~57% to ~44% within hours** (CNBC, 2026-08-07), drifting to **~31% hike / 69%
   hold by 8/14** (CME FedWatch via press, per the [`FOMC doc`](fomc-2026-09-16.md)). The regime
   read: *weak labor is currently supportive for long-duration tech because it lowers hike odds.* The
   caveat is a tipping point — labor weak *enough* to signal recession (not just hike relief) would
   flip the sign to recession-fear-sold, and a weak-labor + hot-CPI pairing inside this corridor is a
   stagflation read (worse for our names). JOLTS is unlikely to be the print that trips that tipping
   point, but the shape governs how any single labor read is interpreted.
4. **JOLTS moves markets less than the jobs report — SUPPORTED.** Cross-indicator research
   converges that the Employment Situation draws the largest immediate trader reaction of the labor
   releases; JOLTS is a slower, slack-signal read the Fed weights over time rather than a single-print
   volatility event (consistent with the CME-research framing cited in the
   [`consumer-confidence doc`](consumer-confidence-2026-08-25.md)). The Fed's own attention is on the
   **openings-per-unemployed ratio** and the **quits rate** (a wage-pressure lead) more than the
   headline openings number — so the sub-components carry the signal, not the top line.
5. **This print opens a dense pre-FOMC data week — SUPPORTED, cross-checked against the calendar.**
   Independently confirmed in `src/domain/market-events-data.ts` (checked 2026-08-19): 9/1 carries
   **both** JOLTS and `ism-manufacturing-2026-09-01` (ISM Mfg PMI, 10:00 ET, high-impact — its
   prices-paid and new-orders reprice the cut path and 10Y real yields), then `ism-services-2026-09-03`
   (9/3), `jobs-2026-09-04` (9/4), `ppi-2026-09-10`, `cpi-2026-09-11`, and `fomc-2026-09-16`. JOLTS
   is the quiet first domino of that corridor, not the payload.
6. **Tracked-name sensitivity — ranked, with mechanism.** `symbols: []` — market-wide. All nine
   trade as long-duration assets on the rate path (the calendar's own note). Most sensitive to a
   JOLTS-driven hike-odds move: **CRWV** (debt-financed AI-infra buildout — the rate path hits both
   its discount rate and its actual cost of capital; see
   [`ai-energy-constraint.md`](../ai-energy-constraint.md)); then the high-multiple semis **NVDA /
   AVGO / MRVL** (longest-duration AI cash flows — and **AVGO reports the very next morning, 9/2**, so
   its pre-print tape will carry this read); middle **MSFT / GOOG / META** (valuation duration,
   fortress balance sheets mute the financing channel); and **AAPL / AMZN** carry a *dual* labor
   exposure the semis don't — rate-relief on a soft print (supportive) but recession-demand risk if
   the softness reads cyclical rather than benign (discretionary spend). Sympathy plumbing:
   QQQ-level macro reactions transmit to every name — the sweep
   ([`multi-symbol-sweep.md`](../multi-symbol-sweep.md)) showed peer windows are one AI-beta bet, not
   nine.

**What the conditions support.** Nothing directional — no house playbook is macro-keyed (S1/S2/E1/S3/S4
+ G1 are symbol/earnings-keyed), and this print's muted reaction history argues against even the
known-date-variance treatment CPI/FOMC receive. What travels: no new rate-duration-sensitive paper
entries sized specifically around 9/1 10:00 ET without a reason that survives both a soft and a firm
JOLTS, and a note that AVGO's 9/2 print sits one session downstream — its pre-print positioning is
governed by AVGO's own doc, not this one, but this read feeds that tape.

**Honest limits.** D-13: no street consensus for July JOLTS was findable — it firms ~1 week out, so
the base case below is trend extrapolation (the cooling regime), not a sourced figure. JOLTS is
itself a lagging, heavily-revised series (large prior-month revisions are routine), so even the print
is a noisy read of July. Reaction-function evidence is a *single* clean labor-print data point (8/7)
under a new chair — thin, and the non-linear tipping point (leg 3) is reasoned, not measured. No
price instruments run (macro-print mode carries none); every reaction characterization is press

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date confirmed, BLS primary re-checked 2026-08-19).** Treat 2026-09-01 10:00 ET as a
medium-impact, second-tier labor-slack read — real regime information feeding the Sep-16 FOMC, not a
trading event. No directional macro position; no new rate-duration paper entries sized around the
release. Base case (estimate-labeled — no consensus found, trend extrapolation only): openings and
quits soften modestly in line with the three-month labor-cooling read, corroborating the July -23k
from the demand side — a dovish, hike-relief-leaning read the market would treat as supportive but
low-drama. The asymmetric risk is a *firm* JOLTS (openings re-firming, quits rising) that partly
un-does the payroll-miss narrative and keeps the September hike live two days before the jobs report.

**Stance update (2026-08-30, D-2 — receipt: the D-2 ledger row below).** The stance's *direction* is
unchanged (no position, no rate-duration paper sized around 10:00 ET on 9/1) but its **asymmetry has
inverted**. Every prior row was written under ~26–31% hike / ~69–73% hold; Warsh's 2026-08-28 keynote
moved the Sep-16 meeting to **56–60% hike** on CME FedWatch. Two consequences, both estimate-labeled
and caution-widening only. First, the "asymmetric risk is a *firm* JOLTS" line above is now closer to
the market's own base case than to a tail — a re-firming print does not have to overcome a
hold-priced market to be believed. Second, the *soft* print has become the informative side: it is
now the hike-relief case rather than a confirmation of consensus. The doc's underlying labor-cooling
premise also got weaker, not stronger, this pass: the 8/28 CES preliminary benchmark revision marked
the 12 months through March 2026 down by only **−79,000** (vs a **+183,000** survey median) and
jobless claims printed **203k** on 8/27 — "low fire, low hire", consistent with Warsh's "labor
markets are quite stable" rather than with a cooling that a soft JOLTS would extend. Nothing here
licenses an entry; the print stays a slack read feeding the 9/16 FOMC.

**Stance update (2026-09-01, D-0 — receipt: the D-0 ledger row below).** Direction unchanged: stand
aside, no rate-duration paper sized around 10:00 ET. One amendment, and it *reduces* this print's
importance rather than raising it. The D-2 row inverted this doc's asymmetry because Warsh had made a
hike roughly the base case; over 8/30–8/31 the hike got priced further — **62–66%** — but the
marginal driver changed from labor to **energy**: US–Iran combat resumed after a month's pause (Larak
Island 8/30; Iranian strikes on US bases in the UAE and Jordan 8/31), Brent back above **$90**, WTI
~**$86**, the 10y at **4.76%**. A hike priced off an oil-driven inflation impulse is one that a soft
July openings number has little power to talk down — so the "soft JOLTS = hike relief" channel this
doc has carried since seeding is **weaker at D-0 than it was at D-2**, not stronger. Second, smaller
amendment: the D-2 row read consensus as ~7.40M off a single aggregator and called it a slight
re-firming (+41k vs June's 7.359M). A wider sample today — TradingEconomics consensus **7.40M** with
its own model at **7.3M**, Investing.com **~7.33M**, a sell-side week-ahead at **7.39M** — clusters
**~7.33–7.40M**, i.e. roughly **flat**; the re-firming framing was one reading's artifact and is
withdrawn. All figures **estimate**-labeled and caution-widening only; nothing here licenses an entry.

**Kill switches:**

- **Openings re-firm sharply / quits rate rises** — un-does the "labor is cooling" corroboration,
  keeps the September hike live, and elevates this from a confirming read to a genuine surprise worth
  a same-week reassessment ahead of the 9/4 jobs print.
- **Openings-per-unemployed falls below ~1.0** — crosses the Fed's own slack threshold into
  "loose labor market," sharpening the cut-sooner narrative and lowering hike odds materially.
- **A soft-enough JOLTS that reads recessionary, not benign** — trips the tipping point in leg 3;
  the reaction sign flips from hike-relief-bought to recession-fear-sold, and any weak CPI-corridor
  read (through 9/11) would then compound toward a stagflation interpretation.
- **A published July JOLTS consensus emerging before 9/1** — resolves this doc's biggest honest
  limit; re-run the "surprise" framing against it rather than the trend extrapolation above.
