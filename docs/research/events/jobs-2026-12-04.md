## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date confirmed, BLS primary re-checked 2026-08-19).** Treat 2026-12-04 08:30 ET as a
high-impact known-date variance event — the last major payroll before the year-end dots and the opening
leg of the jobs → FOMC+dots → CPI triple-compound week (12/4 → 12/9 → 12/10) — whose reaction sign
cannot be fixed at D-107 because it depends on a Fed regime a full quarter away. No directional macro
position; no new rate-sensitive paper entries sized around the print (or the compound week) once inside
its cadence band. Base case (estimate-labeled — no consensus, regime extrapolation): a print continuing
the cooling, bought as hike relief *if* tightening is still the debate, read as recession confirmation
*if* the debate has tilted to cuts — deferred to a much later pulse. The distinguishing feature that
holds now: the compound week means this print is inseparable from the FOMC and CPI that follow within
six days.

**Stance sharpening (2026-09-02, D-93) — the conditional got much likelier, and the sign did not
flip.** The D-107 base case was explicitly two-branched: a cooling print "bought as hike relief *if*
tightening is still the debate, read as recession confirmation *if* the debate has tilted to cuts."
Jackson Hole re-based that conditional hard toward the first branch. Chair Warsh's 2026-08-28 keynote
(federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/warsh20260828a.htm) put PCE at 3.7% y/y and 4.1% annualized over
six months, called the labor market "effectively at full employment," questioned whether financial
conditions restrain at all, and recommitted to 2% as a "firm, fixed target." September hike odds went
~35% pre-speech → 55.7% same-day → 60.4% by 8/31 (CME FedWatch via CNBC/Benzinga), against the ~31%
hike / 69% hold this ledger carried at D-107; the CNBC analyst roundup (8/31) reports the modal view as
a hike "probably won't come in September, but it will by October or December." Two consequences that
are specific to *this* print rather than generic hawkishness. **(1) Its marginal weight goes up, not
down.** If the Fed tightens in September and/or October, this becomes the last hard labor input to a
"do we go again?" December decision carrying fresh dots — a bigger job than feeding a hold. **(2) The
first kill switch moved further from tripping.** "The intervening FOMCs flip the debate to
hold-vs-cut" is now the tail, not the coin flip it was at D-107; the tightening regime surviving intact
to 12/9 is the new base case, which means the hike-relief reading of a weak print is the one to carry
forward. Everything else holds: still **estimate**-labeled (no November consensus exists, and the
Sep-16 and Oct-28 meetings are unresolved), still no directional position, still stand-aside on every
horizon but "this quarter."

**Kill switches:**

- **The intervening FOMCs (Sep/Oct) flip the debate to hold-vs-cut** — re-bases this print's reaction
  from "weak = hike relief" to "weak = recession confirmation"; re-derive at a pulse after the Oct 28
  FOMC, don't carry the pre-September framing forward.
- **A hot rebound** — re-arms tightening five days before the Dec dots; the hawkish tail, hardest on
  CRWV and the high-multiple semis, and compounded across the three-print week.
- **A recession-signal collapse** — trips the tipping point; weak labor reads growth-negative, and a
  weak Dec CPI (12/10) would compound toward stagflation within the same week.
- **A published November payroll consensus emerging closer in** — resolves the biggest honest limit;
  re-run the "surprise" framing against it. (Not expected until early December.)
