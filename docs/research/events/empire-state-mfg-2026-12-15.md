## Initial research

### The question, plainly

What should we expect from the December Empire State Manufacturing Survey on 2026-12-15 — and, the
question this ledger exists to answer rather than to inherit, **is the proposal that created this
entry right that Empire's December print is "the first Fed-funded manufacturing read after the
12-11 funding cliff"?**

**One-line verdict:** the entry deserves to exist and its headline reason is wrong. The New York
Fed publishes its collection rule on the same page the date came from — sent the 1st, *"most are
completed by the tenth,"* accepted until the 15th — which makes December's print a **pre-cliff
snapshot published four days into a possible lapse**, not the first read of one; a lapse has left
no measurable mark on this survey in four prior instances; and the only genuinely new thing the
12-15 morning offers is that Empire's prices-paid index is the **best available stand-in for a
suspended November PPI** (R² **0.388**, subsuming PPI's own persistence) and is **still not usable**
in the units anyone would act on (a 22% uncertainty cut, 10% post-2021).

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date and time are right, re-enumerated from the primary, and it still stays `estimate` —
   SUPPORTED.** The overview page's 2026 grid reads, verbatim: *"JAN FEB MAR APR — 15 report · 17
   report · 16 report · 15 report; MAY JUN JUL AUG — 15 report · 15 report · 15 report · 17 report;
   SEP OCT NOV DEC — 15 · 15 · 16 · 15,"* above *"Released at or shortly after 8:30 a.m."* The
   published rule reproduces every observation: the 15th, rolled forward off weekends — Feb 15 2026
   was a Sunday (grid: 17), Aug 15 a Saturday (grid: 17) — and **2026-12-15 is a Tuesday**, so no
   roll applies. It stays `estimate` on two grounds, neither being doubt about the date:
   `market-events-data.ts`'s confirmed-prefix taxonomy has **no member for a regional Reserve Bank's
   own survey schedule** (`FED:` covers the federalreserve.gov FOMC calendar only), and this lane
   does not self-confirm. The page documents its own release-time risk: *"Publication of the April
   2023 survey results was delayed due to technical difficulties. The report posted at 9:13 a.m. ET
   on April 17, 2023."*

2. **The collection window is a PUBLISHED RULE, not an inference — SUPPORTED, and this is the
   document's central finding.** The overview page's survey-description block states:
   *"The survey is sent on the first day of each month to the same pool of about 200 manufacturing
   executives in New York State, typically the president or CEO. About 100 responses are received.
   **Most are completed by the tenth, although surveys are accepted until the fifteenth.**"* Three
   consequences, in order of importance.

   **(a) The proposal's premise is half wrong.** December's survey goes out **2026-12-01**, its bulk
   is in by **2026-12-10** — **one day before** `cr-expiry-2026-12-11` / `government-funding-
   deadline-2026-12-11` — and the acceptance tail runs 12-11 → 12-15, i.e. is **truncated by the
   release itself**. So the December print's *content* is overwhelmingly pre-cliff, published on
   **day 4** of a lapse that would begin 00:00 Saturday 2026-12-12 (the CR ledger's own timing). It
   is not "the first Fed-funded manufacturing read after the cliff" in any sense that matters; it is
   the last one taken **before** it.

   **(b) It upgrades the September sibling's flagged limit.** That ledger's leg 8 inferred a Sep 1–8
   window from the single August note (*"collected between August 3 and August 10"*) and recorded in
   its Honest limits that *"the rule is not stated as a rule."* It is — on the same page, in a
   different block. The rule is also **wider** than the inference: the bulk window matches (Aug 1 was
   a Saturday, so Aug 3–10 is exactly "sent the 1st, most in by the 10th"), but the acceptance tail
   to the 15th means late responses are possible in every month. **This does not overturn the
   sibling's conclusion** — its September window still closes its bulk before the 09-11 CPI — but it
   narrows the claim from "cannot have seen it" to "the bulk cannot have seen it," and that
   distinction is exactly what December needs.

   **(c) A 15th-of-the-month release is the year's tightest configuration.** On eight of 2026's
   twelve releases (Jan, Apr, May, Jun, Jul, Sep, Oct, **Dec**) publication falls on the 15th, so the
   acceptance window ends the morning it prints; on Feb, Mar, Aug and Nov there are one to two days
   of slack. December is therefore structurally among the **least** able months to carry late-window
   information — the opposite of what a "first post-cliff read" would need.

3. **A lapse leaves no measurable mark on this survey — MEASURED, and it removes the rest of the
   proposal's argument.** Four of Empire's 302 months had their collection window sit **inside** a
   federal funding lapse:

   | 2025-10 | Oct 1 – Nov 12, 2025 (43 days) | **+14.8** | 84.4th | +3.3 |
   | 2025-11 | same lapse | **+5.7** | 42.9th | −2.5 |

   Signs split **2–2**; the median sits at roughly the **36th** percentile of absolute monthly change
   against a mean |Δ| of **8.62** and a median of **6.9**. **n=4 cannot prove a null** — what it can
   do is refuse to supply the effect the proposal assumed, and it does. The one large move (2025-10,

4. **Empire publishes whatever Congress does — MEASURED, and it is the one proposal claim that

5. **On the lapse branch, Empire's prices paid is the best available stand-in for the suspended

   (`medium`, **confirmed** `BLS:`) shares Empire's 8:30 slot and is exactly the class of release a
   lapse suspends: its own ledger records that in the 2025 lapse this release slipped **Oct 16 →
   Nov 25, 2025, 40 days**. So the honest question the morning poses is: *if November PPI does not
   print, how much of it does Empire's prices-paid index tell you?* Regressing PPI final-demand m/m
   on Empire's prices-paid level, all alignments, FRED data:

6. **And it is still not usable — MEASURED, and this is the refusal leg 5 exists to earn.** R² is

this survey; they can only refuse to supply the effect the proposal assumed. Two of the four
(2025-10, 2025-11) come from a single lapse and are not independent draws, and the 2019-01 episode
was a **partial** lapse in which the Department of Labor was already funded — so the three episodes
differ in what was actually shut, and no attempt is made here to control for that.

persistent trending level inherits some of that trend; leg 6 is the honest restatement in
percentage points and is the number any claim should use. Both are bivariate OLS on overlapping
monthly data with **no correction for serial correlation**, so the t-statistics are optimistic in
level; the argument uses the **ordering** of the four alignments and the **residual sd**, not the
significance stars. The post-2021 degradation (0.284pp → 0.326pp) is reported without an explanation
for it, which is itself a limit — a relationship that decays for unknown reasons may decay further.

differ.
