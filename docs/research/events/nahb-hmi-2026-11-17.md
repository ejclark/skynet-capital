[housing-starts-2026-11-18 lane](housing-starts-2026-11-18.md) filed this id on one explicit

because the HMI lands **10:00**, half an hour after the open — reads **0.9559%** against a
**0.9627%** baseline, **p=0.96**. The decisive form is paired: **71 of 78** HMI days (91.0%) are the

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This id reached the calendar as one `EST:` proposal, from
[`housing-starts-2026-11-18`](housing-starts-2026-11-18.md)'s adjacency sweep, and that proposal
named its reason for existing in one sentence:

> the residential ledgers keep concluding `symbols: []` on federal prints… and the untested
> alternative is that builder SENTIMENT, not builder DATA, is the homebuilder-keyed channel. Nobody
> here has measured it, and the NAHB series is the instrument for it.

It left a second, narrower caution:

> 2026-11-17 is already the corridor's heaviest morning (retail sales 08:30, industrial production,
> import/export prices, MTIS 10:00), so a 10:00 HMI lands buried; whether it is separable on that
> specific session is a question for its own initial research and this lane did not test it.

**Does the publisher's own calendar carry the date; is builder sentiment the homebuilder channel
that builder data is not; and does a stacked morning bury a 10:00 print?**

**One-line verdict:** the date is NAHB's own on four independent checks and promotes to `confirmed`;
the sentiment hypothesis is **REFUTED** on the strongest available design; and the burial question
is **moot**, because the split that does exist belongs to the 09:15 release rather than to the
10:00 one.

### Method

**Dates.** NAHB publishes its HMI schedule as a maintained forward calendar on one page. That page
was fetched direct (HTTP 200, 47,132 bytes, 2026-09-06) for 2026, and the **same page** was pulled
from seven Wayback snapshots — 20200927, 20211021, 20220928, 20230906, 20240909, 20250821, 20260105
— giving **84** publisher-authored dates for 2020–2026. Of the **80** already elapsed, **78** are US
trading sessions; the two exceptions are **2023-06-19** and **2024-06-19**, both Juneteenth. Census
release dates for the head-to-head come from `census.gov/construction/xls/historic_release_dates.xls`
(HTTP 200, 55,808 bytes, **514** rows 1984-02-16 → 2026-12-17), which carries New Residential
Construction (08:30) and New Residential Sales (10:00) in separate columns. G.17 release dates come
from `federalreserve.gov/feeds/g17.xml` (HTTP 200, 577,293 bytes), **413** items, **185** of them
monthly `G.17 Data for <Month> <Year> are now available` entries with `dc:date` stamps carrying the
09:15 release time.

**Prices.** ITB, XHB and SPY daily OHLC from the Nasdaq API, **2,514** sessions 2016-09-06 →
2026-09-04. Yahoo hard-429'd this runner all session, exactly as it did the
[existing-home-sales-2026-11-12 lane](existing-home-sales-2026-11-12.md), and stooq answers with a
JavaScript proof-of-work challenge; both are recorded in `probe-ref.blocked` rather than silently
substituted. The closes reproduce the sibling ledgers to the cent — **ITB 93.91 · XHB 103.25 · SPY
770.19** on 2026-09-04, VIX **14.53** from CBOE's delayed-quote endpoint.

**Metrics, pre-specified before any test was run.** The HMI publishes **10:00 ET**, thirty minutes
after the open. So the window that can contain it is the **open→close** move, `|close ÷ open − 1|`;
the **overnight gap**, `|open ÷ prior close − 1|`, is a **placebo** that the release cannot reach;
session **range** is `(high − low) ÷ low`. This is the deliberate inversion of the sibling ledgers'
design, which used the gap because New Residential Construction lands 08:30 — an hour *before* the
open. Comparisons are two-sided permutation tests on medians, 20,000 iterations, seed 20260906,
over the matched window **2020-01-02 → 2026-09-04** (1,678 sessions) where HMI dates are known.

### Leg 1 — the date is the publisher's own — **SUPPORTED**

NAHB's "2026 HMI Schedule" table reads `Nov. 2026 | November 17, 2026` under the standing note
*"Normal release time: 10:00 AM Eastern Time"*. Three corroborations, all primary:

1. NAHB's live HMI page (`/indices/housing-market-index`, HTTP 200, 61,528 bytes) carries **"HMI Key
   Findings: August 2026"** — the edition its own schedule dates 2026-08-17 — reporting the index
   **up one point to 35**, present sales **39**, six-month expectations **43**, buyer traffic **23**,
   with **35%** of builders cutting prices at an average **6%** reduction and incentives at **63%**.
   The publisher's schedule and the publisher's output agree.
2. Seven consecutive archived years of the same table show an unbroken mid-month Monday/Tuesday
   convention. This is a forward calendar the publisher maintains, not an announcement.
3. 78 of the 80 elapsed rows are trading sessions.

NAHB is the producer of this series, not an aggregator, so the promotion runs on the precedent
[`existing-home-sales-2026-11-12`](existing-home-sales-2026-11-12.md) set with NAR's own calendar
and `challenger-job-cuts-2026-09-03` before it. Two cautions are recorded rather than smoothed: the
two Juneteenth rows mean a scheduled date is NAHB's commitment and not a promise the tape is open;
and the live table's December row reads `December, 16, 2026`, stray comma included. **One
correction to the proposing lane:** it recorded the PDF twin as the 404-served-as-200 trap. As of
2026-09-06 that path **302-redirects** instead. The HTML page is the working source either way.

### Leg 2 — builder sentiment is the homebuilder-keyed channel — **REFUTED**

The unpaired test first, on all three names and all three metrics (n=78 HMI days vs n=1,600
baseline sessions):

| Metric | ITB | XHB | SPY |
|---|---|---|---|
| open→close (**the live window**) | 0.9559% vs 0.9627%, **p=0.96** | 0.9031% vs 0.8695%, p=0.80 | 0.4494% vs 0.4682%, p=0.79 |
| overnight gap (**the placebo**) | 0.5011% vs 0.5034%, **p=0.97** | 0.5037% vs 0.5017%, p=0.96 | 0.4184% vs 0.3283%, p=0.065 |
| session range | 2.2536% vs 2.0913%, p=0.21 | 1.9880% vs 1.9062%, p=0.50 | 0.9417% vs 1.0364%, p=0.30 |

The homebuilder placebo coming back at p=0.96/0.97 is the check that the instrument is pointed the
right way: a 10:00 release leaves no fingerprint before 09:30, and it does not.

Then the paired test, which is the strong form of the proposal's own question. Because **71 of 78**
HMI days are the session immediately before New Residential Construction (**91.0%**; zero same-day
overlaps, zero D-2), the two hypotheses can be compared on **consecutive sessions, same names, same
regime** — sentiment on day one, data on day two:

| Pair | Sentiment day smaller | Medians (sentiment / data) | Sign test |
|---|---|---|---|
| ITB open→close | **35 / 71 = 49.3%** | 0.9604% / 0.9866% | **p=1.00** |
| XHB open→close | 38 / 71 = 53.5% | 0.9257% / 0.9758% | p=0.64 |
| ITB range | 35 / 71 = 49.3% | 2.2715% / 2.0713% | — |

**49.3%** is a coin flip to two decimal places. Sentiment does not beat data, data does not beat
sentiment, and neither beats an ordinary session. The proposal's alternative is not merely
unsupported — it is refuted by the cleanest design this calendar can construct.

### Leg 3 — the New Residential Sales control, and the reproduction — **SUPPORTED**

Two checks that the null is the tape's and not the method's. **New Residential Sales publishes
10:00**, the same clock as the HMI, and it is *data*: n=77 in-window, ITB open→close **0.9481% vs
0.9633%** (p=0.91), gap 0.4878% vs 0.5049% (p=0.81), range 2.0235% vs 2.1077% (p=0.58). A 10:00 data
print is as inert as a 10:00 sentiment print. And the Census 08:30 print reproduces the sibling
ledgers on an **independent data source**: ITB's release-day overnight gap in this window is
**0.5835% vs 0.4998% (n=80, p=0.24)** against the
[11-18 lane's](housing-starts-2026-11-18.md) 2021-26 cell of 0.575%/0.487% (p=0.21), and this
session's release-day **p90 of 1.5837%** lands within four thousandths of the **1.579%** threshold
that lane registered from a Yahoo pull. Two vendors, one answer.

### Leg 4 — a stacked morning buries a 10:00 print — **MOOT, and the split belongs elsewhere**

The one marginal cell in the unpaired table is SPY's overnight gap (p=0.065) — a *market-wide*
number, in a window the HMI cannot reach. Splitting the 78 by whether the Fed's **09:15 G.17**
release shares the morning resolves it completely:

| Class | n | SPY overnight gap | vs clean baseline (0.3275%, n=1,495) |
|---|---|---|---|
| HMI **with** G.17 | 34 | **0.4828%** | **p=0.0385** |
| HMI **without** G.17 | 44 | **0.3391%** | **p=0.85** |
| G.17 days generally | 74 | 0.4444% | **p=0.0219** |

An unstacked HMI morning is *exactly* baseline — 0.3391% against 0.3275%, and 50.0% of those days
clear the baseline median against 50.0% for the baseline itself. A stacked one clears it **70.6%**
of the time. The lift is the 09:15 print's, and it exists whether or not an HMI is scheduled.

That reframes the proposal's caution honestly. It asked whether the HMI is *separable* on a crowded
session. It is not separable — but not because it is drowned out: **HMI+G.17 vs HMI-only makes no
difference to the HMI's own window** (ITB open→close 0.8503% vs 0.9894%, p=0.50; ITB range 2.1769%
vs 2.3290%, p=0.51; XHB open→close 0.8453% vs 0.9437%, p=0.45). Nothing is being buried. 2026-11-17
is in the stacked class, and what that buys is a wider *pre-open*, owned by retail sales,
import/export prices and industrial production.

### Honest limits

- **Multiple comparisons, stated up front.** 27 permutation tests were run in the primary pass.
  Exactly **one** crossed p<0.05: XHB's open→close on New Residential Construction days, 1.1222% vs
  0.8628%, **p=0.047**. Expectation under the null is ~1.35. ITB does not corroborate it (p=0.82)
  and the same print's *gap* — the metric its 08:30 timing predicts — is null (ITB p=0.20, XHB
  p=0.99). It is reported here as noise, not as a finding, and nothing in the stance rests on it.
- **The window is 2020–2026, not a decade.** NAHB's schedule page is archived from 2020 forward and
  no earlier URL exists in the Wayback index; the Eye on Housing blog dates posts only to the month.
  So 78 releases, spanning the COVID boom, the 2022 rate shock and the 2023–26 malaise — a full
  cycle, but a third of the 515-date history the Census ledgers work from.
- **Magnitude only, never direction.** Every metric here is absolute. Whether the HMI's *surprise*
  moves builders directionally is untested, because no machine-readable history of HMI prints and
  consensus was reachable from this runner. That is the honest residual of the sentiment
  hypothesis, and it is not registered as a forward test because it cannot be scored from the data
  in hand.
- **The G.17 split was chosen after seeing the marginal headline**, which is why the `this month`
  call sits at medium and registers a falsifier rather than claiming the result.
- **Blocked sources, recorded not substituted.** `bls.gov` returned **403** (the known lane blind
  spot — CPI dates were wanted as a second stacking axis and could not be had), Yahoo **429**,
  stooq a JS challenge, and `federalreserve.gov/releases/g17/g17_release_dates.htm` **404**, so the
  **October 2026** G.17 date is unverified and the 10-19 proposal says so.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stand aside on every horizon, at `confirmed`.** This is a `low`-impact, `symbols: []` macro print
whose one distinguishing hypothesis has been tested and refuted on its own dates. It stays on the
calendar for three reasons, none of them a trade: it is the **D-1 read** of the starts print 91% of
the time; it is the corridor's only **privately produced** residential release, so it carries no
funding-cliff exposure; and its date series now provides the **unstacked control** the Census prints
cannot.

**What kills this stance:**

- **The tape** — ITB's 2026-11-17 open→close exceeding **2.5734%** (the 78-day HMI-day p90) while
  2026-11-18 stays under it. That is the sentiment channel appearing where 78 prior observations say
  it is not, on the day rather than on the corridor. Registered as `-1`.
- **The attribution** — SPY's 2026-11-17 overnight gap at or below **0.3275%**. The stacked-morning
  finding predicts a wider pre-open; a baseline one on the corridor's heaviest morning of the
  quarter says the 34-day split was noise. Registered as `-2`.
- **The structure** — the November edition not publishing 2026-11-17 at 10:00, or New Residential
  Construction not publishing 2026-11-18. The D-1 pairing is the only durable thing here and a break
  costs the `confirmed` promotion its footing. Registered as `-3`.
- **The playbook** — a macro- or housing-keyed entry landing in `docs/plans/trade-playbooks.md`
  before 2026-11-17, which would give a `symbols: []` print something to attach to for the first
  time.

Estimates only widen caution; `confirmed` licenses nothing on its own. The predictions above are
registered in [`forward-tests/nahb-hmi-2026-11-17.md`](../forward-tests/nahb-hmi-2026-11-17.md).
