## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Two sibling lanes proposed this event on the same day, from opposite directions. The
`m3-full-report-2026-11-03` sweep proposed it on the inverse of its own finding — construction spending
is a GDPNow **trigger** and M3-2 is not, so "the corridor tracks the opportunistic half of a GDPNow
morning and misses the causal half." The `wholesale-trade-2026-11-09` sweep proposed it on position —
2026-11-02 is Q4 posting #2 and 11-09 is #4, so "this release is one of the three vintages that set the
base the proposing print is then measured against." Meanwhile a third lane researched the December twin
in full and left one observation explicitly unexplained: all ten December construction vintages moved
structures by ≥0.059pp, and its test of the obvious mechanism came back p=0.869.

**Is this edition the same event as its December twin, and if not, what actually differs?**

**One-line verdict:** it is not — the two proposals were both circling the right variable and neither
named it, because the thing that sets a construction vintage's nowcast footprint is **where the vintage
sits inside the nowcast quarter**, and on that axis 2026-11-02 is the weakest slot the release ever
occupies while 2026-12-01 is close to the strongest; nothing in that changes the stand-aside, because
the footprint still stops short of the headline and still fires on a number Census declines to call
different from zero.

### Leg 4 — what actually separates this edition from its twin · **SUPPORTED at p=0.0312**, and it is the new finding

The December twin banked an anomaly it could not explain: all ten December-dated construction vintages
moved structures ≥0.059pp, and its test of the natural mechanism — is a December edition the first hard
construction datapoint of the forecast quarter? — returned **p=0.869**. The mirror image shows up
immediately from this event's side:

| Release month | n | median \|Δ Structures\| | ≥0.02pp | ≥0.059pp |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **November editions** | **11** | **0.0290pp** | **63.6% (7 of 11)** | **27.3%** |
| December editions | 10 | 0.0903pp | 100% | 100% |
| All other construction vintages | 113 | 0.0670pp | 83.2% | 54.0% |

November vs December is **p=0.0042**; November vs every other construction vintage is p=0.0991.

The month is a proxy. Re-cut on the vintage's **posting position inside the nowcast quarter** — the
index of its same-quarter delta, so position 1 is the first ordinary vintage after that quarter's
initial nowcast — the gradient is continuous and the two anomalies are its two ends:

| Posting position in the nowcast quarter | n | median \|Δ Struct\| | median \|Δ GDP\| | ≥0.02pp | ≥0.05pp |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1–2** ← **2026-11-02 sits here** | **36** | **0.0381pp** | 0.3526pp | **75.0%** | 47.2% |
| 3–10 | 4 | 0.0483pp | 0.1183pp | 100% | 50.0% |
| **11–15** ← **2026-12-01 sits here** | **41** | **0.0800pp** | 0.4522pp | 87.8% | 70.7% |
| 16+ | 43 | 0.0453pp | 0.2876pp | 79.1% | 48.8% |

**Postings 1–2 vs postings 11–15: structures 0.0381pp vs 0.0800pp, p=0.0312; residential 0.0401pp vs
0.0843pp, p=0.0136; GDP 0.3526pp vs 0.4522pp, p=0.4830.** The calendar is what makes this a clean split
rather than a coincidence: **10 of the 11 November editions sit at posting 1** (the eleventh, 2025-11-17,
is the post-lapse catch-up at posting 30), and the December editions sit at **11–15** without exception.
The months in the 11–15 band are exactly `{03, 06, 09, 12}` — the quarter-ending months — and the months
at 1–2 are `{02, 03, 05, 08, 11}`.

The mechanism this suggests, stated as a suggestion rather than a result: the quarter's initial nowcast
is a full re-estimate published days earlier off the BEA advance GDP release, so the first construction
print after it is diffing against the freshest anchor the model ever has, while a mid-quarter print
diffs against a path that has drifted for six weeks. For 2026:Q4 that opening vintage is dated
**2026-10-29** and this print is three sessions later.

**Two candidate mechanisms were tested and both failed, which is why the This-month call is Medium.**

- *Is it the reference month sitting in the prior quarter?* November prints September data — a Q3 month
  read into a Q4 nowcast — while December prints October, the quarter's first month. Split all 124
  construction vintages that way: base-setting (n=37) 0.0404pp vs in-quarter (n=87) 0.0670pp, p=0.1655.
  But **remove November from the base-setting set and the difference vanishes entirely**: 0.0668pp
  (n=27) vs 0.0670pp (n=87), **p=1.0000**. The base-setting story is the November subset wearing a
  different name.
- *Is September simply a quiet month for construction?* On FRED `TTLCONS` since 2015, September's median
  \|m/m\| is 0.488% against 0.710% for other months — **p=0.5059**; back to 2002 it is 0.736% vs 0.856%,
  **p=0.4277**. No.

So the position gradient survives its two rivals and neither of the refuted stories explains it away.
It is still a post-hoc slice found by asking what distinguishes this edition from its sibling, and
p=0.0312 is not adjusted for the cuts that preceded it.

### Honest limits

- **The position gradient is a post-hoc slice, and its p-value is unadjusted.** It was found by asking
  what distinguishes this edition from its December twin, after the month-of-release cut had already
  been looked at. p=0.0312 on structures and p=0.0136 on residential are honest permutation results on
  n=36 vs n=41, but they are not corrected for the cuts that preceded them, and the December lane's own
  first-of-quarter test (p=0.869) is a standing reminder that the obvious version of this story is
  false.
- **The mechanism is a suggestion, not a measurement.** "The initial nowcast re-anchors the model and
  the first print after it has less to move" is a plausible reading of the gradient and nothing in this
  ledger tests it. Two rival explanations were tested and refuted; that is not the same as the third
  being right.
- **The isolated class is n=9 and the GDP null is a failure to reject on it.** "The print does not move
  the headline nowcast" is p=0.77 against a baseline, not a demonstration of zero — inherited from the
  December twin, independently replicated here, and still the weakest measurement in the family.
- **The classifier is free text.** `ContribArchives`'s `Data releases` column is comma-tokenised and
  case-normalised, not otherwise cleaned; the December twin's tokenisation counted 109 CS+ISM vintages
  where this session counts 96. A vintage whose text silently omits the release would be misfiled into
  the baseline, biasing every test **toward** finding no difference.
- **Pre-2020 reference months are derived.** The Census calendars this session could fetch begin at
  2020; for the 51 of 124 construction vintages dated before that, the reference month is derived as
  release month minus two — the rule that holds in **79 of 84** observed rows, with every exception a
  post-lapse catch-up. Nothing in Leg 4's position analysis depends on the reference month (posting
  position is computed from vintage dates alone), but the base-setting rival test does.
- **All the nowcast work measures a model, not a market.** `ContribArchives` ends 2026-07-28 and
  carries no Q4-2026 vintages, so every class prior here is out-of-sample for the quarter being
  nowcast.
- **The election-eve read is n=5 and is not a market claim.** Four of the five sessions sit in
  materially different vol regimes from today's; the VIX observation is a base rate on five prior
  election eves, and the one prior instance of this exact date-shape (2020-11-02) is n=1.
