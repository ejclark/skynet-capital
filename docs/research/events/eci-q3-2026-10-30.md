## At a glance

**TL;DR.** This is the quarterly wage number the FOMC cites by name, and it lands **08:30 ET Friday
2026-10-30, 42.5 hours after the 10-28 statement** — so like [`pce-2026-10-29`](pce-2026-10-29.md) it
grades the October decision rather than informing it, and its first real audience is **12-09**. Two
findings make it worth a doc anyway. **First, the wage picture has split three ways and only the
weakest measure is cooling.** Average hourly earnings printed **3.1% y/y** on 09-04 — softest since
mid-2021 — and AHE is the one wage series that is *not* composition-adjusted. The two that are both
went the other way: ECI's seasonally-adjusted quarterly pace re-accelerated **0.7% → 0.9%** and has
now held 0.9% two quarters running, and the Atlanta Fed Wage Growth Tracker rose **3.6% → 3.8%** in
July (job-switchers **4.1% → 4.4%**). This print is the fixed-weight adjudication between them.
**Second, the arithmetic is unusually tight and it is computed here, not guessed.** From the BLS
index itself (civilian total comp, NSA: 2025Q3 **172.905** → 2026Q2 **177.244**), the 3.4%-vs-3.5%
line falls at a Q2→Q3 index rise of **+0.918%**. A repeat of the current **0.9% seasonally-adjusted**
pace maps to roughly **+0.93% to +1.08%** once the nine-year Q2→Q3 seasonal wedge is applied —
i.e. **3.5%, the first ECI acceleration since Q4 2022**. A reversion to **0.8%** prints 3.4% and the
flatline holds. The whole release reduces to that one tenth. **And the counterweight is decisive for
what it means:** wages only reach prices net of productivity, and on the **2026-09-03** revised
Productivity & Costs release **unit labor costs ran +1.4% y/y — below the Fed's 2% target — against
productivity +2.2% y/y and a labor share of 52.8%, the lowest since the series began in 1947.** So a
hot ECI is currently a *margin* story, not a price story. The release that settles whether it stays
that way is **Productivity & Costs Q3 (P) on 2026-11-05**, proposed to the calendar in this PR. Date
**confirmed** on two independent BLS primaries. Nothing here is a trade — `symbols: []`, no house
playbook is macro-keyed, and the standing order for this window is [`fomc-2026-10-28`](fomc-2026-10-28.md)'s:
**be flat into 10-27 → 10-30**.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

What should a paper book holding NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL AMZN expect from the Q3
2026 Employment Cost Index on 2026-10-30 — the broadest and most composition-clean US wage measure,
landing at the close of the densest four-session window on this calendar, two days after an FOMC that
will already have decided?

**One-line verdict.** The print itself is structurally incapable of moving the decision next to it,
so the value of this doc is the **measurement fork**: three wage gauges have split, the only one
falling is the only one that is composition-contaminated, and the index arithmetic reduces this
release to a single tenth of a percent — while the productivity data says that tenth is a margin
story rather than an inflation story until proven otherwise.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date and slot are right — SUPPORTED, two independent BLS primaries.**
   `bls.gov/schedule/news_release/eci.htm` fetched today lists **"Third Quarter 2026 | Oct. 30, 2026
   | 08:30 AM"**, and the Q2 2026 news release itself states independently that the September 2026
   ECI **"is scheduled to be published on Friday, October 30, 2026, at 8:30 a.m. (ET)."** Two
   primaries, one agency, agreeing. Status stays **confirmed** with the `BLS:` prefix; the source
   string is re-dated to today's check.

2. **The Fed decides *before* this print, and there is an exact n=1 precedent for the ordering —
   SUPPORTED.** The `fomc-2026-10-28` statement drops **14:00 ET on 10-28**; this releases **08:30 ET
   on 10-30**, 42.5 hours later. That is not a novel configuration: **Q2 2026 ECI released 07-31, two
   days after the 07-29 FOMC** — the identical gap, under this chair. So the transmission channel is
   the weak one `pce-2026-10-29` already named: not "does this change the October outcome" but "does
   this make the October outcome look early or late." Its first live audience is **12-09**.

3. **Three wage measures have split, and the only one cooling is the only one that is
   composition-contaminated — SUPPORTED, and this is the load-bearing leg.** As of today:

   | Measure | Latest | Direction | Composition-adjusted? |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Average hourly earnings (monthly) | **3.1% y/y** (Aug data, 09-04 print) | falling — softest since mid-2021 | **No** — a shift in the mix of jobs moves it mechanically |
   | ECI, civilian total comp (quarterly) | **3.4% y/y**; SA quarterly **0.9%**, two quarters running, up from 0.7% | **flat y/y, accelerating quarterly** | **Yes** — fixed occupation/industry weights, by construction |
   | Atlanta Fed Wage Growth Tracker (monthly) | **3.8%** in July, from **3.6%** in June; job-stayers 3.4 → **3.6**, switchers 4.1 → **4.4** (updated 2026-08-13) | **rising** | **Yes** — individuals matched to themselves 12 months apart |

   The 09-04 tape read the soft AHE as wage disinflation
   ([`jobs-2026-09-04`](jobs-2026-09-04.md)). Both composition-clean measures disagree. That does not
   prove AHE wrong — a genuine slowdown would eventually show in all three — but it does mean the
   **one** measure carrying the cooling narrative is the one whose known failure mode is exactly this
   pattern, and this release is the quarterly arbiter.

4. **The base-effect arithmetic is neutral, and the release reduces to a single tenth — SUPPORTED,
   computed from the published index.** Civilian total compensation, NSA index
   (`CIU1010000000000I`): 2025Q2 **171.444** → 2025Q3 **172.905** → 2025Q4 **173.642** → 2026Q1
   **175.743** → 2026Q2 **177.244**. The Q2-2026 y/y checks out at 177.244/171.444 − 1 = **3.383%**,
   i.e. the published 3.4%. Against the 2025Q3 base of **172.905**, the printed y/y thresholds are:

   | Prints | Requires Q3-2026 NSA index | Requires a Q2→Q3 NSA rise of |
   |---|---|---|
   | 3.3% | 178.526 – 178.699 | +0.72% to +0.82% |
   | **3.4%** | 178.699 – 178.872 | **+0.82% to +0.92%** |
   | **3.5%** | 178.872 – 179.045 | **+0.92% to +1.02%** |

   Nine years of realized Q2→Q3 NSA moves, with the seasonally-adjusted figure BLS published for the
   same quarter and the implied wedge:

   | Year | NSA Q2→Q3 | SA Q3 | Wedge |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 2017 | +0.771% | 0.7 | +0.071 |
   | 2018 | +0.825% | 0.8 | +0.025 |
   | 2019 | +0.877% | 0.7 | +0.177 |
   | 2020 | +0.569% | 0.5 | +0.069 |
   | 2021 | +1.382% | 1.2 | +0.182 |
   | 2022 | +1.315% | 1.2 | +0.115 |
   | 2023 | +1.132% | 1.1 | +0.032 |
   | 2024 | +0.906% | 0.8 | +0.106 |
   | 2025 | +0.852% | 0.8 | +0.052 |

   Two readings, and they conflict, which is the honest finding rather than an embarrassment. **The
   naive one:** the last two realized Q2→Q3 moves (+0.906%, +0.852%) both sit inside the 3.4% band,
   so a seasonal repeat prints **3.4%** and the flatline holds a fourth quarter. **The one that
   accounts for the pace change:** in both of those years the underlying SA quarterly was **0.8**;
   in 2026 it has been **0.9** twice running. Applying the nine observed wedges to an SA 0.9 gives
   **+0.925% to +1.082%**, central ~**+0.99%** — all of which land in the **3.5%** band. So the
   question is not the base, which is neutral; it is whether the SA quarterly holds 0.9 or reverts
   to 0.8. **One tenth decides the headline.** Private wages & salaries run the same way off a
   **175.109** base: a 0.9 SA repeat prints **3.2%**, a 0.8 prints **3.1%** unchanged.

5. **A 3.5% would be a genuine regime marker, not a rounding curiosity — SUPPORTED, full series
   pulled.** The civilian ECI 12-month rate has not risen once since **2022Q4**: 5.1 → 4.8 → 4.5 →
   4.3 → 4.2 → 4.2 → 4.1 → 3.9 → 3.8 → 3.6 → 3.6 → 3.5 → 3.4 → 3.4 → **3.4**. That is **14
   consecutive quarters flat or lower.** Whatever the number is, it should be reported against that
   run — "first acceleration in three and a half years" is the frame, and it is the frame a hawkish
   committee would use.

6. **Wages are not currently reaching prices, and this is the leg that decides what a hot print
   *means* — SUPPORTED, BLS primary, two days old.** The revised Productivity & Costs release of
   **2026-09-03** puts nonfarm business **unit labor costs at +1.2% SAAR in Q2 and +1.4% over the
   last four quarters** — *below* the Fed's 2% objective — because **productivity rose +1.4% SAAR
   and +2.2% y/y**, absorbing hourly compensation of +3.7% y/y. The **labor share was 52.8%, "the
   lowest level in the series, which begins in the first quarter of 1947."** Read plainly: employers
   are paying 3.4% wage growth and passing none of it through, because output per hour is rising
   faster than it has in most of the post-2010 record and the residual is landing in margins. So
   the correct instruction attached to any hot ECI is conditional: **it is a price signal only if
   productivity rolls over.** That is not knowable on 10-30. It is knowable on **11-05**, when
   Productivity & Costs Q3 (P) publishes (`bls.gov/schedule/news_release/prod2.htm`, fetched today
   — "Third Quarter 2026 (P) | Nov. 05, 2026 | 08:30 AM"; revised 12-08). Proposed to the calendar

### What the conditions support

**A reading rule, a conditional, and a refusal — no position.** The reading rule is leg 3's: from
today until the three wage measures reconverge, **no statement in this repo should say "wages are
cooling" on the strength of AHE alone.** AHE is composition-sensitive, and the two composition-clean
measures are both rising; the correct phrasing is *"AHE is cooling, ECI's quarterly pace and the
Atlanta Fed tracker are not, and the fixed-weight arbiter prints 10-30."* The conditional is leg 6's
and it is what stops this doc from being one more hawkish wage note: **a hot ECI is a margin story
until productivity says otherwise.** Unit labor costs at +1.4% y/y are below target while the labor
share sits at a 1947-series low — the wage is being absorbed, not passed through — so the release
that decides whether 3.5% matters is **2026-11-05**, not 10-30. Anyone reading a 3.5% headline as an
inflation signal without checking the productivity print six days later is reading half the data.
The refusal is inherited and unchanged: **no position is opened, closed or sized off this event**,
and the standing order for 10-27 → 10-30 is [`fomc-2026-10-28`](fomc-2026-10-28.md)'s **flat into
it**, where the risk is five mega-cap prints, not a macro release.

### Honest limits

**The load-bearing arithmetic in leg 4 rests on a seasonal wedge estimated from nine observations,
and it is this doc's own computation rather than a published methodology.** BLS re-estimates its
seasonal factors annually and can revise the SA series; the wedge range (+0.025 to +0.182) is wide
enough that its own tails matter. Worse, the "SA 0.9%" input is a **rounded** figure — a true 0.85
and a true 0.95 both print as 0.9, and that slop alone spans most of one printed tenth in the y/y.
So "0.9 repeats → 3.5%" is a central case with real mass on 3.4%, not a mechanical result, and the
forward test is sized accordingly at Medium. **Leg 7's 40.6% response rate is aggregator fidelity** —
the BLS response-rates page states the impairment but carries the number only inside an interactive
chart that the fetch could not read; the rate should be re-verified before it is repeated as fact,
though nothing in the stance turns on its exact value. **Leg 9 is the weakest leg in the doc**: one
observation, a consensus figure from a crypto-news flash, and an *absence* of reaction evidence
rather than a measurement of one — it is recorded because omitting a checked-and-empty search would
be the dishonest option, not because it supports anything. **No Q3-2026 consensus, whisper or nowcast
exists at D-55**; that was checked, not assumed, and none is structurally due for weeks — any number
appearing before roughly mid-October should be treated as a stale-content trap of the kind the CPI
and PCE seeding passes both caught. The Atlanta Fed WGT figures are July data published 2026-08-13,
so leg 3's third row is **two months stale relative to AHE's August reading** — the August WGT update
(~09-10) is the first thing that can break the split, and it is the "this month" falsifier for
exactly that reason. **AHE's 3.1% is carried from [`jobs-2026-09-04`](jobs-2026-09-04.md), not
re-derived here.** Finally, a labelling judgement worth naming: the productivity entry proposed in
this PR is filed **`confirmed`/`BLS:`** rather than `estimate`, against this lane's default
adjacency rule, because the date is primary-fetched from the BLS schedule and the prefix taxonomy in
`market-events-data.ts` provides no `estimate` slot for a BLS-primary date — the identical constraint
the `fomc-2026-10-28` lane recorded when it proposed *this* event. The rule's intent (never
self-confirm an unverified date) is satisfied; its letter is not, and that is flagged rather than
silently resolved.
