## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** the September FOMC is the first two-sided rate decision of the cycle —
hold or *hike* — under a new chair who distrusts his own dot plot. What is priced, what would
surprise in each direction, how has the market actually reacted to this Fed's last few moves, and
which of our names carry the exposure?

**One-line verdict:** a hawkish hold is the priced base case (~60–70%) but a ~30–40% hike is live
and a cut is priced at zero — the SEP/dot plot is the real payload, the reaction runs through the
hike-odds channel in both directions, and for us this is a no-new-risk statement window, not a
trade.

**Method:** sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md) —
primary sources over aggregators (the Fed's own calendar fetched directly today; BLS/BEA schedules
for adjacent prints), press-reported CME FedWatch odds cited with their as-of dates, and the last
three decisions' actual tape as the reaction-function evidence. No price instruments run — this is
a macro print, not a symbol event. All statements below carry the event's **confirmed** date label;
odds and levels are as-checked 2026-08-17 and drift daily.

**Regime context (what makes this meeting different).** Kevin Warsh took the chair 2026-05-22.
The target range has sat at 3.50–3.75% all year. June 16–17 (his first meeting, with SEP): a
unanimous hold, but the dot plot flipped hawkish — the 2026 median moved up from March's
implied-cut path, several participants penciled at least one *hike* by year-end, only one still
projected a cut — and Warsh declined to submit his own dot, on record as a skeptic of the SEP
framework; the statement itself was cut down and stripped of forward guidance (federalreserve.gov
statement 2026-06-17; CNBC, Yahoo Finance 2026-06-17). July 28–29: a 9–3 hold with all three
dissents wanting a **hike** — statement cites solid activity, strong productivity and capital
investment, and Middle East–conflict uncertainty (federalreserve.gov statement 2026-07-29). The
inflation backdrop is oil (tanker attacks, failed ceasefire; Brent +6.0%/wk into 8/15 per the
sibling NVDA assessment) plus slow-but-steady tariff pass-through, against a labor market that
just cracked.

## Stance & kill switches

cut is still 0%. The receipt is the D-17 row below. What did **not** change is the conclusion: for
the book this is still a **no-new-risk day**, not a trade — nothing new opens into the 14:00 ET
statement, defined-risk only across the window, no directional macro bet in either direction. If
anything a ~57/43 branch is *less* tradeable than the ~65/35 it replaced, because there is now no
favored side to lean against. The September dot plot remains the swing payload on top of the rate
decision. Estimates of the odds only widen caution, never license an entry.

**Held, with the odds re-marked (2026-09-02, D-14).** The flip above stands and the conclusion is
unchanged; two updates carry the D-14 row as their receipt. First, CME FedWatch extended to **~66%
hike** (8/31) while Kalshi and the other prediction venues stayed hold-favored at **~41–48% hike** —
a ~20–25 point cross-venue gap, the widest of this event's life and an inversion of D-20's (when
both venues favored hold). Read the base case as hike-modal *on CME*, not as a settled market view;
the dispersion is itself why the stand-aside firms rather than softens. Second, the D-17
cheap-convexity observation is decaying: VIX **16.34** against the 14.43 low, with 10Y at ~4.80% and
Brent ~$91–95 after US/Iran strikes resumed on 9/1. The posture — no new risk into 14:00 ET,
defined-risk only across the window — is unchanged; it is simply a little less cheap to express than
it was three days ago.

**Kill switches:**

- **Base-case kill (hold → hike): FIRED IN SUBSTANCE, 2026-08-30 — partially, and not by its
  literal terms.** The keynote did *not* pre-commit (Warsh: "I stand here today committed to a
  discipline, not to a decision", and he explicitly rejected both forward guidance and a stated
  reaction function), and hike odds at ~56–57% are below the ~60% threshold *and* both the 9/4 jobs
  and 9/11 CPI prints are still ahead. But hike is now the modal branch on the tape, which is what
  the switch was written to catch — so the written base case is flipped above rather than defended
  on a technicality. The **reciprocal** now becomes the live threshold: hike odds falling back below
  ~40% through the 9/4 and 9/11 prints restores a hold-favored base case and flips it back.
- **Two-sided-pricing kill (the interesting one):** cut odds moving off 0% — e.g. a second
  consecutive jobs collapse on 9/4 — would mean the market has repriced the *question itself*
  (from hold-vs-hike to hold-vs-cut), invalidating every reaction-function read above; reassess
  from scratch, don't patch.
- **Reaction-function kill: DID NOT FIRE — resolved 2026-08-30.** The likely venue has now passed:
  Warsh's Jackson Hole text announces no SEP or dot-plot format change (the SEP goes unmentioned in
  the body; a footnote only restates the known concern about the dots' effect on market
  expectations), and the Fed's calendar still carries Sep 15–16 as an SEP meeting. Leg 4's
  dots→tape mapping survives into this meeting. The switch stays armed for a change announced at
  the meeting itself.
