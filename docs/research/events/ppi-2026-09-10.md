## Initial research

**The question.** What is the consensus for the August 2026 PPI, what does the July print and recent
trend say, how has the market reacted to this release in each direction during 2026, and — since it
lands the morning before CPI in the pre-FOMC window — how should we read the 9/10→9/11 pair and
which tracked names (NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL AMZN) carry the most sensitivity?

**One-line verdict:** a medium-impact wholesale-inflation read whose real function is as the
morning-before-CPI tell (and a PCE-component input) in the pre-FOMC window — the July print was a
soft headline the market bought even as the 4.7% annual rate stayed elevated; no firm August street
consensus exists at D-22; the market trades PPI through the same yield channel as CPI but with less
force, and the house answer is guard-shaped, not directional — the play is known-date variance
discipline across the 9/10→9/11 whipsaw pair, not a bet on PPI alone.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md)
(macro-print mode: no price instruments). Primary source is bls.gov (the PPI release schedule and the
July news release); press (CNBC, qz.com, Advisor Perspectives/dshort, NBC News, TradingKey, CME
Group) used only for reaction narration and component detail, each claim dated. Date re-verified
against the BLS PPI schedule primary, fetched today, 2026-08-19.

**Conviction legs, tested:**

1. **The date and time are right — SUPPORTED.** bls.gov/schedule/news_release/ppi.htm (fetched
   2026-08-19) lists "August 2026 | Sep. 10, 2026 | 08:30 AM" ET, matching the checked-in calendar
   entry exactly. The re-verification matters: the 2026-08-15 calendar seeding caught an aggregator
   carrying a wrong CPI date, so the BLS primary is re-checked at each assessment.

2. **PPI lands the morning *before* CPI this cycle — SUPPORTED, and it is the load-bearing framing.**
   For the August data cycle the BLS schedule puts **PPI on 9/10 and CPI on 9/11** — PPI first
   (bls.gov, fetched 2026-08-19). That ordering is the calendar's stated "whipsaw setup": PPI is the
   wholesale tell the desk reads into CPI-day positioning, and a soft PPI that gets bought can be
   reversed by a hot CPI the next morning (or vice versa). Note the ordering *flipped* versus the
   July cycle, where CPI printed 8/12 *then* PPI 8/13 — so the "morning-before-CPI" dynamic is
   specific to this September pair, not a fixed monthly sequence.

3. **The July print was a soft headline with mixed internals — SUPPORTED/MIXED.** July PPI (released
   2026-08-13) came in **flat, 0.0% m/m** for final demand, below a +0.2% estimate — its lowest level
   in four months (bls.gov / CNBC / dshort, 2026-08-13). Core (ex food & energy) rose **0.2%**, below
   a 0.3% estimate. But the internals were not uniformly soft: final-demand goods fell 0.7% (energy
   −3.1%, food −0.9%) suppressing the headline, while **core-ex-trade-services rose 0.4%** and the
   **12-month unadjusted rate held 4.7%** — some underlying measures were flagged as re-heating
   toward multi-year highs (TradingEconomics, 2026-08-13). So the July print reads soft on the
   headline the market watches, sticky underneath — the exact ambiguity a CPI-setup read has to hold
   honestly.

4. **The market trades PPI through the yield channel, but with less force than CPI — SUPPORTED
   (this-cycle precedent).** On the July release day (8/13), the soft headline was bought: the S&P
   500 jumped ~0.65% to a record close, the Nasdaq rose ~0.8%, Treasury yields edged down, and
   traders further cut September hike odds below 40% (NBC News / CNBC / CME Group, 2026-08-13). That
   is the dovish-relief reaction pattern — soft inflation prints get bought on the hike-relief
   channel, symmetric with the tame 8/12 CPI that preceded it. But PPI is medium-impact: it moves the
   tape less than CPI/jobs/FOMC, and its main leverage this cycle is as the *directional tell* for
   the CPI print 24 hours later, not as a standalone volatility event.

5. **PPI feeds PCE — a rate-path channel beyond the CPI tell — SUPPORTED.** Several PPI components
   (health-care services, portfolio management, airfares) map directly into the Fed's preferred PCE
   inflation gauge (the next PCE lands 9/25, per the calendar's macro set). So a hot or soft PPI is
   not only a CPI preview — it is an early read on the PCE the Fed actually targets, giving the print
   a second, quieter rate-path transmission that a pure "CPI tell" framing would miss.

6. **Tracked-name sensitivity — ranked, with mechanism.** `symbols: []` — market-wide; the ranking

**Honest limits.** D-22: no August street consensus exists yet (PPI consensus firms in the days just

not a forecast. Reaction-day characterizations come from press narration, not our own price

against the BLS primary or a dated press source. The PPI-into-PCE mapping (leg 5) is a
methodological channel, not a quantified forecast of the 9/25 PCE.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date confirmed, BLS primary re-checked 2026-08-19).** Treat 2026-09-10 08:30 ET as a
medium-impact known-date variance event whose real function is as the morning-before-CPI tell — not a
tradeable edge, and not a print to lean on alone given the 24-hour whipsaw to CPI. No directional
macro position, no new rate-sensitive paper entries initiated into the 9/10→9/11 pair, and any open
position sized as if both mornings (PPI *and* CPI, in either direction) are live. Base case
(**estimate-labeled**: trend extrapolation, no street consensus exists at D-22): a contained headline
near the July flat-to-soft reading with the 12-month rate holding elevated (~4–5%), a read the market
likely buys as hike-relief *unless* it re-accelerates or the renewed energy shock reverses the goods
deflation that suppressed July. The asymmetric risk is a hot PPI pre-loading a hawkish CPI read into
the FOMC blackout, hitting CRWV and the high-multiple semis hardest.

**Stance change 2026-09-02 (receipt: the D-8 ledger row).** The soft-headline base case above is
**retired**, and the guard around the print is **tightened** — the call itself does not change
(still no directional macro position, still no new rate-sensitive paper into the pair). Three things
moved since 8/26, all one way. (1) **The energy kill switch fired in the reference month, not just
the tape**: Brent ran **+3% across August** (EIA/press, checked 9/2) after +20.5% in July, so the
