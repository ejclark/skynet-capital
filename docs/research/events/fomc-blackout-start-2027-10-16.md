## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Eight blackout ledgers precede this one and the family has been arguing with itself about a single
session — the **overhang**, the gate's last day, which falls after the decision.
[November 2026](fomc-blackout-start-2026-11-28.md) named it,
[January 2027](fomc-blackout-start-2027-01-16.md) turned it into a sizing rule,
[March](fomc-blackout-start-2027-03-06.md) refused it, [May](fomc-blackout-start-2027-05-29.md)
reversed March with the widest measurement in the sample, and
[September 2027](fomc-blackout-start-2027-09-04.md) refused it again on a geometry argument (the gate
lifts into quad-witching). Separately, [October 2026](fomc-blackout-start-2026-10-17.md) — the only
prior October ledger — concluded that the October window's *middle* "carries no tracked event at all"
and that the danger sits at its two edges.

Two proposals filed this event, and both handed the same hint forward: this is the first blackout
entry in the calendar that actually **contains** a tracked print, and the window brackets the
mega-cap reporting cluster. So the question is narrow: **does an October gate have a different
content profile from the rest of the family, and if it does, what does that content do to the
boundary sessions the family keeps trading?**

**One-line verdict.** It does, and the answer inverts the sibling. Measured against SEC filings
rather than this repo's macro calendar, the family splits into **four earnings gates and four clean
gates**, perfectly: March, June, September and December windows contain **zero** mega-cap reports in
20 of 20 years each; January, April/May, July and October windows contain three to five. October is
an earnings gate, its middle is the densest corporate-news stretch of the year, and both its boundary
sessions run **below** October's own baseline (overhang 0.641× median, post-gate 0.697×) in the
second-loudest month of the calendar. Date `estimate`; no directional call.
