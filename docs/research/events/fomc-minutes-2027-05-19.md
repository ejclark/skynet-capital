## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The minutes of the April 27–28, 2027 FOMC publish at 14:00 ET on **2027-05-19**. Six sibling ledgers have
already said *read it, do not trade it*, and the deepest of them
([06-30](fomc-minutes-2027-06-30.md)) proved at n=160 that a minutes day is an **ordinary** session, not
a quiet one. Repeating either would waste the session. The proposal that seeded this id
([`from-fhfa-hpi-2027-05-25`](../../../src/domain/market-events/proposals/fomc-minutes-2027-05-19.from-fhfa-hpi-2027-05-25.json))
named a corridor story instead — these minutes as *"the ONLY scheduled Fed communication"* between the
April decision and the June blackout, sitting *"at its tail"*. So there are two questions worth a
session: **is the corridor claim true**, and **does the one family statistic that still survives — the
VIX-down share — actually apply to this date?**

**One-line verdict:** the corridor claim is **false** (the Beige Book lands 2027-05-26, inside the
window), and the VIX statistic is a **calendar-position artefact** that does **not** apply here — but
the sibling that most recently examined it reached the same non-application through a variable that turns
out to be a proxy, and its rule and mine give **opposite** answers on exactly this date. Date `estimate`;
no directional call.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date derives from a re-fetched primary, and the spring class is the family's cleanest.**
   SUPPORTED. `fomccalendars.htm` (HTTP 200, 164,831 bytes today) states the convention verbatim —
   *"The minutes of regularly scheduled meetings are released three weeks after the date of the policy
   decision"* — and its 2027 panel reads *"January 26-27 | March 16-17\* | **April 27-28** | June 8-9\* |
   July 27-28 | September 14-15\* | October 26-27 | December 7-8\*"*. 2027-04-28 is a Wednesday (verified)
   and +21 days is **Wednesday 2027-05-19**. Re-parsing all fourteen `fomchistorical<year>.htm` pages
   alongside the live panel reproduces **160 release dates** 2007-02-21 → 2026-08-19, **141 at exactly
   D+21**. Within that record the **April-meeting subset is 11 of 11 at exactly D+21** (2008→2016, 2020,
   2021, 2026) and **all 20 May-released minutes fell on a Wednesday**. The reason is mechanical, and it
   is the mirror image of the autumn class: [11-17](fomc-minutes-2027-11-17.md) showed every one of the 7
   autumn displacements had D+21 land on or beside **Thanksgiving**, a Thursday. May's only market
   holiday is **Memorial Day**, always a **Monday**, which is never adjacent to a Wednesday release — so
   the spring window contains no displacement mechanism at all. **Status stays `estimate`**: the 2027
   rows' Minutes cells are empty and the page's own *"tentative until confirmed"* clause binds, so the
   trigger is the **2027-03-16/17** meeting. `newsevents/2027-may.htm` **404s** and is in `probe-ref`.

2. **The proposal's corridor claim is false — there are two scheduled Fed communications in the window,
   not one, and these minutes sit in its middle.** REFUTED. The proposal framed 2027-05-19 as the only
   Fed item between the 2027-04-28 decision and the **2027-05-29** blackout, *"ten days before the window
   shuts"*. The pre-FOMC **Beige Book** publishes at exactly **D−14** from each decision day, reproduced
   **8 of 8** on the Board's own 2026 schedule (editions Jan 14, Mar 4, Apr 15, Jun 3, Jul 15, Sep 2, Oct
   14, Nov 25 against decision days Jan 28, Mar 18, Apr 29, Jun 17, Jul 29, Sep 16, Oct 28, Dec 9 — every
   pair 14 days, as recorded by the **confirmed** canonical
   [`beige-book-2026-11-25.json`](../../../src/domain/market-events/beige-book-2026-11-25.json)). For the
   June 8–9 2027 meeting that is **Wednesday 2027-05-26** — inside the corridor, seven days after these
   minutes and three days before the blackout. So the minutes are the **first** of two, in the middle of
   the window, not the last one at its tail. **Proposed as
   [`beige-book-2027-05-26`](../../../src/domain/market-events/proposals/beige-book-2027-05-26.from-fomc-minutes-2027-05-19.json)**,
   `estimate`, low impact — the Board has posted **no 2027 Beige Book schedule** (`beige-book-default.htm`
   HTTP 200 today, 2026 is its last year) and `newsevents/2027-may.htm` 404s, so the date is rule-derived,
   not listed. This calendar tracks only 2026 Beige Books, so the gap is real rather than a duplicate.

3. **The family's last surviving statistic is a calendar-position artefact — this is the headline.**
   REFUTED as stated by the family. Every sibling quotes *"VIX falls on minutes days"*: measured here on
   the same 160 releases, **64.4% down** against a **54.4%** all-session base (n=4,946), z=2.50,
   **p=0.0125** — the number reproduces exactly. It does not survive being split by **where in the
   expiration cycle the Wednesday sits**, each cell tested against its own **matched unconditional base**
   rather than against the other cells:

   | Minutes days in this cell | VIX-down | Matched base (ALL sessions of that day type) | p |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Plain Wednesday (not opex-week, not settlement) | **74.7%** (n=75) | 58.3% (n=701) | **0.0061** |
   | **Opex-week Wednesday** | **50.0%** (n=36) | 51.1% (n=235) | **0.91** |
   | VX settlement day | 46.8% (n=47) | 53.2% (n=235) | 0.42 |

   The mechanism the family assumed — that a Fed document deflates vol — should not switch off because of
   where the third Friday is. What the cells actually show is that **opex-week Wednesdays and settlement
   days are days when VIX unconditionally rises** (mean ΔVIX **+0.165** and **+0.066** against **+0.001**
   for all sessions), and a minutes release landing on one simply inherits that. *Confound, named and
   load-bearing:* this is still a base rate, not an attribution study, and 74.7% on n=75 is one cell of
   three — the honest reading is that the family's headline number is **not** a property of minutes days,
   not that a mechanism has been located in the residual cell.

4. **The 11-17 sibling reached the right answer for its own date through the wrong variable.** MIXED, and
   this matters because that ledger is three days old. It split the same statistic on **VIX settlement**
   (71.0% off vs 50.0% on, p=0.0122) and read settlement as the mechanism; this session reproduces the
   split (71.7% vs 46.8%, p=0.0028) and finds it is a **proxy**. **30 of the 47 on-settlement minutes days
   are themselves opex-week Wednesdays** — 65.4% of natural VX settlement Wednesdays coincide with the
   opex-week Wednesday over 2007–2026 — and inside opex week the settlement flag moves **nothing**:
   **50.0% on-settlement (n=30) vs 50.0% off-settlement (n=6)**. The variable that survives the 2×2 is
   opex-week position, not settlement. The sibling's **conclusion** for 2027-11-17 is upheld — that date
   is both — but its **rule** misclassifies any date where the two come apart.

5. **2027-05-19 is precisely where they come apart, and it is off-settlement because of a holiday thirty
   days later.** SUPPORTED. May 2027's third Friday is **2027-05-21**, so 2027-05-19 is the **opex-week
   Wednesday**. Under the Cboe VX rule it would *also* be the settlement date — the Wednesday 30 days
   before the following month's third Friday, and June 2027's third Friday is **2027-06-18**, exactly 30
   days later. But the rule's holiday clause fires: **2027-06-18 is Juneteenth observed** (2027-06-19 is a
   Saturday, weekday verified), a Cboe holiday, so settlement moves to the preceding business day,
   **Tuesday 2027-05-18**. This session reproduced that independently by applying the rule cold, and it
   matches the **confirmed** canonical
   [`vix-expiration-2027-05-18.json`](../../../src/domain/market-events/vix-expiration-2027-05-18.json) to
   the day. Only **12 of 240** months 2007–2026 displaced at all, and **opex-week Wednesday + off
   settlement** has just **6 precedents in twenty years** (2009-07-15, 2009-10-14, 2010-07-14, 2012-11-14,
   2021-10-13, 2026-08-19). **The two rules disagree on this date:** the sibling's puts it in the 71.7%
   vol-drop half; leg 3's puts it in the 50.0% cell — where the mean **residualised** ΔVIX is **+0.185**
   and only **1 of 36** releases ever cleared −1.0 pt against an **8.94%** base for the same day type.
   Registered as **FT-3** rather than asserted.

6. **The exact date-for-date precedent exists, and it differs from 2027 in exactly one variable.**
   SUPPORTED, and it is the closest thing to a natural experiment this family has. **2021-04-28 →
   2021-05-19**: same decision date, same release date, also the opex-week Wednesday. It was **on**
   settlement (June 2021's third Friday, 2021-06-18, was a normal session — Juneteenth was not yet
   observed by the exchanges). It printed **S&P −0.29%** and **ΔVIX +0.84** — VIX **up**, consistent with
   the opex-week cell and not with the family's headline. 2027 reproduces that configuration with the
   settlement flag **flipped off**, by a holiday thirty days downstream. If settlement were the mechanism,
   2027 should behave differently from 2021; if opex-week position is, it should behave the same.

7. **Nothing about the S&P channel on this date is distinguishable, and the May class is if anything
   quieter.** SUPPORTED, and deliberately unexciting. [06-30](fomc-minutes-2027-06-30.md)'s n=160 result
   reproduces here exactly: minutes-day |S&P| **0.776%** mean / **0.468%** median against **0.798%** /
   **0.523%** for all 4,946 sessions, bootstrap **p=0.398**, within ±0.3% **33.1% vs 33.4%**, ≥1.5% on
   **20 of 160 (12.5%)** vs a **14.07%** base. The opex-week-Wednesday subset is no different (**0.809%**
   mean vs **0.830%** for all opex-week Wednesdays). The 20 May-released minutes ran **0.645%** mean
   |S&P| with **2 of 20** ≥1.5%. So the correct posture is the family's, unchanged: refuse the trade
   **and** refuse the safety.

8. **The ±5-day corridor is four entries deep and needed nothing added.** SUPPORTED.
   [`pjm-capacity-auction-2027-05`](pjm-capacity-auction-2027-05.md) (05-15, est),
   [`vix-expiration-2027-05-18`](vix-expiration-2027-05-18.md) (**confirmed**),

### Honest limits

Leg 3 is a **cross-tabulation of base rates, not an attribution study** — no work here separates a
minutes-driven ΔVIX from a coincident one, which is exactly why the plain-Wednesday cell's 74.7% is
reported as *unexplained* rather than as a finding. The three cells are also not independent tests chosen
in advance: the opex-week split was suggested by this event's own calendar position, which is a
specification-search risk the forward test exists to answer out of sample. Leg 4 disagrees with a sibling
that used the OCC's published settlement calendar where this session **derives** settlement dates from the
Cboe rule; the two splits differ slightly (71.0/50.0 there, 71.7/46.8 here) and the derivation is the more
likely source of the gap. Leg 6 rests on **one** precedent print. Leg 2's Beige Book date is rule-derived
against a schedule the Board has not published for 2027 at all, on a meeting date that is itself
tentative. Release dates come from the Fed's own pages, but the 2027 month page that would carry the
release *time* 404s, so 14:00 ET is convention, not sourced for this release. And the whole document rests

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `estimate`):** **read it, do not trade it — and stop carrying the family's VIX statistic
onto this date.** The refusal is inherited from [06-30](fomc-minutes-2027-06-30.md) and re-verified here
(minutes-day |S&P| 0.776% vs a 0.798% base, p=0.398; 12.5% moved ≥1.5%): a minutes day is an **ordinary**
session, so refuse the trade *and* refuse the safety. What this ledger adds is the retirement of the one
statistic that had survived. The family's **64.4% VIX-down** share is a **calendar-position artefact** —
**74.7% vs a 58.3% matched base** on a plain Wednesday (p=0.0061), **50.0% vs a 51.1% matched base** on an
opex-week Wednesday (p=0.91) — and **2027-05-19 is an opex-week Wednesday**, where the effect is zero and
the mean residualised ΔVIX is **positive** (+0.185, n=36). The sibling
[11-17](fomc-minutes-2027-11-17.md) reached "no vol drop" for its own date through **VIX settlement**,
which is a proxy: 30 of its 47 on-settlement releases are opex-week Wednesdays, and inside opex week the
flag moves nothing. **On this date the two rules disagree** — 2027-05-19 is off-settlement, because
Juneteenth 2027-06-18 pushes VX May settlement back to Tuesday 2027-05-18 — so the sibling's rule predicts
a drop and this one predicts none. That separation is registered, not asserted. No directional call, no
size; `estimate` widens caution and licenses nothing.

**Kill switches:**

- **Date / relevance kill:** a published 2027 minutes date other than **2027-05-19** while the meeting
  stays April 27–28. The spring class's 11-of-11 D+21 record meets its first counterexample and leg 1 is
  re-derived against holidays generally. (If the 2027-03-16/17 meeting moves the April meeting, this test
  is void, not failed.) Registered **FT-1**, score by **2027-05-20**.

  minutes as the session's named driver*. Leg 7's inherited null fails. Registered **FT-2**, score by

- **Variable kill — the one this ledger exists for:** 2027-05-19's **residualised** ΔVIX clearing
  **−1.0 pt**. Leg 3's opex-week cell fails on the first out-of-sample date and the 11-17 sibling's
  settlement rule is the better one after all. Registered **FT-3**, score by **2027-05-20**.
- **Corridor kill:** the Board publishing a 2027 Beige Book schedule dating the pre-June edition anywhere
  other than **2027-05-26**. Leg 2's D−14 rule fails and the proposal is re-derived or withdrawn.
  Registered **FT-4**, score by **2027-05-27**.
- **Measurement kill:** a defensible attribution study showing minutes releases *do* deflate implied vol
  once coincident macro and expiration flow are controlled for. Leg 3 is a cross-tab of base rates and
  would not survive it; the practical conclusion might, but it would need re-arguing rather than patching.
