## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The Sep 15–16 minutes publish 2026-10-07 at 14:00 ET. This is the calendar's first minutes entry,
so there is no house precedent to inherit, and it has an unusual property: **its entire content is
determined by an upstream tracked event that has not happened yet** ([`fomc-2026-09-16`](fomc-2026-09-16.md),
D-16, currently a coin flip). So the question is not "what will the minutes say" — that is
unanswerable today — but: **what, if anything, about this release is knowable and decision-relevant
at D-37, and what should be pre-registered now so the 10-07 read is mechanical rather than
improvised?**

**One-line verdict:** three things are knowable and none of them is a trade — the release is
**confirmed** on two independent Fed primaries; the Warsh-era minutes-day base rate is **2-for-2
non-driver**; and the minutes are the only Fed document that names **this book's own capex as an
inflation source**, which is the single thread worth reading for and the one thing here that is
scoreable.

**Method:** sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md) —
federalreserve.gov primary documents fetched and parsed directly today (2026-08-31: the October and
November 2026 calendars, the FOMC calendars rule page, and the full June and July 2026 minutes),
with press-cited market reaction carried at its as-of date. No price instruments run: `symbols: []`,
no issuer.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date derives from two independent Fed primaries — flipped `estimate` → `confirmed`.**
   SUPPORTED. federalreserve.gov's October 2026 calendar, fetched today, carries the entry verbatim:
   **"FOMC Minutes / Meeting of September 15-16"** at **2:00 p.m. on October 7**. Independently, the
   FOMC calendars page states the governing rule: *"The minutes of regularly scheduled meetings are
   released three weeks after the date of the policy decision."* The policy decision is
   **2026-09-16**; three weeks later is **Wednesday 2026-10-07**. Two primaries, one arithmetic
   derivation, no judgment of mine. The entry was filed `estimate` only because the proposing sweep
   ([`fomc-blackout-start-2026-10-17`](fomc-blackout-start-2026-10-17.md)) applies the lane's
   no-self-confirm limit to an event discovered in-sweep; a dedicated session re-fetching the
   primaries is exactly what closes that — the same path that ledger used to confirm its own date.
   One caveat kept on the record: the calendars page shows **no** minutes date attached to the
   Sep 15–16 row itself (that link is populated on release), so the October calendar page is doing
   the work and the rule is the corroboration.

2. **A minutes day is not a market event under this chair — 2 for 2.** SUPPORTED, thin n.
   **2026-07-08** (June minutes): markets "reacted little to the minutes release", futures held
   negative and Treasury yields rose (CNBC/press summary). **2026-08-19** (July minutes): the text
   was genuinely hawkish — a 9–3 hold with three dissents for a hike and participants stating
   *"policy tightening would likely be necessary if inflation did not decline"* — yet the S&P closed
   **+0.2% at 7,707.98**, snapping a three-day losing streak, with the tape attributing the session
   to Treasury's announcement that it would **at least double** long-end buybacks (10Y **−5bp to
   4.65%**, 30Y **−9bp to 5.19%**); the live blog's own words are that the minutes *"appear not to
   have derailed the day's gains."* That buyback announcement is itself a tracked entry here
   ([`treasury-buyback-increase-2026-09-09`](treasury-buyback-increase-2026-09-09.md)), which
   corroborates the attribution rather than resting on one outlet. **Honest weakness:** n=2, and in
   *both* cases a competing driver was present — so the evidence is "minutes lost the day twice",
   not "minutes never move a tape."

3. **The minutes are now a larger share of a much smaller communications budget — and that cuts two
   ways.** SUPPORTED. Warsh has cut the statement to roughly **130 words** from 300-plus, dropped the
   easing-bias language (*"Most participants emphasized that they preferred not to repeat the language
   in the previous postmeeting statement that had suggested an easing bias"*), retired forward
   guidance, and argued at Jackson Hole for *"a quieter Fed."* The minutes did **not** shrink to
   match: the July edition runs ~12,000 words. So in relative terms this document now carries more of
   the Fed's total disclosure than under any prior chair. The honest consequence is **interpretive,
   not tradable** — leg 2 says the market still does not price off it, and a document that is three
   weeks old cannot carry new information about a corridor that has since printed PCE, ISM and
   payrolls. High read value, near-zero trade value, and those are separate axes.

4. **The minutes are where this Committee's *institutional* changes surface — including one that
   touches this repo's calendar.** SUPPORTED. Warsh's proposal to move from **eight regular meetings
   to six from 2027** appeared in the **July minutes**, not in any statement, presser or speech:
   *"would allow more information to accumulate between meetings than under current practice and
   provide policymakers and the staff more time to consider strategic monetary policy issues"* —
   with *"no decisions regarding possible changes in the meeting schedule"* taken. The June minutes
   carried the statement-redesign debate the same way (*"A majority of participants remarked that
   they saw advantages in shortening the statement"*), and Warsh's five task forces include one on
   communications. So the 10-07 release is the next scheduled venue where a 2027 calendar decision
   could become public. That is a **housekeeping** consequence for `market-events.ts`, not a trade.

5. **The one leg specific to this book: the Committee has twice named AI demand as an inflation
   source, and the language escalated.** SUPPORTED, and it is the finding. **June minutes**: core
   goods inflation was *"largely reflecting the effects of tariffs and AI-related price pressures"*,
   and *"ongoing strong demand for AI infrastructure would likely sustain upward pressure on prices
   for technology products and electricity."* **July minutes** go further and get specific:
   *"Materials for data centers, such as chips and steel, had registered large price increases and
   that consumer items such as smartphones, computer equipment, software, and electricity had also
   been subject to price pressures"*, plus *"Several other participants viewed investment in AI as
   already having broader effects on prices by pushing up aggregate demand or assessed that it would
   likely do so relatively soon."* The **reflexive loop** that creates: the demand driving
   NVDA/CRWV/AVGO/MRVL is being cited by the body that sets the discount rate as a reason to tighten
   — and per [`fomc-2026-09-16`](fomc-2026-09-16.md)'s tiering, tightening hits those same names
   hardest (CRWV most, through financing cost as well as duration). Two counterweights kept on the
   record: the same minutes carry the supply-side rebuttal (*"productivity gains associated with
   adoption of AI would eventually reduce production costs and increase aggregate supply, a
   development that should put downward pressure on inflation"*), and **Warsh personally holds that
   disinflationary view** — so this is a live internal disagreement, not settled doctrine. That is
   exactly what makes a third observation informative, and it is registered as **FT-31**.

6. **The marginal content is the argument, not the tally — and the fault line to watch is Board vs.
   Reserve Banks.** SUPPORTED. The vote and the named dissenters publish in the **statement on
   9/16**, three weeks before the minutes, so the minutes add nothing there. What only the minutes
   show is how close the non-dissenters were and on what conditions. The July fault line is the
   useful prior: the three hike dissents were **all regional presidents** — Hammack (Cleveland),
   Kashkari (Minneapolis), Logan (Dallas) — and **no Board governor joined**, against a **12–0**
   unanimous June. The Board is the chair's bloc, so a governor crossing over is a materially
   stronger hawkish signal than a fourth regional president doing so, and the minutes are where a
   near-miss governor shows up at all.

7. **The corridor: this lands after the funding cliff and after payrolls, and it is lapse-proof.**
   SUPPORTED. Within five days sit **jobs 10-02** (confirmed), the **68th OPEC+ JMMC 10-04**
   (estimate), **MRVL Investor Day 10-06** (confirmed) and **ISM Services 10-05** (proposed in this
   PR, estimate) — none of which changes what the minutes say, since the document was written three
   weeks earlier. The asymmetry worth naming is the lapse branch: the Fed is **self-funded and
   publishes through a shutdown**, while BLS does not (the 2025 lapse cancelled the October CPI
   outright, per [`government-funding-deadline-2026-09-30`](government-funding-deadline-2026-09-30.md)).
   So a 10-01 lapse deletes jobs 10-02 and CPI 10-14 while leaving 10-07 and the Beige Book 10-14
   standing — this release's **relative** weight rises in exactly the branch where the hard data
   vanishes. It does not become more tradable; it becomes more of what little there is.

8. **It clears the blackout by ten days, which is the underrated part of the timing.** SUPPORTED.
   The gate falls **2026-10-17** ([`fomc-blackout-start-2026-10-17`](fomc-blackout-start-2026-10-17.md),
   confirmed). The minutes publish 10-07, leaving a **seven-session window (10-07 → 10-16)** in which
   Fed officials may still publicly interpret their own record — a luxury the **Beige Book 10-14**
   gets barely two sessions of. That is the precise reading of the calendar entry's "the only one
   that arrives outside the blackout" note: both Fed publications clear the gate, but only this one
   clears it with room for official commentary afterwards. *Caveat, inherited from the blackout
   doc:* federalreserve.gov's October calendar, fetched today, lists **no Board speeches at all**, so
   the interpretation window exists in principle and nobody is currently scheduled to use it.

not a base rate, and no measured distribution of minutes-day S&P moves exists in this repo. Leg 5's
