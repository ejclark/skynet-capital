## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The [ISM Services 10-05 ledger](ism-services-2026-10-05.md) discovered this event during its
2026-09-09 pulse, filed it as a proposal, and made a strong structural claim about it: that S&P
Global's competing services survey lands **15 minutes before ISM**, *inside* the intraday window two
registered forward tests use to measure ISM, and that *"every future intraday test of an ISM Services
release should name this print in its void clause."* Nobody had measured it.

So, three questions: **(1)** is the date right, and can this calendar's zero-S&P-Global-entries gap be
closed honestly? **(2)** Does this print actually move the tape in its own 15-minute window — i.e. is
the contaminant real, or only real on a calendar? **(3)** Does this slot have any property that makes
it worth tracking at all, given the ISM series has measured its louder sibling to nil nine times?

**One-line verdict:** the date holds on a **primary listing** rather than the proposal's derivation;
the contaminant is **refuted as a tape fact** — the 09:45 window is measurably *quieter* than an
ordinary one while the 10:00 ISM window on the very same days runs at p88–p92 in rates; and the slot's
one genuinely distinguishing property is that it is **pre-announced**, which relocates this entry's
value from 10-05 to the **09-23 flash**, proposed here.
