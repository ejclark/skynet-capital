## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Two sibling ledgers have already answered the general question. [`boj-minutes-2027-03-24`](boj-minutes-2027-03-24.md)
established that BoJ Minutes are the slowest meeting account of the three majors and a measured null
(Nikkei median 0.84× an ordinary session). [`boj-minutes-2026-12-23`](boj-minutes-2026-12-23.md)
established that the **October cohort** is quieter still (0.27×) and settled the pair rule at 84/84.
Re-running either is transcription, not research.

This event is the **other** cohort — the Minutes of a September MPM, a meeting that carries **no
Outlook Report**. That gives it two questions of its own:

1. **The sibling's method assumed the tape could adjudicate.** It measured release-day moves against
   a seasonal control and read the low numbers as a null. Does that method survive at a venue whose
   dates are *not* in a quiet week? Or does it manufacture a signal?
2. **The sibling found exactly one reason to open its document — the Outlook Report's drafting
   record.** A September MPM has no Outlook. So what, if anything, is left?

And one thread was handed over explicitly. The 12-23 ledger's honest-limits section records that
leg 1's *"mechanism is arithmetic, not statute"*, and that the statutory explanation for the chaining
law **could not be sourced** — boj.or.jp's law pages returned 404, japaneselawtranslation.go.jp 403.
This session found it somewhere neither lane had looked: **inside the Minutes themselves.**

**One-line verdict: the September cohort is not a quiet cell but the most calendar-confounded one in

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The chaining law is statutory and recorded in the documents themselves — SUPPORTED, primary,
   and this closes the thread the sibling left open.** The 12-23 ledger could only derive the law
   arithmetically and recorded that the statute could not be sourced. The September 2025 Minutes
   state it in their own first footnote, verbatim:

   > *"The minutes of this meeting were approved by the Policy Board at the Monetary Policy Meeting
   > held on October 29 and 30, 2025, as 'a document describing an outline of the discussion at the
   > meeting' stipulated in Article 20, paragraph 1 of the Bank of Japan Act of 1997."*

   And the act of approval is itself a numbered section of every Minutes — **Section VI**, verbatim
   from the same document:

   > *"VI. Approval of the Minutes of the Monetary Policy Meeting — The Policy Board approved
   > unanimously the minutes of the Monetary Policy Meeting of July 30 and 31, 2025, for release on
   > September 25."*

   Two consequences worth stating plainly. **The Minutes structurally cannot publish before the next
   MPM**, because the Policy Board convenes only at MPMs and the Board's approval is a statutory
   precondition — the law is a governance requirement, not a scheduling habit. And **the release
   date is set by a recorded Board vote at the preceding meeting**, which is why the schedule page
   can pre-announce it. Cross-check against the schedule table parsed today: Section VI's *"July 30
   and 31, 2025 … for release on September 25"* is the same chain the 2026 table shows for its own
   July row (`"July 30 (Thurs.), 31 (Fri.)" … "Sept. 28 (Mon.)"`).

2. **The one class of exception is Japan's Golden Week, not BoJ discretion — SUPPORTED, measured
   across the whole population.** Days from the *next* MPM's close to publication, all 84 chainable
   pairs: `{3:14, 4:1, 5:37, 6:22, 7:2, 9:2, 10:2, 11:2, 13:1, 28:1}` — median **5**, and **96%**
   inside 3-7 days. Every one of the eight outliers past +7 is a **March**-meeting Minutes published
   in early May, pushed out by the Apr 29 – May 5 holiday cluster (2016, 2018, 2019, 2021, 2022,
   2023, 2026 at +9 to +13); the single +28 is the **2020-04-27** meeting approved at the
   **unscheduled 2020-05-22** COVID MPM. The law survives its exceptions because the exceptions have
   named causes.
