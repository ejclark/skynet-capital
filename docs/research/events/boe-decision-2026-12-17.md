## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Two sibling ledgers already exist on this cycle, and both of them describe December rather than
assessing it: September called its own rate "dead information" and pointed at the QT review; November
called itself "the one live rate decision on the UK strip" and treated December as a residual. So the
question this ledger has to answer is not "what will the Bank do" in the abstract. It is: **given
that December's 94.6% is a cumulative number, what does it actually say about this meeting — and does
it change how the two sibling calls should be read?**

**One-line verdict:** it decomposes, and the decomposition inverts the framing — **December, not
November, is where the strip-versus-survey disagreement is adjudicated**, the house's own open test on
this meeting is priced at a **coin flip** rather than an under, and a November hold *raises* this
meeting's hurdle to **88%**.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The strip's December column decomposes, and the decomposition is trustworthy because the
   aggregator's own arithmetic checks out on the two meetings where it is over-determined.**
   SUPPORTED, and this is the ledger's central contribution. From centralbank.watch's BoE page
   (fetched today, data stamped **2026-09-08**), against a Bank Rate of **3.75%**:

   | Meeting | Cut | Hold | Hike | Implied |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 2026-09-17 | 0.0% | 93.1% | 6.9% | 3.77% |
   | 2026-11-05 | 0.0% | 45.9% | **54.2%** | 3.89% |
   | 2026-12-17 | 0.0% | 5.4% | **94.6%** | **4.11%** |

   The page states its own semantics: *"the per-meeting percentages below describe the same single
   expected path seen from different dates — they are not separate bets"*, and the methodology the
   `fomc-2026-12-09` ledger quotes from the same publisher is blunter — these are the chance the rate
   is *higher than today's* by that date, and they *"should not be added, multiplied, or compared
   across meetings."* **The check:** if `implied = 3.75 + 0.25 × E[hikes]`, then September's 6.9%
   gives 3.75 + 0.01725 = **3.767 → 3.77 ✓** and November's 54.2% gives 3.75 + 0.1355 = **3.886 →
   3.89 ✓**. Both reproduce exactly. Applying the same identity to December, where the support has to
   be `{0, +25, +50}` because the cut column is **0.0%** at every horizon: `p₀ = 0.054`,
   `p₁ + 2p₂ = (4.11 − 3.75)/0.25 = 1.44`, `p₁ + p₂ = 0.946` → **`p₂ = 0.494`, `p₁ = 0.452`.** So the
   strip prices **no hike 5.4% · exactly one 45.2% · two 49.4%** by 12-17. Sensitivity is stated
   rather than hidden: across the implied-rate rounding band (4.105–4.115) `p₂` runs 0.474–0.514,
   which is the band every derived number below carries.

2. **Therefore the house's own open forward test on this meeting is a coin flip, and reporting it as
   an "under" overstates it.** SUPPORTED, arithmetic, and it is a correction to a sibling's framing
   rather than to its evidence. **FT-boe-decision-2026-09-17-3** predicts *Bank Rate at 4.00% or below
   immediately after the 2026-12-17 decision* — i.e. at most one 25bp increase across both meetings.
   That is `p₀ + p₁` = **5.4 + 45.2 = 50.6%**, inside a **48.6–52.6%** band. The September ledger
   registered it as "the strip is bolder than the UK data" and graded it **medium**; on the strip's own
   numbers it is not a contrarian call at all — the market already assigns it about even odds. This
   does **not** invalidate the test (it is well-formed and dated); it re-prices what a pass would
   *mean*. The genuinely contrarian claim is the one nobody has registered: **no hike at all**, at
   **5.4%**. This ledger registers it as **FT-boe-decision-2026-12-17-1**.

3. **A November hold raises this meeting's hurdle rather than lowering it — the two sibling calls are
   conditionally linked.** SUPPORTED, arithmetic. Zero hikes by December requires a hold at November,
   so `P(no hike by Dec) = 5.4%` sits entirely inside `P(Nov holds) = 45.8%`. Hence
   **`P(hike at Dec | Nov holds) = (45.8 − 5.4)/45.8 = 88.2%`.** Read plainly: if
   **FT-boe-decision-2026-11-05-1** passes, the strip's own structure says December delivers with
   ~88% probability. Any later session that treats a November hold as vindication of the calendar's
   dovish read is misreading the same table it cited — the hold is the setup, and this is the venue.

4. **December, not November, is the meeting where the strip and the surveys must have a winner.**
   SUPPORTED, from the size of the gaps. At November the strip is **54.2% hike** and the Reuters panel
   is ~9% on a 2026 hike — but a coin-flip meeting resolves in one direction or the other without
   embarrassing anyone; a hold at 54.2% is unremarkable and a hike at 54.2% is unremarkable. At
   December the strip says **94.6% higher** and the same panel — **56 of 64 (87.5%)** expecting *no
   change for the rest of 2026*, six a hike, two a cut (Reuters poll **13–18 August 2026**,
   press-relayed, inherited from the November ledger's fetch) — says **~9%**. The Bank's **own Market
   Participants Survey** (BoE primary, **15–17 July 2026**, **78 respondents**) puts the **end-2026
   median at 3.75%** with over 90% on hold after July, then *cuts* to 3.50% / 3.25% / 3.25%. That is
   an **~82-point gap on the same question**, and it has a terminal date: after 12-17 there is no
   2026 meeting left, so one of the two reads is simply wrong, on the record, by 2026-12-18.

5. **The staleness objection has a sharper date than the gilt blow-out — and the sharper date makes
   the strip's number partly American.** SUPPORTED, and it is new to this ledger. The November ledger
   dated its own weakness to the September gilt move (30Y at **5.89%**, a 2056 DMO sale clearing
   **5.8168%** on 09-08, the highest at any DMO auction since 1998). True, but there is an earlier and
   larger common factor both surveys also miss: **Warsh's 2026-08-28 Jackson Hole keynote**. Per the
   [`fomc-2026-12-09`](fomc-2026-12-09.md) ledger's own fetched record, that speech put PCE at 3.7%

   single session, and left the **Dec-9 FOMC distribution with no cut branch at all** (3.50–3.75%
   **11.6%** · 3.75–4.00% **38.5%** · 4.00–4.25% **38.9%** · 4.25–4.50% **11.0%**). The MaPS closed
   **17 July** and the Reuters poll **18 August**; both pre-date it. So the UK December strip is a
   **September** object priced after a global hawkish repricing that neither survey saw — which is
   the honest reason the gap exists, and also the reason it may not be a UK judgment: **the MPC votes
   on UK data, and Warsh's PCE trend is not a UK datum.** The mitigation is dated, not rhetorical: the

   both.

6. **This strip has already over-priced UK tightening once this cycle, by a large margin, and unwound
   it.** SUPPORTED, press-relayed but specific and dated. When the US–Israel strikes began in **March
   2026** the 10-year gilt went above **5%** (highest since July 2008) and the 2-year above **4.6%**,
   and markets **briefly priced four Bank of England hikes in 2026** — against a pre-conflict baseline
   of **two cuts**. None of the four has been delivered; Bank Rate is 3.75% and the MPC has held at
   every meeting since. That is not an argument that the strip is wrong now, and it is not offered as
   one. It is a **measured base rate**: at a 99-day horizon this particular market's UK policy number
   has a demonstrated history of travelling a very long way. The practical consequence is written into
   **FT-boe-decision-2026-12-17-2** — the level of the December probability at D-99 is not a forecast;
   its *conditional structure* is the durable object.
