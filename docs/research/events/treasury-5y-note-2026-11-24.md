not the mechanism it was labelled with — and that changes what a failure on 11-24 would mean.** The

## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** this calendar's most confident rates finding is that a 5-Year auction bends
the curve at its own point by ~1.7bp, and the [10-27 sibling](treasury-5y-note-2026-10-27.md) called
that effect *substantially mechanical* — Treasury's fitted par curve re-anchoring on a fresh
on-the-run. That ledger listed its own weakest link: it never verified which date the curve's
on-the-run input actually switches, and every measurement was inside one 2023–2026 window. This
session takes the cheapest available shot at both.

**One-line verdict:** the effect is **real and usable and mis-labelled** — it is a post-2022 regime
property, absent across 24 years of held-out data, that **reverses within three sessions**, and it
survives unchanged across the whole record at exactly one tenor (the 10-Year); so 11-24 inherits the
base rate but not the mechanism, and the two things that would have argued against this particular
date — the holiday week and the crowded session — are both measured not to matter.

**Method:** sourced research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md), rates mode —
the house instruments are symbol-keyed and this event has `symbols: []`, so no `earnings-cycle` /
`intraday-edges` run applies and no cache bust was required. Every figure below is computed this
session from Treasury primaries fetched today: the Fiscal Data `auctions_query` dataset (**1,839**
Note records, 1995-01-24 → 2026-09-09) and the **29** daily par yield curve year-files 1998–2026
(**7,176** sessions, 1998-01-02 → 2026-09-08, the latest published close). Schedule provenance is the

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The date and terms are right, re-read from the primary, and the entry stays `estimate` —
SUPPORTED.** The schedule carries the row verbatim: `5-Year NOTE · Thursday, November 19, 2026 ·
Tuesday, November 24, 2026 · Monday, November 30, 2026`, with no `R`. That is a new issue, and the
record agrees emphatically — **all 326** nominal 5-Year auctions on the fiscaldata dataset since 1995
are new issues; this tenor has never been reopened in the modern era. The same fetch shows the
schedule's own `Holiday - Thursday, November 26, 2026` line and the rest of the block (2Y Mon 11-23,
7Y Wed 11-25, all off the same 11-19 announcement, all settling 11-30). Two prior proposals
transcribed this row on 2026-09-08/09; this reading is independent of both. The entry stays
**`estimate`** on the one limit that applies at D−76: a tentative schedule is tentative, and the
confirming primary is the 2026-11-19 announcement, 71 days out.

**2. The size is not published, and this doc will not assert one — SUPPORTED (as a negative).**
`sb0590` (2026-08-05), read direct today: the anticipated-size grid is headed `2-Year 3-Year 5-Year
7-Year 10-Year 20-Year 30-Year FRN` and runs `May-26 Jun-26 Jul-26 Aug-26 Sep-26 Oct-26` — six rows,
**no November**. The 5-Year column reads **70** in every one of them. Two verbatim sentences bracket
what that is worth: Treasury "anticipates maintaining nominal coupon and FRN auction sizes for at
least the next several quarters," and in the same release "Treasury continues to evaluate potential
future changes to nominal coupon and FRN auction sizes." So **$70B is an expectation** — 28
consecutive $70B prints (2024-04-24 → 2026-08-26, zero deviations) plus flat guidance — and the
published number arrives at the **2026-11-04** refunding. Every band in this doc is computed on the
$70B era and is void if November deviates.

**3. The curve effect is a regime, not a mechanism — REFUTED as labelled, and this is the finding.**
The 10-27 sibling's measurement is reproduced exactly here (2023+: −1.69bp, t = −9.84, n=43). Run the
identical measure across the whole available record:

| Window | n (auction) | Mean relative | t | Non-auction control | Diff |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1998-01 … 2009-12 | 98 | −0.33bp | −1.34 | +0.02bp | −0.34 |
| 2010-01 … 2019-12 | 114 | −0.12bp | −1.02 | −0.00bp | −0.12 |
| 2020-01 … 2021-09 | 21 | **+0.43bp** | +1.40 | −0.00bp | +0.43 |
| 2021-10 … 2022-12 | 14 | −1.14bp | −2.40 | +0.00bp | −1.14 |
| 2023-01 … 2023-12 | 12 | **−2.00bp** | −4.69 | +0.10bp | −2.10 |
| 2024-01 … 2026-09 | 31 | **−1.56bp** | −9.12 | +0.09bp | −1.66 |

Pooled, **pre-2023 is −0.21bp (t = −1.81, n=247)** against 2023+'s −1.69bp; the permutation test on
100,000 draws gives **p = 0.00001**, and the difference-in-differences against each window's own
non-auction sessions is **−1.78bp now versus −0.22bp then**. That is the same shape of result the
[2Y sibling](treasury-2y-note-2026-11-23.md) found for its outright effect, at a second tenor and on
a different measure — and it is decisive against the *mechanical* reading, because Treasury's par
curve has been fitted to the most recently auctioned securities throughout. A fitting artifact does
not have a start date.

**It has also run both signs and decayed before.** Rolling 24-auction windows on the same series:

| Window | Mean | t |
|---|---|---|
| 1998-01-28 → 2003-10-08 | **−2.96bp** | −5.55 |
| 2002-11-05 → 2005-04-13 | +0.19bp | +0.55 |
| 2008-05-29 → 2010-04-28 | **+1.37bp** | **+3.13** |
| 2016-07-26 → 2018-08-28 | −0.58bp | −3.25 |
| 2021-05-26 → 2023-05-24 | −1.08bp | −3.09 |
| 2024-08-28 → 2026-08-26 (live) | **−1.44bp** | **−8.28** |

Three prior significant episodes, one of them the **wrong sign** at t = +3.13, and all three decayed.
The current run dates to 2021–2022 and has been stable and strengthening since, but it is an episode
in a series of episodes, not a law.

**4. The move reverses, which is what a concession does and a re-anchoring does not — SUPPORTED, and
this is the sharpest single test.** Event time on the relative measure:

| Window | D−3 | D−2 | D−1 | **D+0** | **D+1** | D+2 | D+3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023+ (n=43) | +0.09 | +0.01 | +0.10 | **−1.69** (t −9.8) | **+0.55** (t +5.1) | +0.29 (t 2.5) | +0.49 (t 4.3) |
| pre-2023 (n=247) | +0.14 | +0.01 | +0.02 | −0.21 (t −1.8) | **−0.47** (t −5.3) | −0.02 | −0.02 |

Cumulated, 2023+ gives back **+1.33bp across D+1…D+3** — **t = +8.97, 40 of 43 auctions positive** —
against **+0.03bp (t = 0.75)** for the same three-session forward window measured on every 2023+
session. That recovers **79%** of the D+0 dip. If the par curve had simply re-anchored on a new
on-the-run, the new level would persist; instead it unwinds on schedule. And the pre-2023 sample
shows the reverse pattern (continued drift at D+1), so the reversal is a property of the current
regime, not of the measure.

**5. Only the 10-Year survives out of sample — SUPPORTED, and it rescues the mechanical story for
exactly one tenor.** Own-tenor-minus-neighbours on new issues, split at 2023:

| Tenor | pre-2023 n | pre-2023 mean | t | 2023+ n | 2023+ mean | t |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2-Year | 288 | −0.09bp | −0.7 | 43 | **−4.29bp** | −11.7 |
| 3-Year | 186 | **+0.60bp** | **+4.4** | 45 | **−3.10bp** | −16.0 |
| **5-Year** | 247 | −0.21bp | −1.8 | 43 | **−1.69bp** | −9.8 |
| 7-Year | 167 | **+0.84bp** | **+4.4** | 44 | **−1.09bp** | −8.9 |
| **10-Year** | 92 | **−1.65bp** | **−6.0** | 15 | **−1.60bp** | −8.7 |

The 10-Year's effect is the same size, sign and significance in 1998–2022 as it is now. Everything
else either switched on (2Y, 5Y) or **flipped sign** (3Y, 7Y). The honest synthesis: there probably
*is* a permanent on-the-run/roll mechanic, it lives at the 10-Year benchmark where the roll matters
most, and the 2023+ effect at every other tenor is something else that arrived with the current
supply regime.

**The 10-27 sibling's reopening decomposition dies the same way.** Its second mechanical test was
that 10Y reopenings show a third of a new issue's effect (−0.52bp vs −1.60bp, 2023+). Measured across
1998–2022 on near-the-run reopenings only (`9-Year x-Month` residual terms, so the knot is
comparable), reopenings print **+0.69bp, t = +8.3, n=145** — the opposite sign, strongly significant.
The 2023+ −0.52bp is a window reading, not a decomposition of a standing mechanic.

**6. Thanksgiving week is not a discount at this tenor — SUPPORTED as a clean negative, corroborating
the 2Y sibling.** Fourteen 5Y auctions since 1998 fall in the Thanksgiving week (Mon–Wed plus the

2010+ p = 0.083, 2018+ p = 0.18). Demand does not deteriorate: **bid-to-cover 2.408 in-week versus
2.415 out** on the 2018+ sample, and the two $70B-era in-week sales covered **2.43** (2024-11-26,
relative −1.5) and **2.41** (2025-11-25, relative −3.0), both above the era median of **2.360** and

[2Y sibling](treasury-2y-note-2026-11-23.md) reached on its own data today — worth stating because
two independent negatives are how a folk belief actually dies.

**7. The crowded session does not dilute the effect — SUPPORTED, and it overturns a flagged
confound.** This is where 11-24 differs most from its 11-23 sibling. The schedule fetch puts **four
Treasury sales on 2026-11-24**: this 5-Year note, a **2-Year FRN reopening** (`2-Year FRN R`), a
6-Week bill and a 52-Week bill, all announced 11-19. 11-23 by contrast carries only the 2Y note and
the 13/26-week bills. The 10-27 sibling flagged, on n=5, that the 2Y's effect looked *weaker* when
more supply landed the same day. At the 5Y the sign is the other way: 2023+ auctions sharing their
session with a 2Y FRN print **−1.88bp** (n=28) against **−1.33bp** when the 5Y sells alone (n=15). So
the FRN's presence on 11-24 is not a reason to shade the expectation down, and the sibling's
dilution hint does not generalize.

**8. Far from FOMC is the stronger case, and 11-24 is far — SUPPORTED.** The next meeting is

**−1.10bp, t = −0.99**, mean |move| 3.80bp.
