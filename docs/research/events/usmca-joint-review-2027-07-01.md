### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The trigger is real, and primary-sourced — SUPPORTED.** USTR's own release: *"The United States
   did not agree to renew the USMCA in its current form. As a result, the USMCA is not renewed."*
   Canada and Mexico each confirmed they wished to extend; the US did not. Art. 34.7.4 therefore
   applies. **The agreement remains fully in force** — preferential tariffs, rules of origin,
   investment protections and dispute settlement all continue — and extension stays available at any
   time by written confirmation of the three heads of government. A joint review is a scheduled
   check-in, not an expiry.

2. **THE DATE RATIONALE IS WRONG — REFUTED, and this is the finding that most changes what the entry
   means.** The proposal that filed this event argued *"the YEAR is solid, the MONTH is well supported
   by the anniversary rule, and the DAY is derived."* The treaty text refutes the middle clause. Art.
   **34.7.2**: *"**On the sixth anniversary** of the entry into force of this Agreement, the Commission
   shall meet to conduct a 'joint review'…"* — anniversary-fixed, and that is what makes 2026-07-01
   certain. Art. **34.7.4**: *"If, as part of a six-year review, a Party does not confirm its wish to
   extend… the Commission shall meet to conduct a joint review **every year** for the remainder of the
   term of this Agreement."* **No anniversary. No month. No day.** The drafters used anniversary
   language once and declined to reuse it. So the treaty fixes the **cadence**, and the Free Trade
   Commission fixes the **date** — nothing constrains it to July. What survives is that a review must
   happen within a year of the last one, which makes **2027-07-01 an outer bound**, and the year is
   independently corroborated (*"the Agreement will instead shift to annual reviews starting in 2027"*).
   Carrying it as a deadline rather than a schedule is strictly more conservative, which is the only
   direction an `estimate` is allowed to move.

3. **There IS a nearer, statutory, observable date — and it is 25 days away, not 295. SUPPORTED on
   the statute's text, with its one inference stated.** **19 U.S.C. § 4611(b)(1)**: *"At least **270
   days** before a joint review commences, the Trade Representative shall publish in the Federal
   Register a notice regarding the joint review and shall… provide opportunity for the presentation
   of views… including a public hearing."* **§ 4611(e)(1)** defines *"joint review"* as *"a review
   conducted under the process provided for in article 34.7 of the USMCA relating to extension of the
   term"* — which plainly covers the annual reviews, and **§ 4611(c)(1)** itself calls them *"any
   subsequent annual joint review meeting conducted as required under article 34.7."* The textual
   contrast is the load-bearing part: **(b)(2)** limits its 180-day report to *"a **6-year** joint
   review,"* while **(b)(1)** says only *"a joint review."* Congress distinguished them in adjacent
   subsections, so the 270-day notice reads onto the annual cycle. **The inference stated honestly:**
   USTR has never applied (b)(1) to an annual review, because none has ever occurred. **The 2026
   cycle is the template**, and it is documented: FR **2025-18010**, published **2025-09-17** — *"USTR
   is commencing a public consultation process in advance of the joint review… on July 1, 2026"* —
   287 days out, 17 days inside the statutory floor; comments closed **2025-11-03**; the hearing,
   rescheduled by FR 2025-19816, ran **2025-12-03 to 12-05** at the USITC. Mapped onto a 2027-07-01
   review, the notice is due by **2026-10-04**.

4. **No such notice exists yet — SUPPORTED, verified mechanically rather than by search.** The
   Federal Register API, filtered to the USTR agency for all publication dates in 2026, returns six
   documents: two Section 301 notices (2026-06-05, 2026-07-20, 2026-07-28), a rapid-response labor
   roster invitation (2026-03-26) and two procurement-threshold notices (2026-01-02, 2026-02-03).
   **Nothing about a joint review.** The most recent USMCA-review document of any kind is the
   2025-11-07 hearing-date change. This is a **negative** finding and it is the useful kind: it makes
   the next 25 days a genuine test rather than a wait.

5. **The market channel is nil, and it is measured on the hardest possible case — SUPPORTED.** The
   2026-07-01 review is not a weak test of this claim, it is the strongest one available: the first
   joint review in the agreement's history, and the one at which the United States publicly refused
   to renew the terms governing roughly **US$1.8T** of annual North American trade. Every reading
   below is a fresh FRED pull this session:

### Honest limits

**The § 4611(b)(1) reading is an inference, not a practice.** No annual joint review has ever
occurred, so USTR has never had to decide whether the 270-day notice applies to one. The textual
argument in leg 3 is strong — the (b)(1)/(b)(2) contrast is deliberate drafting — but it is an
argument about statutory text, not an observation of agency behaviour, and **CRS R48787, the source
most likely to settle it, 403'd on both congress.gov and crsreports.congress.gov.** Leg 3 is graded
as supported-on-text and the forward test exists precisely because it might be wrong.
**Absence of a Federal Register notice is not proof of USTR's position** — a notice could be at OFR
awaiting publication as this is written; the query is a snapshot dated 2026-09-09.
**No equity series was pulled at all.** Yahoo 429'd on every attempt, so the autos / rails / EWW /
EWC basket that would have made leg 6 a measurement instead of an argument was never fetched; leg 6
rests on what USMCA covers, not on what those names did. **No Mexican or Canadian domestic data was
pulled either** — the FX series are US-published crosses. **The 2027 review date remains unpublished
by anyone**, and this document does not claim otherwise: it argues the date is *weaker* than filed,
which is the safe direction, not that a better one exists. **`whitecase.com` 403'd** and its analysis
— the proposal's principal citation — reaches this document only secondhand.

**One adjacency deliberately NOT proposed.** The § 4611(c)(1) 70-day report date (~**2027-04-22**) and
the § 4611(b)(1) notice deadline (**2026-10-04**) are both derived *from a date that is itself
derived*. Proposing either would stack a derivation on a derivation — the same refusal the proposing

where a derived observable belongs. The agreement's **2036-07-01** term expiry *is* treaty-fixed and
primary-sourced (art. 34.7.1) and is still not proposed: it is contingent on ten years of
non-extension, it sits eight years past this calendar's furthest entry (2028-11-10), and it would
generate a pulse every 21 days for a decade to inform no decision.
