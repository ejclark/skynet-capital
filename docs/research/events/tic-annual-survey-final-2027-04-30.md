## Initial research

### The question

The proposal that created this event called it *"the OTHER HALF of `tic-annual-survey-prelim-2027-02-26`
… the half that carries the settled numbers rather than the preliminary ones."* The sibling ledger then
handed this event a specific job: *"the prelim-to-final revision is not measured here … the
`tic-annual-survey-final-2027-04-30` entry is where that measurement belongs."* So: what does the April
final actually add over the February preliminary, and is the addition worth a calendar slot?

### One-line verdict

The April final adds **permanence, not information** — it is the survey's only archived artefact and its
numbers are never restated, while the headline it prints is already predictable to **$10bn** from data
published eight months earlier; the revision this session was sent to measure **cannot be measured**,
because Treasury serves no preliminary artefact to measure against.

### Leg 1 — the date · **SUPPORTED**, promoted `estimate` → `confirmed`, with a slip prior the sibling could not see

Treasury's 2027 table puts April's row at **15** in the *Monthly* column and **"30 - final"** in the
column headed *Annual: Foreign portfolio holdings of U.S. securities at end-June of previous year*. The
page's note block states the rule verbatim: *"Preliminary data for the survey are released on the last
business day of February; the final report is released on the last business day of April,"* under
*"All data releases occur at 4 p.m. Washington, D.C. time."*

The mechanical corroboration is trivially clean in this month and worth one sentence: **April carries no
federal holiday at all** under 5 U.S.C. 6103, so the annual rule needs no holiday handling here. The last
business day of April 2027 is **Friday 2027-04-30**, offset **+0** against the published cell. The
sibling verified the same eight published 2026-27 annual dates against the rule and scored **8/8**; that
check is not repeated.

**What is new is a different test, on a source no sibling read.** The schedule page says what Treasury
*intends*. Treasury's **TIC Press Releases by topic** index says what Treasury actually *did* — it lists
every realized release for this survey back to 2004. Restricting to the modern Feb/Apr regime (the
schedule ratcheted forward twice before settling: 2005 and 2006 finals published in **June**, the 2007
final in **May** with a March preliminary, February/April from 2008 on):

| Realized final | Computed last business day of April | Offset |
|---|---|---|
| 2008-04-30 … 2015-04-30 (8 years) | same | **+0** each |
| **2016-05-31** | **2016-04-29** | **+32 days** |
| 2017-04-28 … 2026-04-30 (10 years) | same | **+0** each |

**18 of 19.** The one break is the *Report on Foreign Portfolio Holdings of U.S. Securities at End-June
2015*, published **2016-05-31** against a computed 2016-04-29 — and Treasury's own SHL reports webpage
independently corroborates it (*"There was a press release dated 05/31/2016"*). The **preliminary** over
the same 19 years is **19 for 19**, including that year (2016-02-29, on schedule). So the slip was in the
final only, and it is not isolated: the companion claims survey slipped the same way in the same window,
its End-Year 2014 report publishing **2016-01-29** against a computed 2015-10-30.

Promotion to `confirmed` follows the sibling's standard — one named Treasury primary plus independent
mechanical corroboration — and the `estimate` ground the proposal cited (this lane's **no-self-confirm**
limit on an event discovered in-sweep) is cleared by this session re-fetching the page itself. But the
honest reading of leg 1 is **not** the sibling's: the preliminary's date is the safer of the two, and the
base rate attached to this one is **1 in 19 (5.3%)**, not zero. As on every sibling, `confirmed`
describes the published schedule and nothing else; the same page carries the standing notice that a date
*"will be revised after offices reopen"* if Washington D.C. offices are closed.

### Leg 2 — what the final actually is · **SUPPORTED**, and it inverts the proposal's framing

The proposal filed this event as the half carrying *"the settled numbers rather than the preliminary
ones."* True, and understated. Three independent primaries say the April final is the survey's **only
durable artefact**:

1. **Treasury's SHL reports webpage** is the canonical document index for this survey — it carries one
   report PDF and one press-release line per survey year back to 2004. Reading every such line: *"There
   is a press release dated 04/30/2026 at 4PM"*, *"04/30/2025"*, *"04/30/2024"*, *"04/28/2023"*,

**Why this matters beyond taxonomy, and it is a cross-ledger consequence.** The sibling registered
`FT-tic-annual-survey-prelim-2027-02-26-3`, a **$40.3tn–$42.0tn** window on the February headline, with a

### Leg 3 — does the final restate the survey's own history? · **REFUTED**, decisively

This is the leg that decides what the release is worth, and it is the one with the most data behind it.
Each survey report contains a summary series comparing the current year with previous surveys. Those
series were extracted from the raw PDF streams of five reports published years apart and compared,
line for line, against `shlhistdat.csv` (vintage May 2025):

| Report (published) | Series it publishes | Years covered | Agreement with the current panel |
|---|---|---|---|
| `shla2010r` (April 2011) | long-term total, equity | 2003-2010 | **exact, every year** |
| `shla2011r` (April 2012) | short-term total | 2004-2011 | **exact, every year** |
| `shl2015r` (April 2016) | all-securities total | 2008-2011 | **exact, every year** |
| `shla2017r` (April 2018) | all-securities total | 2010-2013 | **exact, every year** |
| `shl2019r` (April 2020) | all-securities total | 2012-2019 | **exact, every year** |
| `shl2025r` (April 2026), Table 1 | long-term, "Measured, June2024" | 2024 | **29.57 vs 29.566tn** |

Worked example, because "exact" deserves to be checkable: `shl2019r` publishes **13,261 / 14,410 /
16,417 / 17,134 / 17,139 / 18,412 / 19,400 / 20,5…** for June 2012 through June 2019. The current panel
reads **13,261,270 / 14,410,490 / 16,416,776 / 17,134,436 / 17,139,235 / 18,411,517 / 19,400,376 /
20,534,384** ($mn). Six years of subsequent surveys moved **none** of them.

**Not one published total moves.** Across vintages spanning **April 2011 to April 2026**, every
all-securities, long-term, equity and short-term total for every overlapping June equals the current
panel to the last million.

**Three things that do move, all of them documented, none of them the total:**

- **Country allocation.** Treasury's SHL page, verbatim: *"The 2012 data for Hong Kong and Taiwan have
  been revised since publication of this 2012 report. The revision is a shift of $83 billion in holdings
  of long-term agency securities from Hong Kong to Taiwan. This change affects the rows for Taiwan and
  Hong Kong in table 8, and in appendix tables A1, A2, A5, A7, and A9."* A reallocation between rows —
  it nets to zero at the total, which is exactly why the totals above are untouched.
- **Shares of outstanding.** `shl2024r` reports foreign investors owning *"21 percent"* of U.S.
  securities at end-June 2024; `shl2025r` restates the same year as **20.5 percent**. The sibling read
  this as *"the survey revises its own history."* It does — in the **denominator** (U.S. securities
  outstanding, which is not a survey product), not the numerator, which this leg shows is fixed. A half
  point on a $30.9tn base is ~$150bn of *denominator*, not of holdings.
- **Within-report corrections, fast and small.** The 2009 benchmark report's table 2 was revised
  **2010-05-13**, thirteen days after publication.

**One boundary, stated because it is easy to over-read.** This leg concerns the **survey's** own panel.
Whether the survey feeds the **monthly** TIC series is a separate question governed by Treasury's notes
(c) and (d), and the sibling's leg 2 left it refuted for February and undocumented for March. Nothing
here settles that; the two panels are different objects.

### Leg 4 — what the print will say · **SUPPORTED**, by a tighter method that independently reproduces the sibling

The sibling forecast the February headline at **$41.1tn** using one blunt ratio: survey all-securities ÷
SLT long-term = **1.0484, sd 0.0080 (n=6)**. That ratio bundles two unrelated things — a *measurement*
gap between two surveys of the same population, and the survey's *short-term* securities, which the SLT
does not cover at all. Treasury publishes the first of those directly, and no sibling used it.

**`shl2025r` Table 3** — *"Foreign Holdings of U.S. Long-Term Securities as Measured by the Annual TIC
Liabilities Survey (SHL) and the Monthly TIC SLT"* — reports, for June 2025: SHL total long-term
**$33,700bn** against SLT **$33,556bn**, a gap of **$144bn (+0.43%)**. So the two series are the same
number to within half a percent once the definitions are aligned.

Decomposing across every June both files cover:

| End-June | SHL all-sec | SHL long-term | SLT long-term | SHL/SLT | SHL short-term | ST/LT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2020 | $21.954tn | $20.726tn | $20.699tn | 1.0013 | $1.228tn | 0.0592 |
| 2021 | $27.189tn | $26.112tn | $26.175tn | 0.9976 | $1.076tn | 0.0412 |
| 2022 | $24.893tn | $23.768tn | $23.874tn | 0.9956 | $1.124tn | 0.0473 |
| 2023 | $26.872tn | $25.686tn | $25.528tn | 1.0062 | $1.186tn | 0.0462 |
| 2024 | $30.881tn | $29.566tn | $29.566tn | 1.0000 | $1.314tn | 0.0445 |
| 2025 | $35.300tn | $33.700tn | $33.556tn | 1.0037 | $1.600tn | 0.0475 |

SHL/SLT long-term: mean **1.0007**, sd **0.0036**. Short-term as a share of long-term: mean **0.0476**,
sd **0.0056**. Published June-2026 SLT long-term is **$39.190tn**:

```
long-term   = 39.190 × 1.0007 = $39.22tn
short-term  = 39.22  × 0.0476 = $1.87tn
headline    =                   $41.09tn      2σ band $40.4tn – $41.8tn
sibling's blunt ratio:          $41.09tn      (39.190 × 1.0484)
```

**The two methods agree to $10bn.** That is a genuine result rather than a coincidence: the blunt ratio
works *because* a near-unity measurement gap and a stable short-term share are each stable on their own,
and separating them means the two can now fail independently and be diagnosed separately. It also
removes the sibling's stated pre-benchmark bias concern from the larger of the two components — the
SHL/SLT gap has no visible trend and straddles 1.0000 in both directions.

**And it is the finding that sets this event's altitude.** The survey's headline is predictable to
**$10bn on $41tn** from a file Treasury published in **August 2026**. Neither the February preliminary
nor this April final carries news in the headline. What they carry is the country and sector detail —
and, per leg 3, only the April one carries it permanently.

**What could not be measured, and is recorded rather than worked around.** The prelim→final revision —
the measurement the sibling explicitly assigned to this event — requires a preliminary series to
difference against, and leg 2 established that Treasury serves none. No proxy is substituted.
`probe-ref.blocked` stays empty because nothing *failed*: the artefact is not published, which is a
different finding from a blocked fetch. The forward test registered in its place (**FT-…-3**) is bounded
by argument rather than by a fitted distribution, and its confidence is stated as medium for that reason.
The soft bound available is the survey's own concentration statistic (`shl2025r` Table 5): **210**
U.S.-resident reporters, with the **10 largest custodians reporting 85.1%** of total market value across
**4.1 million** records. A February-to-April revision large enough to move the headline would have to
come from that handful of reporters.

**The structural finding is that this release's next session is a scheduled Treasury supply event.**
Treasury's quarterly refunding is the first Wednesday of February, May, August and November, with the
marketable-borrowing estimates at 15:00 ET on the Monday of that week — the rule this calendar already
carries on `treasury-borrowing-estimates-2026-11-02` and `treasury-refunding-2026-11-04`, each promoted
from Treasury's own *"the next release is scheduled for"* lines. Cross-referencing every modern final
against that cycle:

- **6 of 19** — the final fell on a **Friday**, so its next session *is* the borrowing-estimates Monday:
  2010, 2011, 2017, 2021, 2022, 2023.
- **3 of 19** — the next session *is* the refunding Wednesday itself: 2013, 2019, 2024.
- **2 of 19** — the release day itself *is* the estimates Monday: 2012, 2018.
- **11 of 19 in total** land on or one session from a scheduled refunding-cycle release.

**2027-04-30 is a Friday.** So the next session is Monday **2027-05-03**, the borrowing-estimates
release, with the refunding announcement Wednesday **2027-05-05**. Both are proposed as calendar entries
in this PR. The six Friday finals averaged **5.83bp** next-session against **3.31bp** for the other
thirteen (t=+1.07, **p=0.28**) — **not significant on n=6**, and stated as not significant; the point is
not that the refunding moves the tape on those days but that **no move on 2027-05-03 can be attributed
to this survey**, which is a constraint on what FT-2 can ever prove.

### Honest limits

- **The measurement this event was created to make could not be made.** No preliminary artefact exists
  to difference against. FT-…-3 is bounded by argument and a concentration statistic, not by a fitted
  distribution, and its confidence says so.
- **Leg 3 is exact but not exhaustive.** Five report vintages were compared, not nineteen; the extracted
  series are the summary tables each report happens to publish, not every table in every report. A
  restatement confined to a table no sampled report carried would not have been seen.
- **n=22 on the tape** is small in absolute terms even though it is the entire published history of this
  release. The null is stated as a null, not as evidence of equality. The n=6 Friday subset is reported
  because it is 2027's exact configuration, and is explicitly not leaned on.
- **The 5.3% slip prior rests on one observation.** One miss in nineteen is a base rate with an enormous
  confidence interval; what it establishes is that the rate is not zero, which the schedule-page check
  alone would have implied.
- **`shlhistdat.csv` is 16 months stale.** Every leg-3 and leg-4 comparison uses it as the current
  vintage. If Treasury refreshes it with a June-2025 column that restates earlier years, leg 3's
  "exact" becomes a claim about the May-2025 vintage specifically. That is the kill switch, and it is
  registered.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stand aside, permanently, on the trade — and treat this event as the survey's system of record rather
than as a release.** The date is **`confirmed`** (Treasury primary, the last-business-day rule at offset
+0), so the schedule statement is not hedged; it is qualified instead by a measured **5.3%** historical
slip rate specific to this half of the pair. Every other statement here concerns mechanism and
measurement, and none is trading-adjacent in the sense the honesty rules police.

Two behavioural instructions this ledger issues to its successors. First, inherited and now measured
twice: **do not test a last-business-day release against all sessions** — month-end alone clears p=0.001.
Second, new: **do not attribute anything on the session after this release to this release**, because in
11 of 19 modern years that session is a scheduled Treasury supply event, and in 2027 it is.

**Kill switches:**

1. **The date.** No final report published on **2027-04-30**. Unlike the sibling's, this kill has a
   measured prior: **1 in 19** since 2008, the 2016 report slipping 32 days to 05-31. Kills leg 1 and
   makes FT-2, FT-3 and FT-4 unscoreable.
2. **The permanence claim.** A refreshed `shlhistdat.csv` — or any report published after this ledger —
   restating a previously published annual **total** (all-securities, long-term, equity or short-term)
   for any June from 2003 to 2025 by more than **$1bn**. That would refute leg 3 outright and with it
   the reason this event is called a system of record.
3. **The tape null.** |Δ10Y| on **2027-05-03** above **16bp** (~3σ of the n=18 modern distribution, sd
   3.71bp; observed max 15bp on 2023-05-01). Registered as the kill with attribution disclaimed in
   advance: that session is Treasury's borrowing-estimates release, so a large move is more likely to be
   supply than survey.
4. **The prelim/final identity.** The **2027-04-30** headline differing from the **2027-02-26**
   preliminary by more than **±2%**. That would make the two releases two different estimates rather than
   one estimate settled, and would mean the calendar is right to carry both as separate events for a
   reason nobody has yet stated.
5. **The forecast.** A final headline outside **$40.4tn–$41.8tn**. Below says the SHL↔SLT reconciliation
   is less stable than Treasury's own Table 3 implies; above says the short-term share broke its
   six-year range.
6. **The artefact claim.** Treasury publishing an archived preliminary report at a stable path, or
   adding a February press-release line to the SHL reports index. That would refute leg 2 and restore the
   prelim→final measurement this session could not make.
