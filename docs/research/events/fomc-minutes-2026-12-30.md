## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The minutes of the December 8–9 FOMC — the year's final meeting, and an SEP/dot-plot meeting —
publish at 14:00 ET on **2026-12-30**, on the Wednesday of the dead week between Christmas and New
Year. [`fomc-minutes-2026-11-18`](fomc-minutes-2026-11-18.md) already measured that the minutes
channel is weak under this chair. So the question for *this* release is narrower and different:
**does anything about this specific slot — an SEP meeting's minutes, published into the thinnest tape
of the year, as the last Fed document of 2026 — change that answer?**

**One-line verdict:** no, and the reason is unusually clean — the Fed ran this exact experiment on
**2025-12-30** (minutes of the Dec 9–10, 2025 SEP meeting, same dead-week Wednesday) and the S&P
closed **−0.14%** with the minutes as the day's headline. Thin liquidity is the condition most likely
to *amplify* a release, so a null in that venue is the strongest available refusal, not the weakest.
Date flipped `estimate` → **`confirmed`** on two FED primaries. No directional call.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is FED-primary twice over — flipped `estimate` → `confirmed`.** SUPPORTED. Two
   independent federalreserve.gov pages, both fetched today. The **December 2026 calendar** lists
   **"FOMC Minutes" at 2:00 p.m. on December 30** (alongside the Dec 8–9 meeting and the 12-09 2:30
   p.m. press conference). The **FOMC calendars** page states the convention verbatim — *"The minutes
   of regularly scheduled meetings are released three weeks after the date of the policy decision"* —
   and its 2026 rows show it holding all year: Jan 27–28 → **Feb 18**, Mar 17–18 → **Apr 8**, Apr
   28–29 → **May 20**, Jun 16–17 → **Jul 8**, Jul 28–29 → **Aug 19**. Dec 9 + 21 days is **Wednesday
   2026-12-30**. Published row and published rule agree, so the derivation is the source's. `FED:`
   prefix, **confirmed**.

2. **The "Last Update" stamp that held this entry at `estimate` is not a freshness signal — measured,
   and it corrects a sibling ledger's argument.** SUPPORTED, decisively. The calendar entry's standing
   note flagged that the December page's own stamp reads **"Last Update: June 24, 2025"**. Tested
   against three sibling pages fetched today: **September 2026** carries the *same* June-2025 stamp
   yet lists a **Reuters NEXT Newsmaker interview** (Waller, 09-01) and a Second Chance Lending Forum
   appearance (Barr, 09-03); **August 2026** carries the same stamp and names **"Chairman Kevin
   Warsh"** at Jackson Hole — *a title he did not hold on 2025-06-24*. **November 2026** meanwhile
   stamps "November 03, 2025". Conclusion: the stamp tracks the page skeleton, not its contents, and
   is worthless in **both** directions. That closes the objection to leg 1 — but it also means the
   *absence* of speeches on a far-future page is a **lead-time artifact**, not a schedule. December
   2026 lists no speeches; so does November; September (D-1) lists two. So
   [`fomc-minutes-2026-11-18`](fomc-minutes-2026-11-18.md)'s leg 4 — *"November's Fed calendar is
   empty of voices"* as a scarcity argument — is very likely reading a publication lag as silence.
   Its own caveat anticipated this; this session measured it. **This document therefore does not use
   "no scheduled speeches" as evidence for anything.**

3. **The channel is weak, and this slot has an identical precedent rather than an analogous one.**
   SUPPORTED — the headline finding. Three measured releases:
   - **2025-12-30** — minutes of the **Dec 9–10, 2025** meeting (also an SEP meeting), released 14:00
     ET on the dead-week Wednesday. Content was genuinely newsworthy: a *"tight split"* over that
     meeting's quarter-point cut, with *"a few of those who supported lowering the policy rate at this
     meeting"* saying the decision *"was finely balanced or that they could have supported keeping the
     target range unchanged"*, and some participants pointing to holding *"unchanged for some time"*.
     Wraps led with it ("Dow, S&P 500, Nasdaq waver as Fed minutes show division over December rate
     cut"). S&P **−0.14%** to **6,896.24**; Nasdaq −0.24% to 23,419.08; Dow −0.2% to 48,367.06.
   - **2026-07-08** (June minutes, first Warsh meeting): S&P **−0.28%** to 7,482.71; reaction to the
     release reported as minimal.
   - **2026-08-19** (July minutes, explicitly hawkish — *"policy tightening would likely be necessary
     if inflation did not decline"*, 9-3 with three dissents **to hike**): S&P **+0.21%** to 7,707.98;
     the session's actual driver was Treasury doubling its debt buybacks.

   **n=3, every observation inside ±0.3%, across two chairs, two policy directions (a cut and a
   hawkish hold), and both liquidity regimes.** Against that, one chair speech — Warsh at Jackson
   Hole, **2026-08-28** — moved September hike odds **~35–36% → ~58–60%** in a session. *Confounds,
   named:* the 08-19 buyback headline masks; 2025-12-30 sat in a four-session losing streak into
   year-end, so the −0.14% is not a clean isolate either; and the 2025 observation is a **different
   chair and a cutting Committee**, which is why it is offered as a *slot* precedent, not a regime one.

4. **The thin tape cuts against tradability, not for it.** SUPPORTED. The Christmas–New Year week runs
   at **45–70% of normal equity volume** (Russell Investments, "The holiday trading effect", Nov 2025;
   late-December global futures and options volumes average **~40% below normal**). NYSE's 2026
   calendar: **Dec 24 closes 1:00 p.m. ET**, **Dec 25 closed**, then four full sessions **Dec 28–31**,
   with **Jan 1, 2027 closed** — so 12-30 is the third of four dead-week sessions and sits inside the
   Santa-Claus-rally window (last five sessions of the year plus the first two of the new one). The
   naive read is that thin books amplify surprises. Take that seriously and it *strengthens* the
   refusal: **2025-12-30 was the most amplification-friendly venue a minutes release ever gets, and it
   produced −0.14%.** The operative consequence is the reverse of an edge — wider spreads and thinner
   options books make any expression of a view more expensive exactly when the catalyst is weakest.

5. **The SEP already answered the forward question three weeks earlier — the marginal content is
   thinner than 11-18's.** SUPPORTED, with a limit. December meetings carry the Summary of Economic
   Projections and the dot plot (FOMC calendars page: SEP meetings are Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec), plus a 2:30
   p.m. press conference on 12-09. By the time the minutes land, the market has had the *quantified
   distribution of participants' rate paths* for 21 days. What the minutes uniquely add is the **vote
   split, the dissent count and the dissent language**, and any balance-sheet or framework discussion
   the SEP does not encode. That is a real but narrow residual — and it is narrower than for a non-SEP
   meeting's minutes. *Limit:* I have no cross-sectional measurement of SEP-meeting versus
   non-SEP-meeting minutes reactions, so this is an argument from what the documents contain, not a
   measured effect.

6. **Staleness here is the worst on this calendar — the Committee voted blind to the November CPI, and
   four more inflation reads land before publication.** SUPPORTED. `cpi-2026-12-10` prints **the
   morning after** the decision, so the Dec 8–9 vote *and the dots* were set without it (the framing
   [`fomc-blackout-start-2026-11-28`](fomc-blackout-start-2026-11-28.md) already established). Between
   the vote and 14:00 ET on 12-30 the tape also absorbs **PPI 12-15** (confirmed), **import/export
   prices 12-17** (est), **December triple witching 12-18** (confirmed) and **November PCE ~12-23**
   (est — see leg 9). So the minutes describe a Committee that had seen **none** of the quarter's
   closing inflation data. That makes *"did they flag the missing CPI, and how"* the single most
   interesting line in the document — and simultaneously guarantees it is old news by publication.

7. **Scarcity is real: the last Committee-authored document of 2026, inside a 48-day policy vacuum.**
   SUPPORTED. The FOMC calendars page (fetched today) puts the next meeting at **2027-01-26/27** — 48
   days after the 12-09 decision. These minutes are the only Committee-authored document in that gap;
   the next Beige Book would fall ~mid-January 2027 by the two-weeks-before-a-meeting convention
   (**convention-derived, not fetched — the 2027 January calendar page 404s today**), and it is staff
   anecdote, not Committee reasoning. Note what this leg deliberately does **not** claim, per leg 2:
   nothing about whether officials will speak. The blackout ends **2026-12-10**, and speeches get
   scheduled weeks out, so a December–January speaking calendar almost certainly exists and is simply
   not published yet. **Scarcity of Committee documents, not silence.**

8. **The format variable peaks exactly here — and it is the one genuinely open question.** MIXED.
   Warsh's five task forces review the Fed's inflation framework, balance sheet, data, employment/
   productivity, and **communications**; press reporting on the communications task force names its
   scope explicitly as *"the Fed's various communications tools, such as the quarterly summary of
   economic projections, the minutes and transcripts from FOMC meetings and even the post-meeting press
   conference"*, with **initial findings in the fall and conclusions by year-end** (press-reported,
   checked 2026-08-31; no Board primary fetched). **2026-12-30 is the last minutes of 2026, published
   one day before that deadline.** Outside commentary runs both ways — one expert expects *"a much more
   limited communications framework"* centred on the minutes, while Brookings' recommendations *to* the
   task force argue the opposite direction for the SEP (*"link forecasts to rate paths, name the
   participants, and add scenarios"*, on the reasoning that *"a quieter FOMC would be less accountable,
   not more accurate"*). Those are inputs, not the Fed's plan. I cannot pick between them; it is
   registered as **FT-32** rather than argued.

9. **Corridor and exposure — light, and one calendar gap found.** SUPPORTED. `symbols: []`; the
   transmission read carries from [`fomc-2026-10-28.md`](fomc-2026-10-28.md) (a hawkish surprise under

   [`georgia-psc-data-center-cost-shift-2026-12-31`](georgia-psc-data-center-cost-shift-2026-12-31.md)

### Honest limits

The base rate is **n=3**: one identical-slot observation under a **different chair** with a Committee
that was **cutting**, and two same-chair observations in ordinary liquidity. Each has a confound (leg
3). Index closes and the minutes quotations are from dated press wraps, not from a fetched Fed
transcript. The volume figures are one research house's estimate, cited as a range. Leg 5 argues from
document contents with **no measured SEP-vs-non-SEP effect**. Leg 7's Beige Book date is derived from
convention, not fetched — the 2027 January calendar page does not exist yet. Leg 8 rests entirely on
press reporting; no Board primary on the task forces was fetched this session. The `pce-2026-12-23`
proposal is `estimate`-grade: today's fetch of bea.gov/news/schedule returned a **truncated** Dec 23

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date confirmed):** **read it, do not trade it.** Three measured minutes releases moved the
S&P **−0.14%** (2025-12-30, the identical dead-week slot), **−0.28%** (2026-07-08) and **+0.21%**
(2026-08-19), across two chairs and both policy directions; the dead-week tape that will host this
release is the venue most likely to amplify a surprise, and it already failed to in 2025. The SEP and
the press conference will have published 21 days earlier, so the marginal content is the vote split,
the dissent language, and any acknowledgement that the Committee voted before `cpi-2026-12-10`. What
earns it a calendar slot is **scarcity, not power**: the last Committee-authored document of 2026,
inside a 48-day gap to the **2027-01-26/27** FOMC. No directional call, no size; the positioning date
in that window is **opex 2026-12-18** (confirmed). The one open question is **format**, not direction.

**Kill switches:**

- **Date kill:** federalreserve.gov publishing a minutes date other than **2026-12-30** for the Dec
  8–9 meeting, or that meeting's dates moving. Breaks leg 1 and returns this entry to `estimate`.
  Score by **2026-12-30**.
- **Channel kill:** the **2026-12-30** release moving January-2027 hike/cut odds **≥10pp** on the day,
  or an S&P 500 close-to-close move **≥1.5%** attributable to it. Legs 3 and 4 die together and
  minutes become a repricing channel — with the thin-tape amplification argument as the mechanism.
  Score by **2026-12-31**.
- **Format kill:** the Board announcing a change to minutes content, structure or publication
  convention that applies to this release. Leg 8 resolves and the informational claim needs
  re-argument, not a patch. Registered as **FT-32**, score by **2026-12-31**.
- **Redundancy kill:** a **12-09** SEP that removes the split — a unanimous vote with a tight dot
  cluster. Leg 5's residual (vote split, dissent language) empties out and this reverts to a routine
  technical worth no calendar slot at all.
- **Regime kill:** a hike delivered on **2026-09-16** or **2026-10-28**. The question these minutes
  answer changes; the informational read gets re-derived at the next pulse (the tradability call does
  not move — legs 3 and 4 apply with more force after a hike has been interpreted for three weeks).
- **Staleness kill:** `cpi-2026-12-10` moving **before** 12-09. Leg 6's central asymmetry — a
  Committee voting blind to the November CPI — disappears, and the most interesting line in the
  document goes with it.

**Forward test registered:** **FT-32** in [`forward-tests.md`](../forward-tests.md) — the format kill
