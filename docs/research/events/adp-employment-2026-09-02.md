## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** what is the outlook for the August 2026 ADP National Employment Report,
what does the recent trend (July's print, the weekly NER Pulse series) say, how does the market
actually treat this release given its known weak correlation to the BLS print two days later, and
which of our tracked names (NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL AMZN) carry meaningful
sensitivity to it — around 2026-09-02?

**One-line verdict:** ADP's August print is a two-day-early mood-setter for the far more
consequential 9/4 BLS jobs report, not an independent trading signal — its own predictive power for
BLS is weak (high correlation on a rolling multi-year basis, frequent monthly divergence, and a
July gap of +44k ADP vs -23k BLS in the same cycle) — so the house answer is guard-shaped: read it
for tone, do not act on it alone, and let the 9/4 print (already governed by
[`jobs-2026-09-04.md`](jobs-2026-09-04.md)) carry the actual rate-path weight.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md)
(macro-print mode: no price instruments). Primary/near-primary sources: mediacenter.adp.com (the
July release and the weekly NER Pulse preliminary-estimate releases), fred.stlouisfed.org's release
calendar (date/time), cmegroup.com/openmarkets and pewresearch.org (ADP-vs-BLS methodology and
correlation), investing.com (Wolfe Research's BLS-print preview, cited here only for the
two-days-later context it sets). Press (CNBC, Quartz, TechTimes) used for reaction narration only,
each claim dated.

**Conviction legs, tested:**

1. **The date and time are estimate-labeled, not yet primary-confirmed — SUPPORTED (as an
   estimate).** ADP's own release cadence is the Wednesday two sessions before the BLS jobs Friday;
   September 2026's jobs Friday is confirmed for 9/4, and 9/2 (Wednesday) matches that cadence
   exactly (fred.stlouisfed.org release calendar rid=194; mediacenter.adp.com's own July 2026
   release masthead, checked 2026-08-27). No ADP press release yet names 9/2 as the August release
   date directly (ADP typically confirms the exact date only days ahead) — the calendar entry's
   `estimate` status is honest and correctly labeled; treat the date as high-confidence but not yet
   primary-sourced.

2. **July's ADP print missed and continued a cooling trend — SUPPORTED.** July private-sector
   employment rose **+44,000**, below a ~70–75k Dow Jones consensus and the smallest monthly gain
   since January, with annual pay growth for job-stayers flat at **4.4% y/y** and job-changers'
   wage growth accelerating to **7.0%** (mediacenter.adp.com, released 2026-08-05). Service
   industries carried all net job creation (+47k, led by education/health +36k and financial
   activities +10k); goods-producing sectors shed 3k, dragged by natural resources/mining (-6k).

3. **ADP is a weak predictor of the BLS print it precedes — SUPPORTED.** ADP and BLS private
   payrolls correlate strongly on a rolling multi-year basis (R² ~0.98 since 2010, per
   pewresearch.org) but diverge sharply month to month due to differing methodology — ADP counts
   workers on payroll even if unpaid in the reference period, BLS does not (cmegroup.com/openmarkets,
   2026). This cycle supplied a clean example: July ADP printed **+44k** while BLS's July print (the
   same reference month) came in at **-23k** (jobs-2026-09-04.md, sourced from BLS empsit + CNBC).
   ADP itself states its report is not meant "to forecast" the BLS number. The market still reacts
   to a large ADP surprise (dollar/bonds/equities move on a big beat or miss), but treats it as
   noisy, not as a reliable NFP predictor.

4. **The intra-month trend has ticked up, not down — SUPPORTED, but from a private, non-payroll
   proxy.** ADP's weekly NER Pulse (a four-week moving average of week-over-week employment change,
   published Tuesdays) rose across three successive readings: **8,250/week** for the four weeks
   ending 7/25, **9,500/week** ending 8/1, and **11,750/week** ending 8/8 (all mediacenter.adp.com
   preliminary-estimate releases) — roughly 47k/month annualized at the latest reading, a mild
   re-acceleration off July's monthly print. No NER Pulse reading newer than the week ending 8/8 was
   found as of this research (the series carries a two-week lag) — the most current available data
   point is now three weeks stale relative to today, an honest limit.

5. **No August-specific ADP consensus exists yet — MIXED (a gap, not a contradiction).** Search did
   not surface a dedicated Wall Street consensus number for the August *ADP* print specifically (as
   distinct from the BLS jobs-report consensus, which has already formed: Wolfe Research's own call
   is 65k with a ~55k street consensus, Capital Economics separately calls for 90k on the BLS print
   — both cited and governed in [`jobs-2026-09-04.md`](jobs-2026-09-04.md)). ADP-specific previews
   typically firm only in the day or two before release; flagged as this doc's central honest limit.

6. **Tracked-name sensitivity — ranked, with mechanism.** `symbols: []` — market-wide, and given
   ADP's weak standalone signal, sensitivity here is really "how much does this print move
   positioning into 9/4," not a direct trigger. All nine trade as long-duration assets on the rate
   path (the calendar's own note). Most sensitive to any resulting rate-path repricing: **CRWV**
   (debt-financed AI-infra build, longest financing-cost exposure), then the high-multiple semis
   **NVDA / AVGO / MRVL** (AVGO reports earnings 9/2, the same day — its own doc
   ([`avgo-2026-09-02-print.md`](avgo-2026-09-02-print.md)) governs that overlap). Middle: **MSFT /
   GOOG / META**. Least direct: **AAPL / AMZN** (rate-path only; their demand-side read comes from
   retail-sales/consumer prints, not ADP).

**What the conditions support.** Nothing directional — no macro playbook exists in the house set
(S1/S2/E1/S3/S4 + G1 are symbol/earnings-keyed). What travels is the *shape* of the jobs-2026-09-04

question the 9/4 BLS print actually resolves — no new rate-duration paper entries initiated off an
