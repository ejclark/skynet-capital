## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Stand aside on the release — read it the next morning for one number, the Gulf shut-in
figure.** Two things kill the trade before taste enters. First, **nobody can trade the window**: the
OMR publishes at 10:00 Paris = **04:00 ET**, five and a half hours before the US equity open, and
Yahoo's two-year hourly series for USO and XLE contains **zero bars** in it. Second, for the two
instruments that *are* open — CL=F and BZ=F — the window is measurably a non-event. Built this
session across **13 OMR releases** (Jan 2025 – Aug 2026, four of them date-verified against IEA's own
PDFs): **0 of 13** release hours exceeded the p95 of an ordinary same-hour bar on **either** crude
benchmark, Brent managed **0 of 13** even at p90 against 1.3 expected by pure chance, and the largest
move in 26 observations was **0.94%**. Natural gas — the placebo control — is *quieter* in the window
than usual (0.17% vs 0.23–0.26%), which is what an honest null looks like. What the report is
genuinely for is its **number**, and this session used the August edition to correct one this
calendar had been carrying uncheckably: the OPEC+ ledger's "IEA at ~10 mb/d of Gulf shutdowns," taken
from an Argus headline whose body never fetched, is **8.3 mb/d** in the IEA's actual August OMR —
high by ~1.7. But the third read does not settle the argument it was filed to settle. EIA's August
STEO puts July Mideast shut-ins at **~5.5 mb/d**; the IEA puts the same month at **8.3 mb/d** — a
**51% disagreement between two government-grade agencies on one month's number**, about as wide as
the Hormuz-transit spread (4.9 AIS vs 8–10 US-gov/Goldman) it was supposed to adjudicate. The event
is **estimate**-dated with `symbols: []`, so no date-keyed action is licensed regardless of any of

## Initial research

**The question.** What will the September 2026 IEA Oil Market Report say, does its release move
anything, and does any of it change how we sit in the tracked names (NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG
META AAPL AMZN)?

**One-line verdict:** a subscription forecast publication that lands 5.5 hours before the US open, in
a window measurably indistinguishable from an ordinary 4am hour across 13 releases — so: stand aside,
and read it the next morning for one contested number that this session found the calendar had
been quoting **too high**.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md)
(macro-print mode; the house instruments are equity/earnings-keyed and cover no commodity or
publication). **The primary was unreachable and that is stated everywhere it matters**: `iea.org`
returned HTTP 403 to both WebFetch and `curl` with a browser user-agent on 2026-09-06, on the events
page, the OMR data-product page and the August report page alike. What *is* reachable is IEA's own
asset host, `iea.blob.core.windows.net` (HTTP 200, verified this session) — its PDF filenames encode
release dates, which is how four calendar rows got primary corroboration. Report *contents* therefore
come from two independent trade-press reads of the same August edition (Reuters/Robert Harvey via
Investing.com, 2026-08-12; Oil & Gas Journal, 2026-08-12), labelled secondary throughout. **The base
rate in leg 2 was measured by me this session**, not quoted: Yahoo hourly and daily bars for CL=F,
BZ=F, NG=F, USO, XLE and ^VIX, `range=2y`, bucketed by UTC hour with the Paris DST rule applied per
release date.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is aggregator-sourced and stays `estimate`, but the aggregator's table earned real
   credibility — SUPPORTED, with the primary still unread.** investing.com lists the September
   edition at **Sep 11, 2026 09:00** London (= 10:00 Paris, the IEA's standing time, independently
   restated in search results as "published every month at 10 AM Paris time on release day"). The
   Sep-11 row itself could not be checked at source. So instead I checked the rows around it: four of
   the same table's back-history entries match IEA's own primary PDF filenames **exactly, 4 of 4** —
   `-11DEC2025_OilMarketReport.pdf`, `-21JAN2026_OilMarketReport.pdf`,
   `-14APR2026_OilMarketReport_Free_version1.pdf`, `-13MAY2026__OilMarketReport_publicversion.pdf`.
   A table with a 4/4 verified back-history is a better witness than one taken on trust, and this is
   the cheapest available substitute for reading the schedule page. It is **not** a confirmation:
   the status stays `estimate` on three counts that have nothing to do with date doubt — the primary
   404s/403s, the source-prefix taxonomy in `market-events-data.ts` defines no `IEA:` confirmed
   prefix, and this lane may not self-confirm an event. Full 2026 history captured for the base rate:
   **01/21, 02/12, 03/12, 04/14, 05/13, 06/17, 07/10, 08/12**.

2. **The release window is a measured non-event, and this is the leg that kills the trade —
   SUPPORTED, on a tail test rather than a median.** No base rate for an OMR release existed anywhere
   in this repo, so I built one. The release lands at 10:00 Paris — **08:00 UTC** when Paris is on
   CEST, **09:00 UTC** on CET — so each date was bucketed to its own correct hour, and each release
   hour compared against **every** bar in that same UTC hour across the two-year sample:

   | Symbol | Release-hour median \|move\| (n=13) | Ordinary 08Z / 09Z median | Ratio | Exceeded own-hour **p90** | Exceeded own-hour **p95** |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | CL=F | 0.336% | 0.344% / 0.303% | **1.05×** | **2 / 13** (1.3 expected) | **0 / 13** |
   | BZ=F | 0.506% | 0.353% / 0.308% | 1.52× | **0 / 13** (1.3 expected) | **0 / 13** |
   | NG=F *(control)* | 0.168% | 0.231% / 0.259% | **0.69×** | 0 / 13 | 0 / 13 |

   Read the **tail**, not the median — a trade needs an outlier, and there were none: across 26
   crude observations, **zero** beat p95, and the largest single move was **0.94%**. Brent's 1.52×
   median ratio is a median artifact at n=13, and it is contradicted by its own tail (0/13 at p90,
   *below* the 1.3 chance expectation) and by WTI's 1.05× on the same 13 sessions. Direction is
   absent too: **5 up, 8 down** on both benchmarks. And **natural gas is quieter in the window than
   usual** — the placebo behaves exactly as a null predicts, which is the strongest single line of
   evidence here that the crude readings are noise rather than a suppressed signal.

3. **This leg was nearly published wrong, and how it broke is worth more than the result —
   SUPPORTED.** The first cut of leg 2 used the ten dates the aggregator's table showed (Dec 2025 –
   Aug 2026) and produced WTI **0.475% vs 0.344%, a 1.38× "livelier release hour"** — a tidy,
   quotable, publishable finding. Adding three IEA-primary-verified 2025 dates (**08-13**, **09-11**,
   **10-14**) collapsed it to **1.05×**. The effect was **sample selection**, and it did not survive
   three more observations. It is banked here rather than quietly dropped because the same failure
   is one edition away from the sibling STEO ledger's own n=8 base rate, and because the tail test
   (0/13 at p95) would have said "nothing here" at n=10 too — the median would not.

4. **Nobody can trade the window regardless — SUPPORTED, mechanically.** 04:00 ET is **5.5 hours**
   before the 09:30 ET US equity open. Yahoo's two-year hourly series for **USO** and **XLE**
   contains **0 bars** in the 08:00/09:00 UTC window (0 of 13 release dates, 0 of ~500 possible
   sessions) — the instruments do not exist there. Only the round-the-clock futures are open, and
   leg 2 says what they do. So even a real OMR surprise reaches any US-listed energy exposure as a
   five-hour-stale headline competing with an 08:30 ET CPI print.

5. **The calendar has been quoting the IEA's Gulf number too high — SUPPORTED, and this is the
   session's most useful correction.** [`opec-plus-meeting-2026-09-06`](opec-plus-meeting-2026-09-06.md)
   carries "the IEA quoted at ~10 mb/d" of Gulf shutdowns, sourced to an Argus headline whose body
   never fetched — an unverifiable figure that has since been repeated. The August OMR's actual
   figure, read through two independent trade-press reports of the same edition: **"Middle East
   production was 8.3 million bpd below pre-war levels in July"** (Reuters/Robert Harvey via
   Investing.com, 2026-08-12), and "8.3 million b/d Gulf production shut down" (Oil & Gas Journal,
   2026-08-12). Two outlets, same number, same edition. **~10 was high by ~1.7 mb/d.** Both reads are
   secondary and labelled — the primary 403s — but an agreed figure from two independent readers of
   the document beats a headline whose document nobody opened.

6. **The third read reproduces the disagreement instead of settling it — SUPPORTED, and it is the
   honest answer to why this event was filed.** The event's own notes ask the OMR to break a ~2×
   published spread on Hormuz flow (**4.9 mb/d** AIS-derived vs **8–10 mb/d** US-government/Goldman).
   It does not, for two reasons. (a) **The IEA publishes a different metric.** Its public number is
   *production shut in* (8.3 mb/d below pre-war), not *strait transit* — and it separately reports
   Mideast **loadings** whipsawing from **20 mb/d in early July to 12 mb/d later in the month**,
   a third metric again, which brackets Goldman's 15–16 mb/d export estimate without confirming it.
   (b) **On the one metric where the agencies do overlap, they disagree just as widely.** For the
   same month, July 2026: EIA's August STEO says Mideast shut-ins **~5.5 mb/d**; the IEA says
   **8.3 mb/d**. That is **2.8 mb/d — 51%** — between two government-grade agencies, on ~3% of world
   supply. The correct conclusion is not "the truth is in the middle." It is that **no single Gulf
   supply figure currently available is a fact**, and any ledger citing one should cite the pair.

7. **A dated adjacent event this calendar was missing, found by asking who else prints — SUPPORTED.**
   Three forecasting agencies publish within three consecutive sessions: **EIA STEO 2026-09-09**
   (tracked), **OPEC MOMR 2026-09-10** (**not tracked**), **IEA OMR 2026-09-11** (this event).
   investing.com's OPEC Monthly Report calendar lists the next release as **Sep 10, 2026 10:00**
   London, against a printed history of Aug 12, Jun 11, May 13, Apr 13, Mar 11 2026 and Dec 11, Nov
   12, Oct 13, Sep 11 2025 — the same mid-month cadence, from the same aggregator whose IEA table
   verified 4/4 above. Proposed in this PR as `src/domain/market-events/opec-momr-2026-09-10.json`,
   `estimate`/`EST:`/`low`, on identical reasoning to this event and the STEO: a forecast publication
   sets no policy and moves no equity tape. Its research value is that it completes the triple — the
   producer cartel's own number next to the two consumer-side agencies' — on the exact question leg 6
   just showed is unresolved.

8. **Transmission to our tracked names is two-step, discounted by our own hypothesis, and arrives
   after the print it would have to move — SUPPORTED.** `symbols: []`. The only crude path to the
   book is crude → CPI energy / ISM prices-paid → Sep-16 hike odds → real yields → long-duration AI
   names. Our registered **FT-12** holds that CPI reactions are **core**-keyed with energy-driven
   headline surprises discounted, and Waller said the same from the Fed's side on **2026-09-03**
   (federalreserve.gov primary, quoted in the OPEC+ ledger): *"I don't see elevated energy prices and
   tariffs now as a significant source of ongoing inflation pressure."* Worse for the trade: the
   August CPI collection window closed long before this OMR exists, **and** the OMR prints 4.5 hours
   *before* the CPI release on the same morning — so it can neither inform the number nor be
   separated from the reaction to it.

**What the conditions support.** Nothing to enter. Four cheap, checkable deliverables instead:
(a) a **measured base rate** for OMR release windows, new to this repo, that kills "trade the
release" on a tail test rather than an assertion; (b) a **correction** to a figure the calendar had
been repeating from an unfetchable headline (~10 → **8.3 mb/d**); (c) the **honest negative answer**
to the question this event was filed to ask — the third read widens the disagreement rather than
closing it; and (d) one **dated discovery**, the OPEC MOMR of **2026-09-10**, proposed in this PR.

**Limits, honestly.**
- **The IEA primary was never read this session.** `iea.org` 403s to every fetch path tried. Every
  report figure here is secondary, from two independent outlets that agree; every schedule claim is
  aggregator-sourced with a 4/4 back-history check standing in for the schedule page. The September
  edition's own numbers must be read from a primary or from two independent secondaries before
  anything is built on them, and the close-out should re-attempt `iea.org` first.
- **n=13, in a war-distorted sample, with no significance test.** Yahoo's hourly bars cap at ~730
  days, so no pre-conflict OMR sample was reachable. The *tail* result (0/13 at p95 on both
  benchmarks, plus a control that goes the other way) is the robust part. A precise effect size is
  not, and leg 3 is the standing warning about what n=13 medians do.
- **The 51% agency gap may be partly definitional, not substantive.** "Mideast shut-ins" (EIA) and
  "Middle East production below pre-war levels" (IEA) are worded differently and may use different
  baselines and country sets. I could not reconcile the definitions without the primaries. That
  possibility does not rescue either figure for use as a fact — it is another reason to cite the
  pair — but it means the gap is an upper bound on real disagreement, not a measured one.
- **The OPEC MOMR date is single-aggregator with no primary check.** `opec.org` was not fetched for
  it. It is proposed `estimate`/`EST:` precisely so it widens caution and licenses nothing.
- **There is no consensus and no whisper for an OMR, structurally.** It is a subscription forecast
  publication, not a survey-tracked data release, so the usual "reaction function to surprises in
  each direction" question is malformed here rather than merely unanswered — which is part of why
  the window is quiet.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (set 2026-09-06, D-5): stand aside on the release; read the shut-in number the next
morning, and cite it next to the EIA's, never alone.** The 2026-09-11 OMR is **estimate**-dated,
carries no tracked symbols, publishes 5.5 hours before the US equity open in a window where USO and
XLE have no bars at all, and across 13 measured releases produced **0 outliers at p95** on either
crude benchmark with a maximum move of 0.94%. Its content value is one contested figure whose two
official readings differ by 51%. No entry, no hedge, no pre-positioning is licensed by this event,
and the **estimate** label forbids date-keyed action independently of all of the above.

**Kill switches** — any of these fires and the stance gets re-argued, not tweaked:

1. **The release window stops being quiet.** CL=F's 08:00–09:00 UTC bar on **2026-09-11** exceeds
   **±0.91%**, or BZ=F's exceeds **±1.01%** (each benchmark's own-hour p90), with the OMR
   identifiable as the cause → the base rate is wrong or regime-dependent and leg 2 needs rebuilding
   on a longer sample before it is cited again. Check: Yahoo hourly bars, 2026-09-11.
2. **The date slips.** No September OMR published by **10:00 Paris on 2026-09-11** → the aggregator
   table is wrong despite its 4/4 back-history, and every IEA date this calendar carries needs
   re-derivation. Check: iea.org (retry the 403) or the blob asset host, 2026-09-11.
3. **The agencies converge.** The September edition's Gulf shut-in figure lands within **±0.5 mb/d**
   of the EIA's own August-month number → leg 6's "the third read reproduces the spread" finding
   dies, the pair becomes quotable as a range, and the Hormuz magnitude question is closer to settled
   than this ledger says.
4. **The shut-in reverses hard.** The September edition puts August Gulf shut-ins **below 5 mb/d**
   → the Goldman recovery thesis (15–16 mb/d exports, ~2/3 of pre-war, note 2026-08-28) is confirmed
   by the agency that has been most pessimistic, and the crude leg of the CPI channel changes sign
   for the October print. That re-opens the transmission question in leg 8, not this ledger's call.
5. **The 2027 glut premise breaks.** An OMR edition cuts its 2027 supply rebound below **+4 mb/d**
   (August carries **+8.3 mb/d to 110.3**), or a dated primary-sourced Hormuz reopening agreement
   appears → the structural forecast under the quarter call is void and crude stops being a
   discountable input to the book.

this session: **FT-iea-omr-2026-09-11-1** (the release window is a non-event, stated as a tail test
