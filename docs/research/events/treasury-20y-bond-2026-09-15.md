new issue and lands the day before the September FOMC, in the middle of the long-end term-premium

the market reacted to weak vs. strong 20Y results, and which tracked names carry the most exposure

**One-line verdict:** the setup is a two-sided variance event whose supply risk (a soft reopening
extending the July deterioration) is stacked directly against the next-day FOMC's policy-rate risk —

*Second, and larger: three figures this doc has repeated in every row are wrong or overstated, and
Treasury's own data says so.* (a) **The reopening size has been knowable all along at $13B** —
Treasury's 2026-08-05 refunding statement `sb0590` names it by month (its Sep-26 anticipated-size
row reads 20-Year `13`), and every 2026 reopening came at exactly $13B (five for five). Saying "size
does not exist" in seven consecutive rows treated a *formality* (the 9/10 announcement) as an
*absence of information* — while the number had been in print since before this doc's first row. (b) **July's direct-bidder share was 16.1%, not 10.2%** — the "collapsed direct
demand, less than half its norm" framing that this doc's weak-print trigger, its stance, and its
"awkward tenor" verdict all rest on came from a secondary tracker. July is still the weakest
reopening of 2026 on direct participation, and dealer take (14.5%) was its highest, so the direction
holds; the magnitude does not. (c) **The demand "trend" is confounded by a settlement-regime
change** this ledger never named: from the 2026-06-16 reopening, 20Y reopenings settle the Friday of
auction week (new issues still month-end) explicitly to shorten the when-issued window and mitigate
repo specialness, at the primary dealers' own request. June and July are the *only two auctions ever
run under that convention* — n=2 across a regime change is not a demand trend, and it also explains
why no pass of this doc has ever found a published 20Y when-issued level.

*Third, the supply-leg offset this doc has credited since D-17 does not exist in the operations
data.* D-17 called the 10–20Y buyback increase "support landing directly under this tenor" and D-11
upgraded it to "a mild positive for the supply leg." Across **all 23** of 2026's buyback operations
whose eligible list reached into the 2044–2046 maturity band — **193** eligible line-items —
Treasury accepted par on **five lines totalling $1.05B**, all low-coupon legacy bonds (0.750%–2.250%
coupons), four of them under $60M, and **none since 2026-05-28**. The last two 10–20Y operations
(6/9, 8/11) each filled their entire $2B cap *outside* the band; 8/11's whole $2B went into two 2042
lines. And the then-on-the-run 20Y was **absent from 8/11's eligible list** while three older 20Ys
were on it — so the doubled cap effective 9/9 enlarges a bid that has not bought 20-year-maturity
paper in over three months, and is unlikely to include the very CUSIP being reopened. Honest bound:
the cap doubling is untested and could change behaviour, and cheapest-to-deliver economics favour
low-coupon bonds, so $0 in high-coupon 2045/2046 lines is partly mechanical rather than evidence of
disinterest. Either way, the *claim* was unsupported when it was written.

guard is protecting against: less "the buyback is quietly helping" and more "the policy leg came
