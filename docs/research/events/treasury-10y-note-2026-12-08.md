back a null — so the two things actually worth carrying out of this document are a size correction
and a relocated risk.** At 1:00pm ET on Tuesday 2026-12-08 Treasury reopens the November 10-Year
benchmark, expected **$39B** (not published until **2026-12-03**), while the FOMC is **in session**:
federalreserve.gov lists the December meeting as **`December 8-9*`**, a two-day meeting whose first
day is this auction day and whose `*` marks a dot-plot, with the decision (**confirmed**) roughly
**25 hours later**. That closeness is new — **2026-12-09 is the earliest December FOMC decision in
the 2009–2026 record** (day 9; next earliest is 2025's day 10, and every other year is day 11 or
later). **But the auction's position is not new at all.** Six nominal 10-Year auctions have already
priced on day one of a two-day meeting, and against matched controls their bid-to-cover delta is

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The proposal that discovered this event made a specific claim about why it deserves its own ledger:
the December block's three coupon legs *"straddle the meeting at three different distances… an
unusually clean natural experiment for anyone testing FOMC-proximity effects on auction demand."*
That is a good question and the right instinct. It is also a question the published record can
already answer, because Treasury has been running this experiment, unintentionally, for seventeen
years.

So this document does not re-derive what three sibling ledgers already own — the September and
October reopenings ([`treasury-10y-note-2026-09-09.md`](treasury-10y-note-2026-09-09.md),
[`treasury-10y-note-2026-10-07.md`](treasury-10y-note-2026-10-07.md)), the November new issue
([`treasury-10y-note-2026-11-10.md`](treasury-10y-note-2026-11-10.md)) and, for the block shape,
[`treasury-3y-note-2026-12-07.md`](treasury-3y-note-2026-12-07.md). It asks one question and tests
it, and it corrects two numbers the family would otherwise carry forward wrongly.

**The question: does selling the 10-Year into an unresolved policy decision cost anything?** The
setup is real and it is sharper than the proposal stated. Reading the Fed's own calendar rather than
inferring from the decision date, the December 2026 meeting is **`December 8-9*`** — a *two-day*
meeting whose **first day is this auction day**, with a Summary of Economic Projections. So the
1:00pm ET stop does not merely precede a decision; it prices **while the Committee is sitting**, into
a dot plot nobody has seen, ~25 hours out. Three intuitive readings follow, and all three are
testable:

1. Dealers will not warehouse duration in front of a binary, so **demand should be visibly weaker**.
2. End investors will stand aside and **dealers should be left holding more of it**.
3. The session should be **more volatile** than an ordinary auction day.

**One-line verdict.** Reading 1 is refuted, reading 2 is mixed and small, and reading 3 is refuted in
the *opposite* direction — the pre-decision session is if anything quieter, because the market is
waiting. What remains is an ordinary $39B reopening whose only unpublished number arrives
**2026-12-03**, and a genuine risk that has nothing to do with the auction: the buyer at 1:00pm holds
through a dot-plot decision, a CPI print and a government-funding deadline before settling.

### Method

Rates mode per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md). `symbols: []`, so no
symbol-keyed instrument applies — `earnings-cycle.mjs` and `intraday-edges.mjs` have no target and
the mandated cache bust has nothing to bust (recorded, not silently skipped). Everything
quantitative below was fetched **this session (2026-09-09)** and derived here:

- **The schedule** — `home.treasury.gov/system/files/221/Tentative-Auction-Schedule.pdf`, plain
  curl, HTTP 200, **17,195 bytes**; the four content streams inflated page by page and rows rebuilt
  from `Tm` coordinates, independently of the proposal that discovered this event.
- **Auction history** — `api.fiscaldata.treasury.gov` `auctions_query`,
  `original_security_term:eq:10-Year, security_type:eq:Note, auction_date:gte:2009-01-01`, **318**
  rows returned on one page, **103** of them 10-Year TIPS, leaving **215 nominal** (**214** cleared;
  the 215th is today's 09-09 auction, which has not printed).
- **FOMC decision dates** — `federalreserve.gov`, `fomchistorical<YEAR>.htm` for 2009–2020 and
  `fomccalendars.htm` for 2021–2027, both HTTP 200. Statement-URL dates harvested, seven
  non-scheduled statements excluded by name, leaving **exactly 8 per year for all 18 years**.
- **The tape** — `home.treasury.gov` daily par yield curve CSVs, one file per year **2009–2026**
  (18 files, **4,423 sessions**); **VIX** from `cdn.cboe.com`'s own `VIX_History.csv` (CBOE primary,
  HTTP 200).

**Three source-reliability notes, all recorded rather than papered over.**

*TIPS contamination.* `auctions_query`'s `security_type` does **not** separate 10-Year TIPS from
nominal notes — both return as `Note`, and the TIPS rows corrupt any naive average. Filtering on a
non-null `ref_cpi_on_issue_date` separates them; every statistic below is **nominal-only**. This is
the same trap the September, October, November and 30-Year siblings recorded, reproduced
independently here rather than inherited.

*The FOMC date list is a derivation, not a published table.* The Fed publishes meeting *calendars* as
prose and statements as dated URLs; there is no machine-readable decision-date series. This session
derived one from the statement URLs and then excluded the seven statements that are not scheduled
decisions — **2010-05-09** (Sunday swap-line reopening), **2019-10-11** (reserve management),
**2020-03-03**, **2020-03-23** and **2020-03-31** (intermeeting crisis actions), and **2020-08-27**
and **2025-08-22** (Longer-Run Goals statements). **2020-03-15 is kept**, because it replaced the
scheduled 3/17–18 meeting. The consistency check is strong and was run before any statistic: the
result is **8 decisions in every one of the 18 years**, which is what the FOMC's standing schedule
says it should be.

*No when-issued data exists.* The sharper demand metric for an auction is the **tail** — the stop
against the when-issued quote — and Treasury publishes neither. Every demand statement below is
bid-to-cover and bidder composition, and the ledger makes **no tail claim**.

**Bidder shares are competitive-only.** Every indirect/direct/dealer percentage is a share of
**competitive** awards, with SOMA add-ons excluded from the denominator.

**Cycle vocabulary, and it carries the document's second correction.** A nominal 10-Year is sold
three times: a **NEW** issue in the refunding month (Feb/May/Aug/Nov), then a **first reopening** the
following month (Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec) and a **second reopening** the month after (Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct).
**2026-12-08 is a first reopening** — the schedule row carries `R` and the page legend reads
`R --denotes reopening`.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The date, security and settlement are what the calendar says — SUPPORTED, primary.** The
page-3 row reads verbatim `10-Year NOTE / R / Thursday, December 03, 2026 / Tuesday, December 08,
2026 / Tuesday, December 15, 2026`. Announcement is the **2026-12-03** coupon announcement, not the
11-04 refunding; settlement is **2026-12-15**, the same date as the 3-Year (12-07) and the 30-Year
(12-10), so all three December legs settle together. 1:00pm ET is Treasury's standing convention and
is **not** separately sourced. Status correctly stays `estimate`: a tentative schedule is tentative
by construction.

**2. The auction is on day ONE of a two-day FOMC meeting, not merely the day before a decision —
SUPPORTED, primary, and it is the sharpening this ledger adds to the proposal.**
`federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm` (HTTP 200, read this session) lists the
December 2026 meeting as **`December 8-9*`**, with the page's own footnote *"Meeting associated with
a Summary of Economic Projections."* So the auction prices **while the Committee is in session**, ~25
hours ahead of a **dot-plot** decision that this repo already tracks as **confirmed**
([`fomc-2026-12-09.md`](fomc-2026-12-09.md), `FED:` prefix, statement 14:00 ET). The proposal's "D-1"
framing is right and understates the case.

**3. 2026-12-09 is the earliest December FOMC decision in the 2009–2026 record — SUPPORTED, and it
is why the gap compresses.** Day-of-month for every December decision:

| Year | Decision | Day | Year | Decision | Day |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2009 | 12-16 | 16 | 2018 | 12-19 | 19 |
| 2010 | 12-14 | 14 | 2019 | 12-11 | 11 |
| 2011 | 12-13 | 13 | 2020 | 12-16 | 16 |
| 2012 | 12-12 | 12 | 2021 | 12-15 | 15 |
| 2013 | 12-18 | 18 | 2022 | 12-14 | 14 |
| 2014 | 12-17 | 17 | 2023 | 12-13 | 13 |
| 2015 | 12-16 | 16 | 2024 | 12-18 | 18 |
| 2016 | 12-14 | 14 | 2025 | 12-10 | 10 |
| 2017 | 12-13 | 13 | **2026** | **12-09** | **9** |

Treasury's December coupon block is anchored to the first full week (announce the first Thursday,
auction Mon/Tue/Thu); the FOMC's date floats. The collision in 2026 is the Fed moving early, not
Treasury moving late — the same shape of calendar accident the November sibling documented from the
other side, and neither mechanism knows about the other.

**4. Pricing into an unresolved decision does not weaken 10-Year demand — REFUTED as a hypothesis,
on a matched control.** Computing signed days from each of the **214** cleared nominal 10-Year
auctions to its nearest scheduled decision gives a natural experiment already run many times:

| Cohort | n | Bid-to-cover | Indirect (comp.) | Dealer (comp.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D-1 — auction on meeting day one** | **6** | **2.560** | 60.8% | 22.9% |
| D-2 | 9 | 2.468 | 62.0% | 24.9% |
| Decision day itself | 4 | 3.053 | 42.6% | 39.2% |
| ≥5 days from any decision | 191 | 2.621 | 57.2% | 28.9% |

Raw pooled means are misleading here for exactly the reason the November sibling documented — era
composition — and the decision-day row shows it starkly: its four members are **2009-08-12,
2011-12-13, 2012-03-13 and 2012-12-12**, all from the era when indirect shares were structurally low
and cover ratios structurally high. **No weight is put on that row.** So this session ran the same
matched control the sibling used: each cohort auction against the mean of its **four nearest
auctions with no decision within 5 days**.

| Matched comparison | n | Δ bid-to-cover | above own control | Δ indirect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D-1 (meeting day one)** | **6** | **−0.025** | **3/6** | −0.34pp |
| D-2 | 9 | −0.010 | 4/9 | −3.28pp |
| **D-2 and D-1 pooled** | **15** | **−0.016** | **7/15** | **−2.10pp (11/15 below)** |

**What this establishes:** the bid-to-cover effect is **indistinguishable from zero** — a −0.025 mean
with 3 of 6 above their own control is a distribution centred on nothing. The individual D-1 prints
carry the same message: 2012-09-12 **2.850**, 2019-12-10 **2.430**, 2020-06-09 **2.260**, 2024-06-11
**2.670**, 2025-05-06 **2.600**, 2025-12-09 **2.550**. Five of the six were day one of an **SEP**
meeting, so the dot-plot variant is the *majority* of the sample rather than an untested case.

**5. The closest analogue is one year old and nearly identical — SUPPORTED, and it is the single
most useful row in the document.** **2025-12-09** was a December 10-Year **reopening** at **$39B**
auctioning on day one of the **2025-12-09/10** two-day SEP meeting — same month, same size, same
security class, same position. It cleared **bid-to-cover 2.550** against a $39B-era mean of
**2.555**, with an indirect share of **70.2%** and a dealer take of **8.8%**, the *lowest* dealer
share in the era. Whatever the FOMC-eve position does, it did not show up there.

**6. Dealers being left holding more of it — MIXED, small, and probably an era artifact.** This is
the reading that would signal genuine stress, so it is tested separately from leg 4 rather than
folded into it. Across the pooled pre-decision cohort the indirect share sits **below** its own
control in **11 of 15** cases, mean **−2.10pp**. But where the difference goes is **not** consistent
— the dealer delta is positive in only **8 of 15** (mean +2.51pp, driven by two large early
observations) — and the two most recent instances run the *other* way (2024-06-11 **+9.3pp**
indirect, 2025-12-09 **+3.2pp**). Reported as **MIXED**: a small, directionally consistent dip in
end-investor share whose destination the data cannot name, on a sample the modern prints contradict.

**Worth naming beside its opposite.** The November sibling measured the *resolved* case — a CPI print
08:30 the same session — and found awards tilting **toward** end investors, **+2.75pp** indirect on
n=30. This ledger measures the *unresolved* case and finds a weak tilt the other way. Same family,
opposite signs, and one mechanism explains both: information that has already landed lets end
investors bid; information that lands tomorrow does not. Neither effect is large enough to be a
position, and this pairing is offered as a hypothesis for the next December, not a finding.

**7. The auction-day session is not more volatile — REFUTED, in the opposite direction.** The
10-Year par yield's move from the prior close to the auction-day close:

| Auction-day class | n | Mean absolute move |
|---|---|---|
| **D-1 (meeting day one)** | 6 | **4.7 bp** |
| **D-2 and D-1 pooled** | 15 | **3.1 bp** |
| ≥5 days from any decision | 191 | 4.0 bp |
| All nominal 10-Year auctions | 214 | 4.0 bp |

The pre-decision session is **quieter**, not noisier, which is what a market waiting for a decision
should look like. The D-1 subset at 4.7bp on n=6 is inside the noise of that and is not read as an
effect.

**8. The risk is the three sessions after the stop — SUPPORTED, and this is the leg that changes
what a reader should do.** The auction is benign; the corridor it settles through is not.

| Session class | n | Mean absolute 10Y move | Max |
|---|---|---|---|
| FOMC decision day | 140 | **5.4 bp** | **51 bp** (2009-03-18) |
| Ordinary session | 4,422 | 4.2 bp | 51 bp |
| Auction close → 5 sessions on, D-1 cohort | 6 | 9.0 bp | 19 bp |
| Ordinary 5-session roll | 4,418 | 9.0 bp | 56 bp |

A decision day runs about **1.3×** an ordinary session and owns the record's single largest move. On
top of it, **CPI 12-10** (confirmed, high) and the **government-funding deadline / CR expiry 12-11**
(estimate, high) land before **12-15 settlement**. The auction-to-settlement window is statistically
ordinary (**9.0bp vs 9.0bp**) — but that is a base rate across quiet corridors, and this one carries
three high-impact dated events. Being flat into 12-08 is a corridor call, not an auction call.

**9. There is no precedent for an adverse gap into the decision, and the direction is explicitly not
a forecast — SUPPORTED as a null, small-n by construction.** From each D-1 auction's close to the
decision close:

| Auction | Decision | 10Y move |
|---|---|---|
| 2012-09-12 | 2012-09-13 | −2 bp |
| 2019-12-10 | 2019-12-11 | −6 bp |
| 2020-06-09 | 2020-06-10 | −9 bp |
| 2024-06-11 | 2024-06-12 | −8 bp |
| 2025-05-06 | 2025-05-07 | −4 bp |
| 2025-12-09 | 2025-12-10 | −5 bp |

All six rallied. Against the series' own **46.7%** base rate for a down session, six of six one-signed
is **p ≈ 0.01** under a naive independence assumption — but five of the six sit in easing or dovish
policy windows, which is a confound the sample cannot separate from an effect. What this establishes
is the only form of the claim a stance can rest on: **no precedent for a large adverse gap from a
FOMC-eve 10-Year auction into the decision.** The signed direction is not a forecast and is not
registered as one.

**10. The size is $39B, not $42B — SUPPORTED, and it is the proposal's one material error.** The
proposal reads: *"EXPECTED SIZE, per the grid rather than any published document: $42B, the standing
10-Year size."* $42B is the **new-issue** size; this is a **reopening**, which the proposal itself
identifies two sentences earlier. The reopening grid is just as flat and just as long: **21
consecutive $39B reopenings** from **2024-03-12** through today's **2026-09-09**, of which **20 have
cleared** — bid-to-cover mean **2.555**, σ **0.089**, range **2.34 – 2.70**, across clearing yields
**3.648% → 4.680%**. Competitive shares over those twenty: indirect **71.7%** (range 61.4 – 87.9%),
direct **17.0%**, dealer **11.3%**. Restricting to *first* reopenings only (n=10) tightens it: b/c
mean **2.585**, range **2.45 – 2.70**, indirect mean **72.9%**. **2026-12-08 would be the 23rd** —
09-09 is the 21st and 10-07 (also `R` on the schedule) the 22nd. All of this sits inside the fact the
coupon-announcement siblings established: **no nominal coupon or FRN size has changed anywhere since
April 2024**. The number is **not published today** and becomes published **2026-12-03**.

|---|---|---|---|---|
