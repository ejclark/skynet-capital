## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The proposal that created this event argued that 2026-12-10 is *"the SCORING DATE for the advance print's
own claim"* — the release where the 11-19 advance number stops being provisional — and priced that claim
off a 12-pair sample whose maximum advance→full revision was **0.10pp**. **Is the date real, is that
bound real, what does the full report contain that the advance does not, and does anything downstream
read it?**

**One-line verdict:** the date is real and now `confirmed` on two Census primaries, the *bound* is not —
the newest pair on record breaks it by 2× — and the release is worth reading for its **seasonally
adjusted subsector detail** rather than its headline, in a session that belongs entirely to CPI.

### Leg 3 — what the full report contains that the advance does not · **the subsector seasonal adjustment**

This is the leg the proposal did not ask, and it is the one that gives 12-10 a reason to exist. Compare
the *same paragraph* across an advance and a full report, both extracted from Census PDFs this session:

| | Advance (CB26-139, Q2 2026) | Full report (CB26-39, Q4 2025) |
|---|---|---|
| Selected Services Total | "adjusted for seasonal variation but not for price changes" | "adjusted for seasonal variation but not for price changes" |
| **Utilities** | "Advance U.S. utilities total revenue … **not adjusted for seasonal variation** or price changes" | "U.S. utilities revenue … **adjusted for seasonal variation** but not for price changes" |
| **Transportation and warehousing** | "**not adjusted for seasonal variation** or price changes" | "**adjusted for seasonal variation** but not for price changes" |

Verified on **two** advance captures (Q4 2025, Q2 2026) and **three** full captures (Q3 2025, Q4 2025,
Q1 2026) — the split is structural, not a drafting accident. **In an advance, only the headline total is
seasonally adjusted; every subsector is raw.** The full report three weeks later is where the sector
detail becomes comparable quarter-over-quarter at all.

That matters specifically for this quarter. Census's Announcements block, verbatim:

> **Upcoming Benchmarking** — Effective with the November 2026 advance release for the third quarter of
> 2026, we anticipate that not seasonally adjusted quarterly services estimates will be revised to
> reflect historical corrections and results of the 2023 and 2024 Annual Integrated Economic Survey. The
> seasonally adjusted Selected Services Total will be revised to reflect historical corrections, results
> of the 2023 and 2024 Annual Integrated Economic Survey, **and new seasonal factors**.

The announcement names **the advance**, and is silent on 12-10. But the advance has no seasonally
adjusted subsector detail to restate — so **the new seasonal factors first reach the subsectors on
2026-12-10**. The benchmark's headline lands on 11-19; its effect on everything below the headline lands
here.
