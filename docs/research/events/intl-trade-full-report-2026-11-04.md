## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The sibling ledger closed on 2026-10-06 with a solo, cleanly-attributable vintage and a two-print kill
switch whose second leg is this date. **Does anything the sibling measured transfer to 2026-11-04, and
is the switch it handed forward a test that can actually fire?**

**One-line verdict:** almost nothing transfers — this edition prints *after* its own quarter's GDP has
been estimated, into a nowcast of the next quarter, on the most contaminated morning of the quarter —
but the inherited regime switch survives that scrutiny as the strongest discriminator in this ledger
family, because the "regime" it tests has **no month-to-month persistence** and its two legs are
therefore near-independent draws.

### Leg 4 — the inherited two-leg regime switch is a real test · **SUPPORTED**, and this is what earns the ledger

The book's only channel into this series is the end-use split reading "datacenter hardware," and the
sibling registered a two-print switch on it: capital goods failing to be the largest-magnitude m/m
mover among the six major import end-use categories in **both** the 10-06 (August data) and 11-04
(September data) reports. Re-deriving the base from the 71 parsed vintages reproduces the sibling
exactly — capital goods leads in **12 of 71 months (16.9%)**, and in **5 of the last 7** (Jan, Feb, Apr,
Jun, Jul 2026; binomial p=**0.0021**).

The new measurement is **persistence**, because a two-leg switch is only demanding if the legs are not
the same draw twice:

| Prior month | n | capital goods leads the next month |
|---|---|---|
| Was a hit | 11 | **2 (18.2%)** |
| Was a miss | 59 | **10 (16.9%)** |

Fisher's exact p = **1.00**. Month-to-month there is **no persistence at all** — and both of the two
consecutive-hit transitions in the entire record are 2026's (Jan→Feb, Jun→Jul). So the months are
near-independent draws, and the switch's firing probability separates the two hypotheses cleanly:

| Hypothesis | Per-month hit rate | P(both 10-06 and 11-04 miss) |
|---|---|---|
| 2026 regime is real | 71.4% (5/7) | **8.2%** |
| 2026 was noise; pre-2026 rate rules | 10.9% (7/64) | **79.3%** |

A double miss is **9.7× more likely** under "no regime" than under "regime," and a double hit is
**42.6×** more likely the other way. That is a genuine test — the mirror image of the sibling's central
finding, where the switch it inherited needed a revision **4.9× larger than any on record** and could
not fire. Same ledger family, opposite verdict, and the reason to state both is that the difference is
measurable rather than rhetorical.

Graded SUPPORTED as a *test*, not as a claim about the regime: `n=7` supports registration, never a
conclusion, and the trio's share of the capital-goods move (median **52.2%** across months with a ≥$1B
change) is not stable enough to lean on.
