## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This event existed only as `proposals/fhfa-hpi-2027-09-28.from-consumer-confidence-2027-09-28.json` —
the July-2027 monthly edition, proposed by the Conference Board lane for two stated reasons: FHFA posts
its calendar a year ahead and so independently checks that chain's rule-derived dates (12 of 12, banked
by the proposer), and **it lands "on the same morning one hour ahead of the Conference Board print —
the same 9:00-then-10:00 pairing the 08-31 sibling proposed for August, and the second instance of it
in 2027."**

Five FHFA ledgers now exist, and every one of them records that pairing as a limit it could not
resolve: *"the remaining confound is the Conference Board at 10:00, so the 9:00–10:00 ET window is the
only clean design and no daily bar is."*

**Is the pairing actually a property of particular dates? And is the confound it creates unresolvable,
or has nobody tried to remove it?**

**One-line verdict:** the pairing is an **identity** covering **100%** of post-policy FHFA releases, so
it can never distinguish one date from another — and the confound **is** removable, because 104 CB-free
FHFA release days exist in the pre-policy era and they are null on everything.

### Leg 1 — the release exists on 2027-09-28, at 9:00 a.m. ET · **SUPPORTED**, and promoted to `confirmed`

`fhfa.gov/data/hpi` lists `Tuesday, September 28 | Monthly Index | July 2027` as the ninth of twelve

2027* appendix page above the sentence:

> *Public FHFA HPI® releases occur at 9AM ET and include a press release, tables, figures, and
> associated data.*

Status moves `estimate` → **`confirmed`** on the precedent seven sibling lanes have already set: the
no-self-confirm convention binds the *discovering* sweep — here `consumer-confidence-2027-09-28` — and
this is the independent second look.

**The September slot is the July-data monthly index, always.** From the 271-row archive, **18 of 18**
September releases in the monthly era (2008 → 2025) are monthly editions covering July, never a
quarterly one — the counterpart of February's 18-of-18 Q4 and August's 19-of-19 Q2 findings. The

third independent parse.

### Leg 2 — **THE IDENTITY: the Consumer Confidence pairing covers every modern FHFA release** · **SUPPORTED**

The proposal's distinguishing argument was that this print lands "one hour ahead of the Conference
Board print — the same 9:00-then-10:00 pairing the 08-31 sibling proposed for August, and the second

> *The Conference Board publishes the Consumer Confidence Index® at 10 a.m. ET on the last Tuesday of

| Era | FHFA releases | On a last Tuesday (= a CB Consumer Confidence morning) |
|---|---|---|
| **Post-policy, 2021-01 → 2026-08** | **67** | **67 — 100.0%** |

**So it is not "the second instance in 2027" — it is all twelve, every year since 2021.** Two publishers

**Why this is worth writing down rather than filing as trivia.** Every FHFA ledger in this chain closes

the whole instrument**: no daily bar of any post-2020 FHFA release day can ever attribute anything to
FHFA, for any date, ever, until the publication rules diverge. That is a stronger and more useful claim
than five separate apologies, and it is the reason Leg 3 exists.

### Leg 5 — **WHERE IT ACTUALLY LIVES: a late-September term-structure effect, in both eras** · **SUPPORTED**, and not the release's

| Test | n | VIX9D/VIX | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Late September (day ≥ 20) vs the rest of September, 2008–2020** | 101 vs 165 | 0.9742 vs 0.9447 | **0.0176** |
| **Late September (day ≥ 20) vs the rest of September, 2021+** | 40 vs 71 | 0.9908 vs 0.9309 | **0.0170** |
| All September sessions vs all other sessions, 2008–2020 | 266 vs 2,967 | 0.9661 vs 0.9475 | **0.0163** |
| All **late-month** (day ≥ 22) sessions vs the rest of the calendar, 2021+ | 449 vs 1,008 | 0.9199 vs 0.9309 | 0.1374 |

**Three independent windows, the same direction, p≈0.017 each — and it is September's, not month-end's.**
The front-end term structure (9-day vol against 30-day) steepens in the last third of September in both
the pre-policy and post-policy eras, and the generic late-month control comes back null, so this is a
dated seasonal window rather than a calendar-mechanical artefact.

**It is a soft regularity, and the recency runs against it.** Year by year, late-September VIX9D/VIX
exceeds early-September in **11 of 15** years (median gap **+0.0271**) — but **2024 (−0.0606) and 2025
(−0.0421)**, the two most recent, both go the other way. That is the honest strength: a directional
tendency with a live counter-run, not a rule.

**And the FHFA release contributes nothing to it.** Set the five September release days against other
late-September sessions and every p is **0.35–0.87**, with the term-structure reading itself at
**0.5334**. The print sits inside a measurably different environment; it does not create it.

### Leg 6 — **THE METHOD UPGRADE: the control has to match the class's granularity** · **SUPPORTED**, and the siblings survive it

Leg 5 exposes a defect in how this chain has controlled its results. February's trap was killed with a
*month-level* control (release days vs other February sessions); August's mirror was killed the same
way. But a month-level control **cannot see a late-month effect** — and FHFA's last-Tuesday rule puts
**every** release in the final third of its month, by construction. So every FHFA ledger in this chain
