## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Never a trade, and now that is measured rather than asserted.** Across 22 G.19 release
days over two years, SPY's 15:00–16:00 ET release hour moved a mean **0.1378%** in absolute terms
against **0.1445%** on the other 472 sessions — sd **0.2145%** vs **0.2142%**, a variance ratio of
**1.054**. The release hour is marginally *quieter* than an ordinary final hour. The one slice that
looked like signal (+0.10% mean drift, Welch t=2.16, 14/22 positive) is two contaminated sessions:
**2025-05-07 was an FOMC decision day** (federalreserve.gov's own May 2025 calendar) and
**2025-04-07 had an 8.59% full-session range** against the sample's 0.88% median. Strip those two and
the drift is **+0.04%**, indistinguishable from the control's −0.005%. **The date is now `confirmed`**
(the Board's October 2026 calendar, 3:00 p.m. ET, the 7th) — and it is the *third* release of a
stacked afternoon, behind a 13:00 ET 10-year auction and **14:00 ET FOMC minutes**; the one prior
G.19 + minutes double in sample, **2026-07-08, produced a +0.01% final hour**. **So why keep it on the
calendar at all?** One reason, and it is not the flow number: the August-data edition is a
**quarterly terms-of-credit edition** — the Board's terms table is quarterly and its monthly columns
carry a rate only in the mid-quarter month (Apr `n.a.` | May **20.94** | Jun `n.a.`, Q2 = 20.94), so
**2026-10-07 publishes the first Q3-2026 commercial-bank credit-card APR**. That is what revolving
credit *costs*, it has no substitute on this calendar, and Q3 has printed above Q2 three years
running. **And one thing this print cannot do:** the Board's own Technical Q&A #19 states that for
BNPL, "many of the major BNPL lenders are newly established non-bank lenders, where there is more
limited coverage," and "the G.19 is not currently able to separately track the volume of originations
and outstanding balances of such loans." The proposing ledger sent this print to adjudicate whether a
record BNPL-funded back-to-school total was borrowed. It can answer the revolving half. It is
structurally blind to the BNPL half.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

[`retail-sales-2026-09-16`](retail-sales-2026-09-16.md) discovered this event on 2026-09-08 and
assigned it a job: it has carried a "dollars up, units flat, and borrowed" thesis since D-15, citing
a record **$43.3B** K-12 back-to-school total (from $39.4B in 2025) explicitly funded on BNPL and
revolving credit against "moderate at best" unit demand — with no measurement attached. Its proposal
called G.19 "the hard-data Fed release that answers whether that gap was borrowed."

Two questions follow, and they have different answers. **Can this release answer that?** And **does
this release do anything to the tape**, given it lands at 15:00 ET behind FOMC minutes?

### One-line verdict

**No and no — but it is worth keeping for a third thing neither question asked about: it is the
quarter's only read on what revolving credit costs.**

### Leg 1 — "G.19 answers whether the August spend was borrowed" → **MIXED, and REFUTED on the BNPL half**

The Board documents its own blind spot. Technical Q&A #19, *"Are 'Buy Now, Pay Later' (BNPL) loans
included in the G.19 Consumer Credit estimates?"*, answers verbatim:

> The extent to which these loans are captured on the G.19 Consumer Credit statistical release
> largely depends on the type of lender who originates the loan and on how the lenders classify
> these loans according to their internal accounting practices. Generally, loans originated by banks
> and credit unions are well captured on the G.19 estimates. However, many of the major BNPL lenders
> are newly established non-bank lenders, where there is more limited coverage. Moreover, the G.19 is
> not currently able to separately track the volume of originations and outstanding balances of such
> loans.

So: bank- and credit-union-originated BNPL is inside the aggregate but not separable; the major
non-bank BNPL originators are largely outside it. **A release that cannot separately track BNPL
originations or balances cannot adjudicate a claim about BNPL funding**, in either direction — a
soft revolving print is *not* evidence the spend was not borrowed, because the borrowing the
proposing ledger named would not appear here.

The revolving half is answerable. G.19's *About* page: revolving is "credit card loans … most of
revolving consumer credit measured in the G.19," seasonally adjusted, monthly, with the growth rate
computed from break-adjusted flows. That is a real read on card balances, and it is the half this
doc registers a test on.

### Leg 2 — "the print moves the tape" → **REFUTED**

Release dates came from the Board's own calendars, not a derived fifth-business-day guess; 22 of
them fall inside the 501-session SPY hourly sample (2024-09-09 → 2026-09-08).

| 15:00–16:00 ET hour | n | mean | mean abs | sd |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G.19 release days | 22 | +0.0998% | **0.1378%** | 0.2145% |
| all other days | 472 | −0.0037% | **0.1445%** | 0.2142% |

Dispersion — the actual test of "did a release land here" — is **flat**: variance ratio **1.054**,
and the release hour's mean absolute move is *smaller* than the control's. The mean drift is the only
thing that looks like signal (Welch t=2.16, 14/22 positive), and it does not survive inspection: the
two largest release-day hours are **+0.79% on 2025-04-07** and **+0.60% on 2025-05-07**.

- **2025-05-07 was an FOMC decision day** — `federalreserve.gov/newsevents/2025-may.htm` lists "FOMC
  Meeting, Two-day meeting, May 6–May 7, Press Conference 7". The 14:00 statement owned that hour.
- **2025-04-07 had an 8.59% full-session high/low range**, against a **0.88%** median across the
  sample — roughly ten times an ordinary day.

**Drop those two and the release-day mean is +0.04%**, against the control's −0.005%. There is no
G.19 effect; there were two days on which something else happened at 15:00.

### Leg 3 — "the 10-07 session is clean enough to measure it" → **REFUTED, by the calendar**

2026-10-07 stacks, in order: **treasury-10y-note-2026-10-07** (13:00 ET), **fomc-minutes-2026-10-07**
(14:00 ET, confirmed on the same Board calendar page as this print), then this release at 15:00 ET.
Attribution on that afternoon is not available.

The sample contains exactly one prior G.19 + FOMC-minutes double, **2026-07-08**. Its 15:00–16:00
hour was **+0.01%**. FOMC-minutes days as a class also show no dispersion footprint in this sample

**0.1445%**) — small n, stated as such, but it points the same way.

### Leg 4 — "there is nothing else in this release" → **REFUTED; this is the one reason to keep it**

The August-data edition is a **quarterly terms-of-credit edition**, and that is not obvious from the
release's face. Two Fed primaries establish it:

- `releases/g19/hist/cc_hist_tc_levels.html` is **quarterly only** — rows run `1994 Q4 … 2026 Q2`,
  footer "Last Update: August 07, 2026" (i.e. refreshed by the June-data release).
- The current release's monthly columns carry a rate **only in the mid-quarter month**: new-car
  60-month reads `Apr n.a. | May 7.14 | Jun n.a.` with Q2 = **7.14**; credit-card all-accounts reads
  `Apr n.a. | May 20.94 | Jun n.a.` with Q2 = **20.94**.

August is the middle month of Q3. **So 2026-10-07 publishes the first Q3-2026 commercial-bank
credit-card APR and new-car-loan rates.** The recent path, from the history table:

| | 2024 Q3 | 2024 Q4 | 2025 Q1 | 2025 Q2 | 2025 Q3 | 2025 Q4 | 2026 Q1 | 2026 Q2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Credit card, all accounts | 21.76 | 21.47 | 21.37 | 21.16 | **21.39** | 20.97 | 21.00 | **20.94** |
| New car, 60-month | 8.40 | 7.82 | 8.04 | 7.67 | 7.64 | 7.24 | 7.53 | **7.14** |

**Q3 has printed above Q2 in each of the last three years** — 2023: 20.84 → **21.19**; 2024: 21.51 →
**21.76**; 2025: 21.16 → **21.39**. That is a 3-for-3 base rate on a series that is *not* seasonally
adjusted, and it is the falsifiable content of this print.

### Leg 5 — "August will show above-trend borrowing" → **UNSUPPORTED on the base rates**

From the Board's SA flow history (monthly, $B):

| Window | n | positive | mean | median | sd |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revolving, 2024–2026 | 30 | **25 (83%)** | +$3.99B | +$3.53B | $5.03B |
| Revolving, 2022–2026 | 54 | 48 (89%) | +$7.13B | +$7.60B | $6.34B |
| Revolving, **August only**, 2015+ | 11 | 9 (82%) | +$4.10B | +$3.40B | $6.83B |

Two things follow. First, "revolving prints positive in August" is an **83% base rate** — a test of
it would be near-free and would prove nothing. Second, and more usefully: **August 2025 — the last
back-to-school month — printed revolving at −$1.97B.** The seasonal adjustment already carries
back-to-school, so the thesis needs an *above-trend* August, not a positive one. The last 24 months
put **+$7.0B at the 75th percentile** (6 of 24). That is the threshold this doc registers, in the
direction of *no* above-trend borrowing showing up.

Most recent monthly context, from the current (June-data) release: revolving **+10.5%** annualized in
April, **−4.7%** in May, **+6.0%** in June; Q2 total **+2.6%**, revolving **+3.9%**, nonrevolving
**+2.1%**; total outstanding **$5,166.9B**, revolving **$1,351.1B**. Month-to-month, this series is
noise around a trend, and one print is a data point.

### Honest limits

- **n=22 release days, one instrument (SPY), one venue (Yahoo hourly bars).** Hourly bars cannot
  resolve a 15:00 print finer than the hour it lands in; a 5-minute study would be sharper and was
  not run.
- **The FOMC-minutes comparison is n=13**, and two calendar months (2025-11, 2026-04) are missing
  from the harvest because those Board pages 404.
- **No published August consensus was reachable** from this lane — two aggregators 403'd and a third
  renders client-side. Consistent with a release that does not carry a street number worth the name,
  but it is an absence of evidence, recorded as such.
- **The $43.3B back-to-school figure is inherited** from the proposing ledger, not independently
  sourced here. Nothing in this doc's calls depends on it.
- **The July-data edition was NOT in hand when this was written.** It publishes today at 15:00 ET;
  `releases/g19/current/default.htm` still served the June-data edition ("Release Date*: August 7,
  2026") on a re-fetch at **14:31 ET**. Every level and flow quoted here is therefore the June-data
  vintage, and the next pulse should re-read the series before leaning on any of it.
- **The terms-of-credit timing claim is structural, not from a published schedule** — it rests on the
  quarterly history table plus the mid-quarter-month convention in the current release. FT-…-3
  voids rather than scores if the 10-07 edition publishes no Q3 figure.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `confirmed`, FED: primary):** **stand aside on the print and read one line.** This
release has no measured effect on the tape (22 release days, dispersion flat, the apparent drift
explained by an FOMC decision day and an 8.59%-range session), lands third on a stacked
2026-10-07 afternoon behind FOMC minutes, and is structurally unable to answer the BNPL question
the proposing ledger assigned it. It stays on the calendar for one thing: the **first Q3-2026
commercial-bank credit-card APR**, which is the quarter's only read on the price of revolving
credit and which has risen from Q2 in each of the last three years. Nothing here licenses a
position, and the `confirmed` date changes that not at all — a confirmed date on a null is still a
null.

**Kill switches:**

- **The null dies** if SPY's 2026-10-07 15:00–16:00 ET absolute move exceeds **0.30%** *and* exceeds
  the same session's 14:00–15:00 ET move — the release hour beat the minutes hour on a stacked day.
- **The "blind to BNPL" reading dies** if the Board amends Technical Q&A #19 or adds a BNPL line item
  to the release, at which point this print becomes the adjudicator the proposing ledger wanted.
- **The "not a volume story" reading dies** on an August revolving flow at or above **+$7.0B**
  (75th percentile of the last 24 months) — the card channel would be visibly carrying the spend.
- **The cost call dies** on a Q3-2026 credit-card rate below **20.94%**, breaking a three-year
  Q3-above-Q2 pattern.
- **Low impact is wrong** if two consecutive release-day final hours exceed 0.30% absolute with no
  competing 14:00-or-later release — the class would have a footprint after all.
