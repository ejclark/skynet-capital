### The question

This id reached the calendar as one proposal, filed by the
[`russell-style-quarter-end-capping-effective-2027-09-30`](russell-style-quarter-end-capping-effective-2027-09-30.md)

`src/domain/market-events/russell-quarterly-ipo-review-effective-2027-09-20.json` was written by this session.

The inheritance that matters, though, is a two-ledger argument that has already run to a verdict. The
[September 2026 leg](russell-quarterly-ipo-review-effective-2026-09-21.md) found a 15-of-21 small-cap drag on this

[March 2027 leg](russell-quarterly-ipo-review-effective-2027-03-22.md) then went further and killed the class on
dose-response: the June reconstitution, roughly twenty times the flow, prints an exact coin flip.

Both of those are arguments *from outside* the September cell — one from its twin, one from a bigger event. **Nobody
has ever interrogated the September cell on its own terms.** So: does the September drag survive tests aimed at
*it* — seasonality, beta, stationarity, outlier dependence, and its own placebo panel? And if the class is dead,
is there anything in this chain actually worth carrying to 2027?

**One-line verdict:** the September cell is a better-behaved statistic than either sibling realised — it is not a
seasonal, not beta, and its placebo panel is clean — but it is **not stationary and not outlier-robust**, which
is fatal in a different way and for a better reason than "March disagrees"; and the one thing in this family that
*is* stationary is the 5-session drift the chain filed away as an artefact.
