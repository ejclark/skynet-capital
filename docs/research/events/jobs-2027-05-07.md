## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This id reached the calendar as a proposal from `treasury-borrowing-estimates-2027-05-03`'s own
initial research, which derived the date from BLS's scheduling rule and filed it as "the corridor's
far edge." Three questions follow, none of them answered by that proposal. **Does the derived date
survive an independent re-derivation, and is the primary retrievable yet?** **Is the refunding
adjacency a coincidence of this particular May, or a recurring structure?** And the one that matters
for a paper-options book: **does a tier-1 payroll that lands between a refunding announcement and its
auctions behave differently from an ordinary payroll Friday?**

**One-line verdict:** the date reproduces 7-for-7 and stays `estimate` because every BLS route 403s;
the adjacency is **structural** — 116 of 120 refunding-month payrolls sit in the same 4-to-6-day slot,
verified against Treasury's own primary API; and the behavioural question resolves to a **clean,
seven-instrument refutation** of the supply-overhang story, with the one apparently-significant
finding failing its own control and going to the forward-test register instead of the stance.

### The measurement sets

| Set | Definition | n (SPY / bond ETFs) |
|---|---|---|
| **R** | Payroll Friday followed by the quarterly 3y/10y/30y refunding cycle **3–7 calendar days** later | 116 / 93 |
| **O** | Every other payroll Friday, windowed over the same **5 sessions** | 263 / 178 |
| **B** | **Within-R control** — the *immediately following* equal-session window, same month, auctions already done | 116 / 93 |

**B is the load-bearing control.** R is by construction Feb/May/Aug/Nov, so "refunding week" and
"quarter-opening month" are collinear and set O cannot separate them. B holds the month fixed and
moves only the supply — it is the test that decides whether leg 4 is about auctions or about the
calendar.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date reproduces independently — SUPPORTED, and it stays `estimate`.** The rule (release on
   the third Friday following the end of the Sun–Sat reference week containing the 12th) was coded
   from its definition and run over 1993–2027 before being applied. It reproduces **7 of 7** knowns:
   the four `confirmed` entries on this calendar (2026-09-04, 2026-10-02, 2026-11-06, 2026-12-04) and
   the three historical Sunday-July-4 releases (2004-07-02, 2010-07-02, 2021-07-02). April 2027's
   reference week is Sun **04-11** → Sat **04-17** (the 12th is a Monday); the third Friday after
   04-17 is **2027-05-07**. It stays `estimate` on two counts: no primary was retrievable (four
   blocked fetches above), and this lane does not self-confirm. **2027-05-07 is a full NYSE session**
   with no holiday within a week, so the rule's known failure mode cannot fire here.

2. **The refunding adjacency is structural, not a coincidence of this May — SUPPORTED, from a
   primary source.** Taking the first 3y/10y/30y auction of each Feb/May/Aug/Nov from Treasury's own
   API as the refunding cycle, and generating every payroll 1993–2026 from the rule: of the **120**
   payrolls that publish in a refunding month, the gap to the refunding 10-year is **4 days for 11,
   5 days for 93, and 6 days for 12** — **116 of 120**, with 3 at 12 days, 1 at 18, and the rest
   falling outside the fetched range. The payroll is not *near* the refunding week; it is reliably
   **inside** it, in the same slot, between the announcement and the auctions. For 2027 that slot is
   **2027-05-11 … 2027-05-13**.

3. **Looming refunding supply does not change the print-day reaction — REFUTED, cleanly, on seven
   instruments.** Median absolute close-to-close move of the print session itself:

   | Instrument | **R: refunding-week payroll** | **O: ordinary payroll Friday** | Ratio | Permutation *p* |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | TLT | 0.786% | 0.764% | 1.03× | 0.791 |
   | IEF | 0.408% | 0.394% | 1.03× | 0.830 |
   | SHY | 0.071% | 0.073% | 0.97× | 0.875 |
   | LQD | 0.340% | 0.354% | 0.96× | 0.739 |
   | AGG | 0.259% | 0.238% | 1.09× | 0.723 |
   | SPY | 0.653% | 0.649% | 1.01× | 0.982 |
   | GLD | 0.612% | 0.735% | 0.83× | 0.481 |

   *p* is a two-sided permutation test on the difference of medians, 20,000 relabelings. **Not one
   instrument clears 0.05, and the ratios straddle 1.0 in both directions.** With seven tests at
   α=0.05 roughly one false positive was *expected* and none appeared — this is a null with teeth,
   not an underpowered shrug. The intuition that a payroll lands harder when $100bn+ of supply is
   four days out is simply wrong on this sample.

4. **The carry window that contains the auctions looks compressed — MIXED, and it fails the control
   that matters.** Measured payroll close → close of the first session after the 30-year auction
   (median **5 sessions**), against a matched 5-session window on ordinary payrolls:

   | Instrument | **A: R's supply window** | **O: ordinary** | A/O | *p* | **B: same month, next window** | A/B | *p* |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | IEF | 0.435% | 0.600% | **0.72×** | **0.016** | 0.534% | 0.81× | 0.288 |
   | AGG | 0.279% | 0.408% | **0.68×** | **0.015** | 0.316% | 0.88× | 0.510 |
   | LQD | 0.433% | 0.564% | **0.77×** | **0.040** | 0.605% | **0.72×** | **0.031** |
   | TLT | 0.997% | 1.224% | 0.81× | 0.193 | 1.153% | 0.87× | 0.709 |
   | SPY | 1.015% | 1.230% | 0.83× | 0.120 | 1.270% | 0.80× | 0.223 |

   Against the pooled baseline three of five clear 0.05. Against **B** — same month, same season,
   auctions already done — **only LQD survives**, and one hit at *p*=0.031 across five tests is what
   chance produces. The direction is admittedly consistent (every ratio below 1.0 in both columns),
   but consistency is not significance at n=93. **Graded MIXED and registered as
   FT-jobs-2027-05-07-2 rather than carried into the stance.**

5. **The season is not the explanation either — and that is what makes leg 4 honest rather than
   merely negative.** If Feb/May/Aug/Nov were simply quiet months, set B would also be small against
   O. It is not: **B vs O** returns *p* = 0.58 (TLT), 0.32 (IEF), 0.11 (AGG), 0.60 (LQD), 0.87 (SPY),
   ratios 0.78–1.07. So the correct reading of leg 4 is neither "it is a seasonal" nor "it is real"
   — it is **under-determined at this n**, which is precisely why it goes to the register.

6. **The refunding auction dates are NOT reliably derivable, so none is proposed — SUPPORTED, and
   recorded as a hole.** Two candidate rules were tested against Treasury's 122 refunding 10-year
   auctions: a settlement-anchored rule (the last Wednesday on or before the 13th, since refunding
   issues settle the 15th) scores **88/122**, and the modal weekday pattern is only **90/111 Tue**
   (3y), **98/122 Wed** (10y), **82/89 Thu** (30y). Veterans Day and short weeks break both. A
   68–82% rule is not a date. **What survives is a corridor, not a date:** leg 2's 116-of-120 puts the
   2027 auctions in **2027-05-11 … 2027-05-13** at ~97%, and a corridor cannot be a calendar entry.
   Following [`jobs-2027-07-02`](jobs-2027-07-02.md)'s precedent — *a recorded hole beats a
   fabricated date* — this is written down and nothing is filed.

7. **It is the FIRST payroll after the April FOMC, not the last before the June one — SUPPORTED, and
   it corrects the natural assumption.** `fomc-2027-04-28` (est) is **D-9**; `fomc-2027-06-09` (est)
   is **D+33**. The same 7-for-7 rule puts the May 2027 Employment Situation on **2027-06-04** — five
   days before that meeting and *inside* the blackout opening at `fomc-blackout-start-2027-05-29`
   (est). So this print gets a full month of on-the-record Fed reaction and its successor gets none;
   the two are mirror images. **Proposed in this PR** as
   `proposals/jobs-2027-06-04.from-jobs-2027-05-07.json`, `status: estimate` — at D+28 it is outside
   the 5-day corridor and proposed anyway, because it is the fact that makes this leg checkable.

8. **The 30-year auction session is not itself a volatility event — REFUTED.** Median absolute move
   on the refunding 30y auction session against a matched control five sessions later: TLT
   **0.542%** vs 0.675% (*p*=0.263), IEF 0.303% vs 0.284% (*p*=0.698), AGG 0.163% vs 0.175%
   (*p*=0.755), LQD 0.266% vs 0.269% (*p*=0.778), SPY 0.603% vs 0.521% (*p*=0.638). All five null,
   and TLT's point estimate is *below* its control. Whatever the auction does to the curve, it does
   not show up as a bigger day in these ETFs.

9. **Direction is not predictable — SUPPORTED as a refusal.** How often the carry window continues
   the print-day sign: TLT **42/93** (*p*=0.41), IEF 39/92 (*p*=0.18), AGG 43/88 (*p*=0.92), LQD
   46/92 (*p*=1.00), SPY 62/116 (*p*=0.52), GLD 47/83 (*p*=0.27), SHY 39/89 (*p*=0.29). Nothing
   clears, and the print-oriented signed carry is statistically indistinguishable between R and O
   everywhere (TLT −0.217% vs −0.012%, *p*=0.55). This replicates
   [`jobs-2027-07-02`](jobs-2027-07-02.md)'s directional refusal on an entirely different geometry.

10. **The 2027 coverage gap is real, and re-verified rather than inherited — SUPPORTED.** Enumerated
    this session: **87** canonical `macro-print` entries dated 2027+, and **zero** US CPI, PPI, JOLTS,
    ADP, ECI, retail-sales or PCE among them. Every US hard-data series this calendar carries stops

    `retail-sales-2026-12-16`, `pce-2026-12-23`). The consequence here is a **refusal, not a claim**:
    the run-up to 2027-05-07 looks emptier on our board than it is in the world — the April 2027 CPI
    lands in it — and CPI publishes no rule this lane can derive a date from.

11. **Nothing in the house system is calendar- or print-keyed — SUPPORTED, re-verified not

### What the conditions support

Nothing directional, and — unusually — the deliverable is mostly a set of *removals*. Three stories a
reasonable person would carry into this date are now measured and gone: that the payroll lands harder
because supply is four days out (leg 3, seven-for-seven null), that the auction session is a risk
event in its own right (leg 8, five-for-five null), and that the print sets the tone for the auction
week (leg 9, coin flip). What is left is one genuine structure — the payroll reliably occupies the
slot between the announcement and the auctions, 116 times in 120 — and one under-determined
observation about that window's width, which is now a registered test rather than a belief. For a
paper-options book that means the refunding calendar is **not** a reason to size 2027-05-07
differently from any other payroll, in either direction. The honest position is that this is an
ordinary tier-1 print in an unusually legible calendar slot.

### Honest limits

D-240, and the biggest limit is in the date field: **the primary is unretrievable**, so a 7-for-7
derivation stands in for a published schedule. Leg 4's n is **93** and its collinearity with the
quarter-opening months cannot be fully broken by any control available — set B narrows it and does not
eliminate it, which is why the leg is MIXED and not REFUTED. The permutation tests are two-sided on
medians and make **no** multiple-comparison correction; leg 3's value comes from *no* instrument
clearing rather than from any single *p*. The ETF sample begins where each fund does (TLT/IEF/LQD 2002,
AGG 2003, GLD 2004), so the "1993–2026" span is real only for SPY — the bond legs are a 2002+ study
and inherit that era's vol regime (TLT's supply-window median is 0.637% pre-2010 against 1.150%
after). Treasury's API gives auction dates, not announcement dates, so the "two days after the
announcement" framing rests on `treasury-refunding-2027-05-05`, itself an `estimate`. The FOMC dates
in leg 7 are `estimate` too. No consensus, whisper or projection for April 2027 payrolls exists this
far out and none will for many months. No price instruments were run (macro-print mode carries none).

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `estimate`, derived and 7-for-7 validated, primary 403 on 2026-09-09).** Treat
2027-05-07 08:30 ET as a **high-impact known-date variance event with no supply premium attached**.
Its distinguishing feature is positional and now measured: it occupies the recurring slot between the
May quarterly refunding announcement (`treasury-refunding-2027-05-05`, est, D−2) and the 3y/10y/30y
auctions that sell what the announcement sizes (D+4…D+6) — a slot **116 of 120** refunding-month
payrolls have occupied since 1993. **That proximity is not tradable.** Refunding-week payrolls move
**0.96–1.09×** an ordinary payroll Friday across all seven instruments tested (*p*=0.47–0.98); the
30-year auction session is not a volatility event (five of five null); and the print's direction does
not carry into the auction window (TLT 42/93, *p*=0.41). The one finding that looked real — a
compressed carry window — **fails its within-season control in four of five instruments** and is
registered as a forward test, not carried here. **Stand aside on every horizon.** This is also the
**first** payroll after `fomc-2027-04-28` (est), not the last before `fomc-2027-06-09` (est) — that
is **2027-06-04** (est, proposed in this PR), which lands inside the blackout. Every line here is
`estimate`-labelled and none licenses an entry.

**Kill switches:**

- **bls.gov's 2027 schedule becomes retrievable and names a date other than 2027-05-07** — kills the
  whole document; leg 2's slot arithmetic and every measurement keyed to the refunding week go with
  it. Re-attempt the three blocked routes on every pulse; this is the first thing each pulse does.
- **Treasury announces a May 2027 refunding auction outside 2027-05-10 … 2027-05-14** — leg 2's
  116-of-120 slot does not hold for this instance and the whole "payroll inside the refunding week"
  premise fails on the one occasion it is being applied. Checkable from the same primary API that
  established it. Registered as **FT-jobs-2027-05-07-1**.
- **All three of IEF / AGG / LQD exceed their ordinary-payroll 5-session medians (0.600% / 0.408% /
  0.564%) over 2027-05-07 → the session after the 30y auction** — the leg-4 compression failed its one
  out-of-sample observation, and MIXED resolves toward refuted. Registered as
  **FT-jobs-2027-05-07-2**.
- **TLT's 2027-05-07 absolute move exceeds 1.57%** (2× the 0.786% refunding-week median) — the
  seven-instrument null in leg 3 meets a print that does behave like a supply event, and the
  month-horizon refusal needs re-reading. Registered as **FT-jobs-2027-05-07-3**.
- **A US CPI or PPI entry dated 2027-04-01 … 2027-05-07 lands on this calendar** — leg 10's coverage
  gap narrows and the run-up is no longer being read through a hole. Route the finding, do not defend
  the sentence.
- **The refunding cycle changes shape between now and 2027-05** (a tenor added or dropped, a move off
  the Tue/Wed/Thu block, a buyback programme rerouting supply) — leg 2 is an empirical regularity of
  the current issuance regime, not a law, and a regime change invalidates it without warning.
- **The stance starts predicting a direction** — nothing in this document fixes the *sign* of a
  reaction and nothing should try to at D-240. That drift is itself the kill switch.
