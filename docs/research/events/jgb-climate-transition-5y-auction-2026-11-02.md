## At a glance

**TL;DR.** Japan sells ¥250bn of 5-year Climate Transition (GX) paper on **2026-11-02**, three days
after the BoJ decides and one day before the US midterms. **Nothing here is ours to trade**, and the
single most useful thing this ledger does is tell you **not to read the number the tape will quote.**
The proposing ledger created this entry to get "a second, differently-structured demand read" out of
cover and tail. **Both halves fail, from MOF's own primaries.** The GX auction is **Dutch-style
uniform-price** — every one of its twelve announcement pages says so, the regular 5-year's does not —
so all accepted bids clear at one yield and **a tail is identically zero by construction**: the
≥2bp / per-tenor-p90 apparatus four sibling ledgers built is **undefined here, not unpublished**. And
**cover on this line is a size artifact**: across the ten non-inaugural GX auctions yen bid volume is
near-constant at **¥1,115.3bn (sd 137.5, cv 12.3%)** while the offering fell **¥350 → ¥250bn
(−28.6%)**, giving **corr(offer, cover) = −0.478**. At the 5-year, cover rose **3.192 → 4.622
(+44.8%)** while bids rose **+3.5%**. What the entry *is* worth: it is the **first Japanese coupon
auction after the 2026-10-30 BoJ decision**; it is a **same-maturity twin of the regular 5-year, 5 of
5**, which puts issue 6 at **2031-09-20**; and matched on that maturity against MOF's own par curve
there is **no greenium — −0.64bp mean, t = −0.43, n=5.** Date and size both **estimate**; they widen
caution and license nothing.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

[`jgb-5y-auction-2026-11-12`](jgb-5y-auction-2026-11-12.md) proposed this entry on 2026-09-09 and
wrote its job description into the proposal:

> It also gives the 5-year sector a second, differently-structured demand read: across the six
> auctions of its five issues since 2024-02 the GX 5-year has covered 3.19x to 4.62x on offerings a
> fraction the regular auction's size, so a weak GX print alongside a normal regular print (or the
> reverse) separates "the 5-year sector is fine" from "the regular 5-year's bid is fine."

So: **can this auction actually deliver that read?**

**One-line verdict:** **no — and the reason is structural, not statistical.** The GX auction is
Dutch-style uniform-price, so it has no tail to report; and its cover ratio, the one number it does
report, moves with MOF's offering size rather than with demand. What the entry is worth instead is
three things the proposal did not know it had: it is the first coupon auction after the BoJ, it is a
same-maturity twin of the regular 5-year in 5 of 5 quarters, and that twinning makes it the cleanest
greenium test available on Japanese sovereign paper — which comes back **zero**.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The tail does not exist here, and it is a format fact rather than a publication gap — REFUTED
for the proposal, and this is the leg that reaches furthest.** Every one of the twelve GX
announcement pages ends with the same note, verbatim:

> \* The auction method is Dutch-style-yield-competitive auction at intervals of 0.1bp.

The regular 5-year's announcement for the 2026-05-18 auction (`auct20260511e.htm`, HTTP 200, read
this session in full) carries **no such note** — the note exists on the GX pages precisely because
the GX auction departs from MOF's default conventional multiple-price format. The consequence shows
up on every GX result page: all twelve print `-` for **Weighted Average Price** and `-` for **Yield
at the Average Price**, while the regular 5-year pages print both (e.g. 2026-05-18: lowest accepted
99.85 / 2.034%, weighted average 99.89 / 2.024% — a 1.0bp tail). **In a uniform-price auction every
accepted bid clears at the same yield, so the average IS the marginal and a tail is identically zero
by construction.** This is not a missing field to be recovered from another source; it is a
quantity that does not exist for this security. **The ≥2bp threshold, the per-tenor pre-2024 p90s,
the scale-free deterioration metric — the whole apparatus four sibling ledgers built — is undefined
on this auction.** Registered as `FT-jgb-climate-transition-5y-auction-2026-11-02-3`.

**2. The cover ratio on this line is a size artifact — REFUTED as a demand read.** The proposal
quoted the 5-year's cover range (3.19x–4.62x) as evidence of a usable demand signal. It is an
arithmetic consequence of MOF cutting the offering:

| Series | corr(offer size, cover) | corr(offer size, bids) |
|---|---|---|
| GX 5-year (n=6) | **−0.473** | +0.979 |
| GX 10-year (n=6) | **−0.877** | +0.998 |
| Both (n=12) | **−0.478** | +0.970 |

The positive bid correlation is entirely the inaugural ¥800bn pair. Drop those two and look at the
**ten auctions offered between ¥250bn and ¥350bn**: yen bid volume is near-constant at **¥1,115.3bn,
sd ¥137.5, cv 12.3%**, while the offering fell **¥350bn → ¥250bn, −28.6%**. At the 5-year
specifically, from 2025-01-29 to 2026-05-25 **cover rose 3.192 → 4.622 (+44.8%) while bids rose
¥1,116.4 → ¥1,155.6bn (+3.5%)**. And the artifact was caught in the act three auctions ago: on
**2026-08-24 the GX 10-year drew ¥875.9bn — the lowest bid volume in the programme's history — and
printed cover 3.505, its second-best ever.** **Cover here measures the denominator.** The honest
metric is the yen bid volume, and on that metric GX demand has been flat to falling, not improving.
Registered as `FT-jgb-climate-transition-5y-auction-2026-11-02-5`.

### Honest limits

- **The greenium test is n=5, and it is not a controlled experiment.** Five matched pairs, each with
  a 7-to-18-day gap between the two auctions. The par-curve netting removes the market's level move
  and the small maturity difference; it does not remove anything that moved the 5-year *sector's*
  auction concession between the two dates.
- **The Dutch-vs-conventional format difference is an unremoved confound in leg 6, and it is the
  reason that call is Medium rather than High.** A uniform-price auction reduces the incentive to
  shade bids away from true value, which should push the GX clearing yield *down* relative to a
  conventional auction of the same paper — i.e. the format effect and a genuine greenium have the
  same sign, and this session cannot separate them. What can be said is that the *sum* of the two is
  indistinguishable from zero, which bounds the greenium alone at no more than a few basis points.
- **Par-curve interpolation between the 4Y and 5Y grid points introduces error.** The GX bonds sit at
  4.81–4.95 years, close to the 5Y node, so the error is small — but it is not zero, and it is
  applied identically to both legs of each pair, which is what makes the *difference* robust rather
  than the levels.
- **The cover ratios of the GX and the regular 5-year are computed identically (bids ÷ competitive
  accepted) and are still not comparable**, because the format difference changes how much bidders
  submit. The 2026-05 pair illustrates it: the GX drew 18.6% of the regular's bid volume for 12.9%
  of its competitive size, which reads as stronger demand and may be nothing but the absence of
  winner's-curse shading.
- **"Issue 6" is a 5-of-6 base case, not a rule.** The GX line has opened a new issue number at every
  auction except 2025-01-29. One counter-example in six is a much weaker record than the maturity's
  5 of 5, and the maturity is the prediction that carries weight.
- **The maturity prediction inherits the 11-12 sibling's chain.** 2031-09-20 for 2026Q4 rests on the
  regular 5-year's quarter map, which the sibling measured at 34 of 35 — and on the 2026-10-14
  auction actually opening that maturity, which has not happened. If the sibling's chain breaks,
  this prediction breaks with it, and the two forward tests will fail together rather than
  independently.
- **The programme's fiscal-year size constant rests on two completed fiscal years.** FY2024 at
  ¥350bn and FY2025 at ¥300bn is 8 of 8, and FY2026 is 2 of 2 — but two completed years is two
  observations of the *policy*, however many auctions sit inside them. A mid-year change has never
  happened; it has also never had many chances to.
- **FY2026's fourth GX auction is expected in Feb/Mar 2027 and is deliberately not proposed**, because
  MOF has published no 2027 calendar (`2612e.htm` already 404s) and this lane does not propose an
  event without a date from a primary.
- **The BoJ figure is a one-month spot check, not the 32-workbook series.** `ope2608.xlsx` reproduces
  the sibling's ¥641.0bn exactly, which is what a spot check is for; the −62.3% path itself is the
  sibling's measurement, cited rather than re-derived here.
- **Whether the BoJ actually buys GX paper in its 3–5y operations is inferred, not observed.** The
  operation's exclusion list covers only floating-rate and inflation-indexed bonds, so a fixed-coupon
  GX bond is eligible by construction — but BoJ publishes bucket totals, not issue-level purchases,
  so no GX bond has been *seen* in an operation.
- **The auction result time is derived, not sourced.** MOF's announcement pages carry no time field,
  which this session checked directly; the ~12:35 JST convention inherits the limit every sibling
  records.
- **The auction result does not exist**, and neither does its size or its issue number. Everything
  here is prior.
- **VIX and USD/JPY are Yahoo, i.e. secondary**, and both 09-09 readings are intraday. MOF's par
  curve runs through **2026-09-08**.
- **The date and the size are both `estimate`.** MOF is a primary and the date is not in doubt; the
  label reflects an unannounced size plus a gap in `market-events-data.ts`'s prefix taxonomy, which
  has no slot for a non-US sovereign debt office. Estimates widen caution and license nothing.
