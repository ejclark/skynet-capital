## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Both sibling proposals called this "the latest December 20-Year on record." That is true and it
understates the fact by a wide margin — and the story they attached to it is wrong.** Measured this session
across the full nominal-coupon census (Treasury's own `auctions_query`, Notes + Bonds, 2010-01-11 onward,
**1,281** auctions after excluding TIPS and FRNs): **no 10-Year, 20-Year or 30-Year has ever auctioned on or
after December 22 — not once, in seventeen years.** The latest long-end December auction on record is the
**21st**. So 2026-12-23 is not a late instance of a known slot; it is the **first observation of a slot that
has never existed**. But the proposals' *mechanism* — "the thinnest dealer-balance-sheet week of the year" —
is refuted by the same pull: **37 of 103** December coupon auctions (36%) sold on or after the 23rd, and
Treasury has run a 2-Year/5-Year/7-Year block in that terminal week for **16 consecutive years without a
break**. The week is Treasury's most reliably-used December week. What is unprecedented is putting
**duration** in it. Three further tests: the long end shows **no December penalty at all** (z = **+0.118**,
n=36, t=0.62); the "later in December is worse" dose-response is **untestable** because inside December date
and tenor are perfectly collinear (Dec 1-14 is 100% 10Y/30Y, Dec 15-31 is 100% 20-Year); and the one live
tilt — the constant-$13B **first**-reopening slot has printed December in the bottom of its own band **3 for
3** (ranks 1, 3, 4 of 12) — **does not survive the full history**, because the two pre-$13B Decembers were
*strong* (size-neutral z **+1.38**, **+1.19**). Net: this calendar has been grading the December 20-Year
against the wrong band. It is a **leg-1 (first) reopening** — floor **2.50** · p25 **2.64** · median **2.74**

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This event arrived on 2026-09-09 as **two** proposals from two different sweeps — the successor sale
[`treasury-20y-bond-2027-01-20`](treasury-20y-bond-2027-01-20.md), which wanted its own predecessor on the
calendar, and the same-day [`treasury-2y-frn-2026-12-23`](treasury-2y-frn-2026-12-23.md), whose 11:30 a.m.
closing-time call depends on this security taking the 1:00 p.m. coupon slot. Both were read in full before
anything here was written, per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md); the canonical
`src/domain/market-events/treasury-20y-bond-2026-12-23.json` is written in this PR and both proposals are now
shadowed and inert.

Between them they had already fixed the date, the reopening marker, the 1:00 p.m. deadline and the $13B size
expectation. What they also both did — in near-identical words — was attach a **story**: 12-23 is *"the
latest December 20-Year on record,"* selling into *"the thinnest dealer-balance-sheet week of the year,"* and
therefore *"a poor baseline"* whose print *"must not be read as the cycle's demand level."*

So the question this session asked was the one that pays: **is that caution measured, or is it a plausible
story that two sweeps repeated to each other?**

**One-line verdict:** the caution is repeating an *understatement* wrapped around a *refuted mechanism*. The
unprecedented fact is far stronger than either proposal claimed — no long-duration coupon has *ever* sold
this late in December — but the "thin week" reasoning behind the caution is wrong, and the only demand tilt
that survives any test is a three-observation, one-regime pattern that reverses on the full history.

### Method

Sourced primary research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md) (rates mode: no price
instruments apply, `symbols: []`). Three primaries, all fetched direct this session, all HTTP 200:

- **Tentative Auction Schedule PDF** (home.treasury.gov, 17,195 bytes), text layer inflated stream-by-stream
  and glyph tokens reassembled into 909 ordered lines — an **independent third read** of the primary both
  proposing sweeps used, not a carry-forward of their reading.
- **fiscaldata `auctions_query`, 20-Year** (`original_security_term:eq:20-Year`, 2020-01-01 onward,
  **77** auctions, 275,209 bytes) — the tenor's full history since its 2020-05 revival.
- **fiscaldata `auctions_query`, the whole nominal-coupon census** (`security_type:in:(Note,Bond)`,
  2010-01-01 onward, two paged fetches, **1,633** rows → **1,281** nominal auctions after excluding TIPS and
  FRNs, 2010-01-11 → 2026-09-10). This is the pull that makes legs 2-6 possible; every prior 20-Year ledger
  here worked inside the 77-auction 20-Year file alone, which cannot answer a question about *other tenors'*
  December behaviour.
- **Daily par yield curve (CMT)** 2026 CSV (14,042 bytes) for levels; **VIX** from Yahoo's chart API.

**The demand metric.** Bid-to-cover is not comparable across tenors, sizes or decades, so every cross-slot
comparison below uses a **within-slot z-score against a local baseline**: the trailing **12** auctions of the
same `(original_security_term, reopening)` slot, strictly prior, requiring at least 8 priors — which scores
**1,184** of the 1,279 usable auctions and leaves each slot's earliest prints unscored rather than compared
to a mean they helped set. Records with under $1B competitive tendered are dropped as non-results (this
removes exactly one row, the anomalous **2021-12-02** 20-Year the 01-20 sibling also identified).

All percentages are on the **competitive** base (indirect + direct + dealer); dividing by `total_accepted`
folds in SOMA add-ons and gives materially different numbers.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The date, the reopening marker and the `estimate` status are right — SUPPORTED.** The schedule PDF
carries the block verbatim: `20-Year  BOND` / `R` / `Thursday, December 17, 2026` / `Wednesday, December 23,
2026` / `Monday, December 28, 2026`. The same fetch shows `20-Year  BOND` **without** the `R` on 2026-11-18
and `20-Year  BOND` + `R` again on 2027-01-20 — which fixes this sale as the **first** reopening of the
November CUSIP, the fact leg 7 turns on. The 1:00 p.m. deadline comes from `closing_time_comp`, `01:00 PM` on
all 77 auctions in the 20-Year pull. `estimate` is correct on both stated counts, and the confirming primary
is the 2026-12-17 announcement — which was not on this calendar until this PR (leg 10).

**2. "The latest December 20-Year on record" is true and understates it — SUPPORTED, and this is the
finding.** Across the full 2010-2026 nominal-coupon census (n=1,281), the count of **10-Year, 20-Year or
30-Year** auctions held on or after **December 22** is **zero**. The latest long-end December auction ever
recorded is **December 21**, hit three times (2020-12-21, 2021-12-21, 2022-12-21 — all 20-Year). The full
December long-end day-of-month distribution:

| Dec day | 2 | 8 | 9 | 10 | 11 | 12 | 13 | 14 | 17 | 20 | 21 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| long-end auctions | 1 | 2 | 5 | 4 | 6 | 9 | 5 | 1 | 2 | 1 | 3 |

Nothing past the 21st, ever. The proposals framed 12-23 as an extreme value of a known distribution; it is
**outside the support of that distribution entirely**. That is a materially different claim, and it is the
reason this event earns its own ledger rather than a line in a sibling's.

**3. The "thinnest dealer-balance-sheet week of the year" premise is wrong about the week — REFUTED.** The
same census says the December terminal week is Treasury's *most reliably used* December week: **37 of 103**
December coupon auctions (36%) sold on or after the 23rd, and the 2-Year/5-Year/7-Year block has run there in
**every one of the 16 years** 2010-2025 (2010-12-27/28/29 · 2014-12-22/23/24 · 2015-12-28/29/30 · … ·
2024-12-23/24/26 · 2025-12-22/23/24). This calendar already tracks this cycle's as
[`treasury-2y-note-2026-12-28`](treasury-2y-note-2026-12-28.md),
[`treasury-5y-note-2026-12-28`](treasury-5y-note-2026-12-28.md) and
[`treasury-7y-note-2026-12-29`](treasury-7y-note-2026-12-29.md). Whatever is unusual about 2026-12-23, it is
not that Treasury is selling into a week nobody sells into. **Recorded because the wrong mechanism generates
the wrong watch-list** — a "thin week" story tells the next session to watch balance-sheet and repo
conditions; the correct story (leg 2) tells it to watch whether *duration* clears in a week only front-end
paper has ever had to.

**4. The terminal week does look expensive for the tenors that have been there — MIXED, and it cannot be
cleanly attributed.** Within-slot z(bid-to-cover) for 2/5/7-Year auctions:

| Sample | n | z(bid-to-cover) | vs control | t |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2/5/7Y, December, day ≥ 22 (the terminal block) | 38 | **−0.511** | −0.447 | −1.86 |
| 2/5/7Y, December, day < 22 | 9 | −0.447 | −0.382 | −1.07 |
| 2/5/7Y, all December | 47 | −0.499 | −0.434 | −2.11 |
| 2/5/7Y, all other months (control) | 514 | −0.065 | — | — |

Roughly **half a standard deviation** of cover, with dealer take moving the other way (+0.24 to +0.33 z, all
non-significant). But the terminal-week figure (−0.511) and the early-December figure (−0.447) are
**indistinguishable at n=9**, so this sample cannot say whether the cost belongs to the *week* or to
*December*. Reported as MIXED rather than promoted, because the difference is exactly what would license or
kill the proposals' caution.

**5. The long end carries no December penalty at all — SUPPORTED, and it cuts against the caution.**
10/20/30-Year December auctions score z = **+0.118** (n=36) against **−0.044** for the same tenors in all
other months — t = **0.62**, and the sign is *positive*. Month by month across all 1,184 scored nominal
coupons, December is **−0.198 ± 0.144**, inside noise; the actual monthly outlier is **January** (+0.301 ±
0.129). So the half-sigma of leg 4 is a property of the 2/5/7 block, not of the calendar month, and the
tenor most like the one selling on 2026-12-23 shows nothing.

**6. The tempting dose-response — "the later in December, the worse" — is untestable here, and the naive
version of it is a tenor effect in disguise — REFUTED.** Regressing z(bid-to-cover) on day-of-month within
long-end December auctions gives slope **+0.044** z per day (t = **0.63**, r = 0.108) — nothing, and pointing
the *wrong way*. Worse, the binned version that would look like evidence is structurally void: inside
December the long end's date and tenor are **perfectly collinear** — Dec 1-14 is 30 auctions, 100% 10-Year
and 30-Year; Dec 15-31 is 5 auctions, **100% 20-Year**. Any "late December is different" number computed from
that split is reporting the 20-Year, not the date. Named explicitly because it is the first regression a
later session would reach for, and it would have produced a publishable-looking result.

**7. This calendar has been grading the December 20-Year against the wrong band — SUPPORTED, and it is a gap
rather than a correction of anyone.** The 20-Year cycle sells new issues in Feb/May/Aug/Nov, first reopenings
in Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec and second reopenings in Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct — so **every December 20-Year on record is a
first (leg-1) reopening**, and 2026-12-23 is one too. Splitting the 24 constant-$13B reopenings by leg:

| Slot | n | min | p25 | median | p75 | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **leg 1 — first reopening (this event)** | 12 | **2.50** | **2.64** | **2.74** | **2.75** | **2.79** |
| leg 2 — second reopening ([01-20's band](treasury-20y-bond-2027-01-20.md)) | 12 | 2.53 | 2.62 | 2.68 | 2.76 | 2.86 |

The first-reopening body sits **6bp of ratio higher at the median and is much tighter** (p25→p75 spans 0.11
against 0.14). A grader reaching for the published second-reopening band would set the bar too low. Same
split on direct share of the competitive base: leg 1 is floor **16.3%** · p25 **19.2%** · median **20.4%** ·
p75 **22.3%** · ceiling **27.9%**, against leg 2's 12.3 / 16.0 / 18.9 / 22.1 / 29.1. The
[11-18 sibling](treasury-20y-bond-2026-11-18.md) already warned that the reopening yardstick must not be
carried onto a new issue; this is the same warning one level finer, and neither sibling had cause to derive
it.

**8. The December first-reopening tilt is real in the $13B era and reverses on the full history — SUPPORTED
then REFUTED, and this is the one live grading question.** Within the 12 constant-$13B first reopenings,
sorted by quarter-month:

| Slot month | n | bid-to-cover | mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| March | 3 | 2.79, 2.78, 2.76 | **2.777** |
| June | 3 | 2.74, 2.68, 2.75 | 2.723 |
| September | 3 | 2.74, 2.51, 2.74 | 2.663 |
| **December** | 3 | 2.55, 2.50, 2.67 | **2.573** |

Monotone, and the three Decembers occupy ranks **1, 3 and 4** of the twelve. Each December also prints below
the *following* March, **3 for 3** (2.55 < 2.79 · 2.50 < 2.78 · 2.67 < 2.76). Exact probability that three
randomly drawn auctions all land in the bottom four of twelve is **0.0182** — but this month was selected
*after* looking, and there are four months to select from, so the honest adjusted figure is ≈ **0.073**.

**And then it fails the wider test.** Size-neutral, using the same trailing-slot z as everywhere else, all
six December 20-Year prints:

| Date | offering | bid-to-cover | z vs own slot | dealer share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2020-12-21 | $24B | 2.39 | *(baseline too short)* | 26.0% |
| 2021-12-21 | $20B | 2.59 | **+1.38** | 14.3% |
| 2022-12-21 | $12B | 2.68 | **+1.19** | 8.7% |
| 2023-12-20 | $13B | 2.55 | −0.23 | 12.9% |
| 2024-12-17 | $13B | 2.50 | **−1.47** | 17.9% |
| 2025-12-17 | $13B | 2.67 | −0.05 | 12.6% |

The two pre-$13B Decembers were **strong**, and the five-observation mean is **+0.16**. So the tilt exists
only inside one issuance regime and on three observations. It is registered as a hypothesis
([FT-treasury-20y-bond-2026-12-23-1](../forward-tests/treasury-20y-bond-2026-12-23.md)), **not** applied as a
discount — which is the narrow, evidence-backed version of what the proposals asked for. **And the elevated
December dealer take in that table is not a second witness:** r(bid-to-cover, dealer share) = −0.849 at this
tenor per the 01-20 sibling, so 17.9% against a leg-1 median of 9.3% is the 2.50 cover restated in different
units.

**9. Composition does *not* share the tilt — SUPPORTED, and it is the genuinely second dimension.** Direct
share on the three constant-size Decembers is **20.7% · 20.1% · 22.2%**, all at or above the leg-1 p25 of
19.2% and two of three at or above the median. So whatever December costs the ratio, it is not showing up as
investors stepping away — it is showing up as dealers absorbing. That split is registered as
[FT-treasury-20y-bond-2026-12-23-2](../forward-tests/treasury-20y-bond-2026-12-23.md), and it is close to
independent of FT-1 (the 01-20 sibling measured r(bid-to-cover, direct share) = +0.384, non-significant), so
the two together are two observations rather than one.

**10. The adjacency corridor is the densest of any 20-Year auction this calendar tracks — SUPPORTED, and it
is why the print will be hard to read.** Twenty-three tracked events sit within five days. The auction's own
session carries `pce-2026-12-23` (**high**), `gdp-q3-2026-third-2026-12-23`, `durable-goods-2026-12-23` and
`new-home-sales-2026-12-23` between 08:30 and 10:00 ET, the **2-Year FRN reopening at 11:30**, a 17-week bill,
and BoJ minutes — with this sale bidding at 1:00 p.m., **after all of it**. The day-to-day close is therefore
a joint observation of the macro block and this auction and cannot be attributed to either alone; the honest
read of this print is its **auction statistics**, not its session. The next day is the Christmas Eve half day
plus the 12-24 coupon announcement, and the 2/5/7 terminal block follows on 12-28/29. **One dated adjacency
was missing and is proposed in this PR:** `treasury-coupon-announcement-2026-12-17` — this event's *own*
announcement, and the sole primary that would flip it to `confirmed` and publish its size. The calendar
tracks the 12-03 and 12-24 announcements on either side of it and had no row for the one in between.
`medium` remains right: structural density, no same-session Fed release, `symbols: []`.

**11. Current conditions are logged and are close to worthless at D-105 — stated rather than dressed up.**
CMT at the 2026-09-08 close: 2Y **4.39** · 5Y **4.57** · 10Y **4.80** · 20Y **5.26** · 30Y **5.25**;
**20s30s +1bp**, unchanged in character from the readings the 10-21 and 01-20 siblings logged on 09-04 and
09-08. VIX **16.53** (2026-09-09, Yahoo chart API) against **15.72** on 09-08 and **14.53** on 09-04 —
**+2.00 over five sessions**, still inside the 3-point screen threshold but the largest move any 20-Year

baseline for that pulse, not information about 2026-12-23; at least two FOMC meetings resolve in between.

None. `symbols: []`, the date is `estimate`, no house playbook is rates-keyed, and legs 5-6 say the event's

### Honest limits

- **The headline fact is an absence, and an absence has no base rate.** Leg 2 says no long-end coupon has
  ever sold on or after December 22. That means the population for "how does duration clear in the terminal
  week" is **n = 0**. Nothing here forecasts this print; the doc says which yardstick applies and what to
  watch, and is explicit that it cannot say more.
- **Leg 8 rests on three observations and was selected post-hoc.** The 0.0182 exact probability is reported
  *with* its four-month multiplicity adjustment (≈0.073) precisely because the tempting move was to report
  the unadjusted number. It is registered as a forward test for exactly this reason.
- **The census excludes bills, TIPS and FRNs.** "No long-end auction after December 22" is a claim about
  **nominal coupons**. The 5-Year TIPS reopening on 2026-12-22 and the 2-Year FRN on 12-23 are outside it by
  construction, as are every bill.
- **The 20-Year has only six Decembers, ever.** The tenor did not exist between 1987 and its 2020-05
  revival, so its seasonal record is six auctions across two issuance regimes — which is why leg 8 splits
  rather than pools.
- **The trailing-12 baseline leaves the earliest prints in each slot unscored** (1,184 of 1,279 usable), and
  it is a *local* baseline, so a slot in a slow multi-year drift would show z ≈ 0 throughout. It controls for
  regime, not for trend.
- **Correlations and t-statistics here are not causal and carry no multiple-comparison correction** beyond
  the one stated in leg 8. The leg-5 null (t = 0.62) and the leg-6 null (t = 0.63) are reported as failures
  to find an effect, never as proof of absence.
- **No when-issued yield and no tail** — inherited: `auctions_query` publishes high, median and low award
  yields but not the WI level, so the single most diagnostic auction statistic still cannot be computed from
  the primary. Every tail figure this calendar carries remains press-sourced.
- **The size is not published anywhere yet.** $13B is a base rate, not a source, until the 2026-11-04
  refunding statement; every band here assumes it and dies with it (leg 7's kill switch).
- **The date itself is `estimate`.** The tentative schedule is tentative, and the fact that this slot is
  unprecedented (leg 2) is, if anything, a mild reason to think Treasury could still move it — which is one
  more argument for watching 2026-12-17 rather than pricing anything today.
