### Leg 2 — the nowcast footprint · **REFUTED as inherited**: the corridor's number does not apply to this print

This is the leg that makes the edition distinguishable, and it reverses the sibling lane's implicit prior.

**The mechanism is settled and inherited, not re-litigated.** The 12-17 ledger established that
`SplicedNewHousingConstruction` is *construction spending's* ticker (VPIP / C30) and that housing starts
enters GDPNow through the **activity factor**, supplying nine series in `FactorAugARCoeffs`. This session
reproduces its contribution figures exactly (above) and takes that wiring as given.

**What is new is the conditioning.** GDPNow nowcasts a quarter from roughly a month before it opens until
the advance estimate a month after it closes, so a starts vintage can land either **inside** the quarter it
is nowcasting or **after** it. `PostedUpdates` fixes where 2026-10-20 lands:

| Date | Time | Vintage |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-10-15 | 10:00 | Retail sales + inventories, Producer Price Index |
| **2026-10-20** | **08:30** | **Housing starts** *(solo)* |
| 2026-10-27 | 10:00 | Advance Census manufacturing (M3-1), New-home sales |
| 2026-10-28 | 08:30 | **Final nowcast of 2026:Q3 GDP growth**: Advance Economic Indicators |
| 2026-10-29 | 08:30 | **Initial nowcast of 2026:Q4 GDP growth**: GDP (Q3 1st estimate), … |

So 2026-10-20 is a **post-quarter** vintage — **20 days past** the 2026-09-30 quarter-end it nowcasts, and
the second-to-last 2026:Q3 vintage of any kind. Splitting the archive's **143** starts vintages on exactly
that line:

| Cut | n | median \|Δ residential\| | p (20,000-iter permutation) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Starts, post-quarter** (`dq > 0`) | **48** | **0.0250pp** | — |
| **Starts, in-quarter** (`dq ≤ 0`) | **95** | **0.0869pp** | **0.0001** |
| Starts, all (the corridor's figure) | 143 | 0.0609pp | <0.0001 vs baseline |

**A 3.48× attenuation.** The corridor's 0.0609pp overstates what this edition should do by roughly **2.4×**.

**Three controls, because a post-hoc split needs them.** *(a)* Is it just that all post-quarter vintages are
quiet? Partly, and nowhere near enough: pooled across all **1,822** deltas, post-quarter moves residential
**0.0059pp** against **0.0084pp** in-quarter — a ratio of **1.42×** at **p=0.056**, against this release's
3.48× at p=0.0001. *(b)* Does the sibling release show the same gradient? **No** — construction spending's
43 post-quarter vintages move residential **0.0632pp** against its 81 in-quarter ones at **0.0596pp**,
**p=0.879**, a flat line. Existing-home sales sits in between (0.0308 vs 0.0543, **p=0.012**). *(c)* Is the
post-quarter starts vintage simply lost in the noise? No — it still moves residential **5×** harder than
every other post-quarter vintage in the archive (**0.0250pp vs 0.0049pp**, n=582, **p=0.0001**).

**A mechanism is available and is stated as a hypothesis, not a finding.** A post-quarter starts vintage
delivers the quarter's *third* month into a nowcast that has already absorbed the first two; an in-quarter
one reshapes a quarter still mostly unobserved. Construction spending is a direct expenditure input whose
revisions carry equal weight whenever they land, which is consistent with its flat gradient. This session
did not test that explanation and does not claim it.

**What the slot gives back: attribution becomes perfect.** The 10-20 vintage is **solo**, and the solo cut
is where this release's signature is cleanest:

| Cut | n | residential is the largest of 8 component moves |
|---|---|---|
| **Post-quarter, solo starts** | **35** | **35 / 35 = 100.0%** |
| In-quarter, solo starts | 65 | 61 / 65 = 93.8% |
| All solo starts (the corridor's figure) | 100 | 96.0% |
| Unconditional base rate | 1,822 | **20.7%** |

**And the October cut agrees, on its own.** The archive holds **11** October starts vintages — 2014 through
2024, one per year — with a median \|Δ residential\| of **0.0209pp** and residential the largest component
move **11 of 11**. Ten of the eleven are solo; the exception is 2019-10-17 (shared with industrial
production) and residential led it anyway.

**The twelfth October is missing, and its absence is a Leg 4 finding arriving from a different file.** There
is **no October, November or December 2025 starts vintage in the archive**. The last pre-lapse one is
**2025-09-17**; the next is **2026-01-09**, and the Atlanta Fed's own release text carries the merge —
`Employment Situation, Housing starts` — followed by bundled catch-ups on 2026-02-18, 2026-03-12 and
2026-04-29, with the first solo starts vintage again on **2026-05-21**. GDPNow's archive records
deferral-and-merge independently of Census's calendar.
