### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The Tokyo flash is not a skilled lead indicator — REFUTES the framing (not the arithmetic) of
   the January ledger, on the same data.** The [japan-cpi-2027-01-22
   ledger](japan-cpi-2027-01-22.md) measured the Tokyo-minus-national same-month core gap over
   2018-01 → 2026-07 at mean absolute **0.201pp**, within ±0.3pp **78%**, and concluded the national
   print is "~80% pre-published." Recomputed here from the same e-Stat files, those numbers
   replicate to three decimals. The missing test is a **benchmark**: an 0.20pp error is only
   informative against what a free alternative costs. The obvious free alternative is *persistence*
   — predict national core y/y for month *m* by simply repeating the national print for month
   *m−1*, which is published a week **before** the Tokyo flash and so is strictly available.

   | Sample | Tokyo flash meanAbs / median | Persistence meanAbs / median | Tokyo win rate |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | All months 2000-01 → 2026-07 (n=319) | 0.250 / 0.219 | **0.182 / 0.115** | **114/319 = 36%** |
   | All months 2018-01 → 2026-07 (n=103) | **0.201 / 0.135** | 0.231 / 0.218 | 54/103 = 52% |
   | Februaries 2000 → 2026 (n=27) | 0.288 / 0.225 | **0.178 / 0.116** | **9/27 = 33%** |
   | Februaries 2018 → 2026 (n=9) | **0.253 / 0.118** | 0.353 / 0.227 | 6/9 = 67% |

   Over the full quarter-century the Tokyo flash is **worse on every statistic** — mean, median and
   head-to-head — than doing nothing. Its apparent edge exists only inside the 2018+ window, where
   it wins **52%** of months: a coin flip. The mechanism is not mysterious: Japanese core y/y is
   highly persistent, so month-over-month change in the *rate* is small, while Tokyo is a regional
   subsample carrying its own idiosyncratic noise. The flash's honest role is as a **cross-check
   against a large national surprise**, not as a point estimate.

### Honest limits

**The persistence benchmark is a statistical comparison, not a claim about the BoJ's reaction
function** — the board does not forecast a single number, and 0.18pp of y/y error can matter at a
turning point even when it is immaterial on average. **The win-rate and mean-error columns
disagree in the 2018+ window** (Tokyo has the lower mean but only 52% of head-to-heads), which is
exactly the signature of an estimator with fewer large misses and more small ones — leg 1 states
both rather than picking the flattering one, and neither supports "skilled." **The February
month-of-year ranking rests on n=27** (n=9 post-2018) and is not statistically separable from the
other eleven months; the defensible claim is "not better than average," not "worst." **Both series
span the 2019 consumption-tax hike and the 2020-base → 2025-base change**, and the Bureau's own
rule is that rates of change are spliced rather than recomputed — so all of leg 1's history is a
real historical average over a spliced series, not a stationary property. **No projection of the
February 2027 print is offered** — eight of twelve intervening months are unpublished. **The 08:30
JST release time in this event's title is the Bureau's standing convention and is still not read
