## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The [June sibling](consumer-confidence-2027-06-29.md) filed this id for one headline reason, stated in its
proposal: *"IT IS AN FOMC DAY-ONE COLLISION, and the direct within-year replication of the 04-27 collision
print… That gives 2027 TWO collisions from one release rule, which no year in the 2021-2026 tape has."* It
also named, honestly, the one thing it could not do: *"July's own cut-off lag is unmeasured — the June
editions give the July RELEASE dates but not July's cut-offs."*

So: **is the July date sound, is the collision really the rarity the proposal described, what are July's own
cut-off lags, and — the question no sibling has asked — what is structurally true of the last Tuesday of
July that is not true of the other eleven?**

**One-line verdict:** the date is three-for-three and every one of the three is double-sourced; the rarity
claim is **refuted twice over** and replaced by a deterministic rule that makes a July collision certain
rather than notable; the cut-off lags are **5 / 6 / 8** days, the shortest and widest-spread in the chain;
and what is structurally true of a July print is that **it has never once been observed clean**, which means
the null every stance in this series quotes was measured on a sample this print is structurally excluded
from — while a second fetch in the same session refuted the **80-threshold premise** every ledger in the
chain carries.
