day the UK borrowing series produces.** Two OBR precedents put the pre-measures forecast **34–36 days**
ahead of a fiscal event — October 2024's was *"finalised on 26 September"* (**34d** before 2024-10-30),
November 2025's fiscal determinants closed over the ten working days to **2025-10-21** (**36d** before
2025-11-26). **2026-09-22 is exactly 36 days before 2026-10-28**: inside on the 2024 convention with
**two days** to spare, exactly **on the line** on the 2025 one. The 2024 analogue — August data

### Honest limits

**First, the central finding is search-relayed and the governing document was never obtained.**
`obr.uk` returned **403** twice (recorded in `probe-ref.blocked`), so neither the November 2025 forecast
timetable nor the October 2024 EFO nor — crucially — the **autumn 2026** timetable was read directly.
The 34-day and 36-day figures in leg 2 are relays of the OBR's own published material through search.
Leg 2 is graded **MIXED** and registered as **FT-…-1** for exactly this reason, and legs 2 and 3 share
the same single point of failure: if the relayed 2024 close date is wrong, both fall together.

**Second, "the pre-measures forecast closed" and "the outturn data cut-off" are not the same object.**
The November 2025 figure is explicitly the window for the fiscal forecast's **determinants**; an outturn
cut-off could sit later. The 2024 round is the stronger evidence because it names the whole pre-measures
forecast as *"finalised on 26 September"* — but two rounds is two rounds, and a convention inferred from
them is a hypothesis about how the OBR works, not a rule. It is also possible the OBR incorporates a
print published **on** the close date, in which case even the 2025 convention puts this one inside.

**Third, the sharp result rests on n = 5.** Leg 5's September subset is five observations. The t of
**-2.86** is real arithmetic on real bars and every one of the five sits below the control mean, but
five prints cannot distinguish "the re-basing is a non-event" from "September happens to have been calm
five times." It is registered as **FT-…-2** rather than treated as established, and the threshold used
in the forward test (**±0.486%**, the general PSF-day mean) is deliberately **2.3x** looser than the
subset mean so the test is not rigged toward passing.

**Fourth, the market study's limits are inherited and still bind.** IGLT.L is an **all-maturity** gilt

**Fifth, the no-co-print claim is scoped, not a census.** The ONS release-calendar day filter would not

claimed to be absent.

**Sixth, there is no statutory-register corroboration and no consensus.** The gov.uk pre-announcement
