## Initial research

### The question

This event arrived proposed twice on the same day, `estimate`, from two different sweeps that **disagreed
about it**. [`consumer-confidence-2027-12-22`](consumer-confidence-2027-12-22.md)'s initial research filed
it `high` and flagged a mechanism it explicitly declined to research: *"December triple witching is the one
expiration that also carries annual index reconstitution flow in some index families; this lane did not
research that and makes no claim about it."* [`fomc-2027-12-08`](fomc-2027-12-08.md)'s filed it `medium`
and issued the opposite warning — that its own lane had **retired** the FOMC monthly decision-day base-rate
table on selection-correction grounds, so *"whoever researches this id should NOT reach for a
December-specific volatility cell to size an expiry against. Use the pooled figures or measure the expiry
class on its own terms."*

Three questions follow. **Is December different from the other quarterly expirations at all** — the one
the fomc lane put directly? **What does the year-end position actually buy**, given that this is the last
monthly-equity expiration of 2027 and the week after it is the thinnest tape of the year? And **which
proposal is right about impact**?

**One-line verdict:** December quarterlies are inert on every channel and the whole quarter-month family
is inert; the one result that looks like an edge — the post-expiration week's drift — is a **calendar
window, not an expiration**, and the technique that would normally have validated it (era-ranking) is the
technique that hid the answer.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is 2027-12-17 and OCC names it — SUPPORTED, and the entry is promoted.** The `data` array
   carries type `6937B1D1-F5D5-46CD-BBE7-041C7BE7B85B` on **2027-12-17**, and the December block reads
   coherently end to end: **12-15** `EEF1643B` (monthly volatility products), **12-16** `15AA2B9D`
   (A.M.-settled index options cease trading), **12-17** the monthly equity / P.M.-settled index marker,
   **12-24** *"Exchange/OCC holiday"*. Third-Friday arithmetic agrees independently — December 2027's
   Fridays are the 3rd, 10th, **17th**, 24th and 31st — and the payload's own 2027 holiday set puts no
   December closure before the 24th that could displace it.

2. **THE VALIDATION THAT MATTERED, BECAUSE A SIBLING HAD ALREADY CAUGHT THIS PAYLOAD LYING TWO ROWS
   AWAY — SUPPORTED, 36 of 36 against 35 of 36.**
   [`vix-expiration-2027-10-20`](vix-expiration-2027-10-20.md) refused to promote partly because *"the
   one calendar that does name the date carries a demonstrated error one row later"* — the publisher's
   **2027-12-15** volatility row. That is this event's own month, so the promotion could not simply cite
   the sibling's 12-of-12 precedent. Both series were therefore scored against independently coded rules,
   across all 36 months the payload covers:

   | Series | Rule applied | Agreement | The disagreement |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Monthly equity / PM index | third Friday, displaced back on the payload's own holidays | **36 / 36** | — |
   | Monthly volatility products | Cboe's rule: the Wednesday 30 days before the *following* month's third Friday, moved back one business day if that Wednesday **or that Friday** is a holiday | **35 / 36** | **2027-12** — rule gives **2027-12-22**, payload says **2027-12-15** |

   The volatility rule's holiday clause is load-bearing and was initially coded wrong here: without it the
   score is 32/36, the three extra misses being **2025-03, 2026-05 and 2027-05**, each of whose *following*
   third Friday is a holiday (Good Friday 2025-04-18; Juneteenth 2026-06-19 and 2027-06-18). With the full
   clause, **2027-12 is the only break in three years** — reproducing the sibling's finding from a
   different direction, and by a session that arrived at it by fixing its own bug rather than by looking
   for it. **The two series are not equally reliable at the far end of this payload**, and the promotion
   rests on the one that is rule-consistent 36 of 36 *and* independently derivable. The monthly-equity
   series also reproduces the in-year Juneteenth displacement to **Thursday 2027-06-17** and all five 2026
   dates this calendar already holds `confirmed`.

4. **THE ONE NUMBER THAT LOOKS LIKE AN EDGE — and the control that kills it is not the one this family
   usually reaches for. REFUTED, and this is the document's main output.** The **five sessions after** a
   December expiration return **+0.963%**, against **−0.268%** for the other quarterlies. Raw
   **P = 0.064** — and then it gets *stronger* under exactly the robustness check
   [`opex-2027-11-19`](opex-2027-11-19.md) used:

   | Test | n | means | P |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Dec-Q forward-5 return vs other quarterlies, raw | 19 / 59 | +0.963% vs −0.268% | **0.064** |
   | …**era-ranked** (±3y / ±5y / ±8y) | 19 / 59 | 0.605 vs 0.386 | **0.0047 / 0.0062 / 0.0075** |
   | …dropping the largest single observation (3 tried) | 18 / 59 | ≈ +0.81% vs −0.268% | 0.112 – 0.114 |
   | …dropping 2007-2011 from both sides | 14 / 44 | +1.024% vs −0.195% | 0.093 |
   | **vs all other December sessions** | 19 / 382 | +0.963% vs **+0.221%** | **0.143** |
   | **vs other Dec 15–24 sessions** | 19 / 115 | +0.963% vs **+0.605%** | **0.487** |
   | all December sessions vs all other months | 401 / 4,545 | +0.256% vs +0.198% | 0.653 |
   | Dec 15–24 window (any session) vs the rest of the year | 134 / 4,545 | +0.656% vs +0.198% | 0.030 |

   **Read the last three rows together and the effect dissolves.** December as a *month* is nothing
   (P = 0.653). A **narrow late-December window** carries the drift (P = 0.030). And **the expiration adds
   nothing to that window** — +0.36pp, **P = 0.487**. The expiration is not the cause; it is simply a date
   that always falls inside the window. Nineteen of nineteen December expirations land in the Dec 15–24
   range by construction, which is precisely why the naive comparison group was the wrong one.

   **And the window itself is not established either.** Its 134 overlapping five-session windows are not
   independent observations, so the honest unit is the year: the window beat its own year's mean forward-5
   return in **14 of 19 years**, mean **+0.433pp**, exact two-sided sign test **P = 0.0636**. So this
   ledger banks neither the expiration effect nor the seasonal.

5. **THE TRANSFERABLE FINDING — era-ranking is not a substitute for the right comparison group.
   SUPPORTED, and it is a scope limit on a technique three ledgers in this family now lean on.**
   Era-ranking replaces each observation by its percentile inside a ±N-year band of peers, which controls
   for the **volatility regime**. That is the right control for a claim about magnitudes in different
   eras — [`opex-2027-11-19`](opex-2027-11-19.md) used it correctly, and correctly noted it *"controls for
   the regime's volatility, not for a single outlier inside the regime."* It is the **wrong** control for a
   claim about a **calendar position**, and here it actively misled: it drove P from 0.064 to **0.0047**,
   because ranking December's forward returns against a band of *all-month* peers preserves the very
   seasonal confound the test needed to remove. A lane that ran only the family's habitual controls would
   have registered a candidate at P = 0.005.

### Honest limits

**Leg 3's null is a null, not a proof of absence.** n = 19 against 59 can only exclude a large effect;
December could carry a drift of a tenth of a VIX point invisibly here. **Leg 4's refusal is a
decomposition, not a disproof of the seasonal** — the honest statement is that the *expiration* explains
none of the late-December drift (P = 0.487), and that the drift itself does not survive the year-level
unit (P = 0.064); a larger sample could establish the seasonal and this ledger would still say the
expiration is not its cause. **Roughly thirty-four comparisons were run**, and only the twelve-cell
quarter-month family was multiplicity-corrected; the Bonferroni and Šidák figures reported cover that
family alone. **The Friday confound is structural** — only 6 of 236 expirations in the sample are not
Fridays — so leg 7 bounds the effect and does not decompose it. **The VIX channel is a daily
close-to-close change on a 30-day index**, not a realized-volatility measurement. **The `^VIX` series
carries a bar on 2026-09-07, a Labor Day closure `^GSPC` has no bar for** — a vendor artifact; the equity
