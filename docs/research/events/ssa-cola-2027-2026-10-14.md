## At a glance

**TL;DR.** Nothing to trade here, in any direction — this "event" has **zero independent information
content**. The 2027 COLA is pure arithmetic on the September CPI-W that BLS publishes at the same
08:30 ET instant, so anyone reading the CPI knows the COLA before SSA says it; the tradeable object
is `cpi-2026-10-14`, and it is already covered there. What this session *does* add is a number worth
disagreeing with. The COLA equals the (index-weighted) **average of the July/Aug/Sep 2026 CPI-W
year-over-year rates**, July is already locked at **exactly 3.400%**, and reproducing the arithmetic
off BLS's own index levels shows the widely-republished **3.6%** projection requires CPI-W y/y to
**accelerate to ~3.70% across August and September** — a 30bp two-month acceleration, not the
steady-state most coverage implies. Hold the current 3.4% y/y and the COLA prints **3.4%**. Base

the 2026-08-30 Larak Island strike lands in September's collection window, and CPI-W weights motor

## Initial research

**The question:** what will the 2027 Social Security COLA be, when will it actually be announced,
and does the announcement carry any market-relevant information the September CPI print does not
already carry?

**One-line verdict:** the COLA is a deterministic function of a number published at the same
instant, so it is not an information event at all — but the arithmetic is worth doing, because it
puts the honest central estimate at **3.4%** against a widely-republished **3.6%**, and it shows the
event's one genuinely useful property (a statutory tether that protects the September CPI's release
date) became moot on **2026-09-02**.

**Method:** sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md), primary
sources over aggregators. All four CPI-W index levels used below were read from BLS news releases
themselves, not from secondary coverage; the COLA band was then recomputed from those levels rather
than quoted. No price instruments — `macro-print` kind with `symbols: []` carries none.

### Leg 1 — the announcement date is 2026-10-14 — SUPPORTED, but it stays `estimate`

Two independent dated press sources name it: Yahoo Finance 2026-08-04 ("the SSA will announce the
2027 Social Security COLA on Oct. 14, based on third-quarter CPI-W inflation data") and The Senior
Citizens League 2026-08-12 ("October 14th, 2026"). The Motley Fool (2026-09-02) states SSA "should
be in a position to announce an official 2027 COLA the same day" as the September CPI-W release.
The mechanism behind all three is BLS-primary: the September-2026 CPI publishes **2026-10-14,
08:30 ET** (bls.gov CPI release schedule, the basis on which [`cpi-2026-10-14`](cpi-2026-10-14.md)
is `confirmed`), and 2026-10-14 is a **Wednesday**, so at least one aggregator calling it "that
Tuesday" is wrong on a checkable fact — logged as a source-hygiene rejection, not used.

**Why it does not flip to `confirmed`:** ssa.gov returned HTTP 403 on `/cola/`, `/oact/cola/cpiw.html`
and `/blog/en/` on 2026-09-04, so no primary SSA statement of the 2026-10-14 date is held. What is
held is a *derivation* — BLS's date plus SSA's invariable same-day practice — and a derivation is
not the primary source the lane's hard limits require. Upgrade condition recorded in the stance.

### Leg 2 — the announcement date IS the September CPI release date, mechanically — SUPPORTED

The 2025 cycle proves the coupling in both directions, and it is SSA's own words. The 2026 COLA was
due 2025-10-15; the funding lapse furloughed BLS and the September-2025 CPI slipped. SSA's
2025-10-14 email, quoted by CNBC/CBS/Federal News Network: *"The Bureau of Labor Statistics has
announced they will issue the September 2025 Consumer Price Index on October 24. The Social Security
Administration will use this release to generate and announce the 2026 cost-of-living adjustment on
October 24 as well."* BLS's own September-2025 release (bls.gov archive `cpi_10242025.htm`, embargoed
to 08:30 ET Friday 2025-10-24, read directly 2026-09-04) confirms the date and points readers to SSA
for the COLA. So the announcement does not have an independent schedule — it inherits the CPI's.

### Leg 3 — the arithmetic, recomputed from primary index levels — SUPPORTED

SSA's rule: COLA = (Q3-2026 CPI-W average ÷ Q3-2025 CPI-W average) − 1, rounded to the nearest 0.1%.
Base quarter, all three read from BLS releases:

| Month | CPI-W index (1982-84=100) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Jul 2025 | **316.349** (y/y +2.5%) | BLS archive `cpi_08122025.htm` |
| Aug 2025 | **317.306** (y/y +2.8%) | BLS archive `cpi_09112025.htm` |
| Sep 2025 | **318.139** (y/y +2.9%) | BLS archive `cpi_10242025.htm` |
| **Q3-2025 average** | **317.2647** | computed |
| Jul 2026 | **327.104** (y/y **+3.4%**) | BLS `cpi.nr0.htm`, released 2026-08-12 |

**Control — the method reproduces a known answer before being pointed at an unknown one.** Q3-2024
CPI-W, each level read from the BLS release that published it: Jul **308.501** (`cpi_08142024.htm`),
Aug **308.640** (`cpi_09112024.htm`), Sep **309.046** (`cpi_10102024.htm`), average **308.7290**.
Then 317.2647 ÷ 308.7290 = 1.027650 → **2.765% → 2.8%**, which is exactly the 2026 COLA SSA
announced. The arithmetic below is the same arithmetic, one year forward.

Because the three base-quarter levels are nearly equal, the ratio decomposes into an
**index-weighted average of the three monthly y/y rates**, weights **0.3324 / 0.3334 / 0.3343**.
July 2026 is locked at 327.104 ÷ 316.349 = **1.034000**, i.e. exactly **3.400%**. Solving for what
August and September must do (both at the same y/y rate `r`):

| 2027 COLA prints | Q3-2026 avg index | Aug+Sep avg index | requires Aug & Sep CPI-W y/y ≈ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.3% | 327.734 | 328.050 | **3.25%** |
| **3.4%** | 328.052 | 328.525 | **3.40%** (i.e. simply *hold*) |
| 3.5% | 328.369 | 329.001 | **3.55%** |
| 3.6% | 328.686 | 329.477 | **3.70%** |
| 3.7% | 329.003 | 329.953 | **3.85%** |

Rounding boundaries on the same basis: 3.325% · 3.475% · 3.625% · 3.775%.

### Leg 4 — the 3.6% consensus implicitly assumes acceleration — SUPPORTED (as arithmetic), MIXED (as a forecast)

Published projections: TSCL **3.6%** (cut from 3.8% after the July print), AARP **3.5%**, Mary
Johnson **3.4%** (all per The Motley Fool, 2026-09-02; TSCL's own COLA-watch page carries 3.6% with
no dated methodology exposed on the 2026-09-04 fetch). Mapped onto the table above, TSCL's 3.6%
requires a **30bp two-month acceleration** in CPI-W y/y off July's locked 3.400%; Johnson's 3.4%
requires only that it hold. The seasonal control cuts the same way: last year's Aug and Sep CPI-W
m/m were **+0.303%** and **+0.263%** (317.306/316.349 and 318.139/317.306) — repeat that path on
this year's July and the Q3 average lands at ~328.06, which is **3.4%**, not 3.6%.

Marked MIXED as a *forecast* rather than SUPPORTED for one honest reason: TSCL's model was not read
(no dated methodology was obtainable), so this is a disagreement with their published number derived
independently, not a located error in their work. The acceleration they need is not implausible —
see Leg 5.

### Leg 5 — the upside lever is September-specific, and CPI-W amplifies it — SUPPORTED

The [`cpi-2026-10-14`](cpi-2026-10-14.md) D-44 row established that the **2026-08-30** US strike on
Iranian rocket launchers at Larak Island (Brent $89.174, +1.22% d/d, +6.45% m/m, +30.92% y/y on
2026-08-31) lands *after* August's collection window closed — it is a **September** input. Gasoline
was already **+24.6% y/y** through July, and CPI-W weights motor fuel more heavily than CPI-U by
construction (wage-earner spending basket). So the asymmetry here is not symmetric noise: August can
hold at 3.4% and a single hot September still moves the COLA two ticks. Illustrative — Aug 3.4%,
Sep 4.2% y/y gives a weighted average of 3.67% → **3.7%**. This is why the call is "3.4% base, 3.5%
live upside" rather than a point estimate.

### Leg 6 — the delivery risk this event was registered for is gone — SUPPORTED

This entry exists because the September CPI carries a **statutory tether**: in the 2025 lapse BLS
recalled furloughed staff so SSA could meet its benefit deadline, making the 10-14 CPI a *delay*
exposure rather than a *deletion* exposure. As of 2026-09-02 there is nothing left to insure
against on the October cliff. **H.R. 6500, the "Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027,"
was signed into law 2026-09-02** — whitehouse.gov's own signing statement (fetched direct
2026-09-04) says it provides "fiscal year 2027 appropriations to Federal agencies through December
11, 2026"; it is **Public Law 119-103**, having passed the Senate 90–6 (2026-08-08) and the House
370–48 (2026-09-01). The next cliff is **2026-12-11**, already tracked as
`government-funding-deadline-2026-12-11` / `cr-expiry-2026-12-11`, and the December CPI prints
2026-12-10 — the day *before* it. So the tether is spent for this cycle with no exposure left on
either side. This supersedes the framing in
[`government-funding-deadline-2026-09-30`](government-funding-deadline-2026-09-30.md), last assessed
2026-08-29 and therefore written before the signing; that ledger is not edited here (append-only,
and it is another leg's event) — flagged for its own next pulse.

### What the conditions support

Nothing, and that is the finding rather than a hedge. There is no position that keys off an
announcement whose input publishes at the same instant on a different ticker-free release, no
tracked symbol with transfer-income exposure (the nine names are AI-infra and mega-cap), and a
fiscal channel too small and too pre-announced to trade: ~$1.6T of annual OASDI+SSI benefits at
3.4% vs a 2.8% repeat is on the order of **$10B** of extra 2027 transfers against >$20T of PCE —
basis points, spread over twelve monthly payments, known 11 weeks in advance. The value banked here
is (a) a checkable COLA band that disagrees with the republished consensus, (b) the mechanical
coupling of this date to the CPI date, and (c) the fact that its insurance function has expired.

### Honest limits

- **`estimate`, and staying that way.** ssa.gov 403'd on three paths; the date is press-reported plus derived. Nothing above licenses a date-keyed action.
- **CPI-W ≠ CPI-U.** The Cleveland Fed nowcast the sibling CPI ledger uses (~+0.35% m/m August) is a CPI-U product and is not carried into the table above as a CPI-W forecast — the bands are stated as "what Aug/Sep must do," never as "what they will do."
- **The weighted-average identity is exact; the "both months at r" column is a simplification.** Aug and Sep will not print the same y/y; the column is a reading aid, and Leg 5 shows the split case explicitly.
- **TSCL's model was not read.** Leg 4 is an independent recomputation disagreeing with their output, not an audit of their method.
- **Part B is unresolved.** The 2026 Trustees Report projects a 2027 standard premium of ~**$209.50** (+3.25% off $202.90) while private forecasters cluster **$216–219**; CMS confirms in **November 2026** on a date not yet published (checked 2026-09-04, no date exposed — not proposed to the calendar). The *net* check, and therefore the only real consumer-spending read, is not knowable on 10-14.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `estimate`, NEWS-sourced — nothing below licenses a dated action):** stand aside
permanently on this event as a trade; it is an arithmetic restatement of `cpi-2026-10-14`, and any
exposure worth having to the September CPI belongs in that ledger under its own rules. As a
*forecast*, the working read is **2027 COLA = 3.4%, with 3.5% the live upside and 3.6% requiring a
30bp two-month acceleration in CPI-W y/y that nothing currently on the tape has produced.** The
event's registered rationale — that it gives the 10-14 CPI a statutory tether against a funding
lapse — is **spent as of 2026-09-02** (PL 119-103), and this doc says so rather than continuing to
claim the protection as live value.

**Upgrade condition (`estimate` → `confirmed`):** a primary ssa.gov statement naming 2026-10-14
(press release, COLA fact sheet, or the OACT COLA page), fetched directly. BLS's CPI schedule alone
does not satisfy it — that confirms the *input*, not the announcement.

**Kill switches:**

- **The 3.4% base case dies** if August CPI-W (2026-09-11) prints y/y **≥ 3.63%** — one month at
  that level puts 3.6% inside reach on an ordinary September and TSCL's number becomes the base
  case, not the fade.
- **The whole "no surprise content" framing dies** if SSA announces a COLA that does **not** equal
  the Q3 CPI-W ratio rounded to 0.1% — i.e. if a statutory or administrative change (a CPI-E switch,
  a minimum-COLA floor) alters the formula between now and 10-14. No such change is on the record;
  this is the switch that would make the event informative rather than derived.
- **The "delivery risk is gone" claim dies** if PL 119-103 is rescinded, superseded, or a lapse
  otherwise re-emerges before 2026-10-14 — in which case the 2025 precedent reactivates and the
  announcement slips with the CPI rather than being cancelled.
- **The date read dies** if BLS moves the September-2026 CPI off 2026-10-14 for any reason, since
  Leg 2 makes the COLA date strictly derivative of it.

**Forward tests registered** (see [`forward-tests.md`](../forward-tests.md)):
`FT-ssa-cola-2027-2026-10-14-1` (the COLA lands ≤ 3.5%) and `FT-ssa-cola-2027-2026-10-14-2` (the
announcement holds on 2026-10-14), both scored by 2026-10-15. Zero capital by design.
