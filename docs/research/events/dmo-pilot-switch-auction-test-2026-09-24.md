## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Stand aside — this operation moves no stock, and the only thing worth carrying forward
from it is an attribution guard that is already needed.** The DMO said on **17 July 2026** that the
24 September pilot switch auction *"will take place as a test … No gilts will be transferred, issued
or cancelled as part of this test"*, downgrading its own **29 May 2026** plan, which had expected
*"the source and destination gilts"*. Coverage has not caught up: the most recent article found,
dated **2026-09-07**, still describes 09-24 as an operation that would *"retire long-dated bonds and
replace them with shorter maturities."* Two further primaries kill the balance-sheet story outright —
the DMO's **current** rulebook (Operational Notice, **1 April 2026**) names the purposes as benchmark

genuinely new fact is a gap: that same Operational Notice gives switch auctions **two sentences and no
rules**, deferring the mechanics to a future notice — so **the mechanics of a 2026 UK switch auction
are unpublished**, and the test is how they get written. Nothing here is tradeable: `estimate`,

## Initial research

### The question

`dmo-pilot-switch-auction-test-2026-09-24` reached this lane as a proposal from the
[`boe-decision-2026-09-17`](boe-decision-2026-09-17.md) sweep, filed explicitly **as a correction**:
that lane's leg 5 had read coverage saying the DMO was *"exploring whether to absorb long-dated gilts
from the Bank of England's balance sheet"* while cutting long conventional issuance, and 09-24 looked
like the instrument that would do it. The proposal read the DMO primary and said no — it is a test.

So the honest question this lane inherited is: **the proposal already established what 09-24 is not;
is there anything true about it that is worth a standing entry?**

**One-line verdict:** **yes, two things, and neither is a price.** The operation was *de-scoped in
writing between May and July*, which is why live coverage is wrong about it; and the DMO's **current**
rulebook contains no switch-auction rules at all, which is what the test is for.

### Leg 1 — 24 September is a DMO operation that moves no gilts. **SUPPORTED** (two primaries)

`pr170726.pdf`, under *"Planned test switch auction on Thursday 24 September 2026"*, verbatim:

> The DMO is also announcing today that the pilot switch auction planned for Thursday 24 September
> 2026 will take place as a test to be conducted with the Gilt-edged Market Makers (GEMMs). This will
> enable the GEMMs to familiarise themselves with the bidding and allocation processes. **No gilts
> will be transferred, issued or cancelled as part of this test.** Further details of the arrangements
> for the test will be shared with the GEMMs closer to the date of the test.

Independently dated and corroborated by `pr290526.pdf` §4, *"Planned switch auctions"*, which put the
date in the calendar seven weeks earlier. **No time of day is published in either.** (`estimate`.)

### Leg 2 — the operation was de-scoped between May and July. **SUPPORTED** (new this session)

The 29 May calendar reads, verbatim:

> The DMO will be scheduling a pilot switch auction on 24 September 2026. **Feedback nearer the time
> will determine the source and destination gilts.**

On 29 May the DMO expected a source gilt and a destination gilt. On 17 July there are none. That is a
documented change of plan in **49 days**, and it is the reason every downstream description of 09-24
has to be date-checked rather than taken at face value. The proposal did not have this document.

### Leg 3 — it is the mechanism for absorbing long-dated gilts from the BoE's APF. **REFUTED** (four ways)

1. **The no-transfer sentence** (leg 1). An operation that cancels and creates nothing absorbs nothing.
2. **The current rulebook's stated purposes.** Operational Notice **1 April 2026** ¶55: switch auctions
   *"may be employed for various purposes — for example, to maintain a supply of current benchmark
   gilts in a low financing environment or for redemption management purposes."* Neither is APF
   absorption.
3. **The remit's stated purpose.** DMR 2026-27 ¶3.22: *"The DMO may use switch auctions from 2026-27 to
   promote liquidity in certain gilts, including at benchmark maturities."* Also not APF absorption.
4. **The financing arithmetic — the hardest of the four.** DMR 2026-27 **Table 3.A** budgets **gilt
   secondary market purchases of nil** in 2026-27, against £0.1bn in 2025-26. The table reconciles:
   CGNCR £137.2bn + redemptions £140.7bn + sukuk £0.5bn + purchases £0.0bn + adjustment −£5.0bn =
   **gross financing requirement £273.4bn** (2025-26: £324.6bn). A DMO purchase of APF stock in
   2026-27 would need a **remit revision**, not a switch auction.

Note the mechanism mismatch as well: a switch auction cancels a source gilt and creates a destination
gilt against **GEMMs**, via the National Loans Fund. The APF is a **Bank of England** facility, whose
gilts the DMR's own maturity footnote treats as *"having a maturity of zero"*; the Bank held **18.5%**
of gilts at the last reading in the same annex.

### Leg 4 — a switch auction "retires long-dated bonds and replaces them with shorter maturities". **REFUTED as stated**

This is the framing in coverage dated **2026-09-07**. No DMO primary says it. The **only** published
DMO ruleset that has ever addressed source-vs-destination maturity is the **September 2006** GEMM
Guidebook ¶69, which forbids exactly that trade:

> The DMO will only offer a switch auction where the **source and destination gilts fall into the same
> maturity bracket** … [footnote: *"0-7 years, 5-15 years, and over 14 years in conventionals"*]

A switch out of a 2056 into a 5-year is cross-bracket and was barred. ¶68 adds three further source
bars — not below £4.5bn remaining in issue (£1.5bn index-linked), not auctioned in the previous **18
months** (six for index-linked), and never the cheapest-to-deliver gilt or a likely CTD in an active
futures contract — and ¶74 makes the operation **cash-neutral**, allocating destination nominal by the
**ratio of the dirty prices**.

**The honest qualifier, and it matters:** ¶¶67–74 are **not carried into** the 1 April 2026
Operational Notice. So the bracket rule is not in force today; it is the last published precedent, and
the claim is *unsupported by any primary* rather than *contradicted by a live rule*. Whether ¶69
returns is registered as **FT-…-3**.

### Leg 5 — the pilot exists because there is no current published rulebook. **SUPPORTED**

The 2006 Guidebook gave switch auctions eight paragraphs of rules. The **current** Operational Notice
gives them two sentences and an explicit deferral, ¶54:

> The DMO may, at its discretion, use other forms of auction. **Plans to use these and the details of
> how such auctions would operate are likely to be set out in or in conjunction with the annual
> issuance plan, any update or other notice.** Other forms of auction used previously have included
> switch auctions and reverse auctions.

Put beside pr170726's *"Further details of the arrangements for the test will be shared with the
GEMMs"*, the position on **2026-09-15** is that **no public document states how a 2026 UK switch
auction would work**. That is a sufficient reason for a familiarisation test on its own, and it is the
most useful thing this entry knows.

### Leg 6 — which purpose actually fits 2026-27. **MIXED** (by elimination, not by statement)

The Operational Notice offers two. Benchmark supply is conditioned on *"a low financing environment"*,
and 2026-27 is not one: gross financing requirement **£273.4bn**, DMO net financing requirement
**£257.1bn**, total planned gilt sales **£252.1bn**. That leaves **redemption management**, which the
DMR corroborates in its own framing — ¶2.18 names *"an ongoing large gilt redemption profile"* and
sets the objective of *"mitigating refinancing risk … and to avoid concentrating redemptions in
particular periods"*, against **£140.7bn** of gilt redemptions this year. Graded **MIXED** because no
DMO document says "redemption management" *about this operation*; the remit's own words for it are
liquidity at benchmark maturities, which is a third formulation again.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance: stand aside. No position, no play, no hedge — on every horizon.** The event is `estimate`,
`low`, `symbols: []`, and carries **zero** playbook exposure. Its value to this book is entirely
defensive: it stops a 09-24 long-end move being mis-attributed to a UK supply operation that supplies
nothing, and it stops the parent ledger's leg-5 APF hypothesis being carried forward on the strength of
coverage that the DMO's own documents contradict on four counts.

**Four analytical commitments, each with its killer:**

1. **09-24 moves no gilts.** *Killed by:* a DMO notice on or before **2026-09-24** restating it as a
   live operation with named source and destination gilts.
2. **No public result document will be published for the test.** *Killed by:* any DMO publication dated
   **2026-09-24 → 2026-09-29** reporting a switch-auction result. **FT-…-1**.
3. **No live switch auction before 2027-01-01.** *Killed by:* one being held. **FT-…-2**.
4. **When rules are published, source and destination stay in one maturity bracket.** *Killed by:*
   published rules permitting a cross-bracket (e.g. over-14y → 0-7y) switch. **FT-…-3**.

**Estimate discipline.** Every statement above carries the event's `estimate` label. Estimates widen
caution and license nothing; none of these commitments is a trade, and none becomes one if it passes.
