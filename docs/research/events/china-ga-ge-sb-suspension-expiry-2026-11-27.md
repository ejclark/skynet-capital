### What the clock is, tested

1. **The instrument: SUPPORTED, press-grade.** MOFCOM Announcement No. 72 of 2025 (2025-11-09)
   suspended Article 2 of Announcement No. 46 of 2024 "from today until November 27, 2026" (nami-ts
   2026-10-22 quotes the operative sentence; Global Trade Alert, Clark Hill and Fastmarkets agree).
   Article 2 is the US-specific ban: dual-use Ga/Ge/Sb/superhard items to the US "not permitted in
   principle". Sources split on graphite: some say the suspended article also covered stricter US-bound
   graphite review, one says part of Article 2 stayed. Treat graphite scope as unsettled.
2. **What a lapse changes: MIXED, and smaller than the headline.** Worldwide licensing on the three
   metals dates from 2023-24 and was never suspended; the ban on dual-use exports to US military end
   users was never suspended either. A lapse adds the US-only prohibition on top (nami-ts, MaterialsDispatch,
   both October 2026).
3. **Was it extended on 2026-11-09? Unresolved, leaning no.** Skillings (2026-11-09) says the notice
   covering the six October-2025 measures "does not mention Announcement No. 72 and does not extend
   that suspension". The 28 September statement did not name it either (MaterialsDispatch, nami-ts).
   One trade-press source for the 11/9 reading, no MOFCOM text, so this stays an open question rather than a finding.
4. **Practical bite is already thin.** Reuters (2026-09-04): Chinese gallium exports -65% YTD and
   licensed shipments to US buyers refused since early August. Western gallium was quoted above
   $1,800/kg against ~$245/kg in China in October (MaterialsDispatch, single source); non-Chinese
   supply is not ready to absorb a lapse (Alcoa Wagerup decision 2026-07-14, not yet producing).
