### The question, plainly

Two sibling lanes proposed this event on the same day. The `m3-full-report-2026-10-02` sweep proposed

**Does that framework place this edition — and if it does not, what is actually distinctive here?**

**One-line verdict:** it does not place it, and what is distinctive here is not on the model at all —
**2026-10-01 is the one slot in this series that a federal funding lapse has actually destroyed,
twice, and this is the year it does not**, because a signed law already covers the date; on the model

test, and the honest read of the October slot is that it is the family's **widest**, which is a reason
to make no point forecast rather than a reason to make a quiet one.

### Leg 4 — does the November lane's framework place this edition? · **PARTLY — the pattern replicates, the mechanism is REFUTED, and the slot is not what the frame predicts**

The November ledger's central finding was that a construction vintage's footprint is set by its
**posting position** inside the nowcast quarter. That replicates exactly on an independent re-run:

| Posting position in the nowcast quarter | n | median \|Δ Struct\| | median \|Δ Resid\| | median \|Δ GDP\| | ≥0.02pp | ≥0.05pp |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 ← the **November** editions | 36 | **0.0381pp** | 0.0401pp | 0.3526pp | 75.0% | 47.2% |
| 3–10 | 4 | 0.0483pp | 0.0451pp | 0.1183pp | 100% | 50.0% |
| 11–15 ← the **December** editions | 41 | **0.0800pp** | 0.0843pp | 0.4522pp | 87.8% | 70.7% |
| **16+ ← every October edition sits here (positions 23–27)** | **43** | **0.0453pp** | 0.0619pp | 0.2876pp | 79.1% | 48.8% |

Postings 1–2 vs 11–15: **p=0.0299** on structures, **p=0.0144** on residential (the November lane
recorded 0.0312 and 0.0136 on its own draw of the same permutation; the difference is Monte-Carlo
noise). Excluding 2020 entirely the split survives: **0.0357pp vs 0.0747pp, p=0.0495**.

**But the gradient is a hump, not a ramp, and this edition sits on the far side of it.** Postings 16+
fall back to 0.0453pp, and every October edition in the archive sits at position **23–27**. A frame
whose story is "vintages accumulate and the model drifts further from its anchor" predicts a ramp; the
data give a rise and a fall.

**The mechanism the November lane suggested is refuted when tested directly.** That lane offered — and
labelled a suggestion rather than a result — "the quarter's initial nowcast is a full re-estimate, so
the first construction print after it diffs against the freshest anchor the model ever has." The
direct test is to index each construction vintage by its **ordinal inside its own nowcast round**
rather than by raw position:

| Construction vintage's ordinal in its nowcast round | n | median \|Δ Struct\| | median \|Δ Resid\| | ≥0.02pp |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st of the round | 48 | 0.0603pp | 0.0544pp | 81.3% |
| 2nd of the round | 44 | 0.0689pp | 0.0740pp | 86.4% |
| **3rd of the round ← 2026-10-01** | **32** | **0.0405pp** | 0.0571pp | 75.0% |

1st vs 2nd **p=0.6587**, 2nd vs 3rd **p=0.2404**, 1st vs 3rd **p=0.4961**, 3rd vs all others
**p=0.2263**. **Nothing.** If the fresh anchor were doing the work, the first construction reading
after each initial nowcast would be the quiet one; it is the middle one.

**What does line up is the reference month's place in the quarter being nowcast** — and it is the same
cut as the posting bands, wearing a name that has a mechanism attached. A construction release reports
data two months old, so the release month determines which month of which quarter the print is the
first hard read of:

| The reference month is… | release months | n | median \|Δ Struct\| | median \|Δ Resid\| | ≥0.02pp |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **month 1 of the quarter being nowcast** | {03, 06, 09, 12} | 44 | **0.0742pp** | 0.0809pp | 88.6% |
| **month 2 of the quarter being nowcast ← this edition** | {01, 04, 07, 10} | 41 | **0.0438pp** | 0.0619pp | 80.5% |
| **month 3 of the PRIOR quarter (base-setting)** | {02, 05, 08, 11} | 37 | **0.0404pp** | 0.0363pp | 75.7% |

Month 1 vs base-setting: **p=0.0549** on structures and **p=0.0073** on residential. Month 1 vs month
2: p=0.1032. Month 2 vs base-setting: **p=0.9116** — indistinguishable. Excluding 2020: 0.0716 /
0.0405 / 0.0381, with month-1-vs-base-setting **p=0.0487**. The reading is that the model learns most
from the first hard construction month of the quarter it is nowcasting, less from the second, and
least from a month belonging to a quarter it has already closed — a story that survives dropping any
single release month, which the November lane's base-setting test could not (it collapsed to
**p=1.0000** once November was removed, because its binary cut pooled month 1 with month 2).

**On that frame this edition sits in the quiet group.** It is a month-2 print at 0.0438pp,
statistically indistinguishable from the base-setting group the November edition belongs to. That is
the answer to the question this ledger opened with — and it is *not* the answer the posting-position
frame gives, which would put an October edition in the 16+ band with no story at all.

### Honest limits

- **The lapse finding is n=2, and n=2 is n=2.** Two funding lapses in thirteen years both killed this
  slot, and both are documented from primary sources — but "2 for 2" describes a mechanism (a lapse
  beginning on October 1 suspends a release scheduled for October 1) rather than a rate. Its value here
  is entirely that the mechanism is **retired by a signed law for this edition**, which is a fact about
  2026 and not an inference from the two prior cases.
- **The reference-month frame is a re-cut of someone else's post-hoc slice.** It was found by asking
  why the November lane's posting-position gradient does not place an October edition, after that
  gradient had already been found by asking what distinguishes November from December. p=0.0549 on
  structures and p=0.0073 on residential are honest permutation results, they survive dropping 2020,
  and they do not collapse on removing any one release month — but they are not corrected for the cuts
  that preceded them, in this ledger or in the sibling's.
- **Refuting a mechanism is not establishing one.** The ordinal-within-round test kills the "fresh
  anchor" story (p=0.66 / 0.24 / 0.50). The reference-month story that replaces it — the model learns
  most from the first hard month of the quarter it is nowcasting — is a *reading* of a gradient, and
  nothing here tests it directly either. It is offered as the better-behaved description, not as a
  demonstrated cause.
- **The October-width result is a dispersion test on n=10, at p=0.0565.** It does not clear 0.05, the
  month cut is post-hoc, and three readings (2014, 2019, 2020) carry the whole IQR. Its role in this
  ledger is to *withhold* a forecast, which is the direction in which a weak result does least damage.
- **The isolated class is n=9 and the GDP null is a failure to reject on it.** "The print does not move
  the headline nowcast" is p=0.77 against a baseline, not a demonstration of zero — inherited from both
  siblings, independently replicated here, and still the weakest measurement in the family.
- **The classifier is free text.** `ContribArchives`'s `Data releases` column is comma-tokenised and
  case-normalised, not otherwise cleaned. A vintage whose text silently omits the release would be
  misfiled into the baseline, biasing every test **toward** finding no difference.
- **Pre-2020 reference months are derived.** The Census calendars this session could fetch begin at
  2020; for the 51 of 124 construction vintages dated before that, the reference month is derived as
  release month minus two — the rule that holds in **79 of 84** observed rows, with every exception a
  post-lapse catch-up. Leg 4's reference-month frame depends on that derivation; the posting-position
  replication does not.
- **All the nowcast work measures a model, not a market.** `ContribArchives` ends 2026-07-28 and
  carries no 2026:Q3 vintages after that date, so every class prior here is out-of-sample for the
  quarter this print lands in.
- **The revision arithmetic is two pairs of mine on top of the December lane's nineteen.** If that
