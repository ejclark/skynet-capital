## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **The arrangement is unprecedented; the auction is not.** At 1:00pm ET on 2026-12-10 a
30-Year bidder will hold both the FOMC decision (12-09, 14:00 ET) and the November CPI print
(12-10, 08:30 ET) — and **no nominal 30-Year auction since 2009 has ever priced that way** (0 of
210 with CPI in the session and an FOMC decision one to five days behind). But each ingredient
*separately* is routine and measurably benign: CPI-day 30-Years are **39 of 210** and clear
**+0.039 bid-to-cover** against their four nearest non-CPI neighbours (n=37, 22/37 positive);
FOMC-D+1 30-Years are **9 of 210**, and the closest analogue on record — **2025-12-11**, also a
December reopening, also $22B, also the day after a December FOMC — cleared **2.36 bid-to-cover on
a 1.0bp session**. Where the two conditions *do* agree is the **session**, not the demand: the
30-Year's own auction-day par-yield move runs **7.6bp on FOMC-D+1 days and 5.4bp on CPI days
against a 4.1bp all-session baseline**, so this is a be-flat-through-the-session call, not a
weak-auction call. **Two corrections to this event's own proposal, both from the primary record:

nothing here is a trade.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Three sibling ledgers already own most of what a 30-Year auction doc can say — the September one
([`treasury-30y-bond-2026-09-10.md`](treasury-30y-bond-2026-09-10.md)) argued the long-end
term-premium case, the October one
([`treasury-30y-bond-2026-10-08.md`](treasury-30y-bond-2026-10-08.md)) established demand mechanics
across a full sale cycle and the buyback eligibility rules, and the November one
([`treasury-30y-bond-2026-11-12.md`](treasury-30y-bond-2026-11-12.md)) settled a holiday-broken
block to a null. Repeating any of them would be a non-question.

What is new about **2026-12-10** is the *information state of the bidder*. The proposal that
discovered this event put it well: this is the only leg of the December block that stops **after**
the FOMC decision (12-09) rather than before it, and it stops on the **same morning** as the
November CPI print (12-10, 08:30 ET). A 3-Year bidder on Monday 12-07 has neither; a 30-Year bidder
on Thursday 12-10 has both.

So this session asked three questions, in the order that decides whether the framing survives:

1. **Is the arrangement actually unprecedented, or does it just feel that way?**
2. **If it is, does it matter — is there a measurable demand cost to selling long duration into a
   session that already digested a policy decision and an inflation print?**
3. **Is anything else about this auction — its size, its security, its CUSIP — settled or open?**

**One-line verdict:** the arrangement is genuinely unprecedented (**0 of 210** nominal 30-Year
auctions since 2009), the demand cost is **not measurable** (each ingredient is benign on a matched
control, and the closest analogue on record was a 1.0bp non-event), the risk that *is* measurable
sits in the **session's** volatility rather than the auction's book — and the proposal's expected
size is **wrong by $3B**, because a December 30-Year is a reopening and the reopening grid is not
the new-issue grid.

**Three source-reliability notes, recorded rather than papered over.**

*TIPS contamination.* `auctions_query`'s `security_type` does **not** separate 30-Year TIPS from
nominal bonds — both come back as `Bond`, and the TIPS rows ($8–9B at ~2% yields) corrupt any naive
average. Filtering on a non-null `ref_cpi_on_issue_date` separates them; every statistic below is
nominal-only. Reproduced independently here, not inherited from the sibling that first recorded it.

*bls.gov is blocked from this runner.* Plain `curl` against `www.bls.gov` returned **403** on every
schedule page attempted, with and without browser headers — the documented blind spot
[`ppi-2026-11-13.md`](ppi-2026-11-13.md) carries for this lane, recorded in `probe-ref.blocked`.
The CPI dates below were read from BLS's own archive page through the harness's fetcher, which is
not subject to that block: a **BLS primary read through a second-hand renderer**, not one parsed
here. Two independent consistency checks passed — the 2026 schedule it returned puts November-data
CPI on **2026-12-10**, agreeing exactly with the separately sourced `cpi-2026-12-10` calendar entry
(`BLS:`, checked 2026-08-15), and the 2025 series it returned reproduces the shutdown distortion
(September data delayed to **2025-10-24**, October data **absent entirely**).

*VIX came from Cboe, not the first source tried.* `stooq.com` served a JavaScript browser
challenge rather than the CSV (recorded in `probe-ref.blocked`); Cboe publishes the index it owns,
so the substitution is an **upgrade** to the primary, not a fallback, and is named as such.

**Bidder shares are competitive-only.** Every indirect/direct/dealer percentage below is a share of
**competitive** awards (`comp_accepted`), with SOMA add-ons excluded from the denominator. Dividing
by `total_accepted` instead understates each share, because on a reopening the add-on can be a fifth
of the line.

**Cycle vocabulary.** A nominal 30-Year is sold three times — a **NEW** issue in the refunding month
(February, May, August, November), then **reopened** in each of the two following months.
**2026-12-10 is the FIRST reopening** of the bond the 2026-11-12 new issue creates; 2027-01-13 is
the second and last.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The date, security and settlement are what the calendar says — SUPPORTED, primary.** The
tentative schedule's row reads verbatim `30-Year BOND / R / Thursday, December 03, 2026 / Thursday,
December 10, 2026 / Tuesday, December 15, 2026`, with the PDF's own legend confirming
`R --denotes reopening`. All three coupon legs of the block settle **Tuesday 2026-12-15** together.
1:00pm ET is Treasury's standing coupon convention and is **not** separately sourced. Status
correctly stays `estimate`: a tentative schedule is tentative by construction, and the 12-03 coupon
announcement is what fixes it.

**2. The expected size is $22B, and the proposal's $25B is a category error — SUPPORTED, and it is
the correction most likely to change what a reader does.** The proposal names "$25B, the standing
30-Year size." That is the **new-issue** size. Two grids run side by side in this series and they
have never been equal:

| Grid | What it sizes | Streak | Window |
|---|---|---|---|
| **$25B** | refunding-month **new issues** | 11 consecutive | 2024-02-08 → 2026-08-13 |
| **$22B** | the two monthly **reopenings** | **20 consecutive completed** | 2024-03-13 → 2026-07-09 |

2026-09-10's reopening is already **announced at $22B** (the 21st), and 2026-10-08 would be the
22nd. The last reopening at any other size was **2024-01-11 at $21B**. Since a December 30-Year is a
reopening (leg 3), the null for 2026-12-10 is **$22B**. Registered as
**FT-treasury-30y-bond-2026-12-10-1**. The size *decision* is not in this window at all: a quarterly
refunding statement sets coupon sizes, the December one was set on **2026-11-04**, 36 days before
this auction, and **2026-12-03** merely publishes it.

**3. A December 30-Year is always a reopening — SUPPORTED, 17/17.** Every December nominal 30-Year
since 2009 carries `reopening: "Yes"`, which is a calendar identity rather than a behavioural
regularity: December is not a refunding month, so there is no new bond to create. The schedule's `R`
marker agrees with the record.

**4. The CUSIP does not already exist — SUPPORTED, and it corrects the proposal a second time.** The
proposal reasons that "a REOPENING: the security and its CUSIP already exist, so a when-issued level
is quotable well before the auction." True of reopenings in general and **false today**: the bond
being reopened is the **2026-11-12** new issue, which has not been sold. The relevant CUSIP comes
into existence on 2026-11-12 — 28 days before this auction, not 92 — and this ledger **predicts
none**, on the same reasoning the November sibling used when it declined to guess at the 912810U--
sequence. What *is* true, and useful, is the timing: from 11-12 onward a when-issued market in the
actual security exists, so the December leg is the more readable of the two months.

**5. Stopping the day after an FOMC decision is not rare — REFUTED as a rarity claim.** Pairing every
December 30-Year with that year's December FOMC decision date (Fed primary):

| December 30-Year | December FOMC | Offset |
|---|---|---|
| 2011-12-14 · 2012-12-13 · 2019-12-12 · 2025-12-11 | 12-13 · 12-12 · 12-11 · 12-10 | **D+1** (4 of 17) |
| 2016-12-13 · 2017-12-12 · 2022-12-13 · 2023-12-12 | 12-14 · 12-13 · 12-14 · 12-13 | D−1 (4 of 17) |
| the other nine | — | D−5 or D−6 |

Across the whole nominal series, **9 of 210** auctions since 2009 stopped one day after an FOMC
decision. So the ordering is a **quarter of Decembers**, not an anomaly — and **2025-12-11 is last
year's instance**, which makes it the single best analogue this study has.

**6. Sharing a session with CPI is not rare either — REFUTED as a rarity claim.** Against BLS's own
release dates: **39 of 210** nominal 30-Year auctions since 2009 landed on a CPI release day, and
**3 of 17** Decembers did (2020-12-10, 2022-12-13, 2023-12-12). The 30-Year and CPI have collided
routinely since 2018, when BLS's calendar settled into the same 10th–14th window Treasury's
mid-month coupon block occupies.

**7. But the *combination* is unprecedented — SUPPORTED, and it is the session's central finding.**
Requiring both conditions at once — CPI in the auction's session **and** an FOMC decision behind it:

| Window for the FOMC decision | Nominal 30-Years since 2009 also on a CPI day |
|---|---|
| D+1 | **0 of 210** |
| D+1 … D+3 | **0 of 210** |
| D+1 … D+5 | **0 of 210** |
| D+1 … D+7 | 2 (2020-11-12, 2021-11-10 — November refunding new issues, FOMC a full week back) |

**2026-12-10 would be the first.** The honest reading of that zero matters as much as the zero
itself: it is a statement about **arrangement**, not about risk. Because the FOMC decision and the
CPI print both cluster mid-month and Treasury's coupon block sits in the same window, the two have
simply never been ordered *decision → print → auction* within five days. Nothing in the record says
that ordering is dangerous — the record contains no observation of it at all, which is exactly why
the stance below is guard-shaped rather than directional. Registered as
**FT-treasury-30y-bond-2026-12-10-2**.

**8. A CPI print in the session does not weaken 30-Year demand — REFUTED as a hypothesis, on a
matched control.** Pooled means mislead here, because CPI-day auctions concentrate in the post-2018
window when indirect shares were structurally higher. So each CPI-day 30-Year was measured against
the mean of its **four nearest non-CPI 30-Year auctions** in the same series:

| Measure | n | Mean difference vs matched neighbours |
|---|---|---|
| Bid-to-cover | 37 | **+0.039** (22/37 positive) |
| Indirect share (competitive) | 37 | **+0.38pp** |

The sign is positive and the magnitude is inside noise; the usable claim is only that **there is no
demand penalty**, which is the same conclusion
[`treasury-10y-note-2026-11-10.md`](treasury-10y-note-2026-11-10.md) reached independently on the
10-Year (+0.004 bid-to-cover, n=30). Two instruments, two series, one answer, neither derived from
the other.

**9. The measurable risk is the session, not the book — SUPPORTED, and small-n where it matters.**
The 30-Year's own par-yield close-to-close absolute move on the auction day:

| Session class | n | Mean absolute move | Share > 10bp |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FOMC D+1** | 9 | **7.6 bp** | 2/9 |
| **CPI in the session** | 39 | **5.4 bp** | 5/39 |
| Auction day, no CPI | 171 | 4.4 bp | 17/171 |
| Every session 2009–2026 | 4,422 | **4.1 bp** | — |

Both of 12-10's conditions push the same way, and 12-10 stacks them. **What this establishes and
what it does not:** at n=9 the FOMC-D+1 mean is dominated by two prints (2012-03-14 at +17bp,
2020-06-11 at −12bp) and is **not** an effect this ledger claims; the tail rate (2/9 vs 20/201) is
the more consistent reading and points the same way. It is enough to justify being flat through one
session and nowhere near enough to justify a direction. Registered as
**FT-treasury-30y-bond-2026-12-10-4**.

**10. The closest analogue on record was a non-event — SUPPORTED, and it is the counterweight to leg
9.** **2025-12-11** matches this auction on every structural feature available: December, reopening,
**$22B**, Thursday, the day after a December FOMC decision (2025-12-10). It cleared **bid-to-cover
2.36**, indirect **65.4%**, dealer **11.2%**, zero SOMA add-on, on a **1.0bp** session. One
observation is not a base rate, but it is the *most* comparable one that exists, and it argues
against reading leg 9 as a forecast.

**11. The demand band to judge the print against — SUPPORTED.** The **20** completed $22B
reopenings (2024-03-13 → 2026-07-09):

| Statistic | Value |
|---|---|
| Bid-to-cover | mean **2.409**, σ **0.056**, range **2.30 – 2.52** |
| Indirect (competitive) | mean **65.8%**, σ 5.3 |
| Dealer (competitive) | mean **12.8%**, σ 2.4 |
| Total accepted − offering | mean **$1.51B**, positive at **16 of 20** |

Two things a reader is likely to have wrong. First, the σ of **0.056** is remarkably tight — this
grid is the most over-determined thing in the series, which is why a *deviation* is the only
information a print can carry. Second, the SOMA add-on on a **reopening** is far smaller and far
less certain than on a new issue: 16/20 positive averaging **$1.51B**, against 12/12 positive
averaging **$6.48B** for the $25B new issues. The November sibling's "headline understates the line
by ~$6B" finding is a **new-issue** statistic and does **not** transfer to this leg.

policy.** The published Tentative Buyback Schedule, re-fetched this session, ends at the

11-04 refunding publishes the next quarter's. So any buyback claim about this auction today would be

billion** per operation, not the $4 billion the proposal's note implies — $4B is the maximum on the
1Mo–2Y, 3Y–5Y, 5Y–7Y and 7Y–10Y buckets.

**13. The corridor's real risk runs 12-09 → 12-11, and the settlement sits past a funding deadline —
SUPPORTED.** Twenty-six tracked entries sit within ±5 days; three are `high`:

- **Wednesday 12-09** — `fomc-2026-12-09` (**confirmed**, `FED:`), statement 14:00 ET with SEP and

- **Thursday 12-10** — `cpi-2026-12-10` (**confirmed**, `BLS:`) at 08:30 ET, four and a half hours
