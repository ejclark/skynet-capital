### The question, plainly

The minutes of the December 7–8, 2027 FOMC should publish at 14:00 ET on **2027-12-29**. Seven sibling

What is open here is a specific, loud, unresolved warning. **Both** proposals that seeded this id flagged
the same hazard. The
[`from-fomc-2027-12-08`](../../../src/domain/market-events/proposals/fomc-minutes-2027-12-29.from-fomc-2027-12-08.json)

2027-12-29 *"sits squarely inside that same window, a placement with NO PRECEDENT"*, so *"a pull-forward
to Tue 2027-12-28 or a push to a January 2028 date are both live."* The
[`from-fomc-blackout-start-2027-11-27`](../../../src/domain/market-events/proposals/fomc-minutes-2027-12-29.from-fomc-blackout-start-2027-11-27.json)

worth having on the board rather than discovering late."*

So: **is the dead week actually the hazard, or is something else doing the work — and does 2027-12-29
survive?** And behind it, the sibling's unfinished business: [11-17](fomc-minutes-2027-11-17.md) explained
the family's headline vol number away *for its own date* via a VIX-settlement collision. That mechanism
does not apply here. **Does the number reach this release, then?**

**One-line verdict:** the window is not the mechanism and the caveat comes off — displacement tracks
distance from the **Board of Governors' own closure**, `|gap| ≥ 2` is **0-for-14**, and 2027-12-29 sits at
**−2** with the Board's own remedy in 2025 having *chosen* that same configuration. The vol question

**calendar-window artifact**, the release adds nothing over its window, and 2027 moves the release into
the window that runs the other way. Date `estimate`; no directional call at D−477.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The family's D+21 census replicates exactly — 157 releases, 143 at D+21, fourteen exceptions.**
   SUPPORTED, from a third independent construction. The page states the convention verbatim — *"The
   minutes of regularly scheduled meetings are released three weeks after the date of the policy
   decision"* — and its 2027 panel reads `January 26-27 | March 16-17* | April 27-28 | June 8-9* |
   July 27-28 | September 14-15* | October 26-27 | December 7-8*`, December carrying the SEP asterisk.
   2027-12-07 is a Tuesday and 2027-12-08 a Wednesday (computed, not assumed), so the decision is 12-08
   and +21 days is **Wednesday 2027-12-29**. The parse returns **157 releases, decisions 2007-01-31 →
   2026-07-29, exactly 8 per year 2007–2025**, of which **143 sit at exactly D+21**; the 14 exceptions run
   D+19 to D+24 and by **meeting** month are **November ×6, December ×4, June ×2, October ×1** plus the
   **2020-03-15 unscheduled** meeting at D+24 — matching the [10-06](fomc-minutes-2027-10-06.md) and
   [11-17](fomc-minutes-2027-11-17.md) lanes instance for instance.

2. **The seeding proposals' December census was five times too small, and their *direction* of worry was
   wrong.** SUPPORTED, and this is the correction. `from-fomc-2027-12-08` read *"4 of 5 December
   meetings"* from the live page alone (2021–2026). The full record has **19 December meetings and 4
   displacements (21.1%)** — 2007, 2012, 2019 and 2025. The proposal's framing was that the four
   *undisplaced* Decembers *"ALL LANDED IN JANUARY"* and that a dead-week landing is therefore anomalous.
   That reads the coincidence and not the cause: December-meeting minutes land in January because D+21
   from a mid-December Wednesday *is* early January, not because January is preferred. The one December
   meeting early enough to break that — 2025-12-10 — is the only informative case, and leg 3 is what it
   actually says.

3. **Displacement is fully explained by distance from the BOARD OF GOVERNORS' own year-end closure, and
   2027 is in the bucket that is 0-for-14.** SUPPORTED — the deliverable. Board closures come from the
   Fed's own K.8 page, which states the split rule verbatim in its header (*"For holidays falling on
   Saturday, Federal Reserve Banks and Branches will be open the preceding Friday; however, the Board of
   Governors will be closed"*), consistent with 5 U.S.C. 6103(b) and OPM's own *"if a holiday falls on a
   Saturday … the preceding Friday will be treated as a holiday"*. Every December meeting, D+21 measured
   against the nearer of the Board's Christmas and New Year observances:

   | min \|gap\| to a Board closure | n | Displaced | Years |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | **0** | 2 | **2** | 2007, 2019 |
   | **1** | 3 | **2** | 2012, 2025 *(2011 held)* |
   | **2** | 4 | **0** | 2016, 2017, 2022, 2023 |
   | **4–8** | 10 | **0** | 2008–2010, 2013–2015, 2018, 2020, 2021, 2024 |

   So `|gap| ≤ 1` runs **4 displaced of 5** and `|gap| ≥ 2` runs **0 of 14**. The four displacements, with
   the direction each moved: 2007-12-11 (D+21 = Tue 2008-01-01, the closure itself) → **+1**, Jan 2;
   2012-12-12 (D+21 = Wed 2013-01-02, the day after) → **+1**, Jan 3; 2019-12-11 (D+21 = Wed 2020-01-01,
   the closure itself) → **+2**, Fri Jan 3; 2025-12-10 (D+21 = Wed 2025-12-31, the day before) → **−1**,
   Tue Dec 30. **2027-12-29 sits at gap −2** — five days after the Board's Christmas closure (Fri
   2027-12-24) and two days before its New Year closure (Fri 2027-12-31). Both of those dates are named
   outright in K.8's own footnote: *"the Federal Reserve Banks are open, but the Board of Governors is
   closed on … December 24, 2027, December 31, 2027"*, a footnote the page records having **updated on
   2026-07-08** specifically *"to reflect the observance date for New Year's Day from December 31, 2028,
   to December 31, 2027."*

   **And the method result is that this class gets no quantisation.** The [11-17](fomc-minutes-2027-11-17.md)
   lane's Thanksgiving gap is quantised to **−8, −1, 0, +7** because D+21 preserves the decision weekday
   and Thanksgiving is always a Thursday, so an eight-day margin is structurally protected. New Year's Day
   is a **fixed calendar date whose weekday drifts**, so the December gap is continuous — the observed
   values here are 0, 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 — and a December lane inherits none of that protection. **The
   gap must be computed in days, and the margin is 2, not 8.**

4. **The "the Board avoids the dead week" hypothesis is REFUTED by the only observation that can test
   it.** SUPPORTED as a refutation, and it is the single most load-bearing fact in this document.
   2025-12-10's D+21 was Wednesday 2025-12-31; the Board moved it to **Tuesday 2025-12-30** — one day,
   **still inside the Christmas-to-New-Year week**. A Board with an aversion to that week had Monday
   2026-01-05 and Wednesday 2026-01-07 freely available and did not use them. The constraint that bound
   was the *specific date*, not the *window*. Two further checks point the same way: **2011-12-13 held at
   D+21** and published Tuesday 2012-01-03, the first business day after a Monday 2012-01-02 closure — so
   even `gap = +1` is not automatic displacement (4 of 5, not 5 of 5) — and the three `gap ≥ 0`
   displacements all moved to the **next available business day**, which is the behaviour of a scheduling
   constraint, not of a preference about weeks.

5. **The exact structural twin already exists, is confirmed, and lands 364 days first.** SUPPORTED, and it
   is what converts leg 3's thin −2 margin into a checkable claim. [`fomc-minutes-2026-12-30`](fomc-minutes-2026-12-30.md)
   is the December 8–9, 2026 meeting's minutes, `confirmed` in this calendar on a **`FED:`** primary — the
   Board's own December 2026 month page listing "FOMC Minutes" at 2:00 p.m. Its geometry is 2027's,
   feature for feature: a **Wednesday**; inside the **Dec 26–31** dead week; **+5** days from the Board's
   Christmas closure and **−2** from its New Year closure; the **third of four open Board days** in a week
   bracketed by two closures. The Board has therefore already *scheduled and published* a minutes release
   at the configuration this document calls untested — and in 2025 it *chose* gap −2 as the remedy for a
   gap −1 problem. Three independent lines, one conclusion.

### Honest limits

and every number here is close-to-close, so ~4.5 of 6.5 hours precede the release; every sibling shares

2027-12-08 would be the **earliest December decision day in the 21-year record** (previous earliest:

tentative. The **14:00 ET release time is convention**, not sourced for this release; the Board's December
2027 month page does not exist yet and was not requested. And the whole document rests on a meeting —
