## At a glance

14:00 ET** — the last *institutional* Fed publication before the **Sep 15–16 FOMC** and before the
communications blackout that starts Saturday **2026-09-05**. It is **not a trade**: the only
measured study of the question (Zavodny & Ginther, *Southern Economic Journal* 2005) finds the
Beige Book's growth tone moves rates **only until you control for other macro data** — and this

## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** what will the 2026-09-02 Beige Book likely say, does a qualitative
all-districts report move the tape at all inside a live *hike* debate, and does it change how we
sit in the tracked names (NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL AMZN) into the Sep-16 FOMC?

**One-line verdict:** the last institutional Fed read before the blackout, and genuinely the
district-level test of the Warsh framing that turned 9/16 into a coin flip — but the only measured
evidence says it stops mattering once other macro data is in the window, and this window is packed
with it, so the honest call is *stand aside and read two counts*.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md)
(macro-print mode — no price instruments; `symbols: []`). Primary sources over aggregators: the
Board's Beige Book schedule page and its September-2026 calendar page for the date/time, the Board's
own July-2026 national summary for the baseline, and the Board's transcript of Warsh's Jackson Hole
keynote for the framing this report tests. The reaction-function leg is an academic study read from
the source PDF, not press narration. All fetches dated 2026-08-30.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date and time are right — SUPPORTED, and the entry still stays `estimate`.** Two
   federalreserve.gov pages agree, fetched 2026-08-30: the Beige Book schedule page lists the eight
   2026 editions as **Jan 14 · Mar 4 · Apr 15 · Jun 3 · Jul 15 · Sep 2 · Oct 14 · Nov 25**, and the
   Board's **September 2026 calendar** page lists "Beige Book — September 2 at 2:00 p.m." The date
   is also exactly the two-weeks-before-FOMC convention against the Sep 15–16 meeting. Promotion to
   `confirmed` is **not** taken here: this lane may not self-confirm its own proposed entry (the
   same limit applied to `treasury-3y-note-2026-09-08` and
   `government-funding-deadline-2026-09-30`). Every trading-adjacent line above therefore carries
   the `estimate` label and widens caution only.

2. **The measured reaction function is weak, and weakest precisely in a week like this one —
   SUPPORTED.** Zavodny & Ginther, *"Does the Beige Book Move Financial Markets?"*, **Southern
   Economic Journal 2005, 72(1), 138–151** (read from the authors' source PDF, fetched 2026-08-30).
   They build a quantitative index of the qualitative reports and find: more positive Beige Book
   reports on economic growth are associated with **increases in interest rates, particularly
   intermediate- and long-term rates — but not after controlling for several other macroeconomic
   indicators.** Their own reading: markets treat it as *a summary indicator of economic growth,
   but not of monetary policy moves in the near term.* That control result is the load-bearing part
   here, because the 9/2 report does not arrive alone — ISM Manufacturing and JOLTS print 9/1, ADP
   at 08:15 the same morning, ISM Services 9/3, payrolls 9/4. The one condition under which the
   Beige Book historically shows an effect (being the only thing on the tape) is absent.

3. **…but the Warsh regime is the strongest available argument *against* leg 2 — MIXED, and
   labelled as theory, not measurement.** Warsh has withdrawn the two things that normally carry the
   Fed's pre-meeting signal. From the Board's own transcript of the **2026-08-28** Jackson Hole
   keynote: *"In normal times, the role of forward guidance should be limited and circumscribed,"*
   and he opposes mechanical reaction functions because *"our knowledge just doesn't extend that
   far."* Press coverage of his first meeting as chair (June 2026) reports he declined to submit his
   own fed-funds dot and called individual projections "not helpful in the conduct of policy"
   (press-sourced, not primary). With guidance gone and the dot plot devalued, the surviving
   Fed-published inputs carry proportionally more of the signal — so the 2005 finding is being
   applied out of sample, to a Fed that communicates differently than any in that study's window.
   That is a real caveat and it is **why the stance is "read it" rather than "ignore it"** — but an
   unmeasured mechanism does not upgrade a stand-aside into a position.

4. **It is one of the last three Fed-sourced inputs before the blackout — SUPPORTED.** The FOMC
   blackout runs from **12:00 a.m. ET Saturday 2026-09-05 through 11:59 p.m. ET 2026-09-17** (the
   second Saturday before the meeting through the day after; Fed blackout convention, regional-Fed
   and press sources, checked 2026-08-30). The Board's September calendar leaves exactly three Fed
   communications before that gate closes: **Gov. Barr, 9/1 09:05 ET** ("Economic Outlook and
   Financial Inclusion"), **the Beige Book, 9/2 14:00 ET**, and **Gov. Waller, 9/3 08:30 ET**
   ("Economic Outlook"). The Beige Book is the only one of the three that is institutional — all
   twelve districts, published, not one governor's view. That is why it is on this calendar at all,
   and it is also the ceiling on its importance: it is the *last* word, not a *new* one.

5. **The July baseline gives two counts that diff cleanly — SUPPORTED.** From the Board's
   **July 15, 2026** national summary (fetched 2026-08-30): activity increased at a **slight to
   moderate pace in eleven of twelve districts**, one reporting no change. Labor: **five districts**
   showing modest/moderate/solid employment gains and **seven** with little-to-no change — an
   improvement from only one district with gains in the prior edition; wage growth modest-to-moderate
   in most, slight in two; skilled workers (technicians, trades) still scarce; several districts
   noted expanded AI use in hiring and productivity. Prices: **nine districts moderate, two robust,
   one slight**, with input costs in services, construction and manufacturing rising on energy,
   transportation and raw materials — contacts tying these to *"the conflict in the Middle East;
   others mentioned tariffs"* — and margin compression where input costs outran selling prices.
   Manufacturing expanded on data-center, machinery and defense demand; consumer spending edged up
   with fuel prices dampening discretionary categories and trading-down noted. **The two numbers to
   diff on 9/2 are the price count (9/2/1) and the labor split (5/7).**

6. **This edition is the evidence *behind* the Warsh framing, not a response to it — SUPPORTED, with
   a timing caveat.** Warsh's 8/28 keynote (Board transcript, primary): PCE **3.7%** over 12 months
   and **4.1%** over six; **54%** of the PCE basket above 3% over 12 months, **49%** annualised over
   six; labor *"quite stable,"* jobless **4.1%**, *"consistent with full employment"*; and *"I would
   be hard pressed to describe broad financial conditions as restrictive."* The Beige Book's price
   count is the district-side test of the breadth claim and the labor split is the district-side
   test of "quite stable." **Caveat:** each edition states an information-collection cutoff roughly
   a week before release, so most of these anecdotes predate the keynote — the report can corroborate
   or embarrass the framing, but it cannot answer it. That cutoff line was **not verified** for the
   July edition (see limits).

7. **Our exposure is second-order and already bracketed — SUPPORTED.** The entry carries
   `symbols: []`; transmission is hike-odds → long-duration equity (NVDA, AVGO, CRWV, MSFT, GOOG,
   META). Post-Warsh pricing as of the 8/28 close: **CME futures 59% hike** (from 35% Thursday),
   **FedWatch 55.7–57.5%**, **Kalshi 48%**, **Polymarket 69%** for any 2026 hike (CNBC/Benzinga/
   Kalshi, 8/28 — the feeds are not reconciled and are recorded as a spread, not a number). Two
   things bracket the Beige Book out of the day: **AVGO reports 9/2 AMC** (its own ledger), which is
   what the semis complex trades that session, and **payrolls 9/4** is the week's decision print.

### What the conditions support

**No trade, and no structure.** There is no house macro playbook (S1/S2/E1/S3/S4 + G1 are all
symbol-keyed), no consensus number to be surprised against, and the one measured study says the
effect vanishes under macro controls in exactly this kind of week. The report's value here is
**informational**: two counts that either corroborate or contradict the framing that repriced 9/16,
read in the same session as ADP and one day before Waller and two before payrolls.

### Honest limits

- **No consensus, no whisper, no implied move.** A qualitative report has none of the three. That
  absence is not a gap in the research — it is the structural reason this cannot be traded as an
  event.
- **The reaction-function evidence is one study, and it is old.** Zavodny & Ginther's sample ends
  well before 2005, i.e. before the forward-guidance era it is now being applied *past* the far side
  of. We have no own-instrument measurement of Beige Book release days; `intraday-edges.mjs` is
  symbol-keyed and this event has no symbols.
- **The collection-cutoff date is asserted from convention, not verified.** The July edition's
  "information collected on or before …" line could not be read: the Board's PDF would not parse and
  the Minneapolis Fed mirror returned **HTTP 403** (both 2026-08-30). Recorded as a failure, not
  passed over.
- **The Warsh dot-plot detail is press-sourced,** not from a Board transcript — the June-2026
  press-conference characterisation in leg 3 is second-hand and labelled as such.
- **Date is `estimate`.** Two Fed primary pages agree and the two-weeks-before-FOMC cadence checks
  out, but this lane may not promote its own entry. Nothing above is date-keyed action.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stand aside. Read the report as evidence into 9/16, take no position sized to it.** The date is
`estimate`-labelled (federalreserve.gov, checked 2026-08-30), which widens caution and licenses
nothing. Concretely: no new risk opened for the 14:00 ET release, no adjustment to any tracked name
on its content alone, and the two counts (price 9/2/1, labor 5/7 as of July 15) are logged for the
next pulse to diff.

**Kill switches:**

- **Four or more districts describing "robust" or "strong" price growth on 2026-09-02** (vs two in
  July) — the district-side corroboration of Warsh's breadth claim, landing on a market already at
  ~55–59% hike. Kills "informationally inert," not the stand-aside; the next pulse re-reads the
  hike-odds channel.
- **Nine or more districts reporting little-to-no change or outright declines in employment, or
  explicit layoff language** — contradicts "quite stable … consistent with full employment" two days
  before payrolls, and makes the 9/4 print the adjudicator between the report and the chair.
- **A measurable tape move in the 2026-09-02 14:00–15:00 ET hour with the release as the named
  driver** — falsifies leg 2 for this regime and forces the reaction function to be rebuilt from
  scratch rather than patched.
- **The 9/2 release slipping or the time changing** — the entry is `estimate`; a schedule change is
  a calendar correction, not a market signal.

**No forward test registered.** The stance takes no position sized to any branch, so there is
nothing to score in [`forward-tests.md`](../forward-tests.md); the kill switches above carry the
falsification, each with its date.
