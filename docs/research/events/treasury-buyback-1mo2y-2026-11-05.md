   difference between 8/8 and 0/8 at the same cap is therefore a **price-gap** property of the
   sector, not a program-level or cap-level fact — which is the sharpest form of the proposer's
   correction and the reason this row was worth tracking.

4. **The real uncertainty is existence, not size — MIXED, and it is the one thing that could

8. **The $30M outlier is a systems test, not a floating cap — REFUTES the proposal's second

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date and parameters `estimate`-labeled — tentative schedule; the confirming primary is the
11-04 announcement).** The 2026-11-05 1Mo-2Y buyback is a **data release, not a catalyst**: no
position keyed to it, and no attribution of that afternoon's tape to it. Base case
(**estimate**-labeled): Treasury announces the operation at **11:00am ET on 11-04 at a $4 billion
maximum**, dealers offer a multiple of it (every prior $4B operation drew $27.8–69.0B), and the
operation **fills at $4.000B** across roughly 5–16 issues at prices near par, making it the largest
single actual purchase in the nominal liquidity-support program that quarter — and passing without a
tape effect separable from the refunding statement 29 hours earlier, the midterms two days before
and the October jobs report the next morning.

**The correction this doc owns, and the one it inherits.** It **inherits** the 7Y-10Y lane's
finding that cap parity is not purchase parity, and **upgrades it from a comparison to a control**:
holding the cap fixed at $4B, this sector fills 8 of 8 while the other four $4B sectors fill 5 of 33,
so the published maximum explains none of the variation and the sector's price gap explains all of
it. It **corrects** three claims in its own proposal note — cover is 6.95x–17.25x rather than 9x–17x
(leg 9); the 2026-03-02 $30M operation is a systems test with unique `max_nbr_offers: 3` fingerprints
rather than evidence the cap floats (leg 8); and the operation's placement is anchored to the
refunding (7, 14, 7, 8, 0, 1, 1 days) rather than at risk of drifting from it (leg 7). And it
**adds** the risk the proposal did not name: the bucket has skipped exactly one quarterly slot ever,
and it was a November.

**Kill switches:**

- **No 1Mo-2Y liquidity-support operation is announced on 2026-11-04** — the November-2025 outcome
  repeating. The base case dies; record it as the second observed skip, and re-read whether the
  concurrent cash-management program displaced it (2025-12-03/12-11 is the precedent).
- **The 11-04 announced maximum is anything other than $4B** — leg 8 says that is new information
  rather than a repeat of 2026-03-02; re-read the refunding statement published 2½ hours earlier
  before any further inference, and check whether the replacement schedule posted.
- **The 11-05 operation accepts under $4.000B** — the first partial fill this bucket has ever
  printed in ten operations; the front-end price gap would be closing and leg 2's control breaks.
- **Offers on 11-05 come in under ~$15B (cover under ~3.75x)** — well beneath the 6.95x floor;
  the "cap has never been the constraint" mechanism (leg 3) would be weakening even if the
  operation still fills.
- **The 2026-09-09 cash-management operation accepts materially under its $12.5B cap** — the
  nearest live reading of the same front-end bid, and the earliest available warning on legs 2
  and 3.
- **Treasury publishes a replacement buyback schedule, or the 2026-11-04 refunding changes 1Mo-2Y
  cadence, cap or sector definition** — a genuinely different program; propose the changed
  operations as their own dated entries rather than folding them into this stance.
- **The 11-04 preliminary list contains a security maturing before 2026-12-06 or after
  2028-11-05** — the announced window would not be governing eligibility at all, which would
  reopen leg 5 as a data-quality question rather than an unexplained drift.
