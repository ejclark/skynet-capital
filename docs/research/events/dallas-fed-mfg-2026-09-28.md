## Initial research

### The question, plainly

What is the Dallas Fed's Texas Manufacturing Outlook Survey, what should we expect from the
September reading on 2026-09-28, does this release move the market at all, and how should a
paper-trading book holding long-duration tech (NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL AMZN) use a
single-state manufacturing survey?

**One-line verdict:** it does not move the market — measured, not assumed, across 20 releases — so
the honest output is a permanent stand-aside plus a **reading order**: the September survey window
(derived Sep 15–23) is the first to contain both the 9/16 FOMC and the post-Larak oil move, which
makes the prices and wages subcomponents the only rate-relevant lines in the report, while the
headline carries a Low-confidence reversion base case off a +10.3pt regime break whose one analogous
precedent (Jan 2025) collapsed 22.4 points the following month.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date and time are right, and they stay `estimate` — SUPPORTED, with the primary blocked.**
   Three independent secondaries agree the next release is **2026-09-28 at 14:30 UTC = 10:30 ET**:
   fxstreet's calendar entry for the Dallas Fed Manufacturing Business Index (fetched directly today)
   and tradingeconomics' series page (fetched directly today) both name Sep 28; investing.com's
   release-date history (fetched today) independently reproduces the whole 2026 cadence — **Jan 26,
   Feb 23, Mar 30, Apr 27, May 26, Jun 29, Jul 27, Aug 31**, plus Nov 24 and Dec 29 2025. That series
   is *exactly* "last Monday of the month, moving to Tuesday when that Monday is Memorial Day"
   (2026-05-25 was Memorial Day, hence the May 26 Tuesday), and 2026-09-28 is an ordinary Monday with
   no holiday. **The entry nonetheless stays `estimate`**, for the same two compounding reasons the
   [`chicago-pmi`](chicago-pmi-2026-09-30.md) sibling recorded: this lane never self-promotes an
   adjacency it proposed, and `dallasfed.org` — the only source that could carry a `FED:` prefix —
   is 403-blocked to us.

2. **What the survey actually is — SUPPORTED, and the scope is the point.** A monthly diffusion
   survey of Texas manufacturers run by the Dallas Fed, seasonally adjusted, reported as
   (% reporting increase − % reporting decrease), with a headline **general business activity** index
   plus production, new orders, shipments, capacity utilization, employment, hours, wages, raw-materials
   and finished-goods prices, an uncertainty index, and six-month-ahead expectations for each. Two
   structural facts govern everything below: it covers **one state**, and it leads the national ISM
   print by **a full month**, not by two business days the way Chicago does. That is why the calendar
   filed it `low` while Chicago is `medium` — it is context, not a leading indicator of the next ISM.

3. **The August print was a genuine regime break, not a beat — SUPPORTED.** Released 2026-08-31,
   August data: general business activity **11.6** from **1.3**, the highest since January 2025;
   production **16.1** (10.1); **new orders 22.0 (6.4)**; shipments **14.1** (8.8); capacity
   utilization **12.8** (5.9); company outlook **19.2** (+5.8); six-month-ahead future production
   **40.9** (34.6) and future general business activity **37.2** (26.5). Employment growth *slowed*,
   hours were "largely stable," prices paid were "rising further" and prices received "eased
   slightly"; uncertainty stayed elevated with inventories building and delivery times lengthening
   significantly (fx.co and tradingeconomics narration, both dated 2026-08-31; dallasfed.org 403).
   The scale is what matters. The 2026 series ran **−1.2 · 0.2 · −0.2 · −2.3 · 0.4 · 0.0 · 1.3**
   (Jan→Jul) — seven consecutive months inside a **3.6-point band** with a standard deviation of
   **1.17** — and then jumped **+10.3** in a single month. Against that seven-month regime August is
   roughly a **10σ** observation; against the fuller trailing-12-month distribution (σ **6.16**,
   mean −2.1, including the 2025 contraction) it is **~2.2σ**. Either way it is a break, not noise —
   and note it also beat its published forecast by **+9.2** (2.4 expected, mql5).

4. **The base rate says reversion, and it rests on n=1 — MIXED, and graded Low for exactly that
   reason.** The only 2025–26 reading above 11.6 is **January 2025's 14.1**; the very next month
   printed **−8.3**, a 22.4-point collapse, and the series then spent fourteen months below zero
   (bottoming at **−35.8** in April 2025, the tariff shock). That is the single analogous precedent
   and it reverted violently. Set against it: the *composition* of August's break argues the other
   way — new orders at 22.0 and six-month-ahead expectations at 37.2/40.9 are forward-looking legs,
   not a one-month activity spike, which is a different shape from January 2025's. A base rate of
   one, pointing against the internal composition, is not a forecast. It is why this doc's headline
   call is **Low** confidence and why no size attaches to it.

5. **Forecasters have systematically undershot this series in 2026 — SUPPORTED as an observation,
   REFUTED as a durable edge.** Published forecast vs actual by data month, 2026 (mql5's calendar
   history, **single-sourced** — investing.com's table shifts its forecast column and could not
   corroborate it): Jan **+10.1** · Feb +2.7 · Mar +3.4 · Apr −2.7 · May +5.5 · Jun +4.2 · Jul −2.2 ·
   Aug **+9.2**. Six of eight positive, mean surprise **+3.8**, and the two misses are small while
   both large surprises are upside. Tempting — and it does not survive one year back. The same
   column for 2025 gives a mean surprise of **−1.1** with only 5 of 12 positive and dispersion four
   times as wide (+24.6 in January against −21.1 the very next month). So the 2026 pattern is a
   **regime artifact**: forecasters kept extrapolating the 2025 contraction into a series that was
   recovering, and were late the whole way up. August's break is precisely the kind of event that
   ends that regime — the September consensus, whenever it appears, will be anchored to 11.6 rather
   than to a contraction. Recorded as a pattern to *watch die*, not to lean on.

### What the conditions support

Nothing directional, and less than any other event this calendar tracks. What it supports is a
**reading discipline with a measured refusal attached**: never trade the print (leg 6 makes that a
finding rather than a posture); when it lands, read raw-materials/finished-goods prices and the wages
index first, six-month expectations second, and the headline last; treat the headline's Low-confidence
reversion base case as a prior to be *checked*, not a call to be sized; and do not let a single-state
survey — in either direction — move the weight given to the 10/1 ISM and 10/2 payroll prints, which
are what this book actually sits in front of. Its structural job in the calendar is to be the bullish
counterweight to Chicago, so that a soft regional surprise and a firm one are equally visible.

### Honest limits

The dallasfed.org primary is **403 to us in every form attempted**, so this entire doc rests on dated
secondaries; the date cannot be promoted out of `estimate` from inside this lane even though three
independent secondaries agree on it. The forecast-history in leg 5 is **single-sourced** (mql5) and
investing.com's table could not corroborate it because its forecast column is shifted. The
finished-goods/raw-materials **27.6 / 37.0** figures are attributed to April 2026 by **inference**
from a matching 32.7 prior, not by a sourced date. The September collection window in leg 7 is
**derived** from one sourced example (Jul 14–22 → Jul 27) plus the cadence, and is ±2 days. Ten of the
twenty release dates in leg 6's measurement are cadence-derived rather than sourced — the rule is
validated by the ten that are sourced, but a wrong date would silently weaken the null it produces.
**n=20 has low power**: leg 6 rules out a large consistent release-hour effect and nothing subtler,
and it measures the *release hour* only — a slow-bleed or next-day effect would not appear. No
September consensus exists at D-27 and, on the 2026 pattern, none will until the release week. And
the composition argument in leg 4 genuinely cuts against the base rate it qualifies; this doc does not
claim to settle which wins.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `estimate`, triple-sourced secondaries re-checked 2026-09-01; dallasfed.org 403).**
Treat 2026-09-28 10:30 ET as a **low-impact reading exercise, never an event**: no position is
opened, closed or sized off it, no house playbook targets it, and the refusal is now **measured** —
across 20 releases the release hour is statistically indistinguishable from any other 10:30 hour on
SPY or QQQ (median percentile 52.5 / 55.5 against a null of 50; 10/20 and 11/20 above median). The
standing instruction is a reading order: **prices and wages before the headline**, because this Fed's
case is pinned to inflation, Texas manufacturing wage growth is at its strongest since Dec 2023, and
the September collection window (derived **Sep 15–23**) is the first to contain both the 9/16 FOMC and
the post-Larak oil move that August's window closed before. Base case for the September headline
(**estimate**-labeled, **Low** confidence): reversion off 11.6 toward the single digits, resting on a
base rate of **one** (Jan 2025's 14.1 → Feb 2025's −8.3) and explicitly contradicted by August's own
forward-looking composition. This series' job in the calendar is structural — it is the bullish
counterweight to `chicago-pmi-2026-09-30`, so that firm and soft regional surprises are equally
visible.

**Kill switches:**

- **A release-hour move above the 90th percentile on 2026-09-28 with a session wrap naming this
  survey as the cause** — leg 6's null breaks, the `low` impact tier is wrong, and this doc's whole
  stand-aside rests on a measurement that no longer holds. Re-run the leg-6 measurement with the new
  observation before repeating any of it.
- **A second consecutive print above ~10 on 2026-09-28** — August was a regime change rather than an
  air pocket; the Low-confidence reversion base case dies, leg 4's Jan-2025 precedent is retired as
  non-analogous, and the series' weight in the calendar goes up.
- **Prices/wages reverse hard** (raw materials back toward the low 30s, wages index falling below its
  March 25.2) — removes the inflation read that is this doc's only reason to open the report at all,
  and with it the reading order.
- **The Fed stops being inflation-anchored** — the **2026-09-16** FOMC repricing from hold-vs-hike to
  hold-vs-cut, or the 9/4 payroll print collapsing hike odds — at which point the growth headline
  reclaims primacy over the prices lines and this doc's reading order inverts.
- **A CR is enacted on or before 2026-09-30** — leg 10's shutdown branch closes and the October
  edition stays ordinary context. Conversely, **a lapse** is the trigger to file
  `dallas-fed-mfg-2026-10-26` as its own entry (deliberately not filed today — see the ledger row).
- **dallasfed.org becomes fetchable** (or a `FED:`-prefixed route to the TMOS calendar is found) —
  the date can be promoted out of `estimate` by whoever owns that change, and every secondary-sourced
  figure above becomes checkable against the primary.
