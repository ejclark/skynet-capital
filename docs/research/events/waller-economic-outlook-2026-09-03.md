### The question, plainly

A Fed governor is scheduled to take unscripted questions on the economic outlook at 08:30 ET on
2026-09-03. Governor speeches are ordinarily routine. Is this one? Specifically: is the date real,
what does Waller actually think as of today, how much of it is already in the price, and does any of
it change what the book should do?

appearance is **not tradeable in either direction** — its whole significance is *positional*: it is
the last scheduled Fed voice before a 12-day blackout, it lands the morning before payrolls, and it
belongs to the one senior official whose published framework the current data leaves genuinely
undetermined, after 52 days of silence. That combination argues for a timing discipline and a vol
posture, never a direction.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The 9/3 08:30 ET slot is listed on a primary source — and still correctly stays `estimate`.**
   SUPPORTED, with the label deliberately unchanged. federalreserve.gov/newsevents/2026-september.htm,
   fetched directly today, lists *"Speech by Governor Christopher J. Waller — Economic Outlook,
   Reuters NEXT Newsmaker Interview (Virtual)"* at **8:30 a.m. ET, September 3**, alongside Governor
   Barr at 9:05 a.m. ET on September 1. Two independent week-ahead aggregators carry the same
   Thursday slot (ZeroHedge "Key Events This Week"; Neil Sethi's 8/30 "Week Ahead", which calls Waller
   "the highlight of Fed speakers this week"). **Why it does not flip to `confirmed`:** the pulse that
   proposed this entry on 2026-09-01 fetched *this same page*, so re-fetching it adds corroboration of
   persistence, not a new class of evidence — flipping on identical evidence is self-confirmation with
   an extra step, which is precisely what the lane's no-self-confirm limit exists to stop. (Contrast
   [`fomc-blackout-start-2026-09-05.md`](fomc-blackout-start-2026-09-05.md), which flipped legitimately
   because a later session parsed a Fed PDF the proposing pulse never had.) The substantive reason
   agrees with the procedural one: a Board speaking slot is a *schedule*, not a rule-derived date, and
   slots get added and pulled on short notice. **One source divergence, noted not resolved:** a search
   snippet placed Waller at 9:05 a.m. (Barr's time) and ZeroHedge titles his topic "inflation outlook"
   rather than "Economic Outlook" — the Fed's own page wins on both.

2. **Waller has been publicly silent for 52 days, and that silence spans every event that re-based
   the September debate.** SUPPORTED — the load-bearing finding. The Fed's 2026 speech archive
   (fetched today) lists Waller's most recent remarks as **2026-07-13, "Monetary Policy at a
   Crossroads"**, and lists exactly **one** Board speech in all of August: Warsh's 8/28 Jackson Hole
   keynote. Inside that gap: the 7/29 FOMC held 3.50–3.75% on a 9–3 vote with three hawkish dissents;
   July payrolls printed **−23k** on 8/7; headline PCE printed **3.7% y/y** with core **3.3%** on
   8/26; the BLS preliminary benchmark revision came in at only **−79k** through March 2026 on 8/28;
   Warsh abolished forward guidance the same day and hike odds went **~35% → 57–59%** in a session;
   and US strikes on Larak Island (8/30) plus Iranian retaliation on bases in the UAE and Jordan
   (8/31) put Brent back above **$90** with the 10y at **4.76%**, its highest since Jan 2025. Waller
   has publicly reacted to none of it. Whatever he says Thursday is his first word on all of it at
   once — which is what makes a routine governor slot high-variance rather than high-impact.

3. **His own July test has now fired in both directions, leaving his position genuinely
   undetermined.** SUPPORTED. In "Monetary Policy at a Crossroads" (federalreserve.gov, 2026-07-13,
   New York Association for Business Economics) Waller set an explicit two-legged conditional: core
   inflation had "steadily moved up… from 3 percent in December 2025 to 3.4 percent in May," and *"if
   we get another hot reading on core inflation this week, then the FOMC will need to consider
   tightening monetary policy in the near term"* — against a labor market he judged stable, citing
   *"an average of 111,000 jobs a month for the past three months"* and wage growth "around 3.5
   percent… consistent with 2 percent inflation." Since then the **inflation leg fired hawkish**
   (PCE 3.7% headline / 3.3% core, and Warsh's own 6-month annualized figure of 4.1% — inflation did
   not decline) while the **labor leg fired dovish** (−23k against his 111k characterisation). No
   other senior official's published framework is split like this: Warsh resolved it hawkishly on
   8/28 by calling labor "quite stable" at 4.1% unemployment and pinning the case on inflation;
   Hammack resolved it hawkishly on 8/10. Waller has not resolved it at all.

4. **He is the Board's demonstrated reverser — which is why his answer is not predictable from his
   last speech.** SUPPORTED. On **2026-01-30** Waller dissented *against holding*, wanting a 25bp
   **cut**: *"monetary policy is still restricting economic activity, and economic data make it clear
   to me further easing is needed"* (federalreserve.gov statement, fetched today), arguing the policy
   rate sat "50 to 75 basis points above" a ~3% neutral and citing 2025 payroll growth of "just under
   600,000" against a 1.9 million historical average. By 7/13 the same governor was naming the
   conditions for tightening. That is the sharpest documented reversal on the Board inside eight
   months, and it cuts against any model that reads Thursday's answer off his most recent speech.

5. **He votes, and the July minutes show a Committee nearer to hiking than the 9–3 vote suggests —
   with the condition for tightening now met.** SUPPORTED. The 2026-07-29 minutes (federalreserve.gov,
   fetched today) record the hold at **3-1/2 to 3-3/4 percent**, with **Hammack, Kashkari and Logan**
   dissenting for +25bp — but the discussion runs hawkish beyond the dissent count: *"Several
   participants favored an increase of 25 basis points in the target range at this meeting"* (more
   than the three who formally dissented), *"Many participants assessed that policy tightening would
   likely be necessary if inflation did not decline,"* and *"Some participants commented that
   financial conditions might not currently be sufficiently restrictive."* **Inflation did not
   decline.** Waller is recorded voting with the majority to hold — he did **not** dissent in July.
   *(Flagged because a first search pass returned the opposite; the minutes' own voting list settles
   it and is what this doc uses.)*

6. **The reaction channel on this Board is the front end of the curve, not equity volatility.**
   SUPPORTED but single-observation, and inherited rather than re-derived. 8/28 is the clean natural
   experiment scored in [`jackson-hole-2026-08-28.md`](jackson-hole-2026-08-28.md): September hike
   odds near-doubled, the 2y went **+8bp**, and the S&P finished **−0.13% to −0.25%** with VIX closing
   at **14.43**, its 2026 low. One observation, carried at Medium confidence, with the dated falsifier
   already registered there (a 9/16 FOMC that moves the S&P ≥1.5% on a <5bp 2y move). Today's readings
   say nothing has changed: **VIX 14.92**, S&P **7686.14**, 10y **4.758%** (all 2026-08-31 closes,
   pulled live today).

7. **Positioning is one-sided hawkish, so the surprise asymmetry runs dovish.** MIXED — this is
   inference, and it is labeled as such. September hike odds sat at **~31%** on 8/14 after the payroll
   shock, **57–59%** after Warsh on 8/28, and **62–66%** on 8/31 (CME FedWatch via Forbes and press,
   as-of dates attached). A hawkish Waller therefore confirms a majority view already in the price; an
   ambivalent or dovish Waller would be the only senior official to say out loud that the −23k print
   matters, into a market that has spent four sessions deciding it doesn't. That is a real asymmetry
   in *what could surprise*, but it rests on odds levels alone — **no options-positioning, skew or
   dealer-gamma data was pulled**, so it is a caution about sizing, never an edge to trade.

  entirely. So leg 7's asymmetry has no measured reaction-magnitude prior behind it. This is the

  do a whole lot"*), so her marginal information content is near zero. **That is this doc's organising

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `estimate` — primary-listed on federalreserve.gov's own September calendar, held at
`estimate` per the no-self-confirm limit and because Board speaking slots are pulled on short
notice):** **no position keyed to this event, and none contemplated.** Treat **2026-09-03 08:30 ET**
as a *deadline*, not a catalyst: the last scheduled Fed voice before the 09-05 blackout, speaking
before the payroll print rather than after it. Two behavioural consequences and no directional one —
(1) anything in the book that depends on official interpretation of the hold-vs-hike question gets
resolved before Thursday's open, and (2) no short volatility from **09-05 through 09-16**. Where Fed
risk must be hedged at all, hedge it in the front end of the curve, not in equity vol (leg 6). The
dovish-surprise asymmetry in leg 7 widens caution about *sizing into* Thursday; it is explicitly
**not** a licence to position for a dovish outcome, and an `estimate` date licenses nothing on the
date alone.

**Kill switches:**

- **Relevance kill (mechanical):** the 9/3 slot disappearing from federalreserve.gov's September
  calendar before **2026-09-03** — the event evaporates and this doc closes out empty rather than
  being patched.
- **Priced-out kill:** September hike odds back below **~40%**, or cut odds off 0%, before
  **2026-09-03** — the two-sidedness that makes a last scheduled voice matter goes away, and this
  reverts to a routine governor appearance.
- **Variance kill (the doc's core claim, dated and falsifiable):** if Waller's **2026-09-03** remarks
  move September hike odds by **less than ~2 points** on FedWatch, legs 2–4 are wrong — he is as
  fully priced as Hammack, and "highest-variance voice" was a story rather than a measurement. Score
  this at close-out.
- **Channel kill:** inherited from [`jackson-hole-2026-08-28.md`](jackson-hole-2026-08-28.md) — a
  **2026-09-16** FOMC that moves the S&P ≥1.5% while the 2y moves <5bp kills the rates-not-equity-vol
  read that leg 6 and the "this month" call both rest on.
- **Guidance-restoration kill:** Waller publishing an explicit reaction function or rate path on 9/3
  would contradict Warsh's 8/28 framework directly and mean the Board has restored guidance — which
  changes how every subsequent Fed-speak event in this calendar should be read, not just this one.

No forward test registered in [`forward-tests.md`](../forward-tests.md) — the stance is guard-shaped
and deliberately unscored. The variance kill above is a dated falsifier scored at close-out, not a
directional prediction.
