### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The dates are right, and the mechanism is primary-sourced — SUPPORTED.** SEC EDGAR, Select Sector SPDR Trust Form 497 filed **2026-01-31** (fetched direct 2026-09-04), verbatim: *"if, on the second to last business day of March, June, September, or December a company has a weight greater than 24% or the sum of the companies with weights greater than 4.8% exceeds 50%, a secondary reweighting will be triggered with the reweighting effective date being after the close of the last business day of the month. This secondary reweighing will use the Select Sector Index constituent capped index weights as of the second to last business day … utilizing the current additional weight factors and membership, shares outstanding, and investable weight factors as of the reweighting effective date."* September 2026's Fridays run 09-04 / 09-11 / 09-18 / 09-25, so 09-28 is Monday, the second-to-last business day is **Tue 09-29** and the last is **Wed 09-30**. The test is 09-29; the trade, if any, clears after the 09-30 close. **The entry stays `estimate` regardless of the source grade** — not because the date is doubtful but because the *event* is conditional, and no primary can pre-confirm a breach. That is the honest reason, and it is different from the sibling's (an unfetchable methodology PDF).

2. **The capping rule the sibling arithmetic used is superseded — SUPPORTED, and this is the load-bearing correction.** S&P DJI changed the Select Sector capping mechanism effective **prior to the open on 2024-09-23**, published in *Explaining Changes to Select Sector Indices* (Indexology, 2024-09-10) and covered by etf.com. Both describe the same two mechanisms: the **legacy** rule *"reduces the weight of the smallest company in the group to 4.5%, and the process is repeated iteratively, if necessary, until there are no breaches"*; the **new** rule instead sets *"the aggregate weight of the larger companies … to 45% and the larger companies' individual weights … determined by their relative proportions"*, with excess *"proportionally redistributed to all companies with weights below 4.5%, with no company's weight being increased above 4.5%."* The **thresholds themselves did not change** — 24% / 4.8% / 50% survive the update, which is why the trigger language in the 2026 SEC filing still reads the same. The SEC 497 I fetched today independently carries the live aggregate form: *"the weight of such Component Stocks will be reduced proportionately so that the sum of such Component Stocks represent 45% of the total value."* **What this retires:** the sibling ledger's leg 7 named an unreconciled `23/4.5/50` (Morningstar) vs `24/4.8/50` (SEC) conflict and deliberately refused to average it. The conflict is now resolved in the better direction — the `4.5%` in Morningstar's description is the *legacy* iterative floor, not a live threshold, and the two sources were describing two different eras of the same rule rather than contradicting each other.

3. **Under the live rule, the aggregate leg is close to dead after the 09-18 reset — SUPPORTED by arithmetic, with its proxy named.** Post-cap, the over-4.8% group is scaled to 45% *by relative proportions*, which pushes every marginal member well below the 4.8% line rather than parking it at 4.5%. Working XLC's 2026-08-28 holdings: the cohort is META 16.71 + GOOGL 10.38 + GOOG 8.27 + T 5.20 + VZ 4.96 + CMCSA 4.88 + NFLX 4.84 = **55.24%**, so it binds; scaling by 45/55.24 = 0.8146 leaves META 13.61, GOOGL 8.46, GOOG 6.74 above 4.8% — **~28.8% total** — and drops T (4.24), VZ (4.04), CMCSA (3.98) and NFLX (3.94) out of the group entirely. Re-breaching 50% by 09-29 would then need roughly **five** names to climb back over 4.8% in seven sessions. XLE is the same shape with a bigger starting breach: XOM 20.54 + CVX 14.99 + COP 6.14 + MPC 5.43 + PSX 5.28 + VLO 5.09 = **57.47%** (holdings 2026-08-13), scaling to leave XOM ~16.1 and CVX ~11.7 clearly above the line and the four refiners at ~4.0–4.8, needing ~3 crossings. **Contrast with the legacy rule**, which would have left CMCSA and NFLX sitting *at* 4.5% — a single +6.7% relative move away from re-entering the cohort and discretely adding ~4.8pp. So the rule change did not merely alter a trim size; it converted the aggregate leg from a knife-edge into a real buffer, and that is exactly the conclusion the superseded arithmetic could not have reached.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `estimate`, `EST:` rule-derived; mechanism `SEC:`-primary).** Treat 2026-09-30 as an **unannounced, conditional, low-impact backstop** — the one dated afternoon this quarter where a forced Select Sector trade can clear without a pro-forma file to warn of it. **No position is licensed by it, on any horizon.** The refusal rests on two independent arithmetic legs rather than on a general claim. First, **the aggregate leg is buffered, not knife-edged**: under the live proportional-to-45% rule (SEC 497, 2026-01-31), the 09-18 rebalance leaves XLC's over-4.8% group at ~29% and XLE's at ~28%, so re-breaching 50% by 09-29 would take three to five names crossing back over 4.8% in seven sessions. Second, **the one live path is small**: XLY's AMZN at 24.30% (holdings 08-14, ~24.6% after drift) is the only name in six funds near a cap, and re-breaching 24% by 09-29 needs a routine +4.3% relative move — but a fired trim is ~1.3pp of $22.94B ≈ **$300M** in the market's most liquid name, clearing into a quarter-end close that also carries PCE, the third GDP estimate, ADP and a **government-funding deadline**. Carry forward one correction that outlives this event: **any Select Sector capping arithmetic computed with the iterative-to-4.5% rule is describing the pre-2024-09-23 methodology** and must be recomputed before it is cited again — including the sibling [`sp-rebalance-proforma-capped-2026-09-11`](sp-rebalance-proforma-capped-2026-09-11.md) ledger's ~$161M XLC figure and the forward test built on it.
