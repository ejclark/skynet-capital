## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This entry arrived as a proposal from the
[`treasury-coupon-announcement-2026-11-12`](treasury-coupon-announcement-2026-11-12.md) sweep, and
that proposal had already done the obvious work: it established the new-issue cadence, the $16B grid,
the predecessor CUSIP, and it handed the size prediction to a forward test on the announcement's own
ledger. The [10-21 sibling](treasury-20y-bond-2026-10-21.md) then did the next obvious thing — it
built a correct bid-to-cover yardstick and showed that 20-Year auction days are, on average, calmer
than ordinary sessions.

So the question left for this session was narrower and sharper: **the 10-21 doc measured the auction's
reaction function on all 44 auctions pooled — does that measurement survive being asked about the
new-issue slot specifically, which is the population this event is in?**

**One-line verdict:** it does not, and the reason is not statistical. The 20-Year new issue is the
only slot in this complex that routinely shares its session with a 2:00pm FOMC minutes release —
9 of 15 times, against 0 of 29 for reopenings — so for this slot the close-to-close yield move is
**structurally unidentifiable**, not merely noisy. The stance that follows is a grading rule with the
tape removed from it.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The date, `estimate` status and new-issue classification are right — SUPPORTED.** The schedule
PDF carries the row verbatim: `20-Year BOND · Announcement Thursday, November 12, 2026 · Auction
Wednesday, November 18, 2026 · Settlement Monday, November 30, 2026`. The **absence** of the trailing
`R` is the classification, and the same fetch shows `20-Year BONDR` on the September, October,
December and January rows for contrast — so the marker is present in the document and its absence
here is a reading, not a transcription gap. The 1:00pm ET time is Treasury's standing convention and
is **not** separately sourced. `estimate` is correct on both stated counts.

**2. The 20-Year new issue systematically shares its session with the FOMC minutes — SUPPORTED, and
this is the finding.** Crossing the Fed's 30 published minutes dates against the 44 auctions:

| Slot | n | Sessions that also carry a 2:00pm FOMC minutes release |
|---|---|---|
| **New issue** ($16B) | 15 | **9** — 2024-02-21, 2024-05-22, 2024-08-21, 2025-02-19, 2025-08-20, 2025-11-19, 2026-02-18, 2026-05-20, 2026-08-19 |
| Reopening ($13B) | 29 | **0** |

Since 2024-02 the new-issue rate is **9 of 11**. The mechanism is a calendar identity, not a
coincidence: the Fed releases minutes exactly three weeks after the decision day, and the four FOMC
meetings that end in the *last* days of January, April, July and October therefore land their minutes
on the third-or-fourth Wednesday of February, May, August and November — which is the 20-Year
new-issue Wednesday. The other four meetings (March, June, September, December) put their minutes in
the first half of April, July, October and January, which is why they never touch a reopening. The
two misses fit the same rule rather than breaking it: **2024-11-20** missed because the post-election
meeting slipped to Nov 6–7 (minutes 11-26), and **2025-05-21** missed because that quarter's meeting
was May 6–7 (minutes 05-28).

**3. 2026-11-18 is the tenth, and it is confirmed on both legs — SUPPORTED.** This calendar already
carries `fomc-minutes-2026-11-18` as **`confirmed`/`FED:` at 2:00 p.m. ET**, sourced from
`newsevents/2026-november.htm` and cross-checked against the three-week convention (Oct 28 + 21 =
Nov 18). So the collision is not a base-rate inference: the *minutes* leg is confirmed and only the
*auction* leg is `estimate`. `tic-monthly-2026-11-18` — the monthly foreign-holdings release, i.e.
the actual data series that the auction's indirect share is used as a same-day proxy for — publishes
at 4:00pm the same afternoon.

**4. The pooled "auction days are quiet" result is a reopening result — SUPPORTED.** Recomputing the
10-21 ledger's own measure by slot, on 20-Year CMT change from prior close to auction-day close:

| Sample | n | Mean change | t | Mean absolute change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All sessions 2023–2026 | 920 | +0.13bp | 0.74 | **4.19bp** |
| All 20Y auction days (the pooled figure) | 44 | −1.77bp | −2.28 | 4.05bp |
| **New issues** | 15 | **−2.53bp** | −1.62 | **4.80bp** |
| Reopenings | 29 | −1.38bp | −1.59 | **3.66bp** |

The pooled 4.05bp is two-thirds reopenings. The new-issue slot's own absolute move is **4.80bp** —
*above* the all-session 4.19bp, not below it. So the sibling's conclusion ("auction days are calmer
than average") is true of the population it mostly measured and **false of the one this event is
in**. The stance it supports — read, don't trade — is unaffected; the reason given for it was wrong
for this slot.

**5. Whether the minutes cause the extra drift is UNPROVEN, and the interesting part is that it does
not matter — MIXED, reported honestly.** Splitting the 15 new issues:

| Sample | n | Mean change | t | Mean absolute change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| New issue **with** minutes | 9 | **−3.22bp** | −2.11 | 4.33bp |
| New issue **without** minutes | 6 | −1.50bp | −0.45 | 5.50bp |
| Minutes days with **no** 20Y auction | 21 | +0.14bp | 0.11 | 4.52bp |

Minutes days on their own are indistinguishable from ordinary sessions (+0.14bp). Collision days
carry the only |t| above 2 outside the pooled sample. But n=9, this session split one dataset several
ways, and no multiple-comparison correction has been applied — so this is a **hypothesis with a
falsifier, not a result**. What survives regardless of the statistics is leg 2's counting: whatever
the true auction effect is, **no close-to-close measure of this slot can isolate it**, because 9 of
the last 11 such sessions have a Fed release in them. That is the load-bearing claim, and it needs no
t-statistic.

**6. The 10-21 ledger's 2.60 threshold does not travel to this slot — SUPPORTED, mechanically.** The
new-issue bid-to-cover distribution, all 15 since 2023-01, sorted: 2.34, 2.36, 2.39, 2.41, 2.43,
2.46, 2.51, 2.53, 2.54, 2.54, 2.54, 2.55, 2.56, 2.56, **2.58**. The $16B subset (n=13, excluding the
two $15B sales in early 2023) has the identical floor and ceiling. **2.60 has never been reached at a
20-Year new issue.** The sibling registered `FT-treasury-20y-bond-2026-10-21-1` on "the print clears
2.60" for a *reopening*, where the base rate is 12 of 15 — a correct test in its own population and a
guaranteed failure in this one. Stated plainly because the two auctions are eight weeks apart and the
number is memorable.

**7. Calendar month is not a usable prior for this slot — REFUTED as a shortcut.** New-issue
bid-to-cover by month: February **2.430** (n=4), May **2.520** (n=4), August **2.542** (n=4, and
remarkably tight at 2.53–2.56 across four years), November **2.443** (n=3). November's three
observations are **2.58, 2.34, 2.41** — spanning the entire 15-auction range from ceiling to floor.
So "November is the weak one" and "November is the strong one" are both supportable from three points
and neither is worth carrying. Recorded to kill the seasonal prior before someone forms it.

**8. Direct-bidder share has shifted regime, and it is the cleaner cut than indirect — SUPPORTED at
the new-issue slot, MIXED across the complex.** Share of the **competitive** base (indirect + direct
+ dealer, which is what press reports; dividing by `total_accepted` includes SOMA add-ons and gives
different numbers):

| Period | n | Direct share, new issues |
|---|---|---|
| 2023-02 → 2025-05 | 10 | 18.0, 18.1, 20.2, 16.5, 19.7, 19.2, 19.3, **7.9**, 19.5, 14.1 — max **20.2** |
| 2025-08 → 2026-08 | 5 | **26.5, 29.1, 27.2, 22.9, 24.6** — min **22.9** |

No overlap, with a 2.7-point gap between the two groups. Indirect share does **not** separate this
way (2024-02's 59.1% sits inside the recent range), so the usual "watch indirects for foreign demand"
read is the noisier one here. **The honest limit is stated up front rather than buried:** reopenings
lifted over the same period too (27.9 in 2025-09, 29.1 in 2026-01) but inconsistently (19.9 in
2026-06, 16.2 in 2026-07), so the perfect 10-vs-5 split at the new-issue slot is a five-observation
window inside a broader, messier curve-wide shift. That is why the forward test on it is registered
at **medium** confidence and not high.

**9. The predecessor's tape is confounded twice over — SUPPORTED, and it is why leg 4 matters.**
CUSIP **912810UX4** sold as this cycle's new issue on **2026-08-19** at $16B, high yield **5.204%**,
bid-to-cover **2.53**, direct share 24.6%. That session was **both** an FOMC-minutes day *and* the day
Treasury announced (`sb0607`) that it was doubling long-end buyback caps from $2B to "at least $4B"
per operation. The 20-Year moved **−11bp**, the largest of the nine collision days, and the
[08-19 close-out](treasury-20y-bond-2026-08-19.md) already attributes it to the buyback announcement
rather than the sale. So the single most recent, most comparable observation this event has carries
**three** candidate causes in one close-to-close number. Leg 4's identification problem is not
theoretical.

**10. The supporting regime expires two weeks before this sale — SUPPORTED, and it is dated.**
`sb0607`'s doubled long-end caps are "in effect for the remainder of this refunding quarter
(**through November 4, 2026**)", and `treasury-buyback-10y20y-2026-11-04` is on this calendar as the
last operation inside that window. The 2026-11-04 refunding is where Treasury said it "will provide
more information about future buyback sizes." So this auction is the **first long-end coupon sale
under whatever replaces it** — a genuine structural unknown at D-71, and one that resolves on a
published date rather than drifting.

**11. Current conditions are logged and are close to worthless at D-71 — stated so rather than
dressed up.** As of the **2026-09-08** close: CMT 2Y **4.39** · 10Y **4.80** · 20Y **5.26** · 30Y
**5.25**; **20s30s +1bp**, unchanged in sign from the 10-21 ledger's 09-04 reading. VIX **15.72**, up
from 14.53 on 09-04 — a 1.2-point rise, well inside the 3-point screen threshold and not a regime
change. Brent **$99.31**, up **3.1%** from $96.28 on 09-04 and the largest single move in this
session's baseline. Today's own 3-Year note auction covered **2.72** at a **4.474%** high yield —
second-best cover of 2026 against a 2025-onward median of 2.64 — so the front end of the coupon
complex is being absorbed comfortably at the highest yield of the series. All of it resolves through
the **09-16 FOMC and the 10-28 FOMC**, both before this auction. Recorded as the baseline the next
pulse diffs against, not as information about 11-18.

### Honest limits

  minutes effect rather than an auction one." Leg 2's counting is what the stance rests on.
- **CMT is a 3pm close against a 1:00pm auction and a 2:00pm release.** That is exactly why leg 4's

- **No tail and no when-issued yield.** `auctions_query` publishes high, low and median yield but not

- **Leg 8's regime split is five observations** inside a curve-wide shift that reopenings show

- **The $16B size is not this doc's to test** and is deliberately not re-registered — see

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `estimate`; size expected $16B, unpublished until 2026-11-04 and owned by another
forward test).** No position, no directional bet, no exposure sized off this event — `symbols: []`
and the date is an `estimate`, which widens caution and licenses nothing. The substantive stance is a
**two-part grading rule**, and the second part is what this doc contributes:

1. **Grade the auction, not the day.** The 2026-11-18 print is judged on bid-to-cover against the
   **new-issue slot alone** (n=15: floor 2.34, median 2.53, ceiling 2.58), on **direct-bidder share**
   on the competitive base, and on the tail when press publishes it. The session's yield move is
   **not** evidence about this auction and is not to be quoted as such — the FOMC minutes land 60
   minutes after the bid deadline, as they have in 9 of the last 11 new-issue sessions.
2. **The 2.60 threshold does not cross populations.** `FT-treasury-20y-bond-2026-10-21-1` predicts a
   *reopening* clears 2.60 on a 12-of-15 base rate. Eight weeks later this *new issue* is expected to
   print below it, and that is not deterioration — 2.60 has never been reached at this slot.

Two expectations are registered as
[`FT-treasury-20y-bond-2026-11-18-1`](../forward-tests/treasury-20y-bond-2026-11-18.md) (the print
lands inside the new-issue band, at or below 2.58) and `FT-treasury-20y-bond-2026-11-18-2` (direct
share holds at or above 21.5% on the competitive base).

**Kill switches:**

- **The Fed moving the minutes off 2026-11-18, or Treasury moving the auction off it on 2026-11-12.**
  The identification problem dissolves, the day's tape becomes readable, and part 1 of the stance is
  retired for this instance (though leg 2's base rate would still stand for the next new issue).
- **A bid-to-cover above 2.58 on 2026-11-18** — the new-issue ceiling breaks out of sample after 15
  auctions, and leg 6's population claim, on which the whole "2.60 doesn't travel" argument rests,
  fails in the one observation it was written for.
- **A bid-to-cover below 2.34** — the 15-auction floor breaks. Would say something about November
  supply absorption that no yardstick correction can explain away, and would make the first
  post-buyback-window long-end sale (leg 10) the live question rather than a footnote.
- **Direct share below 21.5%** — leg 8's regime split was a five-observation artifact, and the
  composition dimension this doc adds goes back to being noise.
- **The 2026-11-04 refunding publishing a Nov-26 20-Year cell other than 16** — the supply leg stops
  being settled and every claim above that treats issuance as a constant gets re-derived rather than
  patched. Owned and scored by `FT-treasury-coupon-announcement-2026-11-12-1`; listed here because it
  invalidates this doc too.
- **The 2026-11-04 refunding letting the doubled long-end buyback caps lapse without replacement** —
  leg 10 becomes the dominant fact about this sale, and a soft print would have a structural
  explanation that the demand-side grading rule above would otherwise mis-attribute.
- **The 2026-09-15 reopening printing below 2.50** — the reopening population's 44-auction floor
  breaking one cycle early on the same CUSIP. Would say deterioration is live now, and this doc's
  stance would be rewritten before its own event's terms exist.
