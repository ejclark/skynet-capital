## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The calendar carries a soft, `estimate`-dated entry saying the corporate buyback bid "begins
closing around September 12" ahead of Q3 earnings. Is that a date the tape can be traded around,
how large is the flow that leaves, and does the removal of that bid actually do anything measurable
to equities — in particular to the names this book tracks?

**One-line verdict:** the flow is real and its size is roughly knowable, but the **date is not a
date** (09-12 is a Saturday, and issuer policies spread the true start across ~09-10 → 09-16), and
the one formal study of the question finds **no** performance penalty in blackout windows — so the
honest output is a stand-aside on every horizon plus a recommendation to re-derive or retire the
entry at close-out, not a trade.

**Method:** macro/sector mode per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md) — no
earnings-style instrument applies (`symbols: []`, no issuer). Sourced web research with primary
sources preferred; SEC EDGAR queried directly for Apple's fiscal-period ends and exhibit index; a
one-off Yahoo daily-bar fetch used to measure the two-session S&P base rate the forward test needs
(the cached earnings instruments were not touched, so no cache-bust applies). Read against
[`trade-playbooks.md`](../../plans/trade-playbooks.md) and
[`multi-symbol-sweep.md`](../multi-symbol-sweep.md) for whether any house playbook is flow-keyed.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The tracked date is a Saturday — there is no session on it.** SUPPORTED, arithmetic.
   2026-09-12 falls on a Saturday; the first session the ramp can touch is Monday **2026-09-14**.
   This is the same surface shape as [`fomc-blackout-start-2026-09-05`](fomc-blackout-start-2026-09-05.md)
   (also a Saturday) but for the opposite reason: *that* one is a Saturday **by rule** (the Fed's
   own policy text — "12:00 a.m. Eastern Time the second Saturday before a meeting"), whereas this
   one is a Saturday **by coincidence**, because a desk note rounded a distribution of issuer
   policies to a single calendar day. A rule-derived Saturday is precise; a rounded one is not, and
   the two should never be read the same way.

2. **The start is a ramp across ~09-10 → 09-16, and Apple's fiscal calendar is why 09-12 looks
   exact.** SUPPORTED. There is no mandated blackout period; filed insider-trading policies cluster
   on three shapes, all ending ~48 hours / two full trading days after the earnings release:
   (a) fourteen days before fiscal quarter-end, (b) the close of the **tenth day of the quarter's
   third month**, (c) the **fifteenth calendar day** of the quarter-end month. For a calendar-quarter
   issuer (quarter-end 09-30) those give **09-16**, **09-10** and **09-15** respectively. The
   leading edge is Apple: EDGAR's own filing metadata (fetched direct 2026-09-04) shows Apple's
   fiscal periods ending on a Saturday — FY2025 at **2025-09-27**, FQ3 FY2026 at **2026-06-27** —
   which puts FY2026's close at **2026-09-26**, and a 14-day rule therefore puts the market's
   largest single repurchaser into blackout on exactly **2026-09-12**. Rubner's "around September
   12" is a defensible summary of that distribution, not a precise market-wide event, and it should
   be quoted as a ramp from here on.

3. **The flow that leaves is real, and roughly sized — from desk estimates, not primary counts.**
   SUPPORTED (vendor-grade). Goldman estimates **~$1.4T** of US repurchases in 2026, above 2025 but
   still under 2024 in nominal terms, and its Q2-2026 authorization total ran **$1.15T annualized**,
   a record. Its corporate execution desk's own rule of thumb is that executions **"typically
   decline by ~30%"** during blackout, and for the directly analogous Q1/Q2-2026 transition it put
   **$4.5–5.5B of daily corporate bid** disappearing from the tape for roughly four weeks. Citadel's
   note is consistent in direction: >**$1.1T** of announced authorizations moved into an open window
   through August, with support fading through September. **Honest caveat:** every one of these is a
   sell-side desk estimate of its own flow extrapolated to the market — none is an S&P DJI or SEC
   aggregate, and the two desks' numbers were never reconciled against each other.

4. **The folk theory — blackout means weak tape — is REFUTED by the only formal study found.**
   REFUTED. State Street Global Advisors (Matthew J. Bartolini), *"Buyback Blackout Periods Do Not
   Negatively Impact Performance"*, studies the **S&P 500 Buyback Index** (the top quintile of S&P
   500 firms by buyback ratio) on monthly returns from **1994-03-31 to 2018-10-31**, across periods
   before, during and after earnings announcements: firms with the most buybacks produced **no
   significant negative alpha** during blackout windows, and what return there is reads as
   idiosyncratic. CNBC reached the same null in 2018 ("Rumor: Buyback 'blackouts' mean weak stocks.
   Fact: Not really"). The design is exactly right for the question — if the mechanism worked, the
   heaviest repurchasers are where it would show first, and it does not show there. **Limit named:**
   I read this study through two independent secondary summaries, not its own text — the PDF's text
   layer did not decode on fetch, so the numbers above are the summaries' and the sample ends in
   2018, before the 2023 excise tax and before the current authorization records.

5. **The 2018 counter-case is a correlation with an unbreakable confound.** MIXED. The case offered
   against leg 4 is real: Oct–Dec 2018 saw the S&P 500 fall ~20% peak-to-trough with the most
   violent leg inside the pre-Q3 blackout, and Goldman documented buyback executions falling **>50%**
   in that window; when blackouts lifted, corporates returned and the tape stabilized. But the
   blackout window is keyed to quarter-end, and so is the seasonal weakness — the **second half of
   September is the single weakest half-month of the calendar year** (median ~**−0.5%**, against
   ~+0.1% for the first half), and September overall averages ~−0.7% over 75 years with the lowest
   positive rate of any month (44% since 1950). The two variables are collinear **by construction**,
   so a single quarter's coincidence cannot separate them, and one observation cannot outweigh a
   24-year panel. It is a reason to be humble about leg 4, not a reason to overturn it.

6. **The seasonal prior itself is weaker this year than the headline suggests.** MIXED. 2026 is a
   **midterm year**, and midterm Septembers break the pattern — the four best September returns on
   record all occurred in midterm years, five of the top seven. Narrower still: with August finishing
   positive and the year up between 10% and 17.5% (the configuration 2026 is actually in), September
   has averaged **+1.0%** post-WWII. This cuts *against* stacking "blackout" on top of "September is
   bad" and calling the sum a signal — which is the exact reasoning error this entry exists to
   prevent.

7. **For the names this book tracks, most of the bid that would fade isn't there — and this is the
   leg that decides the call.** SUPPORTED, and it is the sharpest finding here. Of the tracked
   set: **GOOG** has repurchased **nothing** in 2026 (with **$69.5B** still unused under
   authorization as of 2026-06-30); **META** repurchased **nothing** in Q1 2026, having redirected
   cash into AI capex while *raising* capex guidance; **AMZN** has only its $10B 2022 program with
   ~$6.1B left and a share count that has **risen** since. **AAPL** is the real exception — a
   **$100B** authorization approved 2026-04-30 and the market's largest single buyer of its own
   stock — and it is precisely the name whose fiscal calendar makes it the ramp's leading edge
   (leg 2). Meanwhile **NVDA** (FY ends late January), **AVGO**, **MRVL** and **MU** are all on
   off-calendar fiscal years, so they do not join a Sep-30 ramp at all — MU is *already* in blackout
   into its `estimate`-dated 2026-09-29 print, and NVDA (**$40.6B** repurchased in FY2026, **$58.5B**
   added) does not enter one until ~mid-October. Citadel's own note closes the loop from the other
   side: **67% of the largest authorized buybacks YTD are outside Technology.** A market-wide
   corporate-bid story therefore transmits to this book's names *weakly and unevenly*, which is a
   much stronger reason to stand aside than "the mechanism is unproven."

8. **No house playbook is buyback- or flow-keyed.** SUPPORTED. Read against
   [`trade-playbooks.md`](../../plans/trade-playbooks.md): S1/G1 are pre-print run-up plays
   (earnings-dated), S2 is the flat-through-the-print guard (earnings-dated), E1 is a don't-trade-the-open
   execution rule, S3 is a reaction-day fade (earnings-dated, shorting blocked), S4 is an
   overnight-vs-buy-and-hold structural note. None references corporate flow, buybacks, or
   expiration mechanics — the identical finding [`opex-2026-09-18.md`](opex-2026-09-18.md) records
   for its own event. There is nothing here for a signal to fire into.

9. **This date can never be promoted to `confirmed` as written.** SUPPORTED. SEC Rule 10b-18 is a
   **safe harbour** governing manner/timing/price/volume of repurchases, not a calendar; Rule 10b5-1
   plans let issuers keep buying *through* a blackout on pre-set terms; and the blackout itself is a
   per-company policy choice. Worse for confirmability, the policies are not reliably public even
   for the largest name: Apple's FY2025 10-K exhibit index (pulled direct from EDGAR this session)
   contains exhibits 4, 21.1, 23.1, 31.1, 31.2 and 32.1 — **no Exhibit 19 insider-trading policy** —
   so its actual blackout rule is not primary-readable at all. The calendar entry's own note
   anticipated this and set the right terms: at close-out, **re-derive per name or retire**, never
   pretend precision.

### What the conditions support

Nothing directional, and nothing sized. Two guards, both about attribution rather than action:

- **Don't trade the date, and don't let it seed one.** The first affected session is 2026-09-14, the
  start is a ramp, and the best evidence says the ramp doesn't mark the tape. Any bot or paper
  position that would fire off "buyback blackout starts" is firing off a rounded desk headline.
- **Treat 09-14 → 09-18 as an attribution trap, not a setup.** Three stories overlap in that
  corridor — this thinning bid, the FOMC (9/16), and a record ~$6.2T gamma roll-off (9/18) — on top
  of the year's weakest seasonal half-month, which is keyed to the same quarter-end. Anything that
  happens there will have four candidate explanations and no way to separate them. The corridor's
  own analysis belongs in [`opex-2026-09-18.md`](opex-2026-09-18.md), which owns it; this doc's job
  is to keep the buyback leg from being over-read there.

### Honest limits

Every flow figure is a sell-side desk estimate of its own book extrapolated to the market, and the
Goldman and Citadel numbers were never reconciled to each other or to an S&P DJI aggregate. The
Citadel desk primary remains unfetchable — citadelsecurities.com returned **HTTP 403** to WebFetch
again this session, unchanged from the proposing pulse's finding — so the $1.1T and 09-12 figures
are press-summary-grade, which is exactly why the entry is `estimate`. The State Street study was

ends in **2018**, before the excise tax and before today's authorization records; leg 4 would be
stronger with a post-2020 replication I did not find. I did **not** measure the corporate bid against
S&P 500 average daily dollar volume, so "small relative to the tape" is an inference from the desk
figures, not a computed ratio. The per-name repurchase facts in leg 7 come from press and filing
summaries with as-of dates, not from a systematic 10-Q pull. And the seasonality figures in legs 5–6
are press-cited historical averages, not a house-run study.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `estimate`, and structurally unconfirmable):** **stand aside on every horizon.** This
is a market-structure footnote whose tracked date is not a trading session, whose true start is a
distribution rather than a day, whose causal claim is refuted by the only formal study of it, and
whose flow transmits weakly to this book's names because two of the tracked mega-caps have stopped
repurchasing entirely and four more are on off-calendar fiscal years. No position, no size, no
direction, in either direction. The one thing this document is *for* is defensive: when the
09-14 → 09-18 corridor produces a move, this entry is the reason not to attribute it here.

**Recommendation carried to close-out:** re-derive per tracked name from their own reported
policies, or **retire the entry**. A market-wide "buyback blackout starts on date X" row can never
be honest, because no such date exists (leg 9). An AAPL-specific row keyed to its 2026-09-26 fiscal
quarter-end could be.

**Kill switches:**

- **Date kill:** Citadel or Goldman publishing a revised open-window close that moves off 09-12 by
  more than two sessions, before **2026-09-14**. The ramp framing survives; the entry's date does not.
- **Mechanism kill (the one that would reopen this):** S&P 500 down worse than **−1.0%** over
  **2026-09-11 close → 2026-09-15 close** — the only two sessions of the ramp's leading edge not
  contaminated by the FOMC or the witching. Registered as a forward test below.
- **Transmission kill:** GOOG or META announcing a resumption of repurchases before **2026-09-30**,
  or AAPL disclosing a suspension. Leg 7's "the bid isn't there for our names" argument is the
  load-bearing one, and either event breaks it.
- **Relevance kill:** any house playbook becoming flow- or expiration-keyed. None is today (leg 8),
  and while none is, this event has nothing to fire into.

**Forward test registered** in [`forward-tests.md`](../forward-tests.md) as
**`FT-buyback-blackout-start-2026-09-12-1`** — the null from leg 4, in the only window that can
score it cleanly. Registered with its base rate stated up front so a pass is scored honestly:
**15.3%** of all two-session S&P 500 windows since 2023-01-03 (n=920, one-off Yahoo daily-bar fetch
this session; 14.2% since 2024, 16.7% YTD 2026) close below −1.0%, so a pass is weak evidence and
**only the kill is informative**. Zero capital, no position taken or implied; a date-keyed *action*
would require the `confirmed` status this event can never have.
