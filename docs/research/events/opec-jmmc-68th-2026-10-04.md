## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Stand aside — but the `low` tier on this entry is probably wrong, and one dated
observation settles it.** The JMMC monitors conformity and *recommends*; it does not set quotas, so
on its own it is a near-nothing event. The catch is the calendar: the seven-country voluntary-
adjustment group (Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria, Oman) meets **monthly, on
Sundays** — 06-07, 07-05, 08-02, 09-06 are all Sundays — and **2026-10-04 is the next Sunday in that
sequence**. So the honest base case is that 10-04 is a **two-body day**: a conformity review *plus*
the meeting that sets **November** quotas. The **2026-09-06** communiqué will name the seven's next
meeting date and settle this; that is the whole watch item. Underneath the procedure sits the only
OPEC+ risk with a demonstrated tail: the third-party **Maximum Sustainable Capacity audits run
January–September 2026** close days before this meeting, making 10-04 the first public checkpoint
after every member's 2027 baseline number exists — and the precedent for how that grievance resolves
is already on the tape, because the **UAE quit OPEC and OPEC+ effective 2026-05-01** over exactly it
(~12% of OPEC output, the largest producer ever to leave). Nothing here is name-keyed
(`symbols: []`), the date is **estimate**, and no house playbook covers commodities — so no
date-keyed action is licensed regardless of how the tier question resolves.

## Initial research

**The question.** What will the 68th JMMC on 2026-10-04 actually decide, is our `low` impact tier
right, and does any of it change how we sit in the tracked names (NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META
AAPL AMZN)?

**One-line verdict:** as a JMMC it is a genuine non-event — but its *date* is very likely shared with
the seven-country group's November-quota meeting, and it is the first public OPEC+ forum after the
2027 capacity audits close, which is the only OPEC+ variable that has already produced a tail (the
UAE's exit); so the deliverable is a stand-aside plus a **dated re-tiering trigger**, not a trade.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md)
(geopolitical mode — the house instruments are equity/earnings-keyed and cover no commodity, so none
were run). Primaries fetched directly from opec.org: the 2026-08-02 **67th JMMC** release, the
2026-08-02 **seven-country** release, and the 2026-06-07 seven-country release. Price/VIX readings
pulled live from the same Yahoo endpoint `scripts/event-material-scan.mjs` uses (^VIX, BZ=F, CL=F,
XLE, USO), 2026-08-31. Secondary reporting (Al Jazeera, CNBC, NPR, Khaleej Times, Enerdata, Astana
Times, IndexBox, Argus/Rigzone summaries, straits.live) used for structure and corroboration, each
claim dated and attributed.

**Source hygiene, stated up front.** Three traps were hit and handled. (1) Searches for OPEC+
baseline context return a heavy **2025-vintage** layer — "eight countries", UAE as an active member
arguing for a higher baseline, "+528 kb/d in September" — all of which the 2026 primaries contradict
(seven countries, +188 kb/d, UAE gone). Used only where a claim is explicitly about 2025 history.
(2) The **42nd Ministerial** date came back as both "29 November 2026" and "November 28" from
different extractions and could not be corroborated against any primary; it is therefore **not
proposed to the calendar** — see Limits. (3) `straits.live` is an open-source-intelligence
aggregator, but unlike the monitor site the sibling ledger used it **names IMF PortWatch as its
transit source**, which is the primary this repo's own kill switches already point at.

**Conviction legs, tested:**

1. **The date is right — SUPPORTED, primary, verbatim.** opec.org's 2026-08-02 67th-JMMC release
   (fetched 2026-08-31) closes: "The next meeting of the JMMC (68th) is scheduled for 4 October
   2026." It stays **estimate** in our calendar per this lane's no-self-confirm limit, not because
   the source is weak. The cadence corroborates: the JMMC meets every two months, and a 67th on
   08-02 puts the 68th squarely in early October.

2. **The JMMC genuinely cannot move a quota — SUPPORTED.** Its mandate, restated in the 40th ONOMM
   release (2025-11-30), is to "closely review global oil market conditions, oil production levels,
   and the level of conformity with the DoC" and to recommend; quota decisions belong to the
   Ministerial and to the voluntary-adjustment group. The 67th's own output was a conformity review
   of **May and June 2026** production data plus language on "safeguarding international maritime
   routes." One membership detail worth writing down because it is easy to get wrong: the JMMC is
   **Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Nigeria, Algeria and Venezuela** — *not* the
   same roster as the seven-country voluntary group (which includes Oman and excludes Nigeria and
   Venezuela). Two overlapping bodies, different rosters, different powers.

3. **…but 10-04 is probably not JMMC-only — SUPPORTED on mechanism, UNRESOLVED on fact.** The seven
   meet **monthly**, and every 2026 meeting date on the record is a **Sunday**: 41st-ONOMM day
   **06-07**, then **07-05**, **08-02**, and **09-06** (each named verbatim in the prior release —
   "The next meeting will be held on 6 September 2026"). **2026-10-04 is the next Sunday four weeks
   after 09-06.** JMMC and voluntary-group meetings have repeatedly been co-scheduled. That makes a
   two-body 10-04 the base case — a conformity review *and* the November-quota decision — which
   would make our `low` tier understated. It is **not yet fact**: no primary names the seven's
   October date, and the 09-06 release is what will. This is the ledger's one live question and it
   is registered as **FT-27**.

4. **The substance is 2027 baselines, and the audit clock lands just before this meeting —
   SUPPORTED.** The 40th ONOMM (2025-11-30) "approved the mechanism developed by the Secretariat"
   for assessing **Maximum Sustainable Capacity** as the reference for 2027 production baselines.
   The assessment runs **January–September 2026**: a US-based auditing firm covers 19 of 22 members,
   Russia and Venezuela use a non-US firm for sanctions reasons, and **Iran's 2027 baseline is set
   by the average of its August, September and October 2026 production as assessed by secondary
   sources** (IndexBox summary of the mechanism, 2026; consistent with the primary's framing).
   Iraq and Kuwait are on record arguing for higher baselines to match capacity plans. So 10-04 is
   the **first public OPEC+ forum after every member's capacity number exists** — days after the
   window closes, and eight weeks before the Ministerial that has to ratify the results. That is
   what makes an otherwise procedural meeting worth a calendar slot at all.

   The Iran clause deserves its own sentence, because it is a mechanically-guaranteed grievance:
   Iran's 2027 quota will be anchored to production during the exact three months in which its
   exports are throttled by a strait **it closed itself** and it is being struck militarily (US
   forces hit rocket launchers on Larak Island 2026-08-30). Whatever number that produces, Iran will
   have a standing case that it is unrepresentative — and the window does not close until
   **2026-10-31**, after this meeting.

5. **Cohesion is the live tail, and it has already fired once — SUPPORTED, and this is new
   information relative to the sibling ledger.** The **UAE withdrew from OPEC and OPEC+ effective
   2026-05-01**, announced 04-28 after 59 years of membership — the largest producer ever to leave,
   roughly **12% of OPEC output** and the group's third-largest (Khaleej Times, CNBC 04-28, NPR,
   Al Jazeera, Enerdata; multiply corroborated, no primary opec.org statement located). The stated
   causes are precisely the two variables this ledger tracks: a **3.5 mb/d quota below a capacity
   built on a ~$150bn program targeting 5 mb/d by 2027**, and **Iranian attacks plus the Hormuz
   closure making its export economy unviable**. Kazakhstan is the named next candidate by analysts
   (Astana Times, 2026-05): its June quota rose 10 kb/d to **1.599 mb/d**, its compensation burden
   is the alliance's largest at **>500 kb/d/month**, and its energy minister said on the record in
   2025 that "we cannot yet fit into those compensation schedules" — structurally, because Tengiz,
   Kashagan and Karachaganak run under 1990s production-sharing contracts Astana says it has no legal
   right to override, with ~80% of exports transiting Russia via the CPC. The compensation period was
   **extended to end-December 2026** (opec.org primary, 2026-06-07). Read plainly: the group's
   enforcement mechanism is a promise Kazakhstan has publicly said it cannot keep, and the last
   member to face that arithmetic left.

6. **The Hormuz denominator in our own files is wrong — SUPPORTED (a correction).** The strait is
   closed to commercial shipping as of 2026-08-31, **day 183**; the latest IMF PortWatch count is
   **3 vessels on 08-23 against a ~85/day baseline ≈ 4% of normal**; war-risk insurance is ~40×
   pre-crisis and 386 vessels are holding position off-berth (straits.live, checked 2026-08-31).
   Diplomacy is live but unconsummated — Iran and Oman agreed a **temporary two-way corridor
   (2 nm separation, ~7 nm total width)** on 08-26 with a 30–60 day negotiation for a permanent
   route, and Qatar's PM met Araghchi in Tehran on **08-27** — but Iran ties full reopening to US
   compliance with a June interim deal that has lapsed. **The correction:** the same-day
   [`opec-plus-meeting-2026-09-06`](opec-plus-meeting-2026-09-06.md) ledger cites "~110 vessels/day
   baseline" and "~20% of pre-war average," sourced to an aggregator of unverified provenance. Both
   figures are off — the baseline by ~30%, the current throughput by ~5×. Its rows are append-only
   and are not edited here, but its **kill switch #2 ("transit recovering above ~60 vessels/day")
   is calibrated against the wrong denominator**: 60/85 is a **71%** recovery, a far higher bar than
   the ~55% it reads as. Any future use of that switch should restate it as a percentage of the
   PortWatch baseline, not an absolute count.

7. **Transmission to our book is the same weak two-step, discounted twice — SUPPORTED.** `symbols:
   []`; we hold no energy names. The only path is crude → CPI energy and ISM prices-paid → hike odds
   → real yields → long-duration AI names, CRWV most exposed. Two attenuators, both already on our
   own record: **FT-12** registers that CPI reactions are **core**-keyed with energy-driven headline
   surprises discounted, and the [funding-deadline ledger](government-funding-deadline-2026-09-30.md)
   notes that a lapse from 10-01 **deletes jobs 10-02 and CPI 10-14 outright** — so in that branch
   the transmission channel has no print to travel through at all. A weak channel, discounted by our
   own hypothesis, possibly with its receiving end switched off, is not a trade.

**The corridor 10-04 sits in.** Eleven tracked events fall within ±5 days: JOLTS, consumer confidence,
the MU print and CRWV Fully Connected on **09-29**; PCE, GDP-Q2-third, Chicago PMI and the FY27
funding deadline on **09-30**; ISM Manufacturing **10-01**; jobs **10-02**; MRVL Investor Day
**10-06**. Two consequences: the week's macro read may be **partly blank** if the CR is not signed,
and any equity move on Monday **2026-10-05** — the first US cash session after a Sunday decision — is
confounded by the 10-02 payrolls two sessions earlier. Unlike 09-06, there is **no holiday** in the
way: crude prices the Sunday-evening open and equities react Monday.

**What the conditions support.** Nothing to enter. The deliverable is a stand-aside plus two cheap,
checkable outputs: a **dated re-tiering trigger** (what the 09-06 communiqué says about the seven's
October date) and a **corrected denominator** for the Hormuz kill switches this calendar already
carries.

**Limits, honestly.**
- **The two-body claim is an inference, not a fact.** No primary names the seven's October meeting
  date. The Sunday-cadence argument is strong (four consecutive dated Sundays) but the group has
  moved dates before, and it is registered as a forward test precisely so the tape settles it.
- **The 42nd Ministerial date is unresolved and deliberately not proposed to the calendar.** Two
  extractions returned "29 November 2026" and "November 28"; a targeted follow-up search
  corroborated neither, and an aggregator gives only "late Nov / early Dec 2026 (date may be
  updated)." Proposing a wrong date is worse than proposing none — the seeding of this calendar
  already caught an aggregator publishing a wrong CPI date. Next pulse should resolve it against
  opec.org directly.
- **No measured base rate for crude on OPEC-decision days exists in this repo** — the gap the 9/6
  ledger flagged is still open, and this session did not close it. Every magnitude claim here is
  narrative comparison, not a distribution.
- **The UAE exit has no located primary.** It is corroborated across Khaleej Times, CNBC, NPR,
  Al Jazeera and Enerdata with a consistent date (announced 04-28, effective 05-01) and consistent
  causes, but no opec.org statement was found. Treat the *fact* as solid and the *framing* of OPEC's
  own reaction as secondary-sourced.
- **Iran production figures are a range, not a print.** Sources give 3.2–3.3 mb/d across 2025–2026
  with no dated August 2026 monthly number located. Leg 4's argument depends on the *direction* of
  the distortion (exports throttled), not on a level.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (set 2026-08-31, D-34): stand aside; the meeting is procedural, the tier is the open
question.** The 2026-10-04 JMMC is **estimate**-dated, carries no tracked symbols, and belongs to a
body that reviews conformity and recommends — it cannot move a quota. No entry, no hedge, no
pre-positioning is licensed, and the **estimate** label forbids date-keyed action independently of
everything above. The one thing this ledger is actually waiting on is a single line in the
**2026-09-06** communiqué: if it names **2026-10-04** as the seven's next meeting, this event should
be re-tiered `low` → `medium` at the next pulse, because it would then carry a November-quota
decision alongside the conformity review.

**Kill switches** — any of these fires and the stance gets re-argued, not tweaked:

1. **The tier is wrong (the expected one).** The **2026-09-06** release names **2026-10-04** as the
   seven's next meeting → 10-04 is a two-body day and `low` understates it. Check: opec.org press
   releases, 2026-09-06. Registered as **FT-27**.
2. **Cohesion breaks again.** Any DoC member announcing withdrawal, or publicly rejecting its 2027
   MSC baseline, on or before **2026-10-04** → the UAE precedent is a pattern rather than an
   idiosyncrasy, and a `low`-impact geopolitical entry is the wrong shape for the risk entirely.
   Check: opec.org, member energy ministries.
3. **The audit slips.** Any dated report that the MSC assessment will not complete by **2026-09-30**
   → 10-04's "first forum after the numbers exist" framing is void and the meeting really is
   routine. Check: opec.org, Argus/Reuters, on or before 2026-09-30.
4. **The quota starts mattering again.** Hormuz transit recovering above **~50 vessels/day (≈60% of
   the ~85/day PortWatch baseline)** and holding a week before **2026-10-04** → spare capacity
   becomes physically deliverable and OPEC decisions regain price power. Check: IMF PortWatch
   directly. *(Stated as a percentage of the correct baseline, per leg 6.)*
5. **The channel gets strong enough to matter.** Brent sustaining **>$100 through 2026-10-04** →
   energy stops being a discountable headline component and begins broadening into core, the
   [CPI ledger](cpi-2026-09-11.md)'s own stated condition for flipping energy from noise to signal.
