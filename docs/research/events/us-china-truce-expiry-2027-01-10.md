## At a glance

**TL;DR.** Stand aside. This is the third time in a year that the same set of US–China clocks has
been rolled forward instead of allowed to lapse: a one-year Busan truce (Nov 2025), a two-month
extension agreed at the September summit, and a second two-month extension filed on 2026-11-09, hours
before the old deadline. All three clocks now end together on **2027-01-10 (estimate)**: China's
suspension of its six October-2025 export-control announcements (the 0.1% extraterritorial
rare-earth rule and the rare-earth foreign-direct-product rule), the US Section 301 maritime/port-fee
action (ends 11:59 p.m. ET **2027-01-09**), and the US Commerce Affiliates Rule. The US two are
primary-sourced (Federal Register, read directly). China's rests on Reuters; the text is unread. The
market gave the last cliff no premium (VIX 28.2 on 11/5, 17.5 on 11/11; SOXX +18% from the 11/5 low).
The pattern is a reason to expect another roll, **not** a reason to buy protection or sell it. The
next venue is APEC Shenzhen, 11/18–19, where a Trump–Xi meeting is expected but not confirmed.
Everything here is an **estimate**, and an estimate widens caution only.

### The three clocks, tested

1. **USTR Sec. 301 maritime/port-fee action: SUPPORTED, primary.** FR doc 2026-22867, publication
   date 2026-11-10: suspended through 11:59 p.m. ET **2027-01-09** (API read 2026-11-12). Filed for
   public inspection 11/9, so the suspension never lapsed.
2. **BIS Affiliates Rule: SUPPORTED, primary.** FR doc 2026-22793, published and effective 2026-11-09:
   the suspension runs to **2027-01-10**.
3. **MOFCOM/GAC export controls: MIXED, press-grade.** Reuters (via The Star, political.org, The
   National, 2026-11-09): the Commerce Ministry extended the pause "for two months until January 10" in
   "a short statement" with no other details. It covers the six October-2025 announcements originally
   suspended by Announcement No. 70 of 2025. One search summary gives an "Announcement No. 58 of 2026";
   no Reuters-derived piece repeats it and the number collides with 2025's joint No. 58, so it is not
   used. A second claim, that Beijing also extended its suspension against 16 US firms on its export-control
   list and 11 on the unreliable-entity list, rests on one source. Both stay press-grade until MOFCOM's
   text is read.
4. **What is not on these clocks.** The April-2025 seven-element heavy rare-earth licensing was never
   suspended. China's suspension of the gallium/germanium/antimony/superhard-materials US ban runs to
   **2026-11-27** on its own clock (Fastmarkets, Engineering News, 2025-11); it was not mentioned in any
   2026-11-09 piece read, so it is proposed as its own calendar entry in this PR.
