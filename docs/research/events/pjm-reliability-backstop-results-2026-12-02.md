## Initial research

### The question, plainly

PJM aims to publish Reliability Backstop Procurement (RBP) results around 2026-12-02, roughly two
weeks before the 2029/30 Base Residual Auction. The sibling ledger
[`pjm-capacity-auction-2026-12`](pjm-capacity-auction-2026-12.md) amended itself on 2026-09-08 to
call this print "the first observable market price for new PJM supply in the collar era." Is it?
What will it actually print, how should it be read, and does it touch any of the nine tracked names?

**One-line verdict.** It is **not** a price-discovery event in the sense the calendar has been
reading it — the RBP is pay-as-bid under a MW-weighted-average budget, so ~2026-12-02 (estimate)
publishes an *average of accepted 15-year asks*, not a clearing price comparable to $325 — and the
date itself is arithmetic downstream of a FERC order that had not issued as of 2026-09-08 against an
outright rejection request from PJM's own Market Monitor. Stand aside; watch the order.

### Legs tested

1. **"This is the first observable market price for new PJM supply in the collar era."**
   (the sibling ledger's 2026-09-08 amendment) — **REFUTED as stated**, on three independent
   primary-source grounds.
   - *It is pay-as-bid.* The filing's own section heading is "The pay-as-bid approach is just and
     reasonable for purposes of the RBP", and its market-power discussion states that "a pay-as-bid
     structure, unlike a single clearing price structure, relies on individual clearing prices rather
     than a single clearing price." There is no single number that is "the price."
   - *$555 is not an offer cap.* Attachment DD §18.5: "The MW-weighted average of the levelized
     procurement cost of all selected RBP Sell Offers shall not exceed $555/MW-day UCAP" — and PJM
     says the design deliberately avoids "restricting Sell Offers to a hard price cap." An individual
     seller can be paid **above** $555; only the weighted average is constrained.
   - *The unit is not comparable.* The figure is a **levelized** cost across a fixed **15-year** term
     for resources with commercial-operation dates out to **2032-06-01**. The BRA's $325/MW-day is a
     one-year clearing price. Comparing them is a category error, and it is the comparison every
     headline will make.

   What *is* observable on ~2026-12-02 is a MW-weighted average levelized procurement cost. That is
   real information about what new PJM supply demands — it is simply not a clearing price, and it
   does not license the "price discovery has arrived early" reading.

2. **"$555 is a willingness to pay ~70% above the collar."** — **REFUTED.** The filing is explicit
   that $555/MW-day UCAP is Point 1 of the VRR curve for the 2028/29 BRA: "the Commission accepted an
   estimated, uncollared price cap of $555/MW-[day] … In other words, but for the collared price cap,
   $555/MW-day would have been the cap for the 2028/2029 Delivery Year BRA." So $555 is not an
   escalation above the market design — it is the cap the collar suppressed, restored once. The RBP
   therefore does **not** demonstrate that PJM and FERC will pay above what the design already
   allowed; it demonstrates they will pay the uncollared number one time. The Market Monitor goes
   further and argues even that is too high: PJM's Net CONE used energy-and-ancillary net revenues
   below a forward-looking calculation, so "PJM has significantly overstated the maximum price for
   the RBP."

3. **The date leg — is 2026-12-02 a date at all?** — **MIXED**, and the honest reading is *a
   date-shaped function of one input.* The filing publishes **no absolute results day**. It publishes
   Attachment DD §18.4's relative schedule: the submission window "is planned to open upon Commission
   acceptance of this submittal, close three weeks after, and the subsequent clearing and selection
   process … conclude six weeks following the closing of the offer submission window." PJM asks for
   an order "by no later than September 29, 2026" and says that on acceptance without modification it
   "intends to commence the RBP on September 30, 2026." The arithmetic follows exactly:
   2026-09-30 + 3 weeks = **2026-10-21**; + 6 weeks = **2026-12-02**. Both press-reported dates are
   that arithmetic, not independent reporting. This makes the estimate far better-founded than
   "press said Dec 2" — and far more fragile than a posted schedule: **every day the FERC order
   slips, 2026-12-02 slips a day.** PJM says so plainly: "Should the Commission require modifications
   to the proposed RBP as detailed in this filing, PJM may need to delay the commencement of the RBP
   beyond September 30, 2026."

4. **Will FERC accept by 2026-09-29?** — **MIXED, and materially less likely than the proposing
   sweep assumed.** On 2026-08-21 the PJM Independent Market Monitor protested ER26-3380-000 asking
   the Commission to reject the filing: it "has not been supported as just and reasonable, and it
   should be rejected … attempting to implement it will create unintended consequences, and compound
   that damage for a period of 15 years." Its objections are the kind a Commission must write
   through rather than wave past — the RBP replaces RPM's must-buy/must-sell symmetry with a
   "must buy/option to sell at the highest price" design that "creates market power for generation
   sellers"; PJM proposes **no market-power screens or mitigation at all**; the pay-as-bid choice is
   unexplained and "inefficient and does not facilitate competition"; the iterative selection logic
   (order offers by expected commercial-operation date, then select and unselect until the
   combination fits the budget) is not cost-minimising — PJM "does not exhaustively examine any more
   offers … to explore the potential for finding a cheaper combination"; the cost allocation to LSEs
   with data-center load is inconsistent with the tariff's own Reliability Backstop Auction
   provisions; and the RBP Shortfall Charges are "vague and unenforceable."

   The important nuance for handicapping: the Monitor does **not** ask for inaction — "The problem
   that the RBP Filing is designed to address is real and needs immediate action" — and offers a
   concrete alternative (15-year bilateral contracts for new data-center load, and for load that
   cannot arrange them, "a full single clearing price auction using the BRA capacity market clearing
   model"). That is the classic setup for a **conditional or modified acceptance**: the outcome that
   slips the date without killing the event, and the one this ledger treats as the live case. The
   docket family is contested on more than one front — EPSA protested the companion Interim Resource
   Adequacy Service filing (ER26-3515) on 2026-09-03, and Maryland's ratepayer advocate is reported
   to be challenging the plan (Inside Climate News, 2026-09-01 — 403 to this session, recorded in
   `probe-ref.blocked`, relayed via search result only and nothing is built on it).

5. **What will the print say — price?** — **Genuinely two-sided, and that is the finding, not a
   hedge.** Pushing the average **down**: PJM reports that the RFI administered by CRA "identified
   over 100 GW of prospective supply interested in participating," against an Initial RBP Target of
   **6,831.3 MW UCAP** — roughly 15× oversubscription of *interest*, which is exactly the argument
   PJM leans on ("this level of interest indicates there is available supply that will compete").
   Pushing it **up**: pay-as-bid pays each selected seller its own offer with no market-power
   mitigation, so a rational seller bids its estimate of the binding constraint rather than its cost
   — the Monitor's central objection. The two effects are not reconcilable from outside, there is no
   published estimate, and the bid window has not opened. A wide distribution with no consensus is a
   **low-confidence** state, which under this book's rule means **stand aside**, never a small
   position.

6. **What will the print say — quantity?** — **The more informative variable, and the one nobody is
   watching.** The Final RBP Target is *not* 6,831.3 MW: it is the Initial Target less verified
   Qualifying RBP Offset UCAP MW (bilateral matching and self-supply, reported to PJM **during** the
   bid window) and less opted-out load's obligation. PJM also concedes it may not buy even that:
   there is a "possibility that PJM will not procure the exact Final RBP Target quantity if doing so
   violates the maximum willingness to pay constraint." So a small procured number has at least three
   distinct meanings — the bilateral track worked, load opted out, or the budget bound — and the
   headline will not distinguish them. This is where the sibling ledger's second consequence (that
   December's shortfall figure stops being a clean read on the same quantity) is right, and it is
   carried forward here.

7. **Does it de-risk the December BRA?** — **REFUTED, from PJM's own filing.** "[G]iven the pace of
   new supply and the latest load forecast that will be used in the next BRA, in December 2026, all
   evidence points to the December auction also clearing short." And: "Even with the intervention of
   the RBP, the 2029/2030 Delivery Year BRA may clear short, as new capacity that qualifies for
   procurement in the RBP are required to provide evidence of feasibility of commercial operation by
   no later than June 1, 2032." Most RBP MW simply cannot be deliverable for the 2029/30 delivery
   year. RBP resources do carry an RPM must-offer obligation and settle as a contract for differences
   against RPM, so they add offered supply eventually — on a 2029–2032 commercial-operation schedule,
   not into the December print.

8. **Do the nine tracked names carry sensitivity?** — **REFUTED as a direct play**, unchanged from
   the sibling's Leg 5 and re-verified here. None of NVDA / MRVL / AVGO / CRWV / MSFT / GOOG / META /
   AAPL / AMZN is an IPP. Second-order only: CRWV as a PJM-footprint datacenter operator whose power
   costs are inputs, then MSFT/AMZN/GOOG/META as PJM-region buyers. And the RBP's cost allocation
   runs to **LSEs with data-center load**, not to hyperscaler equity, so even the cost channel is
   indirect — what reaches the tracked names is narrative, not P&L. The names this actually moves —
   VST, CEG, TLN, NRG — are not on the tracked roster; they live in
   [`ai-energy-constraint.md`](../ai-energy-constraint.md). Their 2026-09-08 closes are recorded in
   the ledger row below so the next pulse has a baseline to diff.

9. **Whisper / consensus.** — **Absent.** No published estimate of an RBP average price or of
   procured MW was findable on 2026-09-08. That is an absence-of-evidence read, not evidence of
   absence: the bid window has not opened and offers are not public.

- **The date leg** dies if FERC's order lands on any date other than 2026-09-29, or accepts subject
  to modification → **re-derive** the results date as (order date + 64 days) and amend the calendar
  entry as an estimate; never patch the old number in place.

- **The oversubscription read** (Leg 5) dies if the window closes ~2026-10-21 with offered MW at or
