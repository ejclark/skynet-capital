2. **The prior session's measurement replicates exactly, independently — SUPPORTED, and this is worth
   stating before anything is added to it.** Re-implemented from the raw Yahoo series without reading

   Aug 5 · Sep 4 · Oct 6 · Nov 3 · Dec 4). A shelf-defining number that no second party had checked
   now has one. **This is the last leg that agrees with the sibling without qualification.**

3. **The measure those numbers come from is not time-normalised, and this event's window is the one
   that breaks it — SUPPORTED, and it is this session's headline.** A five-session close-to-close
   standard deviation treats every return as one unit of time. It is not: a normal post-expiration
   window's first return spans the **3-day** expiration weekend, and its pre-window's first return
   spans the same, so the two cancel — median(raw ÷ √calendar-day-normalised) = **0.993** across the
   76 expirations where both windows span a clean 7 days. When a market closure lands inside one
   window only, they stop cancelling: **1.166** where the post window carries a closure, and
   **1.290** in the sub-class where the closure is the Monday immediately after expiration. That is
   not a subtle correction — it is a **+29% median inflation** applied to the exact quantity all four
   registered opex forward tests score.

4. **Run under four defensible estimators, the monthlies' edge shrinks and the witching inversion
   holds — MIXED for the sibling's stance, and the split is the useful part.** Same 120 expirations,
   same windows, four ways to weight the returns (full table in the signals list). Ordinary monthlies:
   **62.5% · 56.3% · 56.9% · 60.0%** — the house measure returns the **highest** of the four, and the
   two gap-free estimators land ~5.5 points lower. Quarterly witchings: **47.5% · 45.0% · 41.7% ·
   50.0%**, at or below the null on every one, with three of four medians under 1.0. **The honest
   reading:** `opex-2027-05-21`'s *inversion* — the effect vanishing where the gamma roll-off is
   largest — is robust to how you measure it and should keep being cited. Its *62.5%* is the top of a
   56–63% range and should be cited as such.

6. **Holiday placement is NOT the general mechanism behind the month-of-year dispersion — REFUTED,
   and this was this session's own hypothesis.** The obvious story after leg 3 is that the 10%-to-100%
   month spread `opex-2027-05-21` flagged as unexplained is largely closure placement. It is not.
   Outside January and February, expirations whose windows carry a closure run **13/24 = 54.2%**

   of the two five-session windows. "Clean windows" and "not Jan/Feb" are the *same 76 observations*.

   right conclusion is leg 5's — those two cells are unreliable — not a holiday mechanic.
