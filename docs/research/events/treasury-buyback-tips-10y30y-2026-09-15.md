## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** what does a $500M liquidity-support buyback of 10-to-30-year TIPS on
2026-09-15 actually do, why is it the one long-end operation sb0607's doubling left alone, what has
this operation type historically executed at, and what — if anything — does it tell us about a
long-end selloff that our other ledgers have been reading through nominal instruments all year?

**One-line verdict:** a mechanically tiny, analytically clean operation — Treasury's price filter on
off-the-run 30-year TIPS, published at 2:15pm ET on 9/15 — that is worth *reading* and never worth
*trading*, because it is the only scheduled window in this corridor onto the real-yield leg that is
driving 87% of the 2026 long-end move, and because it is buried under a 20Y auction on its own day
and an FOMC the next.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The date, sector and cap are right, and the `estimate` label is right too — SUPPORTED.** The
tentative schedule's row reads verbatim: announce **9/14/2026**, operation **9/15/2026 1:40 pm –
2:00 pm**, settle **9/16/2026**, **Liquidity Support**, **TIPS 10Y to 30Y**, maturity range
**09/16/2036 – 09/15/2056**, min **$0**, max **$500 million**. Footnote 1: "A preliminary list of
eligible CUSIPs will be disclosed at 11:00 am ET on the Announcement Date." It stays `estimate` on
the same two counts every buyback row off this document carries — a tentative schedule is tentative
by construction, and the confirming primary is the 9/14 announcement, not this PDF. Worth stating
plainly: the *date* is not in doubt; the label is about taxonomy and about a document Treasury
itself calls tentative.

**2. sb0607 does not cover this operation, and that is the whole reason to track it — SUPPORTED,
verbatim off the primary.** The release fetched direct this session is titled "Treasury Announces
Increased Sizes of Nominal Long-End Liquidity Support Buybacks Beginning September 9" and its body
reads: *"increasing, by at least double, the size of liquidity support buyback operations for
longer-dated **nominal coupon securities** (the 10-year to 20-year sector and the 20-year to 30-year
sector). The current maximum size of $2 billion per operation will be at least $4 billion per
operation."* TIPS appear nowhere. The stated rationale is equally specific — *"greater liquidity
support in longer-dated **nominal** sectors where there is consistent strong sponsorship from market
participants, as evidenced by the significant volume of high-quality offers Treasury routinely
receives"* — which is a claim about the nominal long end that leg 3 shows does **not** describe the
TIPS long end. So 9/15 runs at its unchanged $500M inside the 09-09→11-04 doubled window: the
untouched control against which the doubled nominal operations are read.

**3. The cap has never bound here — and the reason is price, not scarcity. SUPPORTED, and this is
the load-bearing execution finding.** All four operations under the current **10Y to 30Y** label,
from `buybacks_operations`:

| Operation | Cap | Offered | Accepted | Fill of cap | Hit rate on offers | Lines taken / eligible |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2025-11-12 | $500M | $1,392M | $142M | 28.4% | 10.2% | 5 / 15 |
| 2025-12-23 | $500M | $1,190M | $108M | 21.6% | 9.1% | 4 / 15 |
| 2026-03-25 | $500M | $757M | $110M | 22.0% | 14.5% | 7 / 16 |
| 2026-05-28 | $500M | $784M | $91M | 18.2% | 11.6% | 5 / 16 |

Read the middle two columns together. **Offers exceeded the cap every single time** — 2.78×, 2.38×,
1.51×, 1.57× — so Treasury could have filled $500M on any of these four days and chose not to on all
four. It took **9.1–14.5%** of the par put in front of it. That is a deliberate price filter, and it
is the precise inverse of the nominal long end this window is doubling: the sibling ledger
`treasury-buyback-increase-2026-09-09` measured, off the same dataset, a **25 of 25** cap fill in
10-20Y nominals and **24 of 26** in 20-30Y, on offers never below $6.4B. **In nominals the ceiling
is the constraint; in long TIPS the ceiling is decoration and Treasury's bid is the constraint.**
That asymmetry is exactly why sb0607 raised one and not the other, and it makes the 9/15 accepted
number a genuinely informative read rather than a formality.

**4. "TIPS 10Y to 30Y" is a maturity range that contains only 30-year TIPS — SUPPORTED, and it
changes what the operation is.** `buybacks_security_details` for all four operations returns 15–16
eligible lines, and **every one is a February-15 maturity between 2040 and 2055** — the 30-year TIPS
strip. Not one 10-year TIPS line appears. The mechanism is arithmetic, not policy: the window opens
at operation-date + 10 years + 1 day (here **09/16/2036**), and the longest-dated 10-year TIPS in
existence, CUSIP **91282CRE3**, matures **2036-07-15** — two months *before* the window opens. A
10-year TIPS can only enter this bucket within months of issue, when it is still on- or
near-on-the-run and therefore excluded anyway. So the label is misleading in a way worth writing
down: **this is a 30-year TIPS operation, permanently.** (Derived here from the observed eligible
lists plus the range arithmetic — Treasury has not said this, and the 9/14 CUSIP list is what
confirms it for this operation.)

The accepted lines say the same thing more sharply. Two low-coupon deep-discount bonds do most of
the work: **912810RL4 (0.750% of 2045)** and **912810RR1 (1.000% of 2046)** took $45M+$25M of the
$91M on 5/28 and $55M+$30M of the $110M on 3/25 — 77% and 77% of those operations. RR1's weighted
average accepted price walked **77.994 → 75.531 → 74.578 → 74.641** across the four operations, a
~4.3-point decline that is the real-yield selloff visible in the paper Treasury is actually buying.
The mirror-image finding on the nominal side is already banked: `treasury-20y-bond-2026-09-15`
measured buyback support in the 2044–2046 *nominal* band at "near zero — $1.05B across 5 lines in
23 operations, and nothing at all since 2026-05-28." Same maturity neighbourhood, both instruments,
both barely bid.

**5. The corridor's long-end selloff is a real-yield event, and this is the only operation on it —
SUPPORTED, and the reason a $500M row earns a ledger.** From Treasury's own two daily curves, 2026
year-to-date:

| | 2026-01-02 | 2026-09-04 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30Y real yield | 2.63% | **2.96%** | **+33bp** |
| 30Y breakeven | 2.23% | **2.28%** | +5bp |
| 30Y nominal | 4.86% | **5.24%** | +38bp |
| 10Y real yield | 1.94% | **2.43%** | +49bp |
| 10Y breakeven | 2.25% | **2.35%** | +10bp |

**87% of the 30-year selloff (33 of 38bp) is real yield; 83% of the 10-year's (49 of 59bp) is.**
The 30-year breakeven has spent the entire year inside **2.15%–2.34%** (min 6/24, max 5/19) while
the 30-year real yield ran to a 2026 high of **3.06%** on 8/17 and sits 10bp below it. Independently
corroborated at the auction: the 2026-08-20 30-year TIPS reopening (CUSIP **912810US5**, $8.0B) drew
a **2.973%** real yield with a **2.82** bid-to-cover — the highest 30-year TIPS real yield since
**October 2001** per [tipswatch](https://tipswatch.com/2026/08/20/30-year-tips-reopening-gets-real-yield-of-2-973-highest-in-nearly-25-years/)
(the 2.973% itself is primary, from `auctions_query`; only the 25-year ranking is third-party). Every
other ledger in this corridor reads the selloff through nominal instruments, where real and expected
inflation are fused. TIPS separate them, and the separation says the market is **not** repricing
long-run inflation — it is demanding more real compensation for duration. That is the deficit and
supply story our sibling ledgers have carried all year, now measured in the instrument that isolates
it.

**6. Scale — the honest deflator, and it is brutal. SUPPORTED.** Treasury *issued* **$8.0B** of
30-year TIPS on 8/20 and reopens the 10-year TIPS on **9/17** (size announced 9/10). Against that,
the 9/15 cap is **$500M** and the median take across the last four operations is **$109M**. That is
**16:1** issuance-to-cap, and **73:1** issuance-to-typical-execution, in a three-week span. Treasury
is an overwhelming net *issuer* of long TIPS; this operation is plumbing — swapping illiquid
off-the-runs out of dealer and investor books — not supply management. Anyone reading a buyback as
duration withdrawal has the sign right and the magnitude off by two orders.

**7. The cadence has thinned, and 9/15 is the longest wait in the series — SUPPORTED.** Gaps between
long-TIPS operations, from `buybacks_operations`: under the old **7.5Y to 30Y** label (2024-04-17
through 2025-08-06, 10 operations) the median spacing was ~48 days, range 36–84. Under the current
**10Y to 30Y** label: 41d, 92d, 64d — and now **110 days** from 2026-05-28 to 2026-09-15, **the
longest gap since the operation type began**. The August-refunding schedule confirms it is
deliberate rather than a one-off: **9/15 is the only TIPS 10Y-30Y row on the entire document**
(8/6 → 11/5), while the 1Y-10Y bucket gets two ($750M each, 9/29 and 10/21 — both proposed in this
PR). One long-TIPS operation per refunding quarter is now the shape. This cuts *against* reading the
operation as a policy signal and *for* reading it as a scheduled maintenance window.

**8. The short TIPS bucket is the control, and it just broke its streak — MIXED, and it is the one
live uncertainty.** The **1Y to 10Y** bucket fills its $750M cap almost always: 100%, 98.0%, 99.3%,
98.0%, 100%, 100% from 2025-09-24 through 2026-06-24. Then **2026-07-22 took $405M — a 54.0% fill,
the first miss in the new regime**, on $3.194B offered. Either Treasury tightened its price filter
across both TIPS buckets in July, or that was idiosyncratic. It matters for 9/15 because a
tightening would push the long bucket's take *below* its already-low band rather than toward the cap.
Unresolved as of this writing; **9/29 is the first read** and is why that operation is proposed here.

**9. Tracked-name sensitivity — none direct.** `symbols: []`. Our roster (NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT
GOOG META AAPL AMZN) carries no TIPS exposure and no instrument that prices off a $500M off-the-run
buyback. The transmission, such as it is, runs through the same real-yield/duration channel every
macro print in this window feeds — most sensitive **CRWV** (debt-financed build, real cost of
capital), then the high-multiple semis **NVDA/AVGO/MRVL**, then **MSFT/GOOG/META**, least **AAPL/
AMZN**. Ranked for completeness, actioned nowhere: leg 6's scale arithmetic forecloses any claim
that this operation moves that channel.

### What the conditions support

Nothing directional. The house playbooks (S1/S2/E1/S3/S4 + G1) are symbol- and earnings-keyed and
none of them takes a rates input, so there is no play to fit. What travels is a **reading
discipline**: the 2:15pm ET accepted-par print on 9/15 is a cheap, dated, primary-published
observation about how Treasury prices long real duration at a 25-year real-yield high, and it costs
nothing to read. Two forward tests are registered below so the reading is scored rather than
narrated. The event is `estimate`-dated; per the date policy that widens caution and licenses no
entry, which happens to be the same answer the scale arithmetic gives anyway.

### Honest limits

- **The operation's own terms do not exist yet.** Everything above is off a document Treasury calls tentative, plus history. The 9/14 11:00am ET preliminary CUSIP list is the first authoritative statement of the 9/15 cap, sector and eligible set — and sb0607's promised updated schedule, **still unpublished at 24 days**, could restate the cap before then.
- **Leg 4's "permanently a 30-year operation" is a derivation, not a Treasury statement.** It follows from four observed eligible lists plus maturity-range arithmetic. The 9/14 list is what confirms it for this operation; a Treasury change to how the window is set would break it.
- **n=4 under the current label.** The 18–28% fill band is tight and consistent, but four observations is four observations, and the bucket definition changed in late 2025 — the pre-2025 `7.5Y to 30Y` history (which *did* fill its cap in 5 of 9) is not the same instrument and is not pooled here.
- **Attribution on 9/15 is impossible by construction.** The 20-year auction prices at 1:00pm the same day, FOMC lands the next morning. Nothing in the tape on 9/15 can be attributed to a $500M buyback, which is why both forward tests below score the *operation's own published numbers* and never a yield move.
- **The tape is stale.** Labor Day closed markets 9/7 and the 9/8 session had not settled at write time, so Treasury's par curves still end **2026-09-04**. One data anomaly recorded rather than smoothed: Cboe's `VIX_History.csv` carries a **09/07/2026** row (close 15.30) despite the holiday, while Treasury's curve correctly has no 09/07. This ledger anchors on the **09/04 close of 14.53** and the 09-08 delayed quote of **15.69**, and does not use the 09/07 row.
- **Brent is second-hand past 9/1.** FRED's `DCOILBRENTEU` primary ends **2026-09-01 at $96.02** (from $89.75 on 8/28). The sibling `treasury-20y-bond-2026-09-15` ledger carries $95.04 on 9/4 and $98.62 pre-open 9/8; those are cited to it, not independently verified here.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (set 2026-09-08 at D-7, `estimate`-dated).** **Read it, do not trade it.** No position, no
size, no rate-sensitive exposure keyed to this operation — the scale arithmetic (leg 6: $500M cap and
a ~$109M median take against $8.0B of 30-year TIPS sold three weeks earlier) forecloses any market
impact worth acting on, and the `estimate` label widens that caution rather than narrowing it. What
the operation *is* worth is the 2:15pm ET print on 9/15: a dated, primary-published measurement of
Treasury's price filter on off-the-run 30-year TIPS at a real yield last seen in 2001. **Base case
(estimate-labeled, this doc's own forecast from a four-observation base rate):** accepted par lands
in the **$60M–$200M** band, well under the $500M cap, on offers that again exceed it — the price-
rationing regime persists and sb0607's non-coverage of TIPS remains a non-event. **The framing
claim, which is the part that would actually change our reading of the corridor:** the long-end
selloff is 87% real-yield and the 30-year breakeven has not left 2.15–2.34% all year, so this
corridor's duration risk is a supply-and-term-premium story, not an inflation-expectations story —
and the TIPS complex is where that is measurable rather than inferred. Registered as
FT-treasury-buyback-tips-10y30y-2026-09-15-1 (execution) and -2 (the offer-side read on the 110-day
gap).

**Kill switches:**

- **Accepted par at or above $250M on 2026-09-15** — half the cap, roughly double every operation since Nov-2025. Would mean Treasury has loosened the price filter into a real-yield high, which is a different posture than the one this stance describes; reassess the "control" framing and whether the TIPS long end is being supported after all.
