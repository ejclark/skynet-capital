## At a glance

**TL;DR.** Japan sells its benchmark 10-year on **2026-11-05** — an anticipated ¥2,600bn **first
reopening of issue 384**, terms due **2026-10-29**. **Nothing here is ours to trade.** This entry was
proposed as *"the control arm the retired difference-in-differences actually needed"* — the one JGB
tenor MOF has not cut — with the proposal's own honest caveat that nobody had read the BoJ's
purchase path for the 5–10y bucket. **This session read it, and the rationale is not weakened but
inverted.** The BoJ cut its 5–10y purchases from **¥1,902.7bn to ¥670.7bn a month, −64.8% — the
deepest of its six maturity buckets** (25y+ is the untouched one, at **−0.2%**). A newly issued
10-year sits in the *most*-tapered bucket on the calendar. And because MOF left 10-year issuance
alone, **net supply into private hands at this tenor rose +142%** (¥797bn → ¥1,929bn a month), the
largest increase of any tenor. **Then the mechanism that would predict auction damage from that also
fails.** Tail share ≥2bp, 2007–2023 → 2024+: **2Y 0.5%→0.0%, 5Y 1.5%→0.0%, 10Y 3.4%→19.4%, 20Y
4.4%→32.3%, 30Y 4.7%→19.4%** — the two next-deepest-dosed buckets show **zero** deterioration and the
**undosed** 25y+ bucket shows a fourfold rise. **Deterioration splits at ten years' duration, not at
any BoJ bucket boundary.** What survives is a *pricing* claim, and this session sharpens it: of four
bucket boundaries only **25y** carries a curve discontinuity (**20s25s 93.5th percentile vs 25s30s
1.2nd — 92.3 points**, on a 55.2pp dose gap), and the family's 20s30s spread straddles that boundary
and blurs it. Date and size both **estimate**; they widen caution and license nothing.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The [`jgb-30y-auction-2026-11-10`](jgb-30y-auction-2026-11-10.md) sweep proposed this entry on
2026-09-09 for one measured reason: the 40-year ledger had retired *"the 40-year is the untapered
control"* because the 40-year took the **largest** issuance cut, and the 10-year looked like the
replacement — MOF cut it **−3.7%** against the super-long's −30% to −57%, and *"a newly issued
10-year sits in neither of the two BoJ purchase buckets the family's mechanism claim is about."*

That proposal stated its own limit up front, which is the best thing about it:

> **THE HONEST LIMIT ON THAT RATIONALE, STATED UP FRONT:** this session did NOT read the BoJ's own
> purchase path for the 5-10y bucket, so 'untreated' is established against the super-long taper only
> and not against BoJ policy in general — that is the first thing the 10-year's own initial research
> should measure, and if the 5-10y bucket was tapered too the control rationale weakens rather than
> disappears.

So: **was the 5–10y bucket tapered, and does the control rationale survive?**

**One-line verdict:** it was tapered **more than any other bucket**, so the rationale does not weaken
— it **inverts**, and the 10-year turns out to be the tenor where MOF and the BoJ pull in opposite
directions. Following that inversion into the auction record then **refutes the family's whole
bucket-dose story about auction quality**, because the two deepest-dosed buckets after this one show
no deterioration at all and the one undosed bucket shows the most. What survives is a *pricing*
association at a single boundary, which this session sharpens with a better instrument.

**4. The taper's dose IS calendar time, so no time-series dose-response is identified at any tenor —
SUPPORTED, and it is a methodological correction the family needs.** The obvious within-tenor test is
the 10-year's own auction quality against its own bucket's monthly purchases. Over 32 monthly pairs

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| corr(BoJ 5–10y purchases, tail) | **−0.295**, t = −1.69 |
| corr(net supply to private, tail) | **+0.298**, t = +1.71 |
| corr(BoJ 5–10y purchases, cover) | +0.123, t = +0.68 |

passage of time from 2024 to 2026" are the same regressor at r = 0.990. Any trend in tails over this
window is attributable to either with equal ease. This is the **third** collinearity disqualification
this family has hit — after issue-position vs. month-in-quarter at the 30- and 20-year (94.3% /
94.2%; reproduced here at the 10-year, **96.2%, 101 of 105**) — and it is the one that matters,
because it forecloses the taper test itself rather than a decomposition of it.
