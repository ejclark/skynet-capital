## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This event exists because the [S&P Global Manufacturing PMI 10-01 ledger](sp-global-manufacturing-pmi-2026-10-01.md)
proposed it during its own initial research, seven hours of repo-time ago, and it proposed it on two
specific claims. Verbatim, from that proposal file: **"PROPOSED AS A CLASS THIS CALENDAR TRACKS
NOTHING OF: positioning"**, and **"Its date makes it sharper than its impact tier suggests… it lands
10:00 ET on 2026-09-15, INSIDE the two-day FOMC meeting and the morning before the 09-16 decision…
It is also the last positioning datum before that vote."**

Both are testable and neither had been tested. So: **(1)** Is the date right, on this lane's own
fetch and — better — on something that does not depend on the listing at all? **(2)** Is the
positioning claim true, or does this calendar already carry the class under another name? **(3)**
Does the FOMC-day-one placement actually do anything, on the tape or to the information?

**One-line verdict:** the date holds two ways and the `estimate` label is a taxonomy gap; the
positioning claim survives **only in a much narrower form** than proposed; and the FOMC-day-one
claim **splits cleanly** — refuted on the tape by two independent measurements, supported and
sharpened on the information by a date rule that puts this edition's panel after the month's jobs
report for the first time in 2026.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is 2026-09-15 at 10:00 ET — SUPPORTED twice over, and the second way does not depend
   on the listing at all; the `estimate` label is a schema gap.** The calendar row reads verbatim
   **`September 15 | 14:00 UTC | S&P Global Investment Manager Index`**, and 14:00 UTC is **10:00
   ET** under EDT. But the same page's *"standard monthly release schedules"* block names rules for
   the **Manufacturing PMI (first working day)**, the **Services PMI (third working day)** and the
   **Whole Economy PMI** — and **no rule for the IMI**. So the trick the two PMI siblings used
   (check the listing against the publisher's stated rule) is unavailable here, and this lane had to
   build the rule instead. Three primary mastheads:

   | Edition | Masthead, verbatim | Release date | First Monday of month | +8 days |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | January 2026 | *"Embargoed until 1000 EST (1500 UTC) 13 January 2026"* | **2026-01-13** (Tue) | 2026-01-05 | **2026-01-13** ✓ |
   | May 2026 | *"Embargoed until 1000 EDT (1400 UTC) 12 May 2026"* | **2026-05-12** (Tue) | 2026-05-04 | **2026-05-12** ✓ |
   | August 2026 | *"Embargoed until 1000 EDT (1400 UTC) 11 August 2026"* | **2026-08-11** (Tue) | 2026-08-03 | **2026-08-11** ✓ |
   | September 2026 | *(not yet released)* | **listed 2026-09-15** | 2026-09-07 | **2026-09-15** ✓ |

   **The rule is "the Tuesday eight days after the month's first Monday," and it is 4 for 4 — three
   reproductions and one forward prediction that matches the listing exactly.** It also explains the
   one thing about this edition that looks like an error: January, May and August are all **second**
   Tuesdays and September is a **third**. Nothing moved. September 2026's first Monday is the
   **7th** — the latest a first Monday can be — so the release slides a calendar week without the
   rule changing at all. Stays `estimate` because `market-events-data.ts` defines **no** confirmed
   prefix covering a private survey compiler (`CAL:` is *"automated aggregator cross-ref"*; this is
   the publisher's own primary, so claiming it would be dishonest), and this lane's hard limits
   permit a flip only on `IR:`/`BLS:`/`FED:`. **Fifth lane to hit the same standing gap**; named,
   not re-argued.

2. **"A class this calendar tracks nothing of" — MIXED. The calendar is full of sentiment surveys;
   it carries no *buy-side* one, which is a much narrower claim and the one that survives.** A regex
   census returns **44** tracked titles matching sentiment/survey/confidence/investor. Read
   individually they fall into two families, and neither is the IMI's:

   | Family | Members | What the respondent is asked about |
   |---|---|---|
   | Actor about own business/household | Conference Board Consumer Confidence (13 entries), UMich, NAHB HMI, Empire State, Philly Fed, Dallas Fed MFG + TSSOS, BoJ Tankan, ECB bank lending survey | *your* orders, *your* hiring, *your* household finances |
   | Credit plumbing | **SLOOS** (bank lending standards, 2026-11-02), **SCOOS** (dealer financing terms, **2026-09-24**) | the terms on which leverage is extended to market participants |

   **SCOOS is the closest thing the calendar already had**, and it is genuinely positioning-adjacent
   — it asks dealers about financing terms extended to hedge funds. But it asks the *lender*, not the
   *manager*, and it is quarterly. Nothing here asks an asset manager **what it intends to do with US
   equities in the next 30 days**. That is the IMI's actual claim to a slot, and it is narrower and
   more defensible than the proposal's. The proposal's phrasing — *"a class this calendar tracks
   nothing of: positioning"* — is not sustainable as written and is corrected here rather than
   inherited.

3. **"The morning before the vote" — the information half. SUPPORTED, and the date rule turns a
   scheduling coincidence into a real property.** Every IMI edition reports its own field window, and
   all three primaries say the same thing about where it sits:

   | Edition | Data collected | Released | Gap | Month's jobs report |
   |---|---|---|---|---|

   | May 2026 | **May 5–7** | 05-12 | 5d | after the panel |
   | August 2026 | **August 1–7** | 08-11 | 4d | **after** — the PDF says so verbatim: *"Data were collected August 1-7, **before the August jobs report**."* |
   | September 2026 | *forced to the week of 09-07 by the rule* | 09-15 | — | **before** — jobs landed **2026-09-04** |

   field window must sit in the week of **2026-09-07** (nothing later can precede a 09-15 release at
   a 4–5 day gap), which makes this **the first 2026 edition whose panel answered after the month's
   payrolls** — and September's payrolls were a large upside surprise (**+162k vs +55k**, carried

4. **"Its date makes it sharper than its impact tier suggests" — the tape half. REFUTED, on

   build"* — **0 of 9** on blackout-vs-not (n=23 vs 45), **0 of 9** on blackout-without-collision,
   **0 of 9** on collision-vs-clean within October, across n=1,447 sessions. That is a far larger
   sample than anything this event can assemble, on the same structural configuration, and it says
   the FOMC-day-one placement of a 10:00 sentiment survey has **no measurable tape signature**. The

### The two lessons worth banking

**1. A falsifier that says "the release's own X line" assumes the release has exactly one X line.**
FT-1 was a well-built test by every standard this repo applies — dated, specific, readable on the
day, carrying both a prediction and a base-rate base case. It was defeated not by the world but by a
document that stated the answer **twice, differently, five days apart**. Three prior editions carried
exactly one collection-window statement each, so the assumption was reasonable — and reasonable is
not the same as safe. **The generalizable rule for this calendar's other document-reading tests:
name the section, not the sentence.** *"The Note to Editors' stated collection window"* would have
scored cleanly, and costs nothing extra to write.

**2. A measurement that reproduces is still only a prior.** Leg 5's 08-11 reading re-derived almost
exactly on a cold pull a week later — `^TNX` at 0.000% both times — which is the strongest form of
instrument-stability evidence available here. The conclusion drawn from it was still wrong on the
very next observation. **Reproducibility of a measurement is not generalizability of a finding**, and
an n=1 null deserves the "registered, not banked" treatment this ledger gave it. That part of the
process worked: the claim was registered as a falsifiable test precisely so it could die in public,
and it did, on schedule, at the first opportunity.
