## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Stand aside — nothing here is ours to trade (`symbols: []`), and the date is `estimate` on a
taxonomy gap, not on the evidence.** What this research adds is arithmetic. The September print
was hard because its base, September 2025, was the highest month of 2025. **October's base is the
opposite:** October 2025 fell **−0.9%** m/m to **101.5**, so with September 2026 at **103.3** the annual
rate carries **+1.77%** before October does anything. Base case **y/y ≈ +1.7% (+0.7 to +2.7%)**
against **+0.8%** in September — a re-acceleration that is base arithmetic, not a consumer recovery.
The second finding is about a *neighbour's* test. The `uk-retail-sales-2026-12-18` ledger registered
that this print's m/m should come in **above −0.6%** because three Black-Friday-*outside* years
printed October at −0.6 to −1.0%. On the live ONS vintage, **three of the eight inside years
(2015, 2018, 2025) also printed at or below −0.6%** — so passing that line is a ~60% event even if the
pattern is pure coincidence, and the test cannot tell mechanism from noise at n=1. One session
cannot settle it; it is recorded here, not re-registered.

### Leg 2 — "It is a noisy copy of the September print." **REFUTED — the base flipped.**

| Month | Index (J5EK live) | m/m (J5EC) |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 Sep | **102.4** | +1.4% |
| 2025 Oct | **101.5** | −0.9% |
| 2025 Nov | 101.0 | −0.4% |
| 2026 Jul / Aug / Sep | 103.0 / 103.7 / **103.3** | −0.4 / +0.7 / −0.4% |

September's y/y (+0.8%) was dragged by a base that was the high of 2025. October's base is 0.9% *lower*
than that, so the same flat 103.3 would print **+1.77%**. The two prints are mirror images of one base
effect, and any recap calling October's re-acceleration a recovery is reading the arithmetic wrong.

### Leg 3 — "October is a forecast." **MIXED — the carry is fixed, one clear remains, and October is a skewed month.**

y/y ≈ carry + clear. Clear moments (J5EC live): **all 33 months since 2024 mean +0.20%, sd 1.19pp**;
**eleven ex-covid Octobers 2013–2025 mean 0.0%, sd 0.99pp** — six of the eleven at or below −0.6%.
Centre m/m **−0.1%** → **y/y ≈ +1.7%**, ±1sd **+0.7 to +2.7%**. The annual rate stays at or below
September's +0.8% only if October falls past **−0.87%** (index 102.4), about 1-in-4. The three-month
headline needs only index **>102.3** (**−0.97%**), about 80%. Registered as **FT-…-1** and **FT-…-3**.
Revisions remain a nuisance term: the carry's September end moved +0.2 and +0.1 in the last bulletin.

### Leg 4 — "The BRC proxy is a sign indicator." **REFUTED already; a narrower claim survives.**

The sibling's FT-2 was killed 2026-10-25 at a 0.9pp gap with the sign wrong in August and September. The
six months on record show a different regularity: **ONS volume y/y has exceeded the BRC-implied volume
every month** (wedge +4.1 / +0.7 / +2.7 / +0.7 / +3.2 / +0.9pp, April–September; the September figure is
ONS +0.8% against implied −0.1%). That is **bias, not noise** — the structural causes (member panel vs all
retailers incl. automotive fuel, 4/5-week calendar, NSA vs SA) all have a fixed sign. Registered as
**FT-…-2**. No BRC October monitor date is sourced: a calendar aggregator's year-ago pattern puts it in
the second week of November, which is inference, so **no calendar entry was filed**.

### Leg 5 — "The Black-Friday-outside pattern predicts October." **MIXED — the line is not discriminating.**

The sibling [`uk-retail-sales-2026-12-18`](uk-retail-sales-2026-12-18.md) registered
**FT-…-3**: October 2026 m/m **above −0.6%**, because outside years 2013/2019/2024 printed −1.0/−0.6/−0.7.
On the live vintage, the eight **inside** years print **+1.1 (2014), −1.0 (2015), +1.7 (2016), +0.3
(2017), −0.6 (2018), +1.2 (2022), +0.5 (2023), −0.9 (2025)**: mean **+0.29%, sd 1.03**, and **three of
eight (37.5%) at or below −0.6%** — including October 2025, first published **−1.1%** (ledger of the
sibling: *"revised October 2025 from a 1.1% fall to a 0.9% fall"*). So the registered pass condition
holds in only about **60–65%** of inside-year draws with *no* mechanism at all; a single pass is weak
evidence for the mechanism, and a single kill is weak evidence against it. I do not re-register or touch
that row; the finding is for the 12-18 lane's Outcome. Derived period (from the sibling's 4-4-5 rule,
not a published string): Q4 is 4-4-5, so October 2026 is **4 Oct – 31 Oct**, which contains the 28 Oct Budget.

### Leg 6 — "Something specific should push October." **MIXED — a dip story and a lift story, neither quantified.**

- **Dip:** September CPI rose to **3.5%** from 3.1% (21 Oct, secondary; motor fuels the cited driver);
  pre-Budget saving behaviour (a commentator cited polling that consumers spend less on public-finance
  worries — secondary); September was warm and ONS blamed it for weak autumn ranges.
- **Lift:** October reference-month fuel volumes rose in September despite price rises; Budget measures
  (£100 winter payment bands A–D, electricity VAT cut extended to July 2027, fuel duty freeze — all
  secondary) land after or at the end of the period.
- **Weather:** an unofficial aggregator gives October ~0.7°C above average with a named storm 21–23 Oct;
  the Met Office summary was not found. Treated as neutral.
- **Tilt:** −0.1% on the centre (vs the all-month +0.20%), from Octobers' mean of 0.0%.
