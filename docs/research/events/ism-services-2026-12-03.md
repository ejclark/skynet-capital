## Initial research

### The question, plainly

What should we expect from the November 2026 ISM Services PMI on 2026-12-03, is the date right, and —
given that the [11-04 sibling](ism-services-2026-11-04.md) has already measured this release's market
reaction to nil and called its own slot structurally weak — **is there any reason for a fourth ledger
on this series to exist at all**? And the question that sibling raised and could not answer with the
daily bars it had: does the release move anything *intraday*, at the moment it lands, in a way that
close-to-close measurement would miss?

**One-line verdict:** yes, for two reasons that are both new. **Tradeability:** unchanged and now
tested harder — the release hour itself is an ordinary hour across five instruments, so the sibling's
one honest escape hatch ("an intraday spike that faded is possible") closes as **refuted**, not
merely stated. **Informational role:** the opposite of the sibling's — 12-03 is the **only 2026 ISM
Services print inside an FOMC blackout**, six days before a SEP/dot-plot decision, and the tracked
calendar shows every federal November-reference-month price print landing *after* that vote, which
leaves this survey's Prices index as one of exactly three November-data inflation reads the Committee

### Method

Macro-print mode per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md) — `symbols: []`, so no
symbol-keyed instrument applies; the caches were busted anyway
(`rm -rf node_modules/.cache/earnings-cycle node_modules/.cache/intraday-edges`) because this session
re-fetches Yahoo bars through the same layer. Four strands.

**(a) An intraday measurement run this session — the new instrument.** Yahoo **hourly** bars
(`interval=1h`, which serves ~1 year of history against the daily endpoint's decades) for **TLT,
^TNX, SPY, QQQ, IWM**, 2026-01-01 → 2026-09-01, **n=167 sessions**, against the eight 2026 ISM
Services release dates. For each session the bar containing **10:00 ET** is isolated — 09:30–10:30
for the ETFs, 09:20–10:20 for `^TNX`, which quotes on a different grid — and both its **return** and
its **high-low range** are percentile-ranked against that same clock-hour's own 2026 distribution.
The range test is the point: a spike that fully retraced would leave no footprint in a return but a
large one in a range, so measuring both is what actually closes the sibling's caveat rather than
restating it. Script and raw output are reproducible from the parameters above.

**(b) A calendar-structure derivation, run against the checked-in calendar.** ISM's third-business-day
rule was implemented holiday-aware and run over all twelve months of 2026, then checked against the
nine release dates on the record. `computeAdjacentIds` (`scripts/event-material-decide.mjs`) supplied
the ±5-day corridor. The FOMC-proximity and reference-month claims are computed from
`src/domain/market-events-data.ts` itself, so they are statements about *this calendar*, not about
the world.

**(c) Sourced web research, primary-first.** ISM's release-schedule paragraph and the "next report"
line were fetched verbatim from **PRNewswire primaries** (the July-data report, dateline TEMPE,
Ariz., Aug. 5, 2026, and the June-data report). ismworld.org's ROB calendar was fetched directly
today and recorded, not worked around. NYSE's own hours page was fetched; its extracted list came
back internally inconsistent and is handled in the limits rather than relied on.

**(d) Carried, and labelled as carried where used.** The eight-release daily-bar study, the VIX
futures framing, the tracked-name sensitivity ranking and the 2026 base-rate series come from the
[11-04](ism-services-2026-11-04.md) and [09-03](ism-services-2026-09-03.md) siblings. Spot VIX
**16.34** is this session's own Yahoo `^VIX` fetch (2026-09-01 close). The date is **estimate** and
that label rides every trading-adjacent line below.

### Honest limits

**The intraday instrument is new here and its resolution is coarse.** Yahoo serves hourly bars for
~one year, which covers all eight releases, but the bar containing 10:00 ET is a *straddle* — it opens
30 minutes before the print for the ETFs and 40 minutes before for `^TNX`. So a violent 10:00 move
that fully round-tripped inside 30 minutes would show in the range column but be diluted in the return
column; the range column is flat, which is why the caveat closes, but a finer grid (Yahoo's 5- and
30-minute endpoints reach back only ~60 days) would close it harder. **n=8 across five instruments,
one year, one regime, no multiple-comparison correction** — which cuts toward this doc's conclusion,
not against it, but also means 03-04's twin 93rd percentiles deserve no weight. **Legs 5, 6 and 9 are
statements about the checked-in calendar, not about the world:** they are computed from
`market-events-data.ts`, and three of their inputs (this event, ISM mfg 12-01, the CR expiry) are
themselves `estimate`-dated, so a slip in any one changes the geometry. Leg 6's conclusion in
particular depends on no November-reference federal price print existing before 12-09 that this
calendar has failed to track — a gap of exactly the kind leg 7 just found in the ISM series itself, so
it is stated as a claim about our coverage and given its own kill switch. **NYSE's hours page was
fetched and came back internally inconsistent** — it listed Friday 2026-07-03 as a 1:00 p.m. early
close and omitted Thanksgiving and Christmas from full closures, which cannot all be right; the
material fact (Dec 1–3 are ordinary full sessions) is not in doubt, and July 3 is resolvable from
ISM's own behaviour, since a mere early close would not have displaced June data to 07-06. **No
December VIX futures level was obtained** this session — the contamination argument in the horizon
table is structural (three larger events inside a December-expiry contract) and carries no number.
The **11-04 sibling's ledger is append-only and is not edited** by leg 7's date correction; its next
pulse should read it. And the standing gap survives: **no forecast for the Prices or Employment
subcomponent** has ever been found for this release, a limit the 09-03 sibling closed as *un-fireable*
at the calendar level rather than merely unfound.
