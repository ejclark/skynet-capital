### The question

### One-line verdict

The alternative is **refuted before any observation**, on two readings of the published table — this is a

which leaves the sibling's exclusion with no named mechanism at all; and this event's tape test is
**refused rather than inherited**, because the March TIC print sits one session after the March FOMC by
structural necessity in 12 of 14 years.

### Leg 2 — is "March's multi-cut structure" the mechanism behind the Feb/Mar exclusion? · **REFUTED**

The February sibling left this standing explicitly, and correctly refused to assert it:

> *"A live alternative is left standing rather than asserted: **March is a triple-release month** — the
> 2027 table reads `March | 18 | 18 | 31`, monthly plus quarterly plus gross external debt — where
> February is single-cut. That is a difference between the two months this calendar can actually observe."*

**It is a difference between February and March. It is not a difference between March and the rest of the
year, and that is what the hypothesis needs.** Reading the quarterly column down both published years:

| | 2026 | 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| *Quarterly: nonfinancial firms, derivatives, portfolio claims & liabilities* | **Mar 18 · Jun 18 · Sep 16 · Dec 15** | **Mar 18 · Jun 16 · Sep 16 · Dec 16** |
| *Quarterly: U.S. gross external debt* | Mar 31 · Jun 30 · Sep 30 · Dec 31 | Mar 31 · Jun 30 · Sep 30 · Dec 30 |

The quarterly cut lands in **March, June, September and December** — four vintages a year, in both
published years, exactly as a *quarterly* release must. So the structure the alternative names is shared
by four months and the excess it is asked to explain was found in one.

**And the sibling's own numbers already contain the test.** Its revision-edition axis is Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct;
June, September and December are therefore in its **ordinary** class. That class means **$1.67bn** with
Feb/Mar excluded and **$4.46bn** with them left in. If the multi-cut structure produced restatement excess,
three of the twelve ordinary-class months a year would carry it and the ordinary mean would not sit at
$1.67bn. It does.

**Stated as the inference it is, not more.** This reads the sibling's *reported aggregate*, not its
per-month panel, which this session could not rebuild (leg 6). It is a strong constraint, not a
measurement, and it is registered forward as **FT-tic-monthly-2027-03-18-2** so the June, September and
December 2027 quarterly-cut editions can settle it on data rather than on inference.

**What survives.** Nothing named. The annual-survey mechanism is dated to pre-September-2011 by Treasury's
note (d) — the February sibling's finding, which this session read on the same page and confirms
verbatim. The multi-cut mechanism belongs to four months, not one. `FT-tic-monthly-2027-01-19-3` is
therefore **live, but carried by two March vintages and a post-hoc cut with no mechanism attached** — a
weaker status than "live but weaker than registered," and the honest one. Its arithmetic remains
undisputed and unrebuilt here.

### Leg 4 — the collision is structural, so no March vintage can ever read a clean tape · **SUPPORTED**

The obvious objection to leg 3 is that 2027 is unlucky. It is not. Both dates are pinned to the same
mid-month slot by their own published rules — TIC to the 11th business day plus 0–3, the FOMC to a
mid-March two-day meeting — so they collide by construction:

```
year  11th bd of March   March FOMC decision   gap (calendar days)
2014     2014-03-17         2014-03-19            +2
2015     2015-03-16         2015-03-18            +2
2016     2016-03-15         2016-03-16            +1
2017     2017-03-15         2017-03-15             0   ← same day
2018     2018-03-15         2018-03-21            +6
2019     2019-03-15         2019-03-20            +5
2020     2020-03-16         2020-03-15            −1
2021     2021-03-15         2021-03-17            +2
2022     2022-03-15         2022-03-16            +1
2023     2023-03-15         2023-03-22            +7
2024     2024-03-15         2024-03-20            +5
2025     2025-03-17         2025-03-19            +2
2026     2026-03-16         2026-03-18            +2   ← FOMC and TIC BOTH on 03-18
2027     2027-03-15         2027-03-17            +2
```

Gap range **−1 to +7 days**; the FOMC falls inside the TIC publication window in **12 of 14 years**. In
**2026 the two were the same date** — the March TIC release published at 16:00 on FOMC decision day, and
the next session moved the 10-year 1.0bp.

**The consequence is a refusal, and it is the honest call.** A \|Δ10Y\| threshold on 2027-03-19 does not
test TIC; it tests an FOMC-plus-two session that happens to follow a TIC release. The January sibling set
11bp and the February one recalibrated to 8.7bp, both legitimately, because their corridors were clean
enough to attribute a move. Mine is not, so this ledger **declines to register a TIC-keyed tape test** and
registers **the corridor class itself** instead (**FT-tic-monthly-2027-03-18-3**), scored explicitly as
*corridor*, never as TIC.

**And the threshold is set empirically, because a 3σ rule misprices this class.** Class 3σ is
3.600 + 3×3.08 = **12.8bp** — but the class's own twelve-year maximum is **14.0bp** (2026-03-20, six
months ago), so 3σ has already been exceeded once in 30 observations (3.3%, where it should predict about
0.1%). The class is fat-tailed and a Gaussian threshold is the wrong tool. Set at the **empirical p95,
9.0bp**, which fired **2 of 30** historically (6.7%) — a rate stated rather than assumed. For reference,
the sibling thresholds would have fired 2/30 (8.7bp) and 1/30 (11bp) on this class.

logic does not transfer here, and this is why.
