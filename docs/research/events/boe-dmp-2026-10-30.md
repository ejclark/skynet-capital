## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **This is the last public Decision Maker Panel the November rate vote gets, and like its
predecessor it is built not to surprise.** It publishes six days before the **11-05** MPC decision
and Monetary Policy Report (**estimate**), and the sibling release on 10-02 already settled the
question that made it scarce: the Bank's own *"next release date … will be 30 October 2026"* line.
What the committee reads in it is wider than one cell — the **17 September** minutes cite firms'
own-price expectations (*"had remained elevated"*), expected wage growth (**3.4%**, *"stable since
before the start of the conflict"*) and say the DMP's and Agents' forward-looking indicators *"had
not risen unduly"*. The numbers to read are own-price growth one year ahead (**3.7%** in the three
months to September) and three-year CPI expectations (**2.8%**, unchanged for three releases). Every
headline is a **three-month average** over a **rotating third** of a ~10,400-firm panel
(1,993 responses in September), so one month's fieldwork cannot move it much. **None of this is
ours to trade** — `symbols: []`, no rates- or sterling-keyed playbook, no measured DMP tape reaction.
Date is **estimate**; it widens caution and licenses nothing.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Does the 30 October Decision Maker Panel tell us anything that changes what we do? **No — and the reason is structural, which is why it is a low-tier, stand-aside event.** The question that remains is narrower and useful: *what will the MPC read in it six days before the vote, and what number would make that reading unusual?*

**Verdict (estimate-dated):** a reading assignment, not a trade. The survey is a three-month moving average over a rotating third of the panel, so its headline cells move 0.1pp at a time; the only way it matters to the 11-05 outcome is by breaking a pattern it has held for months.

### Method

Primary sources only, fetched direct from a plain HTTP client on 2026-10-16 (all HTTP 200, no fetch blocked; `probe-ref.blocked` stays empty):

Macro-print mode carries no `earnings-cycle`/`intraday-edges` instrument (`symbols: []`). ### Conviction legs, tested

**Leg 1 — The date is real: SUPPORTED.** Two Bank pages agree, and the sibling event's FT-3 (*does the November round get a public DMP*) scored **PASSED** on this very date. The proposal that created this entry cited only the October placeholder page; the 10-02 release's own *"next release"* line is the stronger evidence. Still `estimate`: this lane may not self-confirm, and the taxonomy has no non-Fed central-bank prefix.

**Leg 2 — It is the last DMP before the vote: SUPPORTED, with the calendar caveat.** 30 October is six days before 11-05. The DMP publishes its schedule one release ahead and broke its monthly pattern in 2026 (July data on 24 July, then a six-week gap), so the November release date is known only from the 10-30 page itself — which is a reason to read that line again, not to assume.

**Leg 3 — The headline cannot surprise: SUPPORTED.** Series, all three-month averages: expected own-price growth 3.9% → 3.8% → 3.7% (July→Aug→Sep releases); three-year CPI expectations 2.8% for three releases; expected wage growth 3.4% for three; one-year CPI expectations 3.7% → 3.4% → 3.1% → 3.1%. The September release carried the survey's own words on it: *"Businesses therefore expect output price inflation to be unchanged over the next year, based on three-month averages."* The panel sends *"one of three sets of questions to one third of the panel members"* each month, so a 0.3pp move in a 3mma headline needs the single new month to swing ~0.9pp.

**Leg 4 — The committee reads more than one cell: MIXED (a correction to the sibling ledger).** The 10-02 ledger took the July minutes' single DMP citation (*"own-price expectations in the DMP survey, wage-price feedback and food inflation, pointed to a likelihood of limited second-round effects to come"*) and concluded own-price was the load-bearing cell. The **17 September** minutes (fetched 2026-10-16) cite the DMP three ways: firms' own-price expectations *"had remained elevated"*; *"DMP respondents had reported that they expected one-year-ahead wage growth of 3.4%, which had been stable since before the start of the conflict"*; and *"forward-looking indicators on price and wage-setting from the DMP and Agents' intelligence had not risen unduly."* So the wage cell is read too; the ledger's "discount the wage cell" advice is about information per print, not about what the MPC reads. Own-price stays the first thing to look at because it is the cell the committee calls *elevated*.

**Leg 5 — A surprise would matter more now than in September: MIXED.** The November lean is a hike to 4.00% ([boe-decision-2026-11-05](boe-decision-2026-11-05.md), strip ~85% at 2026-10-02), with three members (Greene, Mann, Pill) already voting for it in September and the committee saying the risk of second-round effects *"had increased since July."* The hawks' case rests on firms passing energy costs through; the DMP's price-raising intent fell to **57%** in September, the dovish reading, while margin-squeeze intent rose to **70%**, the hawkish one. A hawkish break (own-price ≥4.0%) would be the survey's first real evidence for the dissenters; a dovish one would not move the strip much because the vote rests on services CPI and energy. The asymmetry is why the bar is stated on both sides but the tail is not symmetric in consequence.

None. `symbols: []`; no house playbook (S1/S2/E1/S3/S4, G1) is keyed to UK rates or sterling, and the sibling events' close-outs measured no tracked-name reaction to a DMP release. If a play existed it would be sterling/gilt-shaped and sized by the 11-05 outcome, not this survey.

- No consensus exists for the DMP: it is not a polled release, so "surprise" is measured against the prior reading and the damping band, not against analysts.
- The energy-shock supplementary questions have run since April; they may be rotated or dropped without notice, in which case that signal simply disappears.
- The release time is not sourced; the survey has been published in London morning in the sibling record.
- The minutes' references are paraphrased from the Bank's page text, not from a second rendering.
- Estimate-dated: nothing here licenses action.
