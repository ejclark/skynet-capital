## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **The Philly Fed is running the hottest activity print in 58 years while its price line
cools, and the honest reading of 2026-10-15 is a convergence, not a verdict.** Measured this
session from the Philadelphia Fed's own series on FRED — **700 monthly observations back to May
1968**, the longest regional-Fed manufacturing record in existence: August's headline **47.4** is
the **99.0th percentile** of the whole series, with employment at the **97.7th**, average workweek
**98.6th**, unfilled orders **94.7th** and six-month expectations **93.7th** — yet **prices paid
fell 13.0 points to 40.9**, only the **70.9th** percentile. That is a demand boom with *easing*
input costs, and it is the mirror image of what Empire State reported the same month (headline
**20.6**, prices paid **58.6**, a margin squeeze). **Both halves of the pair are therefore in rare
disagreement at once** — the headline gap is **26.8 points**, the **97.4th percentile** of 302
common months against a median of 8.4; the prices-paid gap is **17.7 points**, the **94.0th
percentile** against a median of 5.9. **The base rates say both gaps close, and that is the finding
that changes what 10-15 means.** A prices-paid gap ≥17.7 has narrowed two months later in **15 of
17** prior instances (**below 10 points in 12 of 17**); a headline gap ≥20 has narrowed in **31 of
33**, to a median of 9.5. So October is overwhelmingly likely to show the two surveys agreeing
again — which tells you almost nothing about *which one was right*, and retires the "10-15
adjudicates the pass-through question" framing this ledger inherited from its sibling. **Philly's
own headline has the same gravity:** a print ≥47.4 has occurred **6 times in 700 months**
(1972-12 · 1973-01 · 1973-03 · 1973-04 · 1983-07 · 2021-04); the next month was lower **6/6**, and
two months later the level was below the starting point in **5/6** at a median of **38.5** — never

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

What is the Philadelphia Fed's Manufacturing Business Outlook Survey, what should we expect from the
October reading on 2026-10-15, does it move the tape, and — the question this ledger inherits rather
than invents — is the sibling [`empire-state-mfg-2026-10-15`](empire-state-mfg-2026-10-15.md)
ledger right that 10-15 at 8:30 ET is "the morning that adjudicates" the Empire/Philly prices-paid
divergence?

**One-line verdict:** the survey is worth reading and never worth trading, and the inherited
adjudication thesis does not survive its own base rate — with both halves of the pair at
97th/94th-percentile disagreement and history closing such gaps **15/17** and **31/33** times
within two months, the modal October outcome is the two surveys agreeing again, which is not the
same thing as one of them having been right.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date and time are right, the whole 2026 calendar is now enumerated from the primary, and it
   still stays `estimate` — SUPPORTED, and this closes an honest limit the sibling recorded.** The
   sibling ledger's own limits section says the Philly schedule "rests on the landing page's
   next-two-releases block plus the third-Thursday rule — the full 2026 calendar was **not**
   enumerated from the primary." It is now. The bank's filtered calendar endpoint returns every 2026
   MBOS release as structured data: **Jan 15 · Feb 19 · Mar 19 · Apr 16 · May 21 · Jun 18 · Jul 16 ·
   Aug 20 · Sep 17 · Oct 15 · Nov 19 · Dec 17**, each at **8:30 a.m.** All twelve are the third
   Thursday of their month, so the cadence rule is confirmed on **12 of 12** observations rather than
   inferred from two. **2026-10-15 is a Thursday and the third Thursday of October 2026** (Thursdays:
   1, 8, 15, 22, 29). The entry nonetheless stays `estimate` on two compounding grounds, neither of
   which is doubt about the date: the confirmed-prefix taxonomy in `market-events-data.ts` has **no
   member for a regional Reserve Bank's own survey schedule** (`FED:` covers the federalreserve.gov
   FOMC calendar only), and this lane does not self-confirm. Same reasoning as
   [`eurostat-hicp-final-2026-09-17`](eurostat-hicp-final-2026-09-17.md).

2. **What the survey is, and the scope limit that governs everything below — SUPPORTED, and it is a
   *narrower* footprint than the sibling's.** From the bank's own About block: the MBOS is *"a
   monthly qualitative survey that asks high-level executives about the direction of change in
   activity at their manufacturing firms located in the Third District (Delaware, southern New
   Jersey, and eastern and central Pennsylvania)."* It *"launched in 1968 and is the longest-running
   manufacturing survey compiled by a regional Federal Reserve Bank."* Respondents indicate direction
   of change — increase / no change / decrease — from the prior month for overall activity plus new
   orders, shipments, unfilled orders, delivery times, inventories, **prices paid**, **prices
   received**, employment and average workweek, each with a six-month-ahead expectation and a capital-
   expenditure question. Two structural facts do the work here. It is a **sub-state footprint** —
   Delaware plus *parts* of two other states, narrower than Empire's whole-of-New-York coverage — and
   it is **not a price level**, so it can corroborate or contradict the *direction* of an input-cost
   story and can never confirm or refute a CPI or PPI print.

3. **The sibling's central reading-order finding replicates — but 700 months show it is
   regime-conditional, not structural — MIXED, and this is a correction rather than an echo.** The
   Empire ledger's headline claim is that the prices line is persistent (0.936) where the headline is
   noise (0.269 since 2024), and it presents that asymmetry as a property of these surveys. On
   Philly's far longer record the *ordering* holds everywhere but the *magnitude* is a recent-regime
   artifact:

   | Window | Headline lag-1 autocorr | Prices-paid lag-1 autocorr |
   |---|---|---|
   | Full, 1968-05 → 2026-08 (n=700) | **0.822** | **0.923** |
   | 2001+ (the Empire-comparable window) | 0.761 | 0.920 |
   | 2021+ | 0.703 | 0.928 |
   | 2024+ | **0.309** | **0.830** |

   Fit an AR(1) on the **full sample** (headline slope 0.824, residual sd 10.65) and forecast two
   steps from August's **47.4**: September **40.6**, October **35.1**, two-step standard error
   **13.79** against an unconditional standard deviation of **18.69** — a **26% reduction in
   uncertainty**, 90% band **12.4 to 57.7**. That is not "unforecastable." The same fit on prices
   paid (slope 0.923, residual sd 8.26) gives October **39.2**, se **11.25** against unconditional
   **21.46** — a **48% reduction**, band **20.7 to 57.7**. On the 2021+ window alone the headline
   reduction falls to **11%** (October central **28.5**, band 2.0–55.1) while prices paid holds at
   **48%**. **So the honest statement is that prices paid is always the more persistent half, but the
   headline is only near-useless in the 2024+ regime** — and the sibling's numbers, drawn from a
   record that starts in 2001, cannot see that distinction. Read prices first here too; just do not
   claim the headline carries no information as a law of the series.

5. **A print this hot mean-reverts, and the base rate is unusually clean — MEASURED.** Across 700
   months a headline **≥ 47.4** has occurred **6 times**: **1972-12 (49.1) · 1973-01 (47.8) ·
   1973-03 (58.5) · 1973-04 (53.6) · 1983-07 (49.5) · 2021-04 (48.8)**. The **next month was lower in
   6/6**, mean change **−8.9**. **Two months later** — the horizon that matters, because October is
   two prints from August — the level was **below the starting point in 5/6**, median **38.5**, mean
   change **−11.4**, and it was **never below 20 and never below 0**. Loosening the threshold to
   ≥45 (n=9) gives next-month-lower **8/9** and two-months-below-start **6/9**, median **40.5**; to
   ≥40 (n=20), **16/20** and **15/20**, median **35.2**. The direction is robust across thresholds;
   the sample is small at the tight one, and that is graded honestly in the forward test.

6. **The 1973 analogue is the only prior instance of *this* configuration, and it is a shape rather
   than a forecast — MIXED, graded down for n=2.** Of the three episodes in leg 5, only one — the
   1972-73 cluster — pairs a ≥47 headline with an energy shock arriving underneath it. The
   trajectory, from the same series:

   | Month | Headline | Prices paid | Prices received |
   |---|---|---|---|

   The activity peak (Mar-1973) **led the price-paid peak (Mar-1974) by twelve months**, and by the
   time prices paid reached its record the headline was deeply negative. The 1979-80 shock does not
   replicate the pattern — there the headline was already negative (+4.1 in Jan-1979, −28.6 by
   Oct-1979) *before* prices paid ran to 77.6, so the lead structure is not a law. With EIA regular
   gasoline at **$4.071/gal** for the week ending 2026-08-31 against **$3.177** a year earlier
   (**+28.1% y/y**), AAA **$4.15** on 09-04 and a second Aramco Jazan strike on 09-07 with Brent
   **$97.29** — all carried from [`cpi-2026-10-14`](cpi-2026-10-14.md) — today's configuration
   *rhymes* with 1972-73's early leg. **It is banked as a shape to watch across quarters, and it is
   explicitly not used to forecast October**, which is one print away.

7. **The pair is at a double extreme, and both extremes close — MEASURED, and it is what retires the
   inherited adjudication thesis.** Over the 302 months Empire and Philly share (2001-07 → 2026-08),
   their prices-paid **levels** correlate at **r = 0.908** — the sibling's number, reproduced — but
   their **headline** levels correlate at only **r = 0.697**. August 2026 breaks both at once:

   | Gap | Aug 2026 | Percentile of 302 months | Median | p90 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | \|Empire − Philly\| headline | **26.8** (20.6 vs 47.4) | **97.4** | 8.4 | 20.9 |
   | \|Empire − Philly\| prices paid | **17.7** (58.6 vs 40.9) | **94.0** | 5.9 | 15.0 |

   The direction of the price gap is ordinary — Philly prints *below* Empire on prices paid in
   198 of 302 months — so what is unusual is magnitude, not sign. **And the magnitude reverts.**
   Conditioning on prior instances: a prices-paid gap **≥ 17.7** (n=17) was **narrower two months
   later in 15/17 (88%)** and **below 10 points in 12/17 (71%)**; a headline gap **≥ 20** (n=33) was
   **narrower in 31/33 (94%)**, median 9.5. **The consequence is a correction to the sibling
   ledger**, which frames 10-15 as "the morning that adjudicates" the divergence. On the base rate,
   the modal October outcome is simply that the two surveys agree again — and convergence is
   uninformative about which reading was correct, because a gap can close from either side or from
   both. Watch the pair, but read a narrowing as regression, not as evidence.

8. **The survey nowcasts prices; it does not lead them — MEASURED, and it is the one genuinely
   useful thing this print does.** Testing Philly prices paid at month *t* against PPI final demand
   less foods and energy, 3-month annualized, at *t+k* (2001-07 → 2026-07, n≈300):

   | Lead | 0m | 1m | 2m | 3m | 4m | 5m | 6m |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | r | **0.637** | 0.540 | 0.409 | 0.295 | 0.237 | 0.198 | 0.178 |

   The correlation is **highest at lead zero** and decays monotonically. So a strong Philly
   prices-paid reading is not a signal about future producer prices; it is a **contemporaneous
   description** of the month it covers. **The edge is timing, not prediction, and on 10-15 it is
   real:** the MBOS collects during the release month and prints on the 15th, so at 8:30 ET it
   describes **October**, while the PPI printing in that identical minute describes **September**.
   One month ahead in coverage, zero months ahead in forecasting power — which is exactly the kind
   of distinction that gets lost when a survey is called a "leading indicator."

9. **The calendar arithmetic is the reason 10-15 is special and the reason it is unreadable —
   MEASURED from the calendar and from the primary.** Philly releases on the **third Thursday**;
   Empire releases on the **15th**, rolled forward off weekends. Those two rules coincide only when
   the 15th is itself the third Thursday — which in 2026 happens **once**: October. Comparing the two
   schedules directly (Empire from the sibling ledger's primary, Philly from leg 1's):

   | Month | Empire | Philly | Same morning? | Other tracked 8:30 majors on Philly's date |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | September | 09-15 | **09-17** | no | housing starts (`low`), pending home sales (`low`) — **readable** |
   | October | 10-15 | **10-15** | **yes** | **retail sales** (`high`, confirmed) · **PPI** (`medium`, confirmed) — **not readable** |
   | November | 11-16 | **11-19** | no | none at 8:30 — **cleanest morning of the year** |
   | December | 12-15 | **12-17** | no | housing starts (`low`), import/export prices (`low`) — partly |

   **The single morning of 2026 on which the pair publishes simultaneously is the single morning
   neither survey is readable against the tape.** That is an identification problem, not a weak
   effect: any 10-15 open gap is jointly caused by a `high`-impact consumption print, a
   `medium`-impact producer-price print and two regional surveys, and no percentile test can
   separate them — which is why the sibling's Dallas-style release-hour test is **deliberately not
   run here** either. Note carefully what this does *not* break: the **pair comparison in leg 7 is a
   data question**, answerable at 8:30 by reading two numbers, and it survives intact. Only the
   trading question dies. One corollary worth banking: the Philadelphia Fed's own calendar page also
   lists the 2026 FOMC blackout windows verbatim — **September 5–17** and **October 17–29** — an

10. **National representativeness is modest and declining — MEASURED, and it caps how much any of
    the above can matter.** Regressing the Philly headline on national manufacturing industrial
    production (`IPMAN`, 3-month percent change): **r = 0.623 (R² = 0.388)** over 1972-01 → 2026-07,
    falling to **r = 0.516 (R² = 0.266)** since 2001-07 and **r = 0.444 (R² = 0.197)** since 2015.
    A sub-state diffusion survey explains roughly **a fifth to a quarter** of recent national
    manufacturing output variance, and the share has fallen as the Third District's manufacturing
    base has shrunk relative to the country's. Nothing in this ledger should be read as a claim about
    national manufacturing.

11. **The six-month-ahead index at 73.6 carries a little forward information, and less than its
    percentile suggests — MIXED.** `corr(future GA at t, headline at t+6) = 0.551` across the full
    sample, against `corr(headline at t, headline at t+6) = 0.425` — so the expectations index is
    modestly the better six-month predictor of activity than the current reading is. But the
    conditional base rate is contaminated by regime: future GA has printed **≥ 70** in **58** months,
    and **56 of those 58 fall between 1970 and 1983**, an era when the expectations index simply ran
    higher. Within that sample the headline was *lower* six months on in only 26/58. **The honest
    read is that 73.6 is a genuinely elevated expectations reading whose historical comparison set is
    almost entirely a different monetary era**, so it is recorded as context and not used to support

### What the conditions support

Nothing directional. What the conditions support is a **reading discipline with three measured
foundations**, two of which are corrections to what this ledger inherited. First, an **order**:
prices paid and the paid-minus-received spread before the headline — but stated correctly, which is
that prices paid is *more* persistent (0.923 vs 0.822 full sample), not that the headline is
information-free; the collapse to 0.309 is a 2024+ regime fact and should be re-checked as the
regime moves. Second, a **framing for the pair**: with the headline gap at the 97.4th percentile and
the prices gap at the 94.0th, October is overwhelmingly likely to show convergence (88–94% base
rates), and convergence should be read as regression rather than as one survey being vindicated —
so the inherited "10-15 adjudicates the pass-through question" claim is downgraded to "10-15 is when
the gap most likely closes." Third, a **use**: this is a nowcast of the month in progress, one month
ahead of the official statistic in *coverage* and zero months ahead in *prediction*, which is worth
reading at 8:30 and worth nothing as a trade. And a refusal that is structural: on this date no move
is attributable to this survey, so no size, no hedge, no directional read attaches to it — while
noting honestly that Philly **is** cleanly readable on **11-19**, where this objection does not
apply at all.
