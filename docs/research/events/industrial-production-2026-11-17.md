## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This id existed only as a proposal, filed by the `mtis-2026-11-17` lane, whose central finding
turned on an *industrial-production co-release classifier* it could not test directly — because
business inventories has never once printed without an 08:30 retail companion, so its ledger stated
plainly that attribution was "unfalsifiable by construction". The proposal's argument for tracking
this event was that the calendar knew two of the three releases in the 2026-11-17 GDPNow vintage and
was missing the one carrying the measured signal. **Is that right — and can this event, unlike its
proposer, actually separate the signal from its co-releases?**

**One-line verdict:** the proposal's facts all survive, the date promotes from `estimate` to
`confirmed` on two Board primaries, and the answer to the second question is yes — the separation
runs, and it says the loudness on a G.17 morning belongs to the 08:30 retail print, not to this
report.

### Leg 3 — the co-release counterfactual the sibling declared impossible · **SUPPORTED**, and it is this ledger's central finding

Joining the Board's 189-date archive to `ContribArchives` gives **143** G.17 release dates carrying a
same-quarter GDPNow delta (**143 of the 146** dates in the archive's span, 97.9%). The join
cross-validates: on **137 of those 143** the Atlanta Fed's own free-text `Data releases` column
independently names industrial production, so the Fed-sourced date archive and the Atlanta Fed's
free text agree without either being assumed.

| Vintage class | n | median \|ΔGDP nowcast\| | \|ΔPCE\| | \|ΔEquipment\| | \|Δchg. in inventory inv.\| |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **G.17 release date** | **143** | **0.1902pp** | **0.0852pp** | **0.0159pp** | **0.0715pp** |
| Every other vintage | 1,679 | 0.0862pp | 0.0054pp | 0.0034pp | 0.0118pp |
| permutation p | — | **0.00005** | **0.00005** | **0.00090** | **0.00005** |

Every column separates — and the shape of the separation is the finding. **The report's own channel
is the smallest number in the table.** Industrial production feeds GDPNow's equipment-investment
bridge; Equipment moves **0.0159pp** on a G.17 day. PCE, which this report does not touch, moves
**0.0852pp** — 5.4× more — and outmoves Equipment on **124 of 143 (86.7%)** against a **70.3%** base
rate elsewhere. Now run the counterfactual MTIS could not:

| G.17 vintages, split by co-release | n | \|ΔGDP\| | \|ΔPCE\| | \|ΔEquipment\| | \|Δinventories\| |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **with the 08:30 retail-sales report** | **59** | **0.4062pp** | 0.3270pp | 0.0220pp | 0.0789pp |
| **without it** | **84** | **0.1033pp** | 0.0383pp | 0.0123pp | 0.0656pp |
| permutation p, the two against each other | — | **<0.0001** | **<0.0001** | 0.0098 | 0.4614 |
| **without-retail vs all 1,679 non-G.17 vintages** | — | **p=0.342** | p=0.0027 | p=0.0257 | p<0.0001 |

**A G.17 vintage with no retail co-release moves the GDP nowcast no more than an ordinary day** —
0.1033pp against 0.0862pp, p=0.342. The entire headline separation in the first table is the 08:30
consumption print. Narrowing further to the **46** purest vintages (industrial production alone, or
with import/export prices only) gives 0.1388pp at **p=0.0502** — the same story at the edge of
significance.

Two honest qualifications, both against the interest of the simple story. The report is **not
inert**: even retail-free, it separates on its own small channels — Equipment (p=0.026) and the
inventory-investment line (p<0.0001, which is the manufacturing-IP → inventories bridge doing its
job). And the position effect is real but **not IP-specific**:

| Reference-month position | n | \|ΔGDP\| | \|ΔEquipment\| |
|---|---|---|---|
| **month 1 of the nowcast quarter (this print)** | **44** | **0.3515pp** | **0.0399pp** |
| month 2 | 46 | 0.2007pp | 0.0173pp |
| month 3 | 48 | 0.0975pp | 0.0071pp |

That monotone decay is ordinary nowcast-uncertainty decay — early months move more because less is
known — and it survives the control only halfway: **within month-1 vintages, with-retail reads
0.4848pp (n=24) and without-retail 0.2160pp (n=20), p=0.0032**, the latter still above the 0.0862pp
base at p=0.0113. So month-1 industrial production carries roughly *half* the movement of a
month-1 retail co-release, on its own account.

instrument. SPY, **2014-05-01 → 2026-09-04**, baseline **n=2,961**, 20,000-iteration permutation

### Primary content read — what the last published edition says, and the revision hanging over it

The current release (2026-08-18, July 2026 data) reads: IP and manufacturing production **each grew
0.2 percent** in July after **+0.3%** in June; mining **+0.2%**, utilities **+0.5%**; manufacturing
excluding motor vehicles and parts **+0.4%**. **Total IP at 103.0% of its 2017 average, +1.1%
y/y.** Manufacturing capacity utilization **76.0%**, *"2.2 percentage points below its long-run
(1972–2025) average"*; mining's operating rate 86.1%.

Against the FRED series the level statements sharpen into something less benign than "+0.2%":

- Total capacity utilization is **76.29%**, **3.13pp** below the 1972–2025 mean of 79.41% and the
  **20.1st percentile** of **715** months since 1967.
- Manufacturing output (`IPMAN`) is **99.31** against its own 2017 average of 100.0 — **0.69%
  below where it was nine years ago.** That is the post-revision picture: the **2025 annual
  revision** (released 2025-11-24) revised manufacturing output down so far that, in the Board's own
  words, *"manufacturing output now declines more than 1-1/2%, on net, from Feb 2020 through Aug
  2025, whereas output was previously estimated to have risen over that period."* A sign flip on
  five years.
- Momentum is unremarkable: the last fourteen m/m readings run +0.51, +0.41, −0.26, +0.04, −0.44,
  −0.18, +0.45, −0.45, +0.86, −0.15, +0.75, −0.01, +0.27, +0.20. Ex-COVID (2015+, excluding
  2020-21) the median month is **−0.002%** with an 0.515 sd; only **30.4%** of months land inside
  ±0.20pp.

### Honest limits

- **The archive is release *dates*, not release *contents*.** Legs 1–5 measure whether, when and
  beside what the report published — never what it said. The content read is a single current
  edition plus FRED history.
- **The co-release classifier is free text.** With/without-retail is read from `ContribArchives`'s
  `Data releases` column, whose spellings drift across a decade ("Retail Trade", "Retail trade",
  "Ind. Prod."); it is case-normalised and comma-split but not otherwise cleaned. The release-date
  join every count depends on does not use it, and 137/143 agreement between the two independent
  sources is the check that it is not badly wrong.
- **`p=0.342` is a failure to reject, not a demonstration of equality.** n=84 retail-free vintages
  cannot prove the report moves nothing; it can only say the movement is not distinguishable from
  ordinary at this sample size — and Equipment and inventories *do* separate even there.
- **One result already reversed under more data** (Leg 5's solo class, n=10 → n=37). Every number in
  this ledger is a median on a finite sample and the November-specific ones run n=11 or fewer.
- **The annual revision sits between this research and this print, and this ledger cannot price
  it.** Every level, percentile and seasonal base rate above is pre-revision, on a 2017 base year
  that the Board has already announced it will replace with 2022. Last year's revision reversed the
  sign of five years of manufacturing output; nothing here bounds what this one does.
- **`ContribArchives` ends 2026-07-28** and carries no Q4-2026 vintages, so every class prior is
  out-of-sample for the quarter being nowcast.
- **All the nowcast work measures a model, not a market.** Every Δ describes the Atlanta Fed's
  estimate. The only price claims here are the session and gap studies, and both are reasons *not*
  to act.
- **`symbols: []` is doing real work.** Even were every measurement twice as strong, this event has
  no instrument attached and no house playbook keyed to it. A `confirmed` date changes the
  assessment cadence and nothing else.
