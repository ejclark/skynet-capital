## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **The headline to expect is a sharp slowdown, and it is old news: April's pay already decelerated in the monthly data, so a low ASHE number is the monthly series arriving late, not a surprise.** ASHE measures the pay period that includes 30 April and is published in late October. Last year it showed median weekly full-time pay up **5.3%** to **£766.60**. The monthly whole-economy regular-pay series (ONS AWE, seasonally adjusted, three months to April) says April 2026 pay grew about **3.4%** against about **5.0%** a year earlier. Across the four prior editions ASHE's provisional growth sat within **−1.4 to +0.5 points** of that monthly figure, so the expected print is roughly **2.0%–4.0%**, and **4.5% or more** would be a genuine break from the monthly data. None of this is ours to trade (`symbols: []`; no UK listing, gilt or sterling instrument in this book). The date is **estimate** on a taxonomy gap, not on the evidence.

### Leg 2 — April 2026 pay growth is about 3.4%, so a slowdown is already in the monthly data — **SUPPORTED**

ASHE's reference is the pay period including 30 April (2025: "the pay period that includes 30 April 2025"; sample 174,000). The monthly series closest to it is whole-economy seasonally adjusted regular pay, three months to April, in £ per week (ONS KAI7, rounded levels, current vintage):

| April | AWE regular pay | AWE y/y | ASHE provisional median weekly FT y/y | ASHE − AWE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | £563 | +4.5% | +5.0% | +0.5 |
| 2023 | £606 | +7.6% | +6.2% | −1.4 |
| 2024 | £642 | +5.9% | +6.0% | +0.1 |
| 2025 | £674 | +5.0% | +5.3% | +0.3 |
| **2026** | **£697** | **+3.4%** | **—** | — |

Mean gap **−0.1**, range **−1.4 to +0.5** (n=4). Applying that range to 3.4% gives **2.0%–3.9%**; the monthly levels are rounded to £1, which alone moves a growth rate by about ±0.3 points. Whole-economy *total* pay (KAB9) is +4.6% for April 2026 (£753 vs £720), so the bonus-inclusive series is firmer than regular pay and a headline toward the top of the range is not absurd. Registered as **FT-uk-ashe-earnings-2026-10-22-1**.

### Leg 3 — the 5.3% base is a revised-versus-provisional comparison — **MIXED**

ONS states 2025 estimates are provisional and 2024 estimates were revised in the 2025 release; the 2026 headline will compare provisional 2026 with *revised* 2025. The 2025 revision is unknown until 10-22, and ONS itself says ASHE has diverged from other sources "since 2020" (LFS-based weighting, a smaller sample, the way bonuses are captured). That is why the expected band is widened rather than centred tightly, and why the call is Medium, not High.

### Leg 4 — the policy hook is real but not a market channel — **MIXED**

ONS says the low-pay bulletin is "particularly relevant for policy around the National Living Wage and National Minimum Wage rates". The Low Pay Commission was asked on 2026-03-16 for advice on April-2027 rates "by the end of October 2026", so the release lands in the final week of that window and six days before the 2026-10-28 Autumn Budget (**estimate**). This is a UK fiscal and wage-policy story; it has no path to a tracked name, and the Bank of England has April's pay in its monthly data already.

### Leg 1 — the print (ONS, release dated 22 October 2026 9:30am)

*Employee earnings in the UK: 2026*: median weekly earnings for full-time employees **£811.20** vs £766.60 in
April 2025, **+5.8%** nominal and **+2.7%** real (CPIH-deflated; implies CPIH near 3.0%). All employees **+4.9%**,
part-time **+2.9%** (−0.1% real), hourly ex-overtime full-time £20.81 vs £19.68 (+5.7%), annual full-time
£40,911 vs £39,157 (+4.5%). The 2025 base stayed **£766.60** after revision, so the "revised base" caveat in
Leg 3 cost nothing. All 2026 figures are provisional; final data October 2027. The 2025 achieved sample was
revised up from 174,000 to **182,000**, and ONS calls 2026 the largest ASHE sample on record.
*Low and high pay*: low-paid jobs ticked up to **2.8%**; **346,000** jobs (1.1%) paid below the NLW/NMW.
*Gender pay gap*: full-time **6.8%**, down from 7.2%.

### Leg 2 — why the anchor missed

The anchor did not move: the monthly AWE regular-pay series (KAI7, current vintage published 2026-10-20)
still reads **£697 vs £674 = +3.4%** for April 2026. So ASHE − AWE came in at **+2.4 points**, against a prior
range of −1.4 to +0.5 (n=4). The registered band assumed that range would hold; it did not, and the miss is
in the gap, not in the AWE reading. The monthly data and the annual survey disagreed by more than in any of
the four editions used. **Candidate causes, none tested here:** the two series measure different things (an
April pay-period snapshot of median full-time weekly pay against a seasonally adjusted three-month all-employee
mean); the largest ASHE sample on record; the April 2026 National Living Wage rise (£12.71, +4.1%,
search-relayed, not re-fetched) bunching the lower half of the full-time distribution. Four points was a
thin base for a gap estimate, as the ledger said; this is what a fifth looks like.
