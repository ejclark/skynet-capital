## Initial research

**The question.** What will the August 2026 CPI print show, how will the market react in each
direction, and does it change how we should sit in the tracked names (NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT
GOOG META AAPL AMZN) around 2026-09-11?

**One-line verdict:** an energy-shock print inside a live Fed *hike* debate, five days before the
Sep 16 FOMC — the reaction function is asymmetric (a hot core print is the tail that hurts our
long-duration names most, a cool/in-line print is a relief rally), and the house answer is
guard-shaped, not directional: no macro playbook exists, so the play is known-date variance
discipline across the Sep 4→Sep 16 compound-risk corridor.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date confirmed, BLS primary re-checked 2026-08-17).** Treat 2026-09-11 as a
high-impact known-date variance event, not a tradeable edge: no directional macro position, no
new rate-sensitive paper entries initiated into the print, and any open position sized as if
both tails (hot-core hike repricing · cool-print relief rally) are live. Base case
(estimate-labeled: nowcast, not consensus): headline ~+0.35% m/m with core ~+0.2% — an
energy-hot / core-tame print the market likely absorbs; the asymmetric risk is core ≥0.3% m/m,
which re-arms the September hike five days before the FOMC and hits CRWV and the high-multiple
semis hardest.

**Addendum (2026-08-27 row, D-15).** Two new, partly offsetting findings widen the picture without
flipping the stance: an emerging Iran-Oman Hormuz de-escalation (Brent eased to ~$86–87 across
several sessions) cuts *against* the energy-hot base case if it holds through the August collection
window, while tariff passthrough into goods prices — not previously tracked in this doc, now ~54%
of US goods imports carrying tariffs at an effective ~11.7% rate, with pre-tariff inventory
cushions reported running out — is a second, non-energy channel that could broaden a hot print into
core even if energy itself cools. Neither is confirmed enough to move the base case; both widen
caution, per the estimate-only-widens-caution rule.

**Addendum (2026-08-30 row, D-12) — the asymmetry flipped sides; the stance did not.** The
Jackson Hole kill switch resolved *inversely*: Warsh's 2026-08-28 keynote refused to pre-commit
September at all (he rejected forward guidance outright — "oversharing policy deliberations and
overcommitting to future decisions can lead markets, businesses, and households astray"; his
commitment is "to a discipline, not to a decision"), so the print's fork status survives intact
rather than being killed. What *did* change is what surrounds it: calling inflation the Fed's
predominant focus repriced September from ~35% hike to ~56–59% (CME FedWatch, 2026-08-28). This
doc's leg 4 argued the asymmetry favored the hot-core tail *because hike odds were already low*;
that premise is gone. With a hike now the modal priced outcome, the larger repricing sits on the
**cool/in-line core** side — a hot core largely confirms what the curve already holds. The call is
unchanged (stand aside, no rate-sensitive entries into the print) because the reason was never a
directional view; only the tail-weighting inside "size both tails" moves.

**Addendum (2026-09-02 row, D-9) — the cool tail is fatter but shallower; energy split in two.**
Two findings refine, not reverse, the D-12 addendum. First, the **energy de-escalation switch
reversed before it could fire**: the Iran–Oman Hormuz framework collapsed into renewed US–Iran
hostilities (strikes on Larak Island, Iranian fire toward the UAE and Jordan, a supertanker mined and
burning in the strait), and Brent ran to **$95.55** on 2026-09-02 — a ~6-week high, +14% on the
month. But August's collection window closed 08-31, so this cannot move the August number. The
energy leg therefore **splits**: for *the print*, energy is a settled and mildly cooling input; for
*the reaction to the print*, energy is live upside pressure on the Fed's forward view. Second, the
hawkish repricing extended to **~65–68%** hike (CME FedWatch, press-cited 2026-09-01), while
prediction markets still price a **hold ~52%** — a divergence that widened rather than closed.
Together these *sharpen* D-12's asymmetry rather than undo it: more priced hike means a cool/in-line
core unwinds more positioning, but $95 Brent gives the Fed a forward-inflation reason to hike
anyway, so the relief a cool print can buy is **capped**. Fatter tail, shallower payoff. The call is
unchanged — stand aside — because it never rested on a directional view.

**Addendum (2026-09-04 row, D-7) — a core consensus finally exists, and it moves the surprise
thresholds.** Every prior row of this doc closed with the same admission: no trustworthy August
*core* consensus could be found, so the print's surprise could not be sized. At D-7 that gap closes,
from two independent directions that agree. The Cleveland Fed nowcast (fetched direct today, model
updated 2026-09-03) puts August core at **+0.20% m/m / 2.38% y/y** — *identical* to the D-25 reading
three weeks and a full energy round trip ago. And a core-specific money market now exists:
Polymarket's "Core CPI MoM — August 2026" prices **0.2% at 51%**, 0.3% at 32%, 0.1% at 14%, ≤0.0% at
8%, and 0.4%+ at ~9% (fetched today; **thin at $8,082 total volume — flagged, not smoothed**). Against
those two, forecaster commentary sits a notch hotter at 0.3% core m/m with an Ameriprise outlier at
0.4% — reaching this doc only as aggregator-relayed range, because the Morningstar page carrying it
**403'd for a third straight pass**.

The consequence is a genuine change to this doc's own signal line, which has read *"core ≥0.3% seals
the hike; ≤0.2% is the bigger surprise"* since it was written with no consensus to anchor it. That
line now cuts straight through the middle of a live disagreement: **0.3% is the hawkish half of the
priced range, not a hot surprise**, and a print at either 0.2% or 0.3% resolves this event as a
non-event. The real tails moved outward to **≤0.1%** (~22% priced, the relief tail) and **≥0.4%**
(~9% priced, the tail that hits CRWV and the high-multiple semis hardest). The *call* is unchanged —
stand aside — because it never rested on a directional view; what changes is what a reader should be
watching the tape for at 08:30 on 09-11.

over-weighted: **ISM Services prices paid hit 72.6, a four-year high** (08-26 data released 09-03),

the first hard evidence this cycle of the energy shock reaching services input costs, the broadening
channel leg 3 flagged as MIXED. But prices-paid is an input-cost diffusion index, not a consumer
price; its named drivers are energy (headline, not core); and core CPI's heaviest weight is shelter,
which ISM does not touch. Both the nowcast and the money market saw that print and still center core
at 0.2%.

**Addendum (2026-09-05 row, D-6) — the variance weighting goes UP, because the input that was
supposed to settle the Fed resolved and didn't.** Two findings, and they compound rather than offset.

First, **a sitting governor pre-committed his September vote to this specific print.** Waller's
2026-09-03 speech (federalreserve.gov primary, fetched direct) states it plainly: *"my decision on
the appropriate stance of policy will be heavily influenced by what we learn about August
inflation"* — with both branches named, *"if there is continued progress toward our 2 percent goal,
then I am willing to support holding"* against *"but if inflation comes in hot, I would consider a
rate hike."* This is the first time in this doc's life that a Fed principal has publicly conditioned
a vote on the number it tracks. It upgrades leg 5 — filed **MIXED** on the reasoning that CPI is
*one of two* decisive inputs — from an inference to a stated reaction function, and it is registered
as this event's first forward test (**FT-cpi-2026-09-11-1**), because "a governor names an input"
is a testable claim about whether such statements bind, not a fact about the tape.

Second, **the Aug jobs print resolved hawkish and settled nothing.** Payrolls came in **+162,000**
against a ~55K consensus, unemployment held **4.1%**, and — the detail that matters more than the
headline — **July was revised from −23K to +21K** and June from +20K to +31K. The negative July
print that drove the 2026-08-07 dovish repricing, and that this doc's own leg 5 cites as having
"moved Sep hike odds more than any CPI print did," **no longer exists in the data**. That premise is
retired. Yet after a blowout labor print, futures went only ~50% → ~60% hike: the market read a
strong economy as *permitting* a hike, not *forcing* one, and left the decision open.

**Together these raise the variance weighting a tier rather than lowering it.** The ordinary
expectation into a corridor is that each passing input resolves some of it; here the last major
input before CPI resolved in the hawkish direction and the Fed got *more* balanced, not less,
because the same session's convergence pulled prediction markets up and futures down toward each
other. Two independent pricing regimes that have disagreed by 18–23pt since D-9 now sit ~10pt apart
and **both inside the 45–65% band**. The call is unchanged — stand aside — because it never rested
on a directional view. What changed is that there is now less between this print and the decision
than at any prior row: one governor's stated condition, one PPI, and a coin flip.
