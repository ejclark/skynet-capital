## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This event reached the calendar as an `estimate` proposal from the `existing-home-sales-2026-12-09`
initial research, which nominated it in one line: *"it is the CONTROL for the confound that makes the
12-09 print unreadable… 11-12 is an ordinary Thursday with no FOMC meeting on the Fed's 2026 panel, so
it is the one nearby session where an EHS print's own tape can be read at all."* That is a claim with
three testable parts and a fourth unstated one. **Is 2026-11-12 actually unconfounded — and if it is,
what can a control on an inert series actually detect?**

**One-line verdict:** the date is the publisher's own and promotes to `confirmed`; the control claim
**survives all three tests** and gains a fourth (no GDPNow vintage posts that day, so the tape is the
only observable); and the honest answer to the second question is that the control's entire value is
one measurement — the sibling's inertness kill switch — whose power turns out to depend on a
volatility regime nobody had stated.

### Leg 3 — is 11-12 actually unconfounded? · **SUPPORTED** on all three axes tested

The proposal asserted one axis (no FOMC). The corridor supplies two more that nobody had checked.

**Axis 1 — the Fed's calendar.** Parsed from the Fed's own 2026 panel: **January 27-28, March 17-18\*,
April 28-29, June 16-17\*, July 28-29, September 15-16\*, October 27-28, December 8-9\***. There is
**no November 2026 meeting**. 11-12 sits 15 days after the October decision and 27 before December's,
and the October minutes do not land until **11-18**. The proposal's claim holds exactly as written.

**Axis 2 — CPI proximity.** 11-12 is the **second trading session after `cpi-2026-11-10`**, which the
proposal never mentioned. Measured against the **135** CPI-naming vintages in the archive, on sessions
2016-01-01 onward, excluding EHS and FOMC days from both sides:

| | n | ITB median range | vs baseline | p | SPY median range | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CPI + 1 session | 109 | 1.844% | 1.840% | 0.96 | 0.855% | 0.74 |
| **CPI + 2 sessions** (11-12's slot) | **111** | **1.703%** | **1.847%** | **0.12** | 0.809% | 0.31 |
| CPI + 3 sessions | 106 | 1.784% | 1.842% | 0.52 | 0.761% | 0.08 |

Nothing is elevated; the CPI+2 slot is if anything *quieter* than baseline, and the drift is not
significant. **CPI proximity does not contaminate this control.** One honest limit in the other
direction: only **4 of 123** EHS release days since 2016 fell within two sessions of a CPI print, so
11-12's macro neighbourhood is genuinely unusual (3.3% of the class) even though it is measured not to
matter.

**Axis 3 — the bond-market holiday.** 11-12 is the session after `sifma-bond-market-closure-2026-11-11`
— Veterans Day, bonds shut, equities open a full session. Across the **17** such equity sessions
2016→ (Veterans Day when it falls on a weekday, plus Columbus Day):

| | n | ITB | p | SPY | p | TLT | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The holiday session itself | 17 | **1.281%** | **0.033** | 0.710% | 0.34 | 0.556% | 0.10 |
| **The session after** (11-12's slot) | 17 | **1.820%** | **0.92** | 0.922% | 0.77 | 0.919% | 0.09 |
| Baseline | 2,476 | 1.840% | | 0.876% | | 0.736% | |

The thin-liquidity effect is real and it lands on the **holiday**, not the day after: homebuilders
trade a quarter narrower on a session with the bond market shut, and are back at baseline the next
morning. The rates instrument shows the mirror image — TLT a touch *wider* the session after, p=0.09,
which is a catch-up shape but not a significant one. **The control's day is measured normal.**

**What 11-12 is not is a bare session,** and the ledger should say so rather than let "unconfounded"
drift into "empty." Census releases **Business Formation Statistics** (A202611121000, October data) at
the same 10:00 slot, and the **Monthly Treasury Statement** (tracked, `medium`) lands 14:00 — the first
month of FY2027. Neither is a homebuilder channel, and neither is in the class the confound test was
about, but a session with two other prints on it is a control, not a vacuum.

### Leg 5 — what the control can actually detect · **MIXED**, and this is the ledger's own contribution

The sibling's fourth kill switch reads: *"ITB's session range on any EHS release day between now and
12-09 exceeds 3.0% … with no FOMC, CPI or jobs print that session,"* with **09-10, 10-13, 11-12** named
as the observation dates. This edition owns the last of them, so the question this ledger has to answer
is not whether the switch is well-motivated but **how often 3.0% fires anyway**:

| Class | P(ITB session range ≥ 3.0%) |
|---|---|
| All sessions 2016+ | **17.1%** (460/2,684) |
| Baseline (no EHS, no FOMC) | **16.5%** (409/2,476) |
| EHS release days | 15.4% (19/123) |
| **Baseline, VIX ≤ 16** | **4.3%** (46/1,064) |
| Baseline, VIX ≤ 15.5 | 3.9% (37/958) |
| **EHS release days, VIX ≤ 16** | **2.0%** (1/51) |

Unconditionally the trigger fires on **about one session in six**, which is a p84 test — a fire would
be weak evidence about anything. Conditioned on the volatility regime the market is actually in today
(**VIX 14.53**), it fires on **4.3%** of ordinary sessions and **one EHS day in fifty-one**, which is a
genuine p95-class test. The supporting quantiles say the same thing twice: the low-VIX baseline ITB
range has p95 **2.881%** and p99 **3.924%**, so 3.0% sits just past the 95th percentile of the regime
we are in — while the unconditional baseline has p90 **3.524%**, so 3.0% is not even a tail event in
general.

Graded MIXED, deliberately. The switch is **well-chosen for the regime it was written in and
under-specified as written**: the condition it carries excludes same-session FOMC, CPI and jobs prints,
which is a *news* filter, and the thing that actually governs its false-positive rate is a *volatility*
filter it does not mention. The amendment this ledger proposes for its own use, and offers to the
sibling's, is one clause: **a 3.0% ITB day falsifies inertness only if VIX ≤ 16 that session.** For
completeness, the EHS-day range distribution itself: p50 **1.927%**, p90 **3.557%**, p95 **4.016%**,
p99 4.327%, max **7.784%** — that maximum is a March-2020 session, which is exactly the regime the
clause exists to exclude.

### Leg 6 — the 10:00 hour, and the paired test this edition makes possible · **SUPPORTED**

The sibling registered `FT-existing-home-sales-2026-12-09-3`: ITB's 10:00–11:00 range takes **under
28.7%** of the 12-09 session, because a 14:00 SEP decision owns that day. The control's counterpart is
the same measurement on a day nothing owns. On **273 sessions 2025-08-04 → 2026-09-04** (Yahoo's 1h
history reaches no further back), share of the 09:30–16:00 range taken by the 10:00 hour:

| | Baseline (n=252) | EHS release days (n=12) | FOMC decision days (n=9) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ITB 10:00–11:00** | median **34.6%**, p25 **26.6%** | median **41.2%** | median **20.9%** |
| SPY 10:00–11:00 | median 44.4%, p25 34.8% | median 45.1% | median 27.4% |
| **P(ITB share ≥ 28.7%)** | **69.8%** | **83.3%** (10/12) | **22.2%** (2/9) |

The window is a quarter the size of the sibling's and the baseline it produces is close to theirs
(34.6% vs 35.8%, p25 26.6% vs 28.7%) — a shorter, more recent window, same shape. Two readings. First,
**an ordinary EHS release day's 10:00 hour is at or above baseline**, not below it: the print does not
compress its own hour, which is the assumption the control needs in order to be a control. Second, the
separation between the classes is large enough to test pairwise — across the **108** EHS×FOMC day pairs
in this window the EHS day's 10:00 share is the larger in **93.5%** of them (SPY: 80.6%).

That last number is what makes this edition worth registering rather than merely observing. The
sibling's `-3` and this ledger's `-2` are **the same measurement on two dates whose only material
difference is the 14:00 confound**, and `-3` below states the comparison directly, so a single reading
in December settles both without either lane needing the absolute threshold to be right.

### Honest limits

- **The release-date archive is what GDPNow chose to name.** The 141 dates are the archive's free text
  plus one date from NAR's calendar; any EHS release that did not coincide with a GDPNow posting day is
  invisible to it. Leg 4's n=123 is a sample, not a census — the same limit the sibling recorded.
- **The CPI date list has that limit twice over.** BLS returned 403, so Leg 3's CPI dates are the
  archive's **135** CPI-naming vintages rather than BLS's own schedule. The direction of the finding
  (CPI+2 is not elevated) would have to reverse on the missing dates to change the conclusion, which is
  not plausible, but the count is not the true count.
- **The hourly window is 273 sessions, not 721.** Yahoo's 1h endpoint refuses ranges older than about
  two years, so Leg 6 rests on n=12 EHS days and n=9 FOMC days. The medians agree with the sibling's
  longer window, but every hourly number here is a small-sample reading and the pairwise 93.5% is a
  count over 108 pairs drawn from those 21 days, not 108 independent observations.
- **The kill-switch conditioning is one variable at one threshold.** VIX ≤ 16 was chosen because it
