## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This event exists because the `mwts-benchmark-revision-2026-10-26` lane's adjacency sweep found that
Census names **two** MTIS editions as carrying its restatement and neither was tracked. Its proposal
argues this is the wholesale one, that a tracked entry on the same date is a different release, and
that nothing about it licenses an entry. **Are those claims right — and does this edition differ in
any measurable way from the 12-16 sibling already on the shelf, or is it the same null one month
earlier?**

**One-line verdict:** every claim survives, and the edition genuinely differs — it is the one MTIS
of the year that sits in the archive's loudest nowcast class, for a reason that has nothing to do
with what the report says.

### Primary content read — what the last published edition actually says

CB26-132 (released 2026-08-14, June 2026 data): combined distributive trade sales and manufacturers'
shipments **$2,111.3B**, down **1.1%** m/m (±0.2) and up **10.0%** y/y (±0.3); inventories
**$2,740.2B**, *virtually unchanged* m/m — the 90% confidence interval includes zero — and up
**3.0%** y/y (±0.5); **total business inventories/sales ratio 1.30**, against **1.39** in June 2025.

Sales growing 10.0% y/y against inventories at 3.0% is a destocking regime. Census's own 414-month
workbook places 1.30 at the **24.4th percentile** of readings strictly below it since 1992 — **28.3rd**
counting the 16 months that also printed 1.30, which is the figure the FRED-sourced sibling ledgers
quote, so the two agree once ties are stated. The obvious read is that the slide continues into
September. Two measurements say otherwise. First, **it has already stopped**: the ratio troughed at
**1.28** in May-2026 and ticked back to 1.30 in June. Second, conditioning on a comparable regime
(12-month ratio change ≤ −0.08; today's is **−0.090**; **n=25** episodes), the ratio **three months
later** — which is September, this print's own month — is at or below its level then only **44.0%**
of the time, median change **+0.010**; and the *monthly* non-increase rate inside a destocking regime
is **57.7%** (n=26) against **68.5%** unconditionally (n=413). **Conditioning on destocking makes a
further decline less likely, not more.** For completeness on the seasonals: the adjusted Aug→Sep
ratio change has a median of **0.000** across 34 years, so nothing in the calendar tilts it either
way.

### Honest limits

- **The archive is release *dates*, not release *contents*.** Legs 2, 3 and 5 measure whether, when
  and beside what the report published — never what it said.
- **Leg 4's class is pandemic-loaded and its post-2021 remainder is thin.** n=11 at a 45.5% hit rate
  is a coin flip measured on eleven observations; `-1` is registered as one and should be read as
  one. `ContribArchives` also ends **2026-07-28** and carries no Q4-2026 vintages, so the class
  priors are out-of-sample for the quarter being nowcast.
- **The IP classifier is free text.** Solo-vs-IP is read from `ContribArchives`'s `Data releases`
  column, whose spellings are inconsistent across the decade; the 44/35 split is case-normalised but
  not otherwise cleaned. The release-date join every other number depends on does not use it.
- **Attributability is unfalsifiable by construction, as it was for the sibling.** 84/84 co-release
  with the 08:30 retail report means no MTIS-only vintage and no MTIS-only session has ever existed.
  "This print owns none of the loudness" is a statement about what can be attributed, not about
  causation.
- **The benchmark restatement sits between this research and this print, and this ledger cannot
  price it.** Census's own word is "tentatively"; every level, ratio and percentile above is
  pre-revision on the retail and wholesale legs, and from 11-17 the aggregate is 64.9%/35.1%

- **The advance-to-final revision size for retail inventories is not measured here.** How much this

- **All the nowcast work measures a model, not a market.** Every Δ describes the Atlanta Fed's

- **`symbols: []` is doing real work.** Even were every measurement twice as strong, this event has
