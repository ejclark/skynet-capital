## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Everyone is waiting for this print to show a −0.10pp VAT cut. It will not, and the sign
of the energy story is the other way round.** The zero-rating of VAT on GB domestic electricity
(SI 2026/987, in force 1 October) is worth **0.098pp** of CPI — but that is a **counterfactual**
number, the gap against a world where the cut did not happen. What a reader of the print actually
compares is **this October against last October**, and there the arithmetic runs the other way.
ONS's own series, fetched today: electricity (**D7DT**) rose **+2.376% m/m** in October 2025 and gas
(**D7DU**) **+0.951%**. Ofgem's 26 August release puts the October 2026 cap at **+4% to £1,723** from
£1,663, with **gas +8%** and electricity-only households **"less than 1%"**. Weight those by ONS's
2026 weights (electricity **20.6065/1000**, gas **10.3002/1000**) and the energy block adds about
**+0.034pp to the annual rate versus last October** — a net *upward* push containing a tax cut.
And the cut is **smaller than the print's own rounding step**: 0.098pp against a headline published
to one decimal. **You cannot see it in the headline. Only the electricity index can show it.** Base
case for the print is **2.9–3.2% y/y**, under the July MPR's ~3.2% Q4 peak. **None of it is ours to
trade** — `symbols: []`, and this calendar's entire symbol universe is eight US mega-caps. Date is
**estimate** on a taxonomy gap, not on the evidence; it widens caution and licenses nothing.

### Leg 2 — "The October print is where the −0.10pp VAT cut shows up." **MIXED, and this is the whole point of the entry.**

Two separate claims are packed into that sentence and only the first survives.

**October is the right month.** SI 2026/987 zero-rates GB domestic electricity for "supplies made in
the period beginning with 1st October 2026", so October is the first month in the index. And it is
not smeared: ONS's April 2026 handling is the precedent — when the government took "an average of
£150 off the cost of energy bills from April 2026 by making policy changes that would result in
lowering the per-unit price", ONS stated that for **"the two fixed tariff items, which use a
12-month rolling average, the unit rates for existing customers starting their contracts during May
2025 to March 2026 will be adjusted downwards for the compilation of the April 2026 indices
onwards"** (ONS March 2026 bulletin, fetched). The effect landed in the month the policy bit:
electricity **−8.4% m/m** and gas **−4.4% m/m** in April 2026 (ONS April 2026 bulletin, fetched).
So a full-month, full-cohort landing in **October** is the documented behaviour, not a hope. This
also settles the pass-through leg the parent ledger graded MIXED more firmly than it did: VAT is
charged at the statutory rate, so a fixed-tariff customer whose ex-VAT unit rate is contractually
fixed sees the fall mechanically, and Ofgem's cap — set VAT-inclusive — forces it for the rest.

**The headline cannot show it.** At **0.098pp** (CJXA weight **20.6065/1000** × 5/105 = **4.7619%**)
against a y/y rate published to **one decimal**, the cut is **98% of a single rounding increment**.
Whether it moves the printed number depends entirely on where the unrounded figure happens to sit.
Any claim of the form "the October headline came in 0.1pp lower because of VAT" is unfalsifiable
from the headline alone. That is why the parent ledger's test, and both of this ledger's own energy
tests, key on the **electricity index (04.5.1)** rather than on the headline.

### Leg 3 — "So energy drags the October print down." **REFUTED. It pushes it up.**

This is the finding that reverses the story, and it is one subtraction the commentary is not doing.
A year-on-year rate moves on the difference between **this** month's m/m and the **same month last
year's** m/m. So the question is not "what did VAT save" but "how does October 2026's energy step
compare with October 2025's".

| | Oct-2025 m/m (ONS, fetched) | Oct-2026 m/m (Ofgem, 26 Aug 2026) | 2026 weight /1000 | Contribution to the Δ in the annual rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Electricity (D7DT) | **+2.376%** (189.4 → 193.9) | **≤ +1%** ("electricity-only households… less than 1%") | 20.6065 | **−0.028 to −0.039pp** |
| Gas (D7DU) | **+0.951%** (147.2 → 148.6) | **+8%** ("gas bills rising by 8%") | 10.3002 | **+0.073pp** |
| **Energy block, net** | | | | **≈ +0.023 to +0.044pp** |

**Central estimate: +0.034pp.** The energy block, containing a legislated tax cut, is a *net upward*
contributor to the October annual rate — because last October's cap step was **+2%** (£1,755, "a rise
of £35", ONS October 2025 bulletin) against this October's **+4%** (£1,723 from £1,663, Ofgem).
The cap is also still **lower** year-on-year (£1,723 vs £1,755, **−1.8%**) than it was in September
(£1,663 vs £1,720, **−3.3%**) — the drag lessens, which is the same fact stated at bill level.

**The distinction that matters, stated once:** −0.098pp is a **counterfactual** delta (this October
versus a hypothetical no-cut October). +0.034pp is a **base-effect** delta (this October versus last
October). Both are true. Only the second is visible in the printed annual rate. Reading the first
number off the second's series is the specific error this entry exists to prevent — and it is a
sharper version of the parent ledger's attribution rule, which said the print would come in "~0.1pp
softer than a naive model" without saying which model.

### Leg 4 — "Gas and electricity are flat m/m inside a cap quarter, by construction." **REFUTED as of April 2026 — and the sibling ledger still asserts it.**

[`uk-cpi-2026-09-16`](uk-cpi-2026-09-16.md) states, of the August print, that "the Ofgem cap does not
step between 1 July and 1 October, so gas and electricity are flat m/m **by construction**", and
grades that leg SUPPORTED. It was true for years. It is not true now:

| Cap quarter | Electricity (D7DT) | Gas (D7DU) |
|---|---|---|
| Jul–Sep **2024** | 175.3 · 175.3 · 175.3 — **flat** | — |
| Apr–Jun **2025** | 196.8 · 196.7 · 196.7 — **flat** | 158.6 · 158.6 · 158.7 — **flat** |
| Jul–Sep **2025** | 189.3 · 189.3 · 189.4 — **flat** | 147.3 · 147.2 · 147.2 — **flat** |
| Apr–Jun **2026** | 184.9 · 185.7 · 186.5 — **+0.43%/month** | 137.1 · 137.8 · 138.6 — **+0.55%/month** |

The mechanism is ONS's own documented one. There are **four** gas/electricity item indices, covering
variable- *and* fixed-term tariffs; the two fixed-tariff items run on a **12-month rolling average**
of contract cohorts, and the April 2026 compilation adjusted the May-2025→Mar-2026 cohorts downwards
in one step (quoted in Leg 2). As those adjusted cohorts roll out of the twelve-month window month by
month, the fixed-tariff indices move **inside** a cap quarter. Jan–Mar 2026 was still near-flat
(201.3 · 201.3 · 201.9); the drift begins in April, exactly where the adjustment does.

Two consequences. First, the sibling's August-print leg is standing on a premise that expired, which
is worth a sentence on the record even though its *direction* (energy contributes little in August)
is unaffected at this magnitude. Second, for **this** print: roughly **+0.014pp/month** of energy
drift accrues in August and September that did not accrue in 2025, so about **+0.03pp** goes into the
October y/y on top of the +0.034pp step computed in Leg 3.

This leg is **scoreable well before the event** — the September 2026 index publishes on **2026-10-21**
— which is why it is registered as **FT-uk-cpi-2026-11-18-3** rather than left as an assertion.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date: estimate on a taxonomy gap; the underlying release is on the publisher's calendar).**
**Stand aside at every horizon — there is no instrument in this book with a channel to a UK CPI
print,** and none is claimed. The entry's operational value is three attribution rules and a base
case, none of which is a trade:

1. **The energy block pushes this print UP, not down.** +0.034pp on the annual rate versus last
   October, because Oct-2025's cap step was +2% and Oct-2026's is +4% with gas at +8%. The −0.098pp
   VAT saving is a *counterfactual*; it is not a base effect and it is not visible here.
2. **The headline cannot show the VAT cut at all.** 0.098pp is 98% of one rounding increment on a
   series published to one decimal. Watch **CPI 04.5.1 electricity (D7DT)**, not the headline —
   the same instruction the parent ledger gave, now with the reason quantified.
3. **A soft print is not a dovish surprise.** The cut was law on 7 September, two months before the
   5 November MPR that forecasts it, and the last print before the 17 December vote is **2026-12-16**,
   not this one.

Base case for the print: **2.9–3.2% y/y**, centred near 3.0%, under the July-MPR ~3.2% Q4 peak — and
under the 3.4% threshold of the sibling test this event scores.

**This print is the scoring venue for two sibling forward tests**, and this ledger does not
re-register either: `FT-uk-cpi-2026-09-16-3` (October headline ≤3.4%, score by 2026-11-19) and
`FT-uk-electricity-vat-zero-rate-2026-10-01-1` (electricity index up ≥2.0% m/m ⇒ pass-through
failed, score by 2026-11-18). Note that the parent's test and this ledger's **FT-…-1** approach the
same number from opposite sides: theirs names the failure threshold, this one names what the
arithmetic actually requires.

**Kill switches** — any one of these and the stance is rebuilt, not adjusted:

- **A tracked name moves >2%** on a session between 2026-09-09 and 2026-11-18 attributable to UK
  inflation data → the "no price channel" premise is false.
- **ONS moves or cancels the 2026-11-18 release** → the date comes off the board.
- **Ofgem revises the 1 October cap** away from +4%/£1,723, or restates the gas and electricity
  splits → Leg 3's entire arithmetic is rebuilt (**FT-…-1**, **FT-…-2**).
- **The September 2026 electricity index (published 2026-10-21) comes in within 0.5% of July's
  193.2** → Leg 4 is wrong, the flat-quarter premise survives, and the sibling ledger needs no
  correction (**FT-…-3**).
- **SI 2026/987 is annulled, or the 2026-10-28 Budget extends it beyond 2027-03-31** → the notch's
  size or its look-through character changes, and Leg 7 is re-argued.
- **A published consensus appears that differs from 2.9–3.2%** → Leg 5 stops being the only estimate
