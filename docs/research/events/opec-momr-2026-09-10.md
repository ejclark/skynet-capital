## Initial research

**The question.** What will the September 2026 OPEC Monthly Oil Market Report say, does its release
move anything, and does the producer side settle the Gulf-supply disagreement the
[IEA ledger](iea-omr-2026-09-11.md) filed this event to test?

**One-line verdict:** the release is a measured non-event that lands thirty minutes before a PPI
print, and the report's value is one recomputable table that breaks the EIA–IEA tie toward the EIA.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md)
(macro-print mode; the house instruments are equity/earnings-keyed and cover no commodity or
publication). **Unlike the two sibling energy ledgers, the primary was reached.** `opec.org`'s HTML
pages, `publications.opec.org/momr` and `momr.opec.org` all return **403** to `curl` with a browser
user-agent and to WebFetch; `www.opec.org/` itself returns 200 but is nav-only. The asset host is
open: `www.opec.org/assets/assetdb/momr-<month>-<year>.pdf` served **21 of 24** requested editions
at HTTP 200 on 2026-09-06. Nineteen usable PDFs were parsed for cover date, the "Next report to be
issued on" line, embedded document metadata, and the production tables. The August 2026 edition was
additionally pulled from an independent third-party mirror (egyptoil-gas.com) and matches the
primary host's chain. **The base rate was measured this session**, not quoted: Yahoo hourly bars for
CL=F, BZ=F, NG=F, USO, XLE, `range=2y`, bucketed by UTC hour, over the 20 primary-dated releases.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is primary-sourced and self-verifying, and stays `estimate` for reasons unrelated to
   the date — SUPPORTED.** OPEC's August 2026 MOMR (cover date **12 August 2026**) prints, in its
   abbreviations page: *"Next report to be issued on 10 September 2026."* That is the issuing body's
   own statement, read from its own PDF. It is not a lone claim — the whole chain was pulled and it
   closes on itself: every edition's next-report line equals the following edition's cover date,
   **18 of 18** consecutive links. Full primary release history, 2025: **Jan 15, Feb 12, Mar 12,
   Apr 14, May 14, Jun 16, Jul 15, Aug 12, Sep 11, Oct 13, Nov 12, Dec 11**; 2026: **Jan 14, Feb 11,
   Mar 11, Apr 13, May 13, Jun 11, Jul 13, Aug 12**. (Oct 13 2025 comes from the September edition's
   next-report line — that one PDF 403s on the asset host.) The status stays **estimate** on two
   counts, neither of them date doubt: `market-events-data.ts` defines no `OPEC:` confirmed prefix,
   and this lane may not self-confirm an event it discovered in its own sweep.

2. **The aggregator that supplied this event got the date right and the time wrong — SUPPORTED, and
   this is the leg with a live consequence.** All nine back-history rows the event's `source` quotes
   from investing.com (Aug 12, Jun 11, May 13, Apr 13, Mar 11 2026; Dec 11, Nov 12, Oct 13, Sep 11
   2025) match the primary chain **9/9**; it merely omitted Jan 14, Feb 11 and Jul 13 2026. But its
   stated time — **10:00 London = 09:00 UTC** — is falsifiable and false. Every MOMR PDF carries an
   embedded `CreationDate` in Vienna local time; a file cannot be published before it exists, so
   that stamp is a hard lower bound on the release moment. Converted to UTC across 19 editions the
   stamps run **07:03 to 11:23, median ~10:21** — and **11 of 19 are at or after 10:00 UTC**, so a
   09:00 UTC release is *impossible* for eleven editions, including June 2026 (10:21) and April 2025
   (11:20). Meanwhile **19 of 19 are before 12:00 UTC**, which is precisely consistent with the
   Reuters wire item quoting OPEC's own email: *"will release its August monthly oil market report at
   1200 GMT (1400 Vienna time)."* The primary artifacts adjudicate between two secondary sources and
   the aggregator loses. **Operative consequence:** on 2026-09-10 the report lands at **12:00 UTC =
   08:00 ET**, thirty minutes ahead of the 08:30 ET PPI and ninety minutes before the US open.

3. **The release window is a measured non-event, and it is a non-event under *either* candidate time
   — SUPPORTED, on a tail test rather than a median.** No base rate for a MOMR release existed
   anywhere in this repo. Each of the 20 primary-dated releases was compared against every bar in
   the same UTC hour across a two-year Yahoo sample:

   its largest move was **1.011%**. Direction is absent too: **13 up, 7 down** on Brent at 12Z. The

   12Z with 3/20 beating p95. The leading explanation is contamination, not signal — MOMR dates

   UTC bar on several of them. That cuts toward the conclusion rather than against it: even with
   macro prints sharing the bar, crude in the MOMR hour is quieter than an ordinary 12Z hour.

4. **Nobody can trade the window in a US-listed instrument — SUPPORTED, mechanically.** Yahoo's

5. **OPEC publishes no shut-in figure at all — SUPPORTED, and it reframes the question this event

   on Saudi's post-1990 low. *(A Reuters survey using LSEG/Kpler flow data put OPEC-11 output

### What a future MOMR session should do differently

- **Probe the appendix first, then the PDF.** `momr-appendix-<mon>-<year>.xlsx` outlived the PDF by
  at least two editions. Probe abbreviated *and* full month names — the naming is not stable.
- **Snapshot the artifact when you read it.** Leg 1 parsed 19 PDFs that it can no longer re-open.
  A close-out nine days later could not re-read the document its own forward tests were keyed to.
- **Key a release-time test to an artifact that persists**, not to the PDF's `CreationDate`.
- **Adjacency, closing.** 33 tracked events sat within ±5 days (4 confirmed high/critical: CPI
  09-11, the 10Y, 20Y and 30Y). No new dated adjacent event is proposed: the October MOMR's date
  lives in the September edition's own "next report" line, which this session could not read, and
  the previous session's refusal to invent one from cadence stands. **Tape at close-out (live
  2026-09-15):** VIX **17.45**, CL=F **103.82**, BZ=F **102.69**, NG=F **2.917** — the BZ=F print
  sits *below* WTI after a 6-point one-session spread swing off 09-14's 105.68, which reads as a
  front-month contract roll in Yahoo's continuous series rather than a real Brent–WTI inversion;
  recorded as fetched, and flagged rather than smoothed.
