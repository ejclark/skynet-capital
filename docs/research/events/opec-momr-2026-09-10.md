## Initial research

**The question.** What will the September 2026 OPEC Monthly Oil Market Report say, does its release
move anything, and does the producer side settle the Gulf-supply disagreement the
[IEA ledger](iea-omr-2026-09-11.md) filed this event to test?

**One-line verdict:** the release is a measured non-event that lands thirty minutes before a PPI
print, and the report's value is one recomputable table that breaks the EIA–IEA tie toward the EIA.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md)
(macro-print mode; the house instruments are equity/earnings-keyed and cover no commodity or
publication). **Unlike the two sibling energy ledgers, the primary was reached.** `opec.org`'s HTML
pages, `publications.opec.org/momr` and `momr.opec.org` all return **403** to `curl` with a browser
user-agent and to WebFetch; `www.opec.org/` itself returns 200 but is nav-only. The asset host is
open: `www.opec.org/assets/assetdb/momr-<month>-<year>.pdf` served **21 of 24** requested editions
at HTTP 200 on 2026-09-06. Nineteen usable PDFs were parsed for cover date, the "Next report to be
issued on" line, embedded document metadata, and the production tables. The August 2026 edition was
additionally pulled from an independent third-party mirror (egyptoil-gas.com) and matches the
primary host's chain. **The base rate was measured this session**, not quoted: Yahoo hourly bars for
CL=F, BZ=F, NG=F, USO, XLE, `range=2y`, bucketed by UTC hour, over the 20 primary-dated releases.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is primary-sourced and self-verifying, and stays `estimate` for reasons unrelated to
   the date — SUPPORTED.** OPEC's August 2026 MOMR (cover date **12 August 2026**) prints, in its
   abbreviations page: *"Next report to be issued on 10 September 2026."* That is the issuing body's
   own statement, read from its own PDF. It is not a lone claim — the whole chain was pulled and it
   closes on itself: every edition's next-report line equals the following edition's cover date,
   **18 of 18** consecutive links. Full primary release history, 2025: **Jan 15, Feb 12, Mar 12,
   Apr 14, May 14, Jun 16, Jul 15, Aug 12, Sep 11, Oct 13, Nov 12, Dec 11**; 2026: **Jan 14, Feb 11,
   Mar 11, Apr 13, May 13, Jun 11, Jul 13, Aug 12**. (Oct 13 2025 comes from the September edition's
   next-report line — that one PDF 403s on the asset host.) The status stays **estimate** on two
   counts, neither of them date doubt: `market-events-data.ts` defines no `OPEC:` confirmed prefix,
   and this lane may not self-confirm an event it discovered in its own sweep.

2. **The aggregator that supplied this event got the date right and the time wrong — SUPPORTED, and
   this is the leg with a live consequence.** All nine back-history rows the event's `source` quotes
   from investing.com (Aug 12, Jun 11, May 13, Apr 13, Mar 11 2026; Dec 11, Nov 12, Oct 13, Sep 11
   2025) match the primary chain **9/9**; it merely omitted Jan 14, Feb 11 and Jul 13 2026. But its
   stated time — **10:00 London = 09:00 UTC** — is falsifiable and false. Every MOMR PDF carries an
   embedded `CreationDate` in Vienna local time; a file cannot be published before it exists, so
   that stamp is a hard lower bound on the release moment. Converted to UTC across 19 editions the
   stamps run **07:03 to 11:23, median ~10:21** — and **11 of 19 are at or after 10:00 UTC**, so a
   09:00 UTC release is *impossible* for eleven editions, including June 2026 (10:21) and April 2025
   (11:20). Meanwhile **19 of 19 are before 12:00 UTC**, which is precisely consistent with the
   Reuters wire item quoting OPEC's own email: *"will release its August monthly oil market report at
   1200 GMT (1400 Vienna time)."* The primary artifacts adjudicate between two secondary sources and
   the aggregator loses. **Operative consequence:** on 2026-09-10 the report lands at **12:00 UTC =
   08:00 ET**, thirty minutes ahead of the 08:30 ET PPI and ninety minutes before the US open.

3. **The release window is a measured non-event, and it is a non-event under *either* candidate time
   — SUPPORTED, on a tail test rather than a median.** No base rate for a MOMR release existed
   anywhere in this repo. Each of the 20 primary-dated releases was compared against every bar in
   the same UTC hour across a two-year Yahoo sample:

   | Symbol | Hour | Release-hour median \|move\| (n=20) | Ordinary same-hour median | Ratio | Exceeded own-hour **p90** | Exceeded own-hour **p95** | Max |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | CL=F | **12Z** *(Reuters time)* | 0.167% | 0.277% | **0.60×** | 1 / 20 (2.0 expected) | **0 / 20** | 1.134% |
   | BZ=F | **12Z** | 0.136% | 0.269% | **0.51×** | **0 / 20** | **0 / 20** | **1.011%** |
   | NG=F *(control)* | 12Z | 1.098% | 0.766% | 1.43× | 4 / 20 | 3 / 20 | 3.213% |
   | CL=F | 09Z *(aggregator time)* | 0.133% | 0.303% | 0.44× | 1 / 20 | 1 / 20 | 2.577% |
   | BZ=F | 09Z | 0.149% | 0.308% | 0.48× | 1 / 20 | 1 / 20 | 1.726% |

   Read the **tail**, not the median — a trade needs an outlier. At the release hour the evidence
   actually supports, Brent produced **zero** outliers in twenty releases at *either* threshold, and
   its largest move was **1.011%**. Direction is absent too: **13 up, 7 down** on Brent at 12Z. The
   robustness that matters is that the answer does not depend on resolving leg 2 — both candidate
   hours are *below* an ordinary same-hour bar on both benchmarks. **The control does not behave as
   cleanly as the IEA study's did, and that is stated rather than buried:** natural gas is 1.43× at
   12Z with 3/20 beating p95. The leading explanation is contamination, not signal — MOMR dates
   cluster on the 11th–15th, and a US CPI or PPI print at 08:30 ET falls *inside* the 12:00–13:00
   UTC bar on several of them. That cuts toward the conclusion rather than against it: even with
   macro prints sharing the bar, crude in the MOMR hour is quieter than an ordinary 12Z hour.

4. **Nobody can trade the window in a US-listed instrument — SUPPORTED, mechanically.** Yahoo's
   two-year hourly series for **USO** and **XLE** contains **0 bars** at 09Z, 11Z and 12Z — across
   all 20 release dates and all ~500 sessions in the sample. The instruments do not exist there.
   Their first bar of the day buckets at 13Z (the 09:30 ET open), by which time the 08:30 ET PPI has
   already printed. Only the round-the-clock futures are open at release, and leg 3 says what they
   do.

5. **OPEC publishes no shut-in figure at all — SUPPORTED, and it reframes the question this event
   was filed to ask.** The August 2026 MOMR, 96 pages, contains **zero** occurrences of "shut-in",
   "shut in" or "pre-war". The producer side does not report the metric the two consumer-side
   agencies argue over. What it reports instead is a **level**: country-by-country crude production,
   monthly and quarterly, from secondary sources. So the third read cannot be a like-for-like
   tie-break — it has to be recomputed, and the baseline has to be chosen and stated.

6. **Recomputed, the producer's own table lands on the EIA's side, not the IEA's — SUPPORTED, and
   this is the session's most useful finding.** Table 5-7 of the August 2026 edition, Middle East
   DoC participants, taking **4Q25 as the last clean pre-war quarter** (Iraq is already down 781
   tb/d by 1Q26, so the war lands inside 1Q26):

   | Producer | 4Q25 (tb/d) | Jul 2026 (tb/d) | Change |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Saudi Arabia | 10,043 | 7,352 | **−2,691** |
   | Iraq | 4,094 | 2,621 | **−1,473** |
   | IR Iran | 3,208 | 2,478 | −730 |
   | Kuwait | 2,564 | 1,845 | −719 |
   | Bahrain | 161 | 57 | −104 |
   | UAE | 3,389 | 3,780 | +391 |
   | Oman | 803 | 926 | +123 |
   | **Total** | **24,262** | **19,059** | **−5,203** |

   **−5.20 mb/d net**, or **−5.72 mb/d** counting only the five countries that fell. Against the
   2025 calendar-year average instead of 4Q25 the same arithmetic gives **−4.28 mb/d**, so the
   baseline choice is worth ~0.9 mb/d and both numbers are stated. Set beside the pair the IEA
   ledger could not reconcile — **EIA ~5.5**, **IEA 8.3** — the producer's own accounts put July's
   Gulf loss **within 0.3 mb/d of the EIA and 3.1 mb/d below the IEA**. Two of three agencies now
   agree; the IEA is the outlier. The correct update to the sibling ledger's "no single figure is a
   fact" is narrower and more useful: *the low figure is corroborated twice and the high figure once*
   — and the honest version of "cite the pair" is now **cite the trio**.

7. **The same document contradicts itself by 0.85 mb/d on one country, which is the caution that
   keeps leg 6 from being over-read — SUPPORTED.** OPEC prints Saudi July 2026 crude three ways in
   one report: **7,352** tb/d (secondary sources, Table 5-7), **7,420** tb/d ("supply to the market",
   direct communication), and **8,202** tb/d ("production", direct communication). The gap between
   the table leg 6 uses and the highest self-reported figure is **850 tb/d** — larger than the entire
   Iran decline. Leg 6 uses the secondary-source table throughout because that is the series the EIA
   and IEA are also reading, but a reader should know the producer's own high number would narrow
   the gap to the IEA by roughly a sixth of it.

8. **What the September edition will most likely say, and why it is already in the tape —
   SUPPORTED as directional, not as a number.** DoC crude production rose **1.42 mb/d m-o-m in July
   to 37.66 mb/d**, the third consecutive monthly recovery off the **33.30 mb/d** May trough, driven
   by Iraq (+665), Saudi Arabia (+590) and Kuwait (+393). OPEC+ completed the rollback of its 1.65
   mb/d voluntary cuts with a 188 kb/d September increase agreed 2026-08-02 and is widely reported to
   be holding quotas steady through year-end, with the next quota decision at the **2026-09-06**
   ministerial. So the September MOMR should show a fourth month of recovery — a continuation the
   crude curve has been pricing since Brent's move from **89.31 (08-28)** to **96.28 (09-04)**.

9. **The forecast side is where OPEC is genuinely alone, and it is a sign disagreement, not a
   spread — SUPPORTED.** The IEF's own comparative analysis of the three August reports (ief.org,
   published 2026-08-13) puts 2026 demand growth at **OPEC +0.6 mb/d**, **EIA −1.2 mb/d**, **IEA
   −1.6 mb/d** — a **2.2 mb/d** range in which the producer is *on the opposite side of zero from
   both consumer-side agencies*. For 2027 they converge inside 0.2 (OPEC and EIA **2.2**, IEA
   **2.4**). This is the mirror image of leg 6: on the backward-looking production count OPEC agrees
   with the EIA and the IEA is the outlier; on the forward-looking demand forecast OPEC is the
   outlier and the two consumers agree. Both facts belong in any citation of either.

10. **Transmission to our tracked names is two-step, discounted by our own hypothesis, and arrives
    on the noisiest session of the month — SUPPORTED.** `symbols: []`. The only crude path to the
    book is crude → CPI energy / ISM prices-paid → Sep-16 hike odds → real yields → long-duration AI
    names. **FT-12** holds that CPI reactions are core-keyed with energy-driven headline surprises
    discounted, and Waller said the same from the Fed's side on **2026-09-03**. On 2026-09-10 the
    MOMR shares a session with PPI, the ECB decision and press conference, the 30Y bond reopening,
    the 10y20y buyback operation and the coupon announcement — five higher-impact events, four of
    them rate-keyed.

**Limits, honestly.**
- **The date's status is a taxonomy artifact, not a doubt.** The issuing body states it in its own
  PDF and the chain self-verifies 18/18, yet the event must stay `estimate` because no `OPEC:`
  prefix exists in `market-events-data.ts` and this lane may not self-confirm. That friction is
  named here for routing, not fixed in this PR — inventing a prefix changes semantics every other
  lane reads.
- **PDF creation timestamps bound the release; they do not measure it.** Leg 2 proves 09:00 UTC is
  impossible for 11 of 19 editions and that 12:00 UTC is consistent with all 19. It does not prove
  12:00 UTC is the time — a 10:30 or 11:30 UTC release would also fit. The one Reuters wire quoting
  OPEC directly is the only positive evidence for 1200 GMT, and it is a single secondary source
  about a single (2025) edition.
- **The control is not clean.** Natural gas is 1.43× with 3/20 beating p95 in the 12Z hour, where the
  IEA study's control went reassuringly the other way. Macro prints inside the same bar are the
  leading explanation and were **not** enumerated this session, so the contamination is asserted,
  not measured.
- **Leg 6 is arithmetic on a level series, not a shut-in estimate.** OPEC never claims a shortfall;
  this ledger computes one from its table against a baseline this ledger chose. The EIA and IEA
  figures may use different country sets, different baselines and may include condensate or NGLs
  differently. The convergence with the EIA is real and recomputable, but it is a convergence of
  *this* construction with their published number, not of two identically-defined metrics.
- **Only the 10th-of-month is unusual about this edition.** Every one of the 20 sampled releases
  fell on the 11th or later; **10 September 2026 would be the earliest-in-month MOMR in the
  two-year sample.** It is primary-stated, so this is a note, not a doubt — but an unexplained
  one-day pull-forward is exactly the kind of thing a slipped date looks like in advance.
- **September's contents are unknown and this ledger forecasts none of them.** Leg 8 is directional
  only. Every figure quoted here is from the **August** edition.
- **n=20, in a war-distorted sample, with no significance test.** Yahoo's hourly bars cap at ~730
  days, so no pre-conflict MOMR sample was reachable. The tail result is the robust part; a precise
  effect size is not.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (set 2026-09-06, D-4): stand aside on the release; read Table 5-7 the same morning and
cite the Gulf figure as a trio, with the producer's own arithmetic on the EIA's side.** The
2026-09-10 MOMR is **estimate**-dated, carries no tracked symbols, publishes at ~08:00 ET into a
session already owned by PPI, the ECB and two long-end Treasury operations, and across 20 measured
releases produced **0 outliers at p95** on either crude benchmark with a Brent maximum of 1.011%.
Its content value is one recomputable table. No entry, no hedge, no pre-positioning is licensed by
this event, and the **estimate** label forbids date-keyed action independently of all of the above.

**Kill switches** — any of these fires and the stance gets re-argued, not tweaked:

1. **The release window stops being quiet.** CL=F's 12:00–13:00 UTC bar on **2026-09-10** exceeds
   **±1.283%**, or BZ=F's exceeds **±1.322%** (each benchmark's own-hour p95), with the MOMR rather
   than the 08:30 ET PPI identifiable as the cause → the base rate is wrong or regime-dependent and
   leg 3 needs rebuilding before it is cited again. Check: Yahoo hourly bars, 2026-09-10.
2. **The date slips.** No September MOMR published on **2026-09-10** → OPEC's own next-report line
   is not binding, the 18/18 chain stops being a forward guarantee, and every MOMR date this
   calendar carries needs re-derivation from the asset host each month rather than from the chain.
3. **The producer crosses to the IEA's side.** The September edition revises July Middle East DoC
   production such that the 4Q25-baseline shortfall exceeds **7.0 mb/d**, or reports August above
   July's implied shortfall → leg 6's reconciliation dies, the EIA loses its corroboration, and the
   Gulf magnitude question reopens wider than the IEA ledger left it.
4. **The recovery reverses.** Middle East DoC production falls m-o-m for two consecutive editions,
   or DoC crude drops back below **35 mb/d** → leg 8's recovery premise breaks, the crude leg of the
   CPI channel changes sign for the October print, and the transmission question in leg 10 reopens.
5. **The release clock moves.** The September edition's PDF `CreationDate` lands before **09:00
   UTC**, or a primary/wire timestamp puts publication at 09:00 GMT → leg 2 is wrong, the aggregator
   was right, and the attribution rule (MOMR thirty minutes ahead of PPI) has to be rewritten before
   any future MOMR session uses it.
