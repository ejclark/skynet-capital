## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This is the **first non-Fed central bank this calendar tracks**, and it was added on placement
rather than on policy — discovered 2026-09-05 during the
[`treasury-30y-bond-2026-09-10`](treasury-30y-bond-2026-09-10.md) adjacency sweep. So the question
is not "what will the ECB do" (that is settled) but: **is there any channel by which a euro-area
policy decision reaches a US-only, equity-and-earnings-keyed book — and if not, what is this entry
for?**

**One-line verdict:** three things are knowable and none is a trade — the hike is **fully priced
and therefore not information**, the only live question is a **~50bp disagreement about the
terminal rate** that this book has no instrument to express, and the entry's entire practical
value is **attribution on a five-input day** where a US 30Y auction would otherwise be handed
credit for a globally-driven long-end move.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The hike is not news — it is 98.9% priced and unanimously forecast.** SUPPORTED, and it sets
   everything below. The ECB's own key-rates table (fetched today) has the deposit facility at
   **2.25%**, MRO **2.40%**, marginal lending **2.65%**, effective **2026-06-17**; the **23 July
   2026** decision left all three unchanged. Reuters' poll of **65 economists**, surveyed
   **2026-08-31 → 09-03**, was **unanimous** for a 25bp move to **2.50%** on 09-10, and market
   pricing on 2026-09-01 put **98.9%** on it (press-cited). Euronews' 09-01 wrap says the same in
   plainer words: *"Most investors are betting on a 25-basis-point rate hike."* A decision priced at
   ~99% carries no surprise to trade against, in either direction — the tradable content of an event
   is the distance between the outcome and the price, and here that distance is ~1%.

2. **The live question is *terminal*, and market and economists are ~50bp apart — this is the
   finding.** SUPPORTED. Futures on **2026-09-04** priced an **almost-100% probability of a 3.00%
   deposit rate by June 2027**, i.e. **two further hikes** beyond 09-10; the same day's FXStreet
   preview writes that *"market rates already imply a peak deposit rate near 3.00% in 2027"* and
   calls those expectations excessive. The Reuters poll runs the other way and hard: **~91%** of
   economists see the deposit rate still at **2.50% at end-2026** and **78%** through **mid-2027** —
   which Reuters frames as the ECB's **shortest hiking campaign since 2011**. So 09-10 is not a
   decision meeting in any useful sense; it is the first venue at which Lagarde and a fresh
   projection round can adjudicate a 50bp gap that two credible camps hold with confidence. That is
   the only thing on 09-10 with information in it.

3. **The ECB's own record describes a supply shock and moderate wages — which argues for the
   economists' side.** SUPPORTED, primary. The account of the 22–23 July meeting (ecb.europa.eu,
   published **2026-08-27**, fetched today) characterises the energy disruption as supply-side
   throughout, states that the July hold left the Council positioned to *"navigate the current
   uncertainty and respond appropriately to the evolving outlook"*, and names September as *"the
   next opportunity for a comprehensive assessment of the inflation outlook and surrounding
   risks."* On wages it is explicit: *"The ECB wage tracker pointed to moderate wage pressures in
   the near term"*, with negotiated growth averaging **2.6% in 2026 and 2.7% in early 2027** — i.e.
   below anything that would sustain 3% inflation. ING's Carsten Brzeski puts the conclusion
   bluntly: *"it's difficult to envisage the ECB being willing to risk a recession to tackle what is
   still a textbook supply-side shock."* **The counterweight is in the same document and is kept on
   the record:** members also warned *"the energy shock could intensify further and its effects on
   other prices and on wages could be stronger than currently expected."* That sentence is the
   hawkish leg, and it is why leg 2's gap is genuinely two-sided rather than a market error.

4. **The print that forced the hike had a decelerating core inside it.** SUPPORTED, primary.
   Eurostat's flash estimate (**released 2026-09-01**, fetched today): euro-area annual inflation
   **3.3% in August**, up from **2.9%** in July and the highest since September 2023. The
   composition is the story — **energy 14.3%** (from 10.3%), while **services fell to 3.0% from
   3.3%**, non-energy industrial goods 1.2% (from 0.9%), food/alcohol/tobacco 1.2% (stable), and
   **core ex-energy-and-food printed 2.4%, below the 2.5% consensus**. So the single most-cited
   trigger for this hike is a headline driven by an exogenous energy shock, sitting on top of an
   underlying series that *decelerated*. This is exactly the "mixed signals … scenarios on core
   inflation could prove dovish" the 09-04 preview flagged, and it is the material available to
   Lagarde if she wants to lean against the 3.00% strip.

5. **The September projections are the actual news, and June's round is the yardstick.** SUPPORTED.
   The June 2026 Eurosystem staff baseline (fetched today) reads HICP **3.0 / 2.3 / 2.0** for
   2026/27/28, core **2.5 / 2.5 / 2.2**, real GDP **0.8 / 1.2 / 1.5**. The Reuters poll's own
   numbers sit close: 2026 inflation **2.9%**, growth **0.8% / 1.2%**, target reached *"in late
   2027."* That makes one cell decision-relevant and scoreable: **2028 HICP**. June has it at
   exactly **2.0%** — the level at which the ECB is, by its own mandate arithmetic, done. Push it
   above 2.0% on 09-10 and the staff are endorsing the market's two extra hikes; leave it at or
   below and the projection round is the "we are finished" signal, whatever the presser says.
   Registered as **FT-ecb-decision-2026-09-10-1**.

6. **The long-end move that made this event visible is global, not domestic — which is the whole
   reason it earned a calendar slot.** SUPPORTED, press-cited levels. The week of 2026-09-01 was a
   synchronised move, not a US one: **10Y Bund 3.3951%** intraday, highest since **2011**; **30Y
   Bund above 3.84%**, also since 2011; **10Y OAT 4.215%**, highest since **November 2008**; Dutch
   10Y **3.43%** (since May 2011); Spanish 10Y above **3.80%**; **10Y JGB 3.00%**, first time since
   **1996** — against a US 10Y at **~4.78%**. Drivers named across the coverage are the same ones
   this calendar already tracks: oil, energy inflation and hawkish central-bank signalling. The
   retracement is on the record too — euro-area bonds *"snapped a six-day losing streak"* later that
   week as energy eased and markets marginally scaled back ECB hike expectations, and EUR/USD closed
   **1.1627** on 09-04, up from the **USD 1.14** the July account recorded. So the synchronised leg
   is real but already partially unwinding, and a ledger that treats it as a standing regime would
   be over-reading a six-session move — the same discipline the 30Y ledger banked as its refinement
   9 (a sub-week swing is noise until it survives a full week).

7. **Placement is the point: 09-10 is a five-input day and the euro leg lands first.** SUPPORTED
   from this calendar's own entries. On 2026-09-10, in ET order: **PPI 08:30** · **ECB decision
   ~08:15 / presser ~08:45** · **coupon announcement ~11:00** · **$22B 30Y reopening 13:00** ·

   is **not trading the Fed path** across three separate observations (Warsh 8/28, Waller 9/3,
   payrolls 9/4). Put those together and the honest conclusion is narrow but real: **on 09-10 a US
   long-end move is not automatically the auction's verdict**, and this entry exists so the next
   session has to check the euro tape before saying it is.

8. **No tracked symbol carries a euro channel — and no play is proposed.** SUPPORTED, and this is
   where the honesty sits rather than a hedge. `symbols: []`. The house playbooks (S1/S2/E1/S3/S4 +
   G1, [`trade-playbooks.md`](../../plans/trade-playbooks.md)) are equity- and earnings-keyed; none
   is rates-keyed, let alone euro-rates-keyed. The two channels that exist are both second-order and

   [`fomc-2026-09-16`](fomc-2026-09-16.md) already tiers for NVDA/AVGO/MRVL/CRWV. Neither justifies
   a position, and inventing one from a `medium`-tiered `estimate` event would be exactly the

25bp hike to **2.50%** and carries no tradable surprise; this book holds nothing with a euro-rates

**Stance amendment — 2026-09-10, post-decision (receipt: the D-0 ledger row below).** The

*The terminal call is wounded and its confidence drops from Medium to Low.* This ledger sided with
