## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Three questions, in the order they earn their keep. (1) The [10-30 sibling](chicago-pmi-2026-10-30.md)
established that this series is structurally confounded — 09:45 ET on the last business day, the same
morning BEA and BLS use for 08:30 month-end releases — and concluded that waiting for a clean session
waits forever. **Is 2026-11-30 the exception, and is that verifiable rather than assumed?** (2) If it
is, does the clean slot license the session-level read both earlier siblings wanted and could not
have? (3) What else is specific to *November's* instance, versus the September and October ones
already researched in depth?

**One-line verdict:** the exception is real and primary-verified — Thanksgiving displaces the entire
month-end 08:30 block, leaving the Chicago print as the first scheduled macro release of the
2026-11-30 session — but the session-level read it seems to unlock is refused on this document's own
measurement, because the post-Thanksgiving Monday in this exact configuration is an ordinary session
carrying its own unrelated down-skew; the five-minute discipline survives, now with the one thing it
lacked, a session where a null cannot be dismissed as an 08:30 shock drowning it out.

   rather than rhetorical. Across 6,286 ^GSPC sessions (2001-09-06 → 2026-09-04), the unconditional

### Honest limits

**The clean-slot finding is about what is *scheduled*, not about what will *happen*.** A geopolitical
shock, an unscheduled Treasury or fiscal headline, or a CR-expiry story (12-11) can own any session,
and the 12-09 FOMC is nine days later — pre-positioning is not on a published calendar. The BLS grid
view's JOLTS contradiction is recorded, not resolved: two of three primaries put the release on
12-01, and if the grid view is the correct one then 11-30 carries a 10:00 release, 15 minutes after
the measurement window closes — which changes the *session* read materially and the *window* read not
at all. The n=17 seasonal subset spans 2001-2023 configurations of a survey whose methodology and
release mechanics have changed over that span, its baseline is the full 25-year distribution rather
than a contemporaneous one, and the sessions in it are uncontrolled for what else happened; it is
weak corroboration, not a test. The carried 5-minute percentile table is the 10-30 sibling's
measurement, not this document's, and its binding limit (**n = 3**) is inherited whole. Beyond that:
the date is `estimate` and cannot be promoted from inside this lane; no November consensus exists at
D-86 and none is expected before late November; the 68% ISM and 60% GDP correlations remain
secondary-sourced summary statistics; Order Backlog was not separately reported in August, so one of
the five weighted subcomponents is missing from any subcomponent read; and the entire November base
case is downstream of two prints that have not happened.
