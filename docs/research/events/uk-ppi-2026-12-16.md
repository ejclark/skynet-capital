## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Two proposals put this print on the calendar as "the last UK price data before the
December vote". The placement is right and the relevance is decorative.** The Bank's own December
2025 document is the exact analogue — a meeting **ending 17 December 2025**, the morning ONS
published both UK CPI *and* UK PPI at 07:00. Its summary opens **"CPI inflation has fallen since the
previous meeting, to 3.2%"** — that morning's CPI number — and the words *producer price*, *PPI*,
*input prices*, *output prices* and *factory gate* appear **zero times**. Same for the June 2026 and
July 2026 minutes, all three fetched today. **The other correction is to a sibling ledger:
16 December is goods-only.** `uk-ppi-2026-11-18` tells readers UK services producer prices publish
"on 10-21 and 12-16"; the publisher's own page for *"December 2026 including services, October to
December 2026"* reads **"Release date: 20 January 2027 7:00am"**. **What is genuinely new here is the
base.** The 18 November print rides three flat base months (Aug/Sep/Oct 2025 input m/m
**0.0 / 0.0 / −0.1%**). November 2025 ran **+0.3%** (GHIP 156.6 → **157.1**) — the first base month
that pushes back. At the effective oil beta this index actually shows — **~9.2%**, a third larger

**ticks down** (~8.7% → ~8.3%) while the level stays on an 8-handle. **None of it is ours to trade** —

### Leg 2 — "This is the print that lands inside the MPC's vote." **SUPPORTED as placement. REFUTED as relevance — and the refutation is a natural experiment, not an argument.**

**The placement is exact.** The BoE's upcoming-MPC-dates page (fetched) gives **"Thursday 17
December"**, *"MPC Summary and minutes"* only — **no Monetary Policy Report**, the thinnest document
set of the four non-MPR meetings' worth of 2026. The minutes convention the CPI lane established
([`uk-cpi-2026-12-16`](uk-cpi-2026-12-16.md)) puts the announcement the day *after* the meeting ends,
so a 17 December announcement follows a meeting **ending 16 December** — the morning this prints.

**Then the question nobody asked: does the MPC read producer prices?** Three Monetary Policy Summary
and minutes documents were fetched today and searched in full for *producer price*, *producer
prices*, *PPI*, *input prices*, *output prices* and *factory gate*:

| Document | Meeting ending | Producer-price terms found |
|---|---|---|
| December 2025 | 17 December 2025 | **none** |
| June 2026 | 18 June 2026 | **none** |
| July 2026 | 29 July 2026 | **none** |

The June 2026 document's nearest miss is *"some indirect effects through higher **input costs for
firms**"* — a narrative phrase, not a citation of the index.

**And the December 2025 row is not one document among three; it is this event's exact analogue.** The
MPC met to 17 December 2025. ONS published UK CPI for November 2025 at 07:00 that morning
(bulletin fetched: *"The Consumer Prices Index rose by 3.2% in the 12 months to November 2025"*) and
UK PPI for November 2025 in the same minute (previous-releases listing: *"November 2025 — Released 17

> "At its meeting ending on 17 December 2025, the Monetary Policy Committee voted by a majority of
> 5–4 to reduce Bank Rate by 0.25 percentage points, to 3.75%."
>
> "CPI inflation has fallen since the previous meeting, to 3.2%."

**The Committee quoted the CPI print published that morning and never mentioned the PPI print
published in the same minute.** One year earlier, same configuration, same two releases, same
meeting-end day. That is as close to a controlled test as this calendar gets, and it says the
"last data before the vote" framing both proposals lean on is real for the CPI half of the 07:00 slot
and decorative for this half.

Registered as **FT-uk-ppi-2026-12-16-1**, which scores first on **17 September 2026** — the same

### Leg 3 — "It carries the cleanest read on the electricity VAT cut." **REFUTED, and this is the second lane to refute it.**

The CPI proposal's mechanism: *"the zero rate covers DOMESTIC supply only, so business electricity
keeps its VAT and PPI input costs see the Ofgem cap without the tax cut — the counterfactual CPI
cannot show."* Both halves fail, on primaries re-fetched today rather than inherited:

**VAT is not in this index in either direction.** The ONS *Producer Price Indices QMI* (fetched
2026-09-09, last revised 22 October 2025), verbatim: **"All index numbers exclude Value Added Tax
(VAT)."** SI 2026/987 cannot appear here — not "appears asymmetrically", **absent by measurement
rule**. A counterfactual requires the index to carry the tax on one side; it carries it on neither.

**The Ofgem cap does not reach it either.** The July 2026 bulletin's *Data sources and quality*
section describes the energy inputs as *"a panel of suppliers relating to the supply of gas and
electricity to **industrial and commercial end-users**"*, data that *"differ from the data provided
for the purposes of the Consumer Prices Index"*. The cap is set under the ***Domestic*** Gas and
Electricity (Tariff Cap) Act.

The sibling ledger reached the same two conclusions on 11-18 from the same sources. This is recorded
as a **repeat** refutation, not a new one: the CPI lane's proposal for 12-16 was written before the
11-18 research landed, so the same wrong mechanism was filed twice on the same day by two lanes. The
useful output is a rule rather than a rebuttal — **any UK energy-tax or price-cap story routed to a
PPI ledger is misrouted twice over**, and belongs on the CPI sibling.

### Leg 4 — "Its collection date is on or near 1 December 2026." **REFUTED — the QMI rule keys off the reference month, not the release month.**

The PPI proposal wrote that this is *"the first print whose collection date, 'on or near' 1 December
2026 per the ONS PPI QMI, sits clear of the 1 October Ofgem step."* The QMI rule, verbatim:

> "All businesses included in the survey are asked to provide the price data for **transactions that
> occur on or near to the first of each month**."

The reference month of the 16 December release is **November 2026** (the release page title says so).
So the index prices **on or near 1 November 2026** and publishes **16 December 2026** — a **45-day**
lag, one month earlier than the proposal assumed. The conclusion the proposal drew from its date
(clear of the 1 October step) happens to survive; the date itself does not, and it is the date that
matters, because it is the *forecasting input*. Anyone modelling this print from 1-December Brent is
modelling the wrong month — and the December reference month is a different release entirely
(20 January 2027, proposed here).

### Leg 6 — "The re-acceleration keeps running through this print." **MIXED — the level does, the annual rate probably does not, and the reason is the base.**

This is the arithmetic that is genuinely this event's rather than the sibling's.

**The base stops being flat, exactly here.** From the ONS November 2025 bulletin (fetched) and the
July 2026 bulletin, input index (GHIP, 2015=100):

| | Aug-2025 | Sep-2025 | Oct-2025 | **Nov-2025** | Jul-2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Input index | 156.8 | 156.8 | 156.6 | **157.1** | 164.5 |
| m/m | **0.0%** | **0.0%** | **−0.1%** | **+0.3%** | +4.9% y/y |

The 18 November print rides three base months averaging **−0.03%**, which is why its annual rate
moves nearly one-for-one with the 2026 path. **November 2025 is the first base month that pushes
back**, by 0.3pp.

**And the effective oil beta is larger than the basket weight — which the sibling's arithmetic
understates.** The July 2026 bulletin tabulates two months of product-group detail. Against the input
index's own monthly change:

| Month | Crude group m/m | Input index m/m | Implied beta |
|---|---|---|---|
| June 2026 | −20.9% | −1.9% | **0.091** |
| July 2026 | −18.0% | −1.7% | **0.094** |

**~9.2%**, not the 6.9% basket weight — a third larger, and consistent across both months. The
excess is other input groups moving with oil (the bulletin attributes plastics, PVC and resin price
moves to the same driver). Modelling this index as `6.9% × crude` and calling the remainder a
residual gives a residual of exactly **−0.46pp** in both months, which is not a residual; it is the
missing beta.

**The step, therefore, is exact arithmetic.** Δ(annual rate, December print − November print) =
(Nov-2026 m/m) − (Nov-2025 m/m) = (Nov-2026 m/m) − **0.3pp**. With m/m ≈ 0.092 × crude-group m/m:

- The annual rate **rises** above the 18 November print only if first-week-November Brent in
  sterling gains **> +3.3%** on first-week-October.
- It rises by **more than 0.5pp** only on a crude-group month above **+8.7%**.

**Level base case.** First-week Brent in GBP (computed today from Yahoo `BZ=F` × `GBPUSD=X`,
averaging the first seven calendar days): Jun-2026 **£71.38**, Jul **£54.25**, Aug **£60.42**
(+11.4%), Sep MTD **£70.52** (+16.7%, three observations). Spot on 2026-09-09 is **$101.24** at
**1.3555**, i.e. **£74.69**. Carrying the July→November cumulative at the 9.2% beta:

| 1-Nov-2026 Brent (GBPUSD 1.3555) | Jul→Nov cumulative, GBP | Input index | **Annual rate** |
|---|---|---|---|
| $85 | +15.6% | 166.9 | **+6.2%** |
| $95 | +29.2% | 168.9 | **+7.5%** |
| **$101 (today's spot)** | **+37.7%** | **170.2** | **+8.3%** |
| $115 | +56.4% | 173.0 | **+10.2%** |

**Base case: input 6.0–10.0% y/y, centred ~8.3%**, against 4.9% in July. On the same method the
18 November print comes in at **~8.7%** (same numerator, the 156.6 base) — so the flat-Brent path has
the annual rate **ticking down ~0.35pp between the two prints while the level stays on an 8-handle.**
That is the call, and it is the opposite of what a reader carrying the sibling's re-acceleration
headline forward would expect.

**Output.** Nov-2025 output index **144.6** (+0.1% m/m). July 2026 output 148.4 (+3.1% y/y, +0.2%
m/m) with coke and refined petroleum **−2.9%** at a **4.5%** weight, so ex-refined drift ran
**+0.33pp**. Refined petroleum passed **16–33%** of crude's move in the two detailed months. Carrying
both: **output 3.8–5.0%, centred ~4.4%. The input−output gap widens to ~3.9pp** from 1.8pp in July —
an imported cost shock passed through only partially, which is the sibling's thesis continuing rather
than a new one.

### Honest limits

- **Three of the four months in the base case have not printed.** August (2026-09-16), September
  (2026-10-21) and October (2026-11-18) are forecast from Brent, and **November's pricing day —
  1 November 2026 — is 53 days out.** This is a materially weaker forecasting position than the
  sibling had at the same distance, and the band is wide on purpose.
- **The 9.2% beta is fitted on two observations**, the only two months of product-group detail the
  July bulletin tabulates. Two points that agree are not a coefficient; they are a reason to stop
  using 6.9%.
- **First-week Brent does not track the crude group month-by-month.** Jun: wk1 −9.0% vs ONS −20.9%;
  Jul: wk1 −24.0% vs ONS −18.0%. Over the two months together (−33.0% vs −38.9%) they roughly agree,
  so the *cumulative* path used above is on firmer ground than any single month — which is also why
  the 3.3% single-month threshold in Leg 6 is the least reliable number in this document, even though
  it is the sharpest.
- **September's first-week Brent is three observations**, 1–9 September, and moves with each day.
- **The crude group is not pure crude** — "crude petroleum and natural gas; metal ores" bundles gas
  and metal ores, so applying a Brent move to the whole group is an approximation.
- **The base is itself revisable, and has already moved.** The November 2025 bulletin puts Oct-2025
  input m/m at **0.0%** and output at **144.4**; the July 2026 bulletin puts them at **−0.1%** and
  **144.5**. The QMI states figures for the latest two months are provisional and the latest 12 are
  revisable, and the oil and energy first estimate is partly *"the suppliers' estimate"* revised
  "after one to two months". No threshold here should be read to a tenth.
