2. **"The keynote is the pricing event."** REFUTED as framed — the *guide* is. What's staged Sept 9
   is the iPhone 18 Pro / Pro Max plus Apple's first foldable ("iPhone Ultra"); the standard iPhone
   18 / 18e / Air 2 slip to spring 2027 (MacRumors iPhone 18 release schedule, checked 2026-08-19).

   2026-07-31 / TrendForce 2026-08-10) modeling **+$250–300** on higher 2nm silicon and DRAM/NAND

   into the **FQ1 (launch-quarter) guide** at the est. Oct-29 print, which also inherits ~$1.1B of
   tariff cost without the ~$2.19B refund that flattered the July beat (per the earnings ledger).
3. **"Apple September events move the stock (a tradable pre/post edge)."** REFUTED. The reveal is
   the canonical **buy-the-rumor / sell-the-news**: pre-leaked features leave little surprise, and

not, since the underlying positioning playbooks are killed on AAPL).** Watch-only, no position, no
play proposed. Base case (estimate): Apple confirms the Sept-9 keynote via a ~Aug-26 invitation,

AAPL entry; the only live AAPL leg (FT-5 post-print drift) keys off the earnings print, not the

**Kill switches.**

- **The date leg** upgrades toward confirmed the moment Apple posts its official event invitation
  (expected ~Aug 26) → propose the calendar flip (per the honesty rules; not edited here) and
  re-band the cadence; a date materially different from Sept 9 re-keys this doc.
- **The sell-the-news read** dies if the announced pricing/foldable surprises hard versus the leaked
  rumors (a genuinely undisclosed feature or a materially higher/lower price) → the event carries
  real surprise for once, and the FQ1-guide framing tightens; still not a pre-event entry, since the
  edge would be unmeasured.
- **The "no direct play on the nine" read** dies only if evidence clears the sweep's stated bar (≥3
  new prints repricing a killed AAPL hypothesis) — nothing in a product keynote meets it.
- **The watch-only stance** escalates to an Eric-facing item only if the price ladder is a genuine
  taste/thesis fork for the sleeve's memory-cost read — never on a sell-the-news keynote alone.
