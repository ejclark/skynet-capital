## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** what is the Primary Dealer Meeting Agenda, does its publication move
anything, does it in fact lead the refunding statement the way this calendar entry claims, and what
should the 2026-10-16 edition be read for?

**One-line verdict:** a **measured non-event as a price date** (TLT p=0.907, SPY p=0.473 across all 15
releases since 2023) that is nonetheless a real **information date** — but its information is
*confirmatory*, not early-warning: the agenda restates issuance changes Treasury has already flagged in
a prior refunding statement (1/1 on the 2023 coupon increase) and gave no advance tell on the one
guidance change that arrived unflagged (0/1 on 2025-11-05).

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. What the release actually is — SUPPORTED, from the documents.** At **12:00 PM ET** on a Friday
about three weeks before each quarterly refunding, Treasury publishes two items: the **Primary Dealer
Meeting Agenda** and the **Quarterly Release Data**. The agenda is a questionnaire (OMB control no.
1505-0261, ~2 hours' reporting burden per response) that every primary dealer fills in and returns
before meeting Treasury's Office of Debt Management. Its structure is fixed across all 15 editions
examined: **Part I** privately-held marketable borrowing estimates (quarter, FY, FY+1, FY+2, each with
a central estimate *and* a "range that would not surprise you"); **Part II** economic estimates (deficit,
real/nominal GDP, headline/core CPI, unemployment); **Part III** note and bond issuance estimates
tenor-by-tenor; **Part IV** bill issuance estimates including CMBs — then **Discussion Topics**, which
are the only variable content, and in May/November editions a **"Part 2"** auction-size expectations
table. The dealers' aggregated *answers* to Part 2 are published separately as the **Primary Dealer
Auction Size Survey**, which exists for every 2nd and 4th quarter on Treasury's archive back to 2015.

agenda is released on the **Friday 19 days before the refunding Wednesday**. Tested against the sibling
ledger's list of the 15 refunding announcements since 2023-02-01, the rule generates a Friday every
time, and each generated Friday is corroborated by the corresponding PDF's embedded creation timestamp
landing 0–2 days earlier:

- **2023-08-02 — the one edition that actually raised coupon sizes. Agenda gave the tell (1/1).** The

  projected intermediate- to long-term borrowing needs, Treasury may need to modestly increase coupon

  public a quarter earlier. The agenda confirmed a live signal; it did not originate one.
- **2025-11-05 — the one edition whose guidance language moved unflagged. Agenda gave nothing (0/1).**

  **Not one word about coupon sizes.**

coupon auction sizes, and it is the pre-signalled Aug-2023 edition. So the honest restatement: *the
agenda is a confirming instrument for issuance changes Treasury has already put in a statement, and it
has never yet front-run one.* Registered as **FT-44**, because the asymmetry is what makes 10-16 worth

### What the conditions support

Nothing directional, and no house playbook is macro-keyed (S1/S2/E1/S3/S4 + G1 are symbol/earnings-keyed).
What travels is a sharpening of the discipline the sibling refunding ledger banked. That ledger's lesson
was *compute an event's reaction function against its collision set, not its date list*. This one adds
the complement: **when a scheduled document has no measurable reaction function, the useful question is
not "does it move the tape" but "what does it let you observe earlier than the thing that does" — and
that lead must itself be scored against the record, not assumed from the calendar position.** Here the
assumption was 19 days of lead time on the refunding statement; the record says the agenda has led
zero times out of one and confirmed one out of one. A date can be worth keeping while its stated reason
for being kept is wrong.

### Honest limits

- **The 15 release dates are derived, not published.** Treasury names only the *next* agenda date on its
  most-recent-documents page; there is no archive of past release dates. The W−19 rule is validated by
  15/15 internal consistency plus PDF creation timestamps and one primary-confirmed anchor (2026-07-17),
  but a creation timestamp is a proxy for a publication date, not the date itself. If Treasury ever
  released an agenda off-rule, leg 3 measured the wrong session for that edition. Feb-2024 is the one
  case where the timestamp sits a full week early and is the most likely candidate.
- **n=15 with a daily TLT σ of 0.895% has limited power.** p=0.907 means "not distinguishable from an
  ordinary session," never "proven zero." Fifteen observations have roughly 80% power against a mean
  effect near ±0.65%; a real effect of 0.2% would be missed. The supported claim is that no *large*
  agenda-release effect exists.
- **Close-to-close only, and this release is at noon.** Unlike an 08:30 print, the agenda lands
  mid-session, so a close-to-close return does cover the window — but it also covers the five hours
  either side of it, and on the five opex dates in the set that window is dominated by expiry flow.
- **Leg 4 is n=1 and n=1.** One pre-signalled coupon change with a tell, one unsignalled guidance change
  without. That is a hypothesis with a mechanism, not a base rate, which is why it is registered as
  FT-44 rather than asserted. It is also the reason FT-44 is framed so that only a *fail* is informative.
- **Leg 5's topic forecast is not measured.** The three open threads are read off Treasury's own
  documents, but which of them makes the 10-16 agenda is a judgment, and Treasury has introduced
  genuinely novel topics before (stablecoin reserve demand, May-2025; SOFR-linked FRNs, Feb-2026).
  Only the Part 2 half of the leg is registered as a forward test.
- **The event's own date is now `confirmed`, but its whole reason for mattering runs through
  `treasury-refunding-2026-11-04`, which is still `estimate`.** Nothing downstream of 10-16 licenses a
  date-keyed action while that remains true.
