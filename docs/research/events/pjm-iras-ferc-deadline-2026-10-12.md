### Legs tested

1. **"The requested effective date is 2026-10-12."** SUPPORTED, and better sourced than the
   proposal had it. **Two independent Federal Register publications** carry the identical entry:
   FR-2026-08-18 notice **2026-16808** (Combined Notice of Filings #2) and FR-2026-08-19 notice
   **2026-16927** (#1), both read verbatim as "PJM Interconnection, L.L.C.", docket
   **ER26-3515-000**, "205(d) Rate Filing: Proposal to Establish Interim Resource Adequacy Service &
   a Large Load Registry to be effective 10/12/2026", filed **8/13/26**, accession **20260813-5118**,
   comments due **5 p.m. ET 9/3/26**. 8/13 + 60 days = 10/12 exactly — the FPA §205 statutory notice
   period, not a date PJM chose freely. Status stays **estimate**: `market-events-data.ts`'s
   confirmed-prefix taxonomy has no FERC member (`PJM:` is scoped to "pjm.com auction schedule"),
   and a requested date on a filing two parties asked FERC to reject is not a confirmed date.

2. **"2026-10-12 is when FERC has to have acted by."** REFUTED as stated — **the operative deadline
   is Friday 2026-10-09**. 2026-10-12 is the second Monday of October: **Columbus Day**, a federal
   holiday. The Federal Reserve's own K.8 schedule ("Holidays Observed by the Federal Reserve System
   2026-2030") carries the row `Columbus Day | October 12 | …` for 2026, read and quoted in this
   calendar's own [`sifma-bond-market-closure-2026-10-12`](sifma-bond-market-closure-2026-10-12.md)
   entry from a direct fetch on 2026-09-05. FERC's offices are closed on federal holidays, so the
   last day the Commission can reject, suspend or set the filing for hearing is **2026-10-09**.
   *Stated as this lane's inference from the holiday calendar, not a claim any source makes.* The
   practical consequence for the next three pulses: an order landing on **10-09** is on schedule, not
   a last-minute save, and silence through the **10-09** close is the tell — not silence on 10-12.

3. **"No FERC order found ⇒ the filing is at risk."** REFUTED as an inference, same structure as the
   sibling docket. The **federalregister.gov API** (`term=ER26-3515`, `publication_date >= 2026-08-01`,
   queried 2026-09-15) returns **count 2** — the two filing notices in Leg 1 and **nothing else**: no
   order, no deficiency letter, no suspension, no acceptance. Under the FPA §205 default a 205(d)
   rate filing takes effect on its requested date unless the Commission acts, so as of 2026-09-15 the
   **absence of an order is the modal path to the tariff taking effect**, not evidence against it.
   Two honest counterweights, and they are heavier here than on ER26-3380. First, **this filing is
   contested by the parties whose protests FERC weighs most**: PJM's own Independent Market Monitor
   and EPSA, both dated **2026-09-03**. Second, **FERC has an open §206 of its own on exactly this
   subject** (Leg 5), which is a Commission that has already said the status quo appears unjust and
   unreasonable — a posture that invites a substantive order rather than silent acquiescence.

4. **"The open-meeting channel is closed, so only a delegated-authority letter can arrive."**
   MIXED — the factual half holds, the inference **overstates**, and this corrects the sibling
   ledger. *Holds:* the federalregister.gov API returns exactly **one** FERC Sunshine Act notice
   published since 2026-09-01 — document **2026-18361**, the **2026-09-10** meeting the sibling read
   in full and found carried **no PJM item** — and FERC's announced 2026 schedule puts the October
   open meeting on **2026-10-15**, after the effective date (search-relayed from ferc.gov's own
   schedule announcement; the page 403'd to this runner and is in `probe-ref.blocked`). *Overstates:*
   FERC's own commission-meeting FAQ states that **Commissioners vote on proceedings on most FERC
   business days and not only at Commission Meetings** — notational voting — so a **Commission-level**
   order in ER26-3515 needs no agenda slot at all. (The FAQ page itself 403'd; this is search-relayed
   from ferc.gov's text, and FERC publishes dated "Notational Orders" pages as routine practice.) The
   correct narrowing is therefore weaker than the sibling's: the October meeting falling after 10-12
   removes one *ceremonial* channel, not the Commission's ability to act.

5. **"IRAS is PJM's answer to FERC's large-load show cause."** REFUTED as a causal claim, though the
   two are plainly the same policy fight. On **2026-06-18** FERC issued six contemporaneous FPA §206
   orders to show cause, one per RTO/ISO; the PJM order is **195 FERC ¶ 61,211**, Docket **EL26-67**,
   and gave PJM **60 days** to justify its tariff or propose revisions. PJM filed its show-cause
   response in EL26-67 on **2026-08-17** — a *separate* filing from the 08-13 IRAS 205(d). So the
   timing proximity (four days) is coincidence of a shared clock, not evidence that ER26-3515 *is*
   the response. What survives, and it matters: the IMM's protest cites the show-cause order's own
   preliminary finding against PJM's tariff as the standard IRAS fails, and asks FERC to issue a
   §206 finding rather than accept the filing — which is a request FERC has already shown itself
   willing to entertain in this exact subject area.

6. **"What IRAS actually does."** SUPPORTED, consistently across two independent trade sources
   (POWER Magazine 2026-08-20; Data Center Knowledge, both fetched 2026-09-15). A **New Large Load**
   is end-use demand with a cumulative peak of **at least 50 MW at a single electrical site**, with
   affiliated facilities inside a **one-mile** radius aggregating. Beginning **2027-06-01**, a New
   Large Load that has not **Brought Its Own New Capacity** (BYONC — capacity that genuinely adds
   supply, not "repackaged") takes IRAS on the uncovered portion of its demand, and PJM curtails it
   **before calling Pre-Emergency Load Management Reductions** — i.e. data centers move *earlier* in
   the emergency sequence than existing demand response. The wholesale rate for actual reductions is
   capped at **50% of PJM's Non-Performance Charge Rate**, with retail-level funding left to state
   regulators. From the **2029/30 delivery year**, uncovered incremental new large-load demand is
   **excluded from the capacity-auction demand curve** until qualifying capacity is procured. The
   filing anticipates roughly **70 GW** of new large-load demand by 2038. Stakeholder support was
   absent: **none of 11 IRAS concepts cleared the required two-thirds** in PJM's June 2026 advisory
   voting, and the Board directed the filing anyway.

7. **"The protests are procedural noise."** REFUTED — and this leg is the one the proposing ledger
   could not run, because the IMM PDF would not parse for it. Decompressed and read this session
   (`monitoringanalytics.com/filings/2026/IMM_Protest_Docket_No_ER26-3515_20260903.pdf`, 585,454
   bytes, md5 `4b641619907352dd3071b472efb2b48f`, 137 streams inflated, 94,847 characters of text),
   the Market Monitor's protest is a demand for **outright rejection plus affirmative relief**:
   *"The IRAS Filing has not been supported as just and reasonable, and it should be rejected"*;
   *"a convoluted, inconsistent, confusing proposal that PJM justifies based on a misreading of
   jurisdictional responsibilities and under which PJM denies responsibility for reliability and
   assigns key undefined and inappropriate roles to the states"*; and a request that *"an order
   should issue, finding that the existing rules relating to resource adequacy for serving data
   centers in the PJM Region are unjust and unreasonable under Section 206"*. Its sharpest
   substantive charge is that IRAS **does not actually remove data-center load from the VRR curve**
   and **creates an incentive not to bring capacity** — because the obligation lapses if the system
   is not short for three years. It also flags an inconsistency between IRAS and the RBP filing "in
   significant ways including the obligation to bring new capacity and the definition of acceptable
   new capacity." **EPSA**, same date, attacks from the opposite side: BYONC is *"unduly
   discriminatory and preferential"* because it bars **existing** capacity from competing to serve
   new large loads, and would *"distort capacity market price signals, disadvantage existing
   resources, increase costs to consumers over time, and undermine investor confidence in PJM's
   market design."* Both the buy side of capacity and the sell side of capacity want this rejected,
   for opposite reasons — which is the strongest single argument that FERC does *something* rather
   than nothing. It is not, however, an argument that anything is **published on 10-12**.

8. **"Do the tracked names carry sensitivity?"** MIXED — genuinely more than the RBP does, and still
   not on this date. IRAS is the **first PJM intervention that charges the demand side**, and the
   demand side is where this calendar's roster lives: **MSFT, META, GOOG and AMZN** operate PJM
   data-center load, and **CRWV** is the most levered of the nine (a PJM-footprint neocloud with no
   generation portfolio and a leveraged balance sheet). A ≥50 MW campus without BYONC is curtailed
   ahead of everyone else's demand response — a real cost of capital and a real siting input. But the
   clock is **2027-06-01**, the demand-curve change is **2029/30**, and 10-12 switches on tariff
   language, not money. **The tape says the same thing louder.** Adjusted closes, caches busted,
   **2026-09-08 → 2026-09-15**: the supply sleeve broke — **CRWV 99.83 → 81.32 (−18.5%)**,
   **TLN 325.77 → 285.42 (−12.4%)**, **CEG 299.05 → 262.27 (−12.3%)**, **NRG 119.64 → 107.04
   (−10.5%)**, **VST 151.72 → 141.91 (−6.5%)** — while the four hyperscalers IRAS would actually
   charge went **META 613.48 → 666.56 (+8.7%)**, **GOOG 335.38 → 339.71 (+1.3%)**, **MSFT 493.95 →
   499.04 (+1.0%)**, **AMZN 256.97 → 248.52 (−3.3%)** (NVDA 225.48 → 211.82, −6.1%). A market pricing

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date estimate — no date-keyed action is licensed regardless).** **Stand aside.** No action
on any tracked name and none contemplated. Base case (estimate): **FERC takes no action rejecting,
suspending or setting ER26-3515 for hearing by the close of business Friday 2026-10-09, and the
tariff revisions take effect 2026-10-12 by operation of the FPA §205(d) default** — a non-event for
the tape, on a day the bond market is shut and the IMF/World Bank meetings open. The base case is
held at **lower confidence than the sibling docket's**, and deliberately so: ER26-3515 drew
rejection demands from both PJM's own Market Monitor and EPSA, and it lands while FERC runs its own
§206 show-cause (EL26-67) preliminarily finding PJM's large-load tariff unjust and unreasonable.
A Commission in that posture has both the appetite and — via notational voting — the means to issue
a substantive order without an agenda slot.

**Two corrections to the proposing ledger, both landed here** (estimate). (1) Its framing that "the
only route left is a **delegated-authority letter**" **overstates** the narrowing on this docket:
FERC votes notationally on most business days, so a Commission-level order needs no open meeting, and
the October meeting falling after 10-12 removes a ceremonial channel rather than the Commission's
capacity to act. (2) The IMM protest it recorded as `PDF_UNPARSED` and read only through search relay
is now **read in full** — it seeks outright rejection **plus** a new FPA §206 finding, and its
substantive charge (IRAS does not actually remove data-center load from the VRR curve, and creates an
incentive *not* to bring capacity) is sharper than "a protest was filed" conveyed.

**One thing this ledger adds that no source states:** the deadline is **2026-10-09**, not 2026-10-12.
Columbus Day closes FERC on the requested effective date itself. Every subsequent pulse on this event
should read silence-through-10-09 as the tell, and should not treat an order issued on 10-09 as a
surprise save.

**On the AI-energy sleeve.** VST's Tier-2 gate in [`ai-energy-constraint.md`](../ai-energy-constraint.md)
stays **closed** on this event's account: IRAS charges the demand side, sets no clearing price, and is
not the RPM cap sunsetting that the gate names. NRG's and CEG's conditions are likewise untouched. The
one channel worth re-reading if FERC *accepts* IRAS substantially as filed is second-order and slow —
a curtailment-first regime for uncovered new load raises the value of a BYONC contract, which is a
mild tailwind to long-dated IPP PPA pricing on a 2027-06-01 clock, not a 2026 re-rate.
