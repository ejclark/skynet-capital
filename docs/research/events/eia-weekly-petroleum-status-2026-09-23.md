reporting the first full data week of the Saudi Petroline outage, 45 minutes after the flash-PMI
window closes. What is likely in it, does it move anything, and is there anything here for us to do?

**One-line verdict:** nothing to do — but this is the one weekly print in the sequence that can
actually test whether a global supply shock pulls barrels out of the US, and the arb says it does
not.

### Leg 2 — does a global supply shock pull barrels out of the US? REFUTED so far, and this is the leg that justifies the ledger

This is the question this particular print is positioned to answer, and neither sibling could ask it:
**09-23 reports the week ending 09-18, the first full data week after the 2026-09-10 Petroline
shutdown.**

**The intuition says exports surge.** Saudi Arabia lost the pipeline that moves crude to Yanbu
*around* Hormuz — press-reported at **4+ mb/d of export capacity at risk** (`NEWS:` Al Jazeera
2026-09-14) — and cancelled September-loading European cargoes. European refiners short a grade
bid the replacement barrel, and the US Gulf Coast is the deepest pool of it.

**The tape says the opposite, and it is measurable.** The Brent–WTI front-month spread is the price
of exactly that trip:

| Window | Mean Brent–WTI (front month) |
|---|---|
| Six months to 2026-09-16 | **$5.38** |
| Pre-attack (through 2026-09-09) | **$5.42** |
| Post-attack (2026-09-10 →) | **$4.31** |
| Latest session (2026-09-16 overnight, 22:48 ET 09-15) | **$3.26** |

*(Front-month `BZ=F` minus `CL=F`, Yahoo daily closes, 2,690 aligned sessions from 2016-01-04.
Brent rose; WTI rose more. The arb that pays a cargo to sail narrowed by roughly two dollars in the
four sessions after the attack.)*

**And the weekly flow data already showed it, one week early.** In the week ending 09-04 (EIA Table 9,
fetched direct): crude **exports fell to 3,417 kb/d from 4,483**, the four-week average **3,940**,
while crude **net imports jumped to 3,407 from 2,287**. That mix builds US crude; it does not draw it.

**So the finding is that the shock is, at the US flow level, a stock-builder — but the timing matters
and this is where the ledger refuses to over-claim.** The spread's lead over exports was measured
across lags (EIA `wcrexus2` weekly exports against the week-mean futures spread):

| Lag | corr (2021+, n≈295) | corr (2023+, n≈190) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 weeks | +0.274 | +0.103 |
| 1 week | +0.217 | +0.098 |
| 2 weeks | +0.336 | +0.177 |
| **3 weeks** | **+0.417** | **+0.274** |
| 4 weeks | +0.403 | +0.295 |

Terciles on the 2021+ sample: narrow (≤$3.43) → median exports three weeks later **3,498 kb/d**; mid
→ **3,876**; wide (≥$4.38) → **4,177**.

**At a three-week lead, this print's week (ending 09-18) keys back to the week ending 08-28 — a wide
$6.21.** So the honest call for *this* print is that **exports recover** toward the four-week
average, not that they collapse; that is `FT-…-2`. The **$3.26** compression is a narrow-tercile
reading whose effect lands on the **early-October** prints — a dated finding handed to the 09-30 and
later lanes rather than dressed up as a claim about a week it cannot reach.

**The honest weight of this.** The peak correlation is **+0.417**, about **17% of variance** — a real
but modest effect, and the 2023+ subsample halves it. The causal direction is arguable both ways (a
wide spread pulls exports; heavy exports can themselves widen Cushing's discount). Both limits are
priced into `FT-…-2`'s **medium** grade and into a threshold set at the *last print* rather than at a
forecast level.

### Leg 3 — how much turnaround shows up by 09-18? SUPPORTED on direction, REFUTED on the sibling's level

**2026-09-23 reports the week ending 09-18 — 14 days past the week ending 09-04.** The 09-30 sibling
ran this study at the **+21-day** offset and found a strong sub-96.0 case. Re-run at **+14 days**
(same method: for every year 1993–2025, base = the September week nearest the 4th, target = the week
exactly 14 days later), the picture changes in a specific and useful way:

| Cohort | n | Declined | Median Δ | Target < 96.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All years 1993–2025 | 33 | 23 | −1.40 | 30 |
| Base ≥ 95.0 | 11 | 9 | −2.70 | 8 |
| Base ≥ 96.0 | 6 | **6** | −2.70 | 4 |
| **Base ≥ 97.0** (the 2026 analogs) | **3** | **3** | **−2.40** | **1** |

The three high-base analogs at +14d: **1997 (99.4 → 98.5)**, **1998 (98.4 → 96.0)**, **2018 (97.6 →
90.4)**. Against the same three at +21d — **98.1, 94.8, 90.4**, median **−3.60**, two under 96.0.

**The reading.** The *decline* is the robust part: 6 of 6 at base ≥96.0, 3 of 3 at ≥97.0, and the
mechanism is stated by the publisher itself (September 2026 STEO, quoted by the 09-30 sibling:
*"Distillate production typically decreases during the fall refinery maintenance season"*). The
*level* is not: at two weeks out, only one analog in three had fallen through 96.0, and the two
convergent point estimates — **97.8 − 2.40 = 95.4**, and 2025's own +14d target of **93.0** plus
2026's base-week y/y spread of **+2.9** = **95.9** — straddle the threshold rather than clearing it.
**So this ledger does not re-use the sibling's 96.0 threshold**; `FT-…-1` registers an interval
(below 97.8, at or above 94.0) that the analog spread actually supports, with 2018 named up front as
the one year in three that breaks its floor.

**The counterweight is unchanged and strong.** Heating oil at **$4.9797/gal** against WTI at
**$104.74** is a crack of roughly **$2.49/gal (~$104.4/bbl)** — the same top-of-band incentive the
09-16 sibling measured. Refiners paid that much defer discretionary maintenance. But 2018 had a 97.6
base in a strong-margin year and still gave up **7.2 points** by +14d, because a turnaround is
contracted months ahead with specialist crews. That is why the interval's floor is real and not
decorative.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (event date `estimate`).** **Stand aside.** No position, no hedge and no watch-list entry is
keyed to this print. The deliverable of this ledger is a **measured counterweight to the bullish
narrative**: at the US flow level, the Petroline shutdown has so far *compressed* the export arb
(Brent–WTI 5.38 → 4.31 → 3.26) rather than pulling barrels out, and the spread's own three-week lead
says that compression lands on the **early-October** prints, not this one. This print should show
exports *recovering*. Both halves are registered rather than asserted.

**Kill switches:**

1. **The arb re-widens** — the Brent–WTI front-month spread closes **≥ $5.38** (the pre-attack six-month mean) on any session through **2026-10-16**. The compression finding dies, the early-October hand-off is withdrawn, and the next session rebuilds Leg 2 from the tape.
2. **Exports do not recover** — the 2026-09-23 print shows crude exports **at or below 3,417 kb/d** despite the wide $6.21 reading three weeks prior. `FT-…-2` dies and the lag structure in Leg 2 stops being usable as a lead.
3. **The turnaround arrives early and hard** — percent utilization prints **below 94.0** on 2026-09-23, the 2018 path. `FT-…-1` dies and the +14d-vs-+21d distinction this ledger rests on collapses into the sibling's simpler story.
4. **The turnaround does not start at all** — utilization prints **at or above 97.8**. `FT-…-1` dies from the other side and the 6-of-6 base-rate stops applying in a ~$2.49/gal crack regime.
5. **The shock resolves** — the Petroline returns to service, or Hormuz transits recover toward the pre-war ~100/day. The regime Leg 2 is scoped to ends and none of the flow reasoning generalizes forward.
6. **We acquire a vehicle** — any house playbook gains an energy leg, or a tracked symbol gains direct crude exposure, before **2026-12-31**. Leg 4 flips from REFUTED to live and this stops being a refusal.
7. **Date failure (void, not killed)** — no WPSR publishes by **13:00 ET 2026-09-23**. That falsifies the date, not the analysis; all three forward tests void rather than score.
