## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This entry was proposed **twice on 2026-09-09**, hours apart, by two sibling sweeps that did not see
each other:

- [`jgb-20y-auction-2026-11-18`](jgb-20y-auction-2026-11-18.md) proposed it as *"the CONTROL LEG of
  the difference-in-differences… this is November's [month-matched partner]."*
- [`jgb-40y-auction-2026-11-25`](jgb-40y-auction-2026-11-25.md) proposed it because *"November 2026
  is the first month on this calendar to carry all three super-long JGB tenors — 30-year 11-10,
  20-year 11-18, 40-year 11-25 — inside fifteen days of one another."*

Both are claims, not observations, and neither had been checked against the auction record. So:

**is either reason to track this auction true — and if not, is there a reason that is?**

**One-line verdict:** the first rationale was retired by its own proposer's power arithmetic before
this session opened; the second is **false on the data**, by 34 counterexamples including one four
months ago, and the effect it implies is **a null**. What the entry is actually worth is an **issue
identity** that corrects the sibling ledger for the very next auction, and a warning that
**2026-11-10 is the most contaminated date this family has drawn** — contaminated in Tokyo as well as
in New York, which is the half nobody would expect.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. "The first month with all three super-long tenors inside fifteen days" — REFUTED, and this is
the reason the entry exists.** Taking every calendar month in which a 20-, a 30- and a 40-year auction
all settled, and measuring the span from the first to the last:

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Months carrying all three super-long tenors | **75** |
| Median span, first to last | **18 days** |
| Tightest spans on record | **12 days** — 2020-05, 2024-05, 2025-01 |
| Months spanning **≤15 days** | **35 of 75** |
| Months spanning **strictly fewer** than November 2026's 15 | **26** |
| **November 2026's rank** | **27th–35th of 75 — the 35th percentile of tightness** |
| Most recent months at ≤15 days | 2024-11, 2025-01, 2025-05, 2025-11, 2026-05, **2026-07** |

The premise is not marginally wrong. **The same configuration occurred four months before the sweep
that called it a first.** And the specific gap the proposal leaned on — 8 days from the 30-year to
the 20-year — is the ordinary shape of this calendar, not a compression of it: across all **225**
30-year auctions the gap to the next super-long auction has a **median of 8 days**, **54%** are within
8, and the modal gap is **7 days (85 occurrences)**. A 30-year auction almost always has a super-long
neighbour the following week. That is the schedule, not an event.

**2. Auction congestion does not damage the 30-year — SUPPORTED as a null, which is what leg 1 needs
next.** Refuting the rarity claim leaves the mechanism unexamined, so it is tested directly. For each
of the **200** 30-year auctions with both a preceding and a following super-long auction:

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| corr(days to **next** super-long, tail) | **−0.050**, t = **−0.70** |
| corr(days to **next** super-long, cover) | **−0.042**, t = **−0.59** |
| corr(days since **previous** super-long, tail) | −0.028, t = −0.39 |
| corr(days since **previous** super-long, cover) | +0.093, t = +1.32 |
| Next ≤10d vs >10d — tail / cover | +0.020bp (t = +0.18) / −0.002 (t = −0.03), n = (128, 72) |
| Previous ≤10d vs >10d — tail / cover | +0.181bp (t = +0.82) / +0.030 (t = +0.25), n = (31, 169) |

Nothing. **The one |t| > 2 in the family of twelve** — next ≤14d cover **+0.238, t = +2.08** — has
**n = 13** in its comparison cell, and one result of that size out of twelve tests is what chance
produces. **No congestion effect is claimed in either direction.** Registered as
`FT-jgb-30y-auction-2026-11-10-2`.

**3. The issue identity is a MOF-stated rule, and it corrects the sibling ledger — SUPPORTED, and it
is the leg with the most downstream consequence.** No sibling has read MOF's **announcement** pages
for this purpose. They carry a footnote, verbatim:

A quarter-opening month carries **no footnote**; a reopening month names its parent. The workbook then

|---|---|

Issue 91 opened **2026-07-07** and has now had its three auctions — **07-07, 08-06, 09-03**, the last
two confirmed off MOF's own result pages (both print issue number **91**, maturity 6/20/2056, coupon
4.0%). **So 2026-10-08 opens issue 92 — a new issue, expected coupon fresh and maturity 2056-09-20 —
and 2026-11-10 is issue 92's first reopening.**

That contradicts [`jgb-30y-auction-2026-10-08`](jgb-30y-auction-2026-10-08.md), whose title reads
*"reopening of issue 91"* and whose source records *"issue 91… has now been sold three times at ¥600bn
— 2026-07-07, 08-06 and 09-03."* **The count is right and the inference is inverted**: three sales is
the complete cycle, so the fourth month starts a new one. The 10-08 ledger's own evidence refutes its
own label. This ledger cannot edit that one (rows are append-only and one lane owns one file), so the
correction is recorded here and in the ledger row below, for the 10-08 event's next pulse to carry.
The same rule **confirms** the 11-18 sibling: issue 197 opened 2026-07-14 and runs to 09-15, issue 198
opens 2026-10-20, and **11-18 is issue 198's first reopening** — exactly as that ledger states. So the
November pair is **position-matched**, first-reopening against first-reopening, which independently
corroborates its lockstep finding on this specific pair. Registered as
`FT-jgb-30y-auction-2026-11-10-1`.

**4. Issue position at the 30-year is 94% collinear with month-in-quarter, so neither is identifiable
