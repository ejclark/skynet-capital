## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Two sibling lanes proposed this print as a quiet solo pre-dawn UK release. The ONS's own
upcoming-release index, fetched directly today, says 07:00 on 2026-12-22 is the quarterly national
accounts stack: PSF publishes the same minute as revised Q3 GDP, the Q3 balance of payments, the UK
Economic Accounts and the Quarterly Sector Accounts.** That collision is genuinely **rare** — **3 of
137** PSF release days since 2015-04-23 (**2.2%**), last seen **2020-12-22** — and it is the inverse
of the sibling's November setup, where the pre-dawn window was clean and the US session was not.
**But measurement drains the alarm from it, twice.** A QNA release day is itself a null and slightly
*quieter* than average (IGLT.L **0.77x**, t = -1.87; US 10y **0.75x**, t = **-2.35**), and the three
historical co-prints moved IGLT.L **0.580% / 0.253% / 0.321%**, all inside the full-sample **p90 of
0.805%**. So the output is an **attribution rule**, not a warning: a 07:00 gilt or sterling move on
2026-12-22 belongs to GDP or the current account first and to a monthly borrowing print last. The
second correction is to the frame both proposals inherited. **ONS re-benchmarking is a three-stage
process, and the first stage lands at the FIRST post-Budget bulletin, not the third** — verified in
both the 2024-11-21 and 2025-12-19 bulletins, whose versus-forecast charts and tables already cited
the brand-new EFO while the *monthly profiles* were still weeks away. This print at **D+55** should
therefore carry an **autumn 2026 EFO** headline basis and, on the base case, **pre-Budget monthly
profiles** — a hybrid, not "still on March 2026." `symbols: []`, no house playbook is fiscal-,

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This event reached the calendar as **two proposals and no canonical file** — from
[`uk-cpi-2026-12-16`](uk-cpi-2026-12-16.md) and
[`uk-public-sector-finances-2026-11-20`](uk-public-sector-finances-2026-11-20.md), both filed
2026-09-09. Both were read in full before anything here was written. They converge on one framing,

> *"This 2026-12-22 print is therefore the LAST outturn still measured against the pre-Budget
> 'Economic and fiscal outlook - March 2026' profile, and the one after it is the switch — which
> makes this the control observation for that lane's FT-1."*

and on one shared assumption neither states explicitly because neither had reason to doubt it: that
the release is a **solo pre-dawn UK print**, whose only competition for a 07:00 gilt move is whatever
else the *US* calendar carries later that day.

**One-line verdict:** the unstated assumption is **refuted** — 07:00 on 2026-12-22 is the ONS's
**quarterly national accounts stack**, five releases in one minute — and the stated framing is
**materially amended**: ONS re-benchmarking runs in three separately-dated stages, the first of which
lands at the *first* post-Budget bulletin, so this print is a **hybrid basis**, not a pre-Budget one.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md), plus a
first-party event study against this repo's own market-data layer
(`scripts/research/market-data.mjs`, split/dividend-adjusted daily bars). Primaries fetched direct

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The date, the time and the designation are all publisher primaries from one page.**
**SUPPORTED.** `ons.gov.uk/releases/publicsectorfinancesuknovember2026` reads *"Public sector
finances, UK: November 2026"*, *"Release date: 22 December 2026 7:00am"*, contents *"A presentational
framework for the headline measures of Public Sector Finance Statistics (net borrowing net cash
requirement and net debt)"*, marked *"This release is not yet published"*, and the same page carries
the OSR *"Accredited Official Statistics"* statement. Cadence corroborates twice: **137** consecutive
PSF bulletins from 2015-04-23 to 2026-08-21 all fall on the **19th–26th**, and the eleven
November-edition bulletins — 2015-12-22, 2016-12-21, 2017-12-21, 2018-12-21, 2019-12-20, 2020-12-22,

naming because the proposing lane flagged the opposite: those dates had drifted *earlier* for five
straight years (22 → 21 → 21 → 21 → 20 → 22 → 21 → 21 → 21 → 20 → 19), and 2026 reverses the drift to
the latest date in the series. The `estimate` label is the taxonomy gap and the no-self-confirm
limit, not a doubt.

**2. The assumption both proposals rest on — a solo pre-dawn UK release — is refuted. 07:00 on

each of the last two rounds already carried the brand-new EFO in the very charts and tables the

Three stages, not one: **(a)** the annual EFO headline figures switch at the **first** post-Budget
bulletin, both rounds, verified; **(b)** the OBR's **monthly borrowing profiles** arrive later —
D+84 in 2024, and in 2025 **on the morning of the D+57 bulletin itself**, too late for the 07:00
publication and retro-fitted into Figure 3 at 09:30; **(c)** the **monthly receipts profiles**
underlying accrued tax estimates switch later still, at the third bulletin in both rounds. A fourth
fact makes the sibling's one-flip model untenable on its own terms: the profiles are **re-published
monthly** and the ONS names the vintage each time — *"monthly profiles published in February 2026"*,
*"…in March 2026"*, *"…in April 2026"* — so there is no single date on which a benchmark "switches."
Applied to **2026-12-22 at D+55**, the base case is a **hybrid**: autumn 2026 EFO in the headline
comparison, pre-Budget (March-2026-consistent) monthly profiles underneath. Registered as
**FT-…-1** and **FT-…-2** respectively, graded separately because the evidence for them is not
equally strong.

**8. The 2026 bulletin format changed the stakes of leg 7, and no precedent covers it.**
**MIXED**, and it is registered as an honest limit rather than resolved. The 2024- and 2025-era
comparison tables were **annual**: *"Provisional estimate FYE March 2025 / OBR forecast FYE March
2025 / Difference"* — a table needing only the EFO's year totals. The **current** format, verified in
the 2026-08-21 bulletin, is monthly and financial-year-to-date (Table 4: *"Comparing our July 2026
estimates with the corresponding OBR forecasts"*; Table 8: *"Comparing financial year to July 2026
estimates"*), and both carry the note *"This table uses the OBR's Economic and fiscal outlook – March
2026 and the corresponding monthly profiles published in April 2026."* So in the format this print
will actually use, the **headline comparison is itself profile-dependent** in a way the older format's
was not. The 2026-10-28 Budget will be the **first autumn Budget under this format** — the March 2026
EFO was a spring forecast — so leg 7's staging is projected across a format change, not observed
through one. That is exactly why FT-2 is graded **medium** and FT-1 **high**.

government borrowed more than anticipated."* Financial year to July: public sector net borrowing
**£56.7bn** against an OBR forecast of **£54.4bn** (**+£2.3bn**), with central government net

### Honest limits

**First, the co-print finding rests on a schedule, and schedules move.** Leg 2 is built on the
publisher's own upcoming-release index plus two individual release pages, all fetched 2026-09-09 and
all marked *"This release is not yet published."* Three months out, the ONS can and occasionally does
move a release. That is why the stack is registered as **FT-…-4** rather than asserted, and why the
attribution rule carries a date kill re-checked every pulse.

**Second, the out-of-sample evidence for the co-print is n = 3.** Legs 3 and 4 measure the QNA day
across 47 releases, which is reasonably powered, but the specific PSF+QNA combination has occurred
exactly three times — 2018-12-21, 2019-12-20, 2020-12-22 — and one of those is a COVID-year December.
Those three moves are reported as three observations, not as an estimate of a distribution.

**Third, leg 6's slice is n = 11 and its decomposition control is n = 36.** Neither is a sample from
which a distribution should be estimated. The claim made from it is deliberately negative — *nothing
here clears significance in either direction, and the one thing that appeared to is the calendar* —
which is the only claim n = 11 can support. The **±0.81%** threshold in **FT-…-3'**s market
counterpart is taken from the **full-sample p90** (2,873 sessions), never from those eleven.

**Fourth, leg 7 corrects a sibling on evidence the sibling did not have, and that cuts both ways.**
The `-2026-11-20` lane's central finding — that the 2026-11-20 bulletin will still be scored against
March 2026 — is, on the bulletins fetched here, **wrong for the annual EFO reference** and right only
for the monthly profiles. Its own registered kill switch (*"the 2026-11-20 bulletin naming an autumn/
October 2026 OBR Economic and fiscal outlook … as the basis for its versus-forecast comparisons"*) is
therefore expected to **fire**. That ledger is append-only and owned by its own lane; nothing there is
edited from here, and this note is recorded so the 11-20 close-out session finds the reasoning rather
than re-deriving it. The reciprocal caution is real: this lane's stage model is built on **two**
Budget rounds and projected across a **format change** it has no precedent for (leg 8).

**Fifth, the market study's limits.** IGLT.L is an **all-maturity** gilt index, so it under-weights
the long end where UK fiscal stress shows; it stops trading at 16:30 London, so every figure above is
a **daily close-to-close** move in which a 07:00 release's signature is diluted by a full session —
and on this particular morning, diluted by four other releases as well. `GBPUSD=X` is a Yahoo spot
series with **106** usable PSF bars against IGLT.L's 136 and unreliable close timing; its rows are
reported for completeness and carry no weight. The `^FTSE` co-print row is n = 2, not 3, for the same
data-coverage reason.

**Sixth, no consensus or whisper was findable** for the November borrowing figure at D-104, so leg 9's
expectation is a read against the OBR's March profile and four months of published drift — not against
a survey. And leg 12 is a **null claim**: its failure mode is a channel that exists and is simply not
instrumented in this book.
