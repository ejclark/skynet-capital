## At a glance

**TL;DR.** Japan sells its 30-year bond on **2026-10-08** — an anticipated ¥600bn reopening of the 4.0%
2056, size not announced until roughly 10-01. **Nothing here is ours to trade.** What makes this entry
worth writing is that it is the one date on the calendar where a **same-tenor** foreign sovereign
auction prints hours before the US one (`treasury-30y-bond-2026-10-08`, same date), so the spillover
test the 20- and 40-year siblings could only run across mismatched tenors finally gets its cleanest
form — **and it fails under control.** Raw, across **197** auctions matched to the same-date US 30-year,
a soft JGB print precedes **+3.00bp** against **+0.18bp** for a firm one, **t = +2.62**, and that is not
outlier-driven. But the tail is mostly a symptom of *that day's own JGB selloff* (corr **+0.328**,
**t = +4.85**), and once you control for it the partial correlation with the US move is **+0.109 at
t = +1.52**, with **nothing left at D+1**. Common factor, not transmission — **and the next session
should expect the naive version of this test to look significant.** Two further findings from
primaries. **(1) The BoJ's taper is entirely in one bucket.** Across 16 monthly BoJ operation files,
purchases of **25y+ have been flat at ¥150bn every single month since 2024-01**, while **10–25y was cut

not: **20s30s sits at its 73.7th percentile (+25.8bp)** while **30s40s sits at its 1.7th (+0.4bp)**.
**The 30-year is the kink.** **(2) The 20-year's tail framework transfers here — its calibration does
not.** Date and size both **estimate**; they widen caution and license nothing.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This entry was proposed on 2026-09-08 by the
[`jgb-40y-auction-2026-09-29`](jgb-40y-auction-2026-09-29.md) adjacency sweep, as "the other leg of
the 30s40s spread that has collapsed to its 1.7th percentile" — a spread that has two legs, of which
this calendar tracked neither. That sibling had just been forced into a correction: the 20-year's
"tail" framework could not be carried to the 40-year at all, because the 40-year is a single-price
auction with no tail. So this entry inherits a live methodological question, and adds a market one it
alone can ask:

**does the 20-year's framework transfer to the 30-year — and since 10-08 is the one date on this
calendar where a same-tenor foreign sovereign auction prints hours before the US one, is there
finally a measurable spillover?**

**One-line verdict:** the framework **transfers but its calibration does not**, at either sibling's
tenor; and the spillover test — in the cleanest form this calendar will ever get — **returns a
significant raw number that does not survive controlling for the JGB's own same-date move**, which
makes this ledger's most useful output a warning about a test rather than a finding from it. Along
the way, the BoJ purchase gap the 40-year ledger flagged as unsourced is **closed**, and it supplies
the mechanism for a curve shape both siblings measured and neither explained.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The 30-year is a multiple-price auction with a real tail — SUPPORTED, and MOF converted it once,
which is itself the finding.** MOF's result page for **2026-09-03** prints, verbatim:

| Field | 30-year, 2026-09-03 | 40-year, sibling ledger's control |
|---|---|---|
| Lowest Accepted Price | 98.65 | 98.68 |
| Yield at the Lowest Accepted Price | **4.100%** | 3.865% |
| Allotment at the Lowest Accepted Price | 30.3370% | 28.8888% |
| Weighted Average Price | **98.93** | **`-`** |
| Yield at the Average Price | **4.079%** | **`-`** |

So a tail exists here — **2.1bp** on 09-03 — and the 09-15 sibling's framework is applicable in a way
it was not at the 40-year.

The workbook then supplies something neither sibling looked for. Of the **225** 30-year auctions since
1999-09-02, the **25** from 1999-09-02 to **2007-01-18** print `―` in both average-price columns, and
**all 200 since 2007-04-17** print real numbers. **MOF converted the 30-year from single-price to
multiple-price in the first quarter of 2007.** That matters beyond bookkeeping: the 40-year ledger's
kill switch #2 — "MOF would have changed the auction format" — reads as an exotic tail risk, and it is
in fact a **documented precedent at the neighbouring tenor**. Registered as
`FT-jgb-30y-auction-2026-10-08-1`.

**2. The framework transfers; the calibration does not — SUPPORTED, at both siblings' tenors.** This is
the generalisation of the 40-year's lesson. That ledger showed a metric can fail to *exist* at another
tenor. The sharper problem is that a metric can exist and mean something different:

| Metric | 20-year | **30-year** | 40-year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tail, mean | 0.99bp | **0.82bp** | does not exist |
| Tail, median / sd | 0.60 / 1.38 | **0.60 / 0.82** | — |
| Tail p90 / p95 | — | **1.69 / 2.19** | — |
| Share of auctions with tail ≥ 2bp | **10.5%** (n=343) | **7.4%** (n=202) | — |
| Clearing minus same-session par, mean | — | **+1.55bp** | −0.25bp |
| …sd | — | **3.79bp** | 1.78bp |
| …share above +3bp | — | **44 of 202 (22%)** | 4 of 95 (4%) |

Two transplants would both have gone wrong. The 09-15 sibling's **"tail ≥ 2bp = soft"** is its own top
decile, but at the 30-year it is the top **7.4%** — a stricter test than intended, and the honest
30-year equivalent of "top decile" is **1.7bp**. The 09-29 sibling's **"+3bp above screen = the top 4%"**
is, at the 30-year, the top **22%** — it would flag roughly one auction in five as cheap. The rule this
ledger hands forward is not a number but a discipline: **compute the tenor's own distribution before
applying anyone's threshold to it.**

**3. The same-tenor spillover looks significant and is a common factor — REFUTED, and this is the
load-bearing result.** The 20-year sibling matched JGB 20Y auctions to the US 20Y (t = +0.80). The
40-year had to match a 40-year to a US 30-year and got a sign that flipped between samples. **Here the
tenors match exactly, and 10-08 is additionally the same date as the US 30-year auction** — the
strongest form of this test the calendar can produce. Each of the **197** 30-year auctions with a
matching US session, "soft" = tail ≥ 2bp, matched to the **same-date** change in US 30Y CMT:

| Sample | Soft | Firm | Difference | t | corr(tail, US move) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full, 2007-04-17 → 2026-09-03 | **+3.00bp** (n=15) | +0.18bp (n=182) | **+2.82bp** | **+2.62** | **+0.169** (t = +2.39) |
| Since 2020-01 | +2.78 (n=9) | −0.10 (n=70) | +2.88 | +1.94 | **+0.272** (t = +2.48) |
| Since 2023-01 | +2.78 (n=9) | +0.32 (n=34) | +2.45 | +1.57 | **+0.352** (t = +2.41) |

The correlation is significant in **all three** samples with a **stable sign** — which is more than
either sibling could say — and it is **not an artifact of a few big days**: dropping the one, two or
three largest |US move| sessions *raises* t to +2.72 / +2.61 / +2.69, and jackknifing the 15 soft
observations one at a time leaves t between **2.27 and 2.94**. A session that stopped here would report
the calendar's first measurable JGB→US channel.

**It would be wrong.** Three checks say the association is a shared global-duration move, not
transmission:

| Check | Result | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| corr(tail, **same-date JGB 30Y par move**) | **+0.328**, t = **+4.85** | the tail is mostly a symptom of that day's own JGB selloff — a stronger relationship than the tail has with the US |
| corr(same-date JGB move, same-date US move) | +0.208, t = +2.97 | the two long ends move together anyway |
| **partial** corr(tail, US move \| JGB move) | **+0.109**, t = **+1.52** | the auction-specific residual is not significant |
| D+1 (next session) US move | +1.17bp, t = +0.66; corr +0.082, t = +1.15 | nothing survives overnight |
| D+2 | +0.52bp, t = +0.37 | nothing |
| D−1 (placebo, day before) | −1.48bp, t = −0.79 | no pre-trend, as expected |

Transmission would leave a residual after controlling for the common factor, and would leave something
at D+1 as the US absorbed information published in Tokyo. Neither happens. **Verdict: no spillover** —
and the specific hazard is that at *this* tenor the naive test returns t = +2.62, so the next session
must run the control or it will report a channel that is not there. Registered as
`FT-jgb-30y-auction-2026-10-08-2`.

### Honest limits

- **The spillover conclusion rests on a control, not an experiment.** Partialling out the same-date
  JGB move is the right control for a common global-duration factor, but the JGB move is itself partly
  *caused* by the auction, so the partial correlation is if anything a **lower bound** on any real
  auction effect. The honest statement is "no evidence of transmission that survives the obvious
  confound," not "transmission is impossible." The D+1 null is the stronger half of the argument.
- **n=15 soft observations carries the headline number.** The tail-≥2bp cell is thin, the jackknife
  range (2.27–2.94) is reassuring about outliers and silent about power, and this test would not
  detect an effect much below ~3bp.
- **This is the third tenor tested.** 20-year t = +0.80, 40-year sign-unstable, 30-year t = +2.62
  raw. One of three at |t| > 2.6 is close to what a three-test family produces by chance, which is a
  second, independent reason not to have believed the raw number.
- **The BoJ mechanism is an association.** Leg 5 shows the taper and the curve kink coincide at the
  same maturity boundary and move in the predicted direction. It has no counterfactual, and a
  demand-side explanation (lifer duration targeting concentrating at 25y+) predicts the same shape.
  Leg 4's ¥150bn line is what makes it falsifiable, not leg 5's spread.
- **No BoJ forward purchase plan was located.** Two index pages 404'd (recorded in
  `probe-ref.blocked`); everything cited is **actuals through 2026-08**. A change to the 25y+ line
  announced for Q4 would be visible to a market this ledger cannot see, and would break leg 4 before
  the December file publishes.
- **The absorption shares in leg 4 are indicative, not a net-supply balance.** They compare BoJ
  purchases to new coupon issuance only, and ignore Liquidity Enhancement auctions (which sell into
  both buckets — `jgb-liquidity-enhancement-11-39y-2026-10-27` is proposed in this PR for exactly
  that reason) and the roll-down of seasoned 30-years out of 25y+ into 10–25y. The direction of the
  two shares is robust to that; the exact percentages are not.
- **The 09-03 and 08-06 auctions are read off MOF result pages, not the workbook**, which stops at
  2026-07-07. The parsing was cell-by-cell against the page's own labels and cross-checked against the
  20-year control page, but those two rows have not been reconciled against a later workbook release.
- **The hedge-cost calculation is an approximation** — short-rate differential, **cross-currency basis
  excluded**, which has historically been negative for JPY and would make hedging *more* expensive, so
  the true domestic advantage is probably wider than +114bp. It ignores credit, liquidity and capital
  treatment.
- **The auction result does not exist**, and neither does its size. Everything here is prior.
- **The result-publication clock is derived, not sourced** — MOF publishes each result on a
  same-dated page inside the Tokyo session; the exact minute was not found on a MOF primary, so the
  "hours before the US 1:00pm ET stop" sequencing claim is an inference from the session calendar,
  the same one the 09-15 sibling made.
- **USD/JPY and VIX are Yahoo, i.e. secondary**, and the 09-08 readings are intraday, not settles.
  MOF's par curve runs through 2026-09-07; the US curve through 2026-09-08.
- **The date and the size are both `estimate`.** MOF is a primary and the date is not in doubt; the
