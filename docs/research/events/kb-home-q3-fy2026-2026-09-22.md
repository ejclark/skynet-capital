### Leg 2 — the instrument's own sample is wrong on this name, and the error leans toward the hypothesis under test: **REFUTED (the instrument), and this is the session's methodological finding**

`earnings-cycle.mjs` reports **n = 12** for KBH's modern era. The complete EDGAR 8-K Item 2.02
record for 2023+ is **n = 15**. Three prints are missing: **2023-03-22**, **2024-03-20**,
**2025-03-24**.

The cause is exact and reproducible. `scripts/research/market-data.mjs`'s `earningsDates()`
de-duplicates "one event per reporting window" on the key `` `${year}-${calendarQuarter}` `` —
sound for a calendar-year filer, wrong for KB Home, whose fiscal year ends **November 30** and
which printed its **Q4 in mid-January** through FY2024. January and March then fall in the same
*calendar* quarter, the later filing loses the key, and **one print per year silently disappears**.
It stops in 2026 only because KB Home moved its Q4 print to **2025-12-18**.

**Two things make this worth a leg rather than a footnote.**

1. **The dropped prints are not random with respect to the thing being tested.** Two of the three
   were **up**-gaps (+7.34%, +2.15%; the third was −8.42%). Truncated, KBH reads **4 up in 12,
   mean −0.72%, median −2.28%** — a shape that *resembles* Lennar's asymmetry. Complete, it reads
   **6 up in 15, mean −0.51%, median −0.83%** — a coin flip. **The silent exclusion biased the
   out-of-sample check toward confirming the hypothesis it existed to test.**
2. **Lennar's own sample is clean, and that had to be checked rather than assumed.** LEN shares the
   1130 fiscal year-end, so the same defect was live there in principle. It is not: LEN prints its
   Q4 in mid-**December**, EDGAR's full 2023+ record is **14** dates, and the instrument returns the
   same **14**, identical. So the sibling ledger's n=14 statistics stand unaltered — the defect
   bites only issuers whose fiscal Q4 print lands in **January**.

This is the multi-symbol sweep's own most recent kill generalized one step. That kill reads: *"an
honest limit that says 'this history is not pooled here' is a flag on the live load-bearing
assumption, not a disclaimer to be discharged by writing it down."* Here the exclusion was never
written down at all — the instrument reported `72 prints` with no indication that any had been
dropped. **Counting the instrument's rows against the source it claims to read is now the cheapest
control in this lane**, and it took one command.

### Leg 4 — "the reaction is an overnight repricing, not an intraday trend": **SUPPORTED, strongly, and against the right control**

On **12 of 15** modern-era prints the overnight leg was the larger half of the reaction
(|gap| > |open→close session move|). Against a coin-flip null that is P = 0.0176 — suggestive, and
**the wrong test**. The honest null is *how often the overnight leg is the larger half on an
ordinary day for this same name*, which is far from 50%:

| Era | Ordinary sessions (earnings windows excluded) | Earnings prints | P(prints ≥ observed given the era's own null) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023+ | **35.6%** (314 / 883) | **12 / 15 = 80%** | **0.0006** |
| 2013–2019 | **20.8%** (350 / 1,681) | **13 / 27 = 48%** | **0.0014** |
| All 2004+ | **25.9%** (1,431 / 5,516) | **40 / 65 = 62%** | **< 0.00001** |

**The naive null does not merely understate this — it inverts one era's verdict.** Tested against
p = 0.50, the 2013–2019 window reads 13/27 and **P = 0.65, "not significant"**; tested against the
20.8% rate those same years actually ran, it reads **P = 0.0014**. So this is **not** a modern-era
regime artifact: it holds in every era against its own era-matched control, which is exactly what a
structural property of *how earnings information arrives* should do and what a regime artifact
should not. And the reverse case is rare — the session was ≥ 2× the gap on **1 of 15** modern
prints.

**This is out-of-sample support for the Lennar ledger's second forward test, on a name where the
first one failed** — and it is registered below as this event's own test rather than scored here,
because scoring another event's row is that event's close-out's job.

**It is also not a trade, and the distinction matters.** The finding says *where the risk lives*,
not *which way it points*. It is the measurement behind S2 rather than an alternative to it: going
flat into the print refuses the dominant half of the move, and the significance above is the size
of what is being refused.
