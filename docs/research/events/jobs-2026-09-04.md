## At a glance

**TL;DR.** The August jobs report lands Friday 2026-09-04 at 08:30 ET and is the single most
consequential print in this window. The **prior** month (-23k, released 8/7) moved September hike
odds harder than any 2026 CPI — from roughly a coin-flip to **~31% hike / 69% hold by 8/14** (CME
FedWatch via press) — so this is the *second consecutive* read the Fed sees before the Sep-16
decision, and it opens the pre-FOMC corridor (→ PPI 9/10 → CPI 9/11 → FOMC 9/16 with SEP + dots). The
reaction function is the sophisticated part: in this live *hike* debate, a moderately weak print is
bought as **hike relief** (bad news is good news for long-duration tech), but a *second collapse*
could reprice the question itself from hold-vs-hike to **hold-vs-cut** (the FOMC doc's "two-sided
kill"), and a hot-CPI-on-weak-jobs pairing a week later is a **stagflation** read that is worse for
our names, not better. No August consensus has formed at D-16 (it firms ~1 week out). Nothing here is
a trade — the house answer is guard-shaped: known-date variance discipline across the compound-risk
corridor. Date **confirmed** (BLS primary, re-checked today); every whisper/consensus figure below is
**estimate**-labeled and widens caution only.

## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** what will the August 2026 jobs report show, how does the market react in
each direction inside a live Fed *hike* debate the prior print reshaped, and does it change how we
sit in the tracked names (NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL AMZN) around 2026-09-04?

**One-line verdict:** the highest-leverage macro print in the pre-FOMC window — a second consecutive
read after the -23k shock, with a non-linear reaction function (moderately weak = hike-relief-bought,
too-weak = recession-sold, hot = hike-re-armed) and a live path to repricing the Fed question itself
from hold-vs-hike to hold-vs-cut; the house answer is guard-shaped, not directional (no macro
playbook exists), and the play is known-date variance discipline across the 9/4→9/16 corridor.

**Conviction legs, tested:**

1. **The date and time are right — SUPPORTED.** bls.gov/schedule/news_release/empsit.htm (fetched
   2026-08-19) lists "August 2026 data: Sep. 04, 2026, 08:30 AM" ET — matching the calendar entry
   exactly, and confirming the whole 2026 payroll cadence (Oct. 02, Nov. 06, Dec. 04 for the
   following reference months, the first Friday pattern). Re-verification matters: the 2026-08-15
   seeding caught an aggregator carrying a wrong December CPI date, so the primary is re-checked at
   each assessment.
2. **The prior print reshaped the Fed debate more than any CPI — SUPPORTED, this is the load-bearing
   context.** July payrolls fell **-23,000** vs a **+80–83k** Dow Jones consensus — a drop of 53,000
   government jobs plus softness in retail, leisure/hospitality, and slower healthcare — with the
   unemployment rate edging to **4.1%** (largely on falling participation) and 12-month average
   hourly earnings slipping to **+3.2%**, the lowest since May 2021, against a prior-12-month average
   gain of just 34,000/month (CNBC, 2026-08-07; BLS empsit). Traders cut September hike odds from
   **~57% to ~44% within hours** (CNBC, 2026-08-07), drifting to **~31% hike / 69% hold by 8/14**
   (FedWatch via press). No 2026 CPI moved the September odds that far — the labor market, not
   inflation, is the swing variable into this FOMC.
3. **The reaction function is non-linear, not just "weak = up" — SUPPORTED direction, MIXED on the
   threshold.** The 8/7 miss was bought as hike relief (yields down, stocks up) — the clean regime
   read is *weak labor lowers hike odds and is supportive for long-duration tech.* But two thresholds
   bend that: (a) labor weak **enough** to signal recession (not benign cooling) flips the sign to
   recession-fear-sold — the market can only buy "bad news is good news" while the news stays a
   Fed-story rather than a growth-story; and (b) a weak-jobs read that is then paired with a **hot
   CPI on 9/11** is a **stagflation** signal (the CPI doc's own worst tail) — worse for our names and
   uglier for the Fed than either print alone. Where exactly the recession threshold sits is not
   measurable from one data point — MIXED, and the reason the stance sizes for both tails.
4. **This print can reprice the Fed *question*, not just the odds — SUPPORTED as a live branch.**
   The [`FOMC doc`](fomc-2026-09-16.md)'s "two-sided-pricing kill" is explicit: cut odds are pinned
   at 0% today, and a *second* consecutive jobs collapse on 9/4 would mean the market has repriced
   the question from hold-vs-hike to hold-vs-cut — invalidating every hike-odds-channel reaction read
   above. That is a genuine, if not base-case, branch for this specific print; no other print in the
   window carries it.
5. **This print opens a four-event pre-FOMC corridor — SUPPORTED, cross-checked against the
   calendar.** Independently confirmed in `src/domain/market-events-data.ts` (checked 2026-08-19):
   `jobs-2026-09-04` → `ppi-2026-09-10` → `cpi-2026-09-11` → `fomc-2026-09-16` (statement 14:00 ET,
   with SEP + dot plot), with JOLTS 9/1, ISM Mfg 9/1, and ISM Services 9/3 in the run-up. Jobs 9/4
   and CPI 9/11 bracket the FOMC blackout — a hot CPI on strong jobs re-arms the hike, a hot CPI on
   weak jobs is the stagflation read. Positioning "for the FOMC" taken before all four land is really
   a bet on the whole corridor resolving together.
6. **Wage growth and revisions carry as much signal as the headline — SUPPORTED.** Average hourly
   earnings is the wage-inflation read the Fed weights directly (the July slip to +3.2% y/y was a
   disinflationary tell inside a weak print); and 2026's payroll revisions have run large and
   downward, so a "beat" that arrives alongside a deep downward revision to prior months is not the
   beat it looks like. The household/establishment-survey divergence (unemployment edging *down* on
   falling participation while payrolls fell) means the top-line and the rate can send opposite
   signals — read the internals, not just the two headline numbers.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date confirmed, BLS primary re-checked 2026-08-19).** Treat 2026-09-04 08:30 ET as a
high-impact known-date variance event opening the pre-FOMC corridor (→ PPI 9/10 → CPI 9/11 → FOMC
9/16), not a tradeable edge: no directional macro position, no new rate-sensitive paper entries
initiated into the print, and any open position sized as if all three tails (relief rally on a
moderate miss · recession-sell on a collapse · hike re-arm on a hot rebound) are live. Base case
(estimate-labeled — no consensus found, regime extrapolation only): a soft-but-not-collapsing print
continuing the cooling, most likely bought as hike relief the way 8/7 was, with wage growth (AHE) and
prior-month revisions as the internals that decide whether "soft" reads benign or recessionary. The
distinguishing risk of *this* print over any CPI: a second consecutive collapse could reprice the Fed
question from hold-vs-hike to hold-vs-cut, at which point the whole reaction function is re-based.

**Kill switches:**

- **A second clear payroll collapse (another negative print, unemployment jumping)** — trips the
  recession tipping point and may flip the debate to hold-vs-cut; the reaction function above is
  invalidated, reassess from scratch (FOMC doc's two-sided-pricing kill).
- **A hot rebound (strong payrolls + firm AHE)** — re-arms the September hike 12 days before the
  FOMC; the hawkish tail, hitting CRWV and the high-multiple semis hardest — reassess the CPI-corridor
  skew (9/11) toward the hot-hike scenario same week.
- **A hot CPI (9/11) landing on a weak jobs print** — the stagflation pairing; worse for our names
  than either print alone, and the tail the corridor most has to guard against.
- **A published August payroll consensus emerging before 9/4** — resolves this doc's biggest honest
  limit; re-run the "surprise" framing against it rather than the regime extrapolation above.
- **Warsh's Jackson Hole keynote (8/28) pre-committing the September decision** — kills the print's
  fork status in either direction; reweight at the next pulse after 8/28.
