## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This id reached the calendar twice, as proposals from
[`jobs-2027-01-08`](jobs-2027-01-08.md) and [`jobs-2027-02-05`](jobs-2027-02-05.md). Both were read
before anything was written here, and both make the same structural claim: that with the 2027 FOMC
meeting on **March 16-17** carrying a Summary of Economic Projections, this is the **last payroll the
projection round sees**. Neither claims a market effect for it.

Meanwhile the sibling that proposed it second landed a large, well-evidenced result one print
earlier: February is the **rank-1 equity-and-credit payroll month of twelve**. That sets up the
question a reader actually has, which is not about February at all:

**Does the amplification carry into the next print — and, underneath that, how much does a month's
*rank* in this statistic actually mean?**

Three legs follow. **Does the derived date survive an independent re-derivation against a BLS
primary, and is a 2027 schedule published yet?** **Is the March-released payroll distinguishable
from an ordinary one on any instrument?** And: **are there structural features of *this* print —
a quarterly quad-witching runway, a projection-round meeting — that any of the nine sibling ledgers
has not already tested?**

**One-line verdict:** the date reproduces against two BLS primaries fetched this session and stays
`estimate` because BLS has published no 2027 schedule; the March print is **statistically ordinary
on all nine instruments and both controls**, February's rank-1 result **reproduces but does not
transfer**, both new structural hypotheses are **refuted**, and the durable finding is a
calibration one — **the month-rank statistic this series leans on has a 90% confidence interval
roughly ten places wide, so rank alone should never carry a call.**

### 1. The date reproduces against two BLS primaries — SUPPORTED, with a named residual limit

The rule, stated once and implemented from scratch: *the Employment Situation is released on the
third Friday following the end of the reference week — the Sunday-to-Saturday week containing the
12th of the reference month*, with one documented correction (a derived Friday landing on an
observed federal holiday is pulled forward a day; the only case that can hit a rule-emitted Friday
is July 4 observed on Friday July 3).

**Against the current schedule page** (`/schedule/news_release/empsit.htm`, HTTP 200 2026-09-10, 13
reference months Nov 2025 – Nov 2026), the rule plus that correction reproduces **10 of 13**, and
**10 of 10 from the February 2026 reference month onward**. The three misses are the 2025 shutdown
displacement chain — Nov 2025 data released 2025-12-16 (rule: 12-05), Dec 2025 data 2026-01-09
(rule: 01-02), Jan 2026 data 2026-02-11 (rule: 02-06) — and the detail worth keeping is **where the
chain ended**: the first release back on the rule was the **February reference month**, this event's
own reference month one year earlier. Also worth recording for the sibling that owns it: this
primary's last row is 2026-12-04, so [`jobs-2027-01-08`](jobs-2027-01-08.md) is **not** on any
published BLS page and remains a derivation.

**Against the release archive** (`/bls/news-release/empsit.htm`, HTTP 200 2026-09-10), whose hrefs

### 4. The rank statistic itself has a ten-place confidence interval — the calibration finding

Leg 3 raises an uncomfortable question the whole series inherits: SPY's March amplification is
**rank 4 of 12** and yet *p*=0.179. What is a rank worth here? Bootstrapping the payroll days 2,000
times and re-ranking all twelve months each resample:

| Instrument | March rank (90% CI) | February rank (90% CI) |
|---|---|---|
| SPY | 4 **[1..10]** | 2 **[1..8]** |
| QQQ | 10 **[3..12]** | 3 **[1..7]** |
| IEF | 7 **[3..12]** | 6 **[2..10]** |
| TLT | 10 **[4..12]** | 7 **[1..11]** |
| HYG | 6 **[3..10]** | 2 **[1..4]** |
| ^VIX | 5 **[1..10]** | 7 **[3..12]** |

At n≈34 prints per month, **a rank is worth roughly ±5 places**. A split-half check agrees: ranking
the twelve months on 1993–2009 and again on 2010–2026 gives a Spearman **ρ=0.545**, with June moving
**#10 → #1** and February **#1 → #3**.

**What this does and does not say.** It does *not* overturn February: that call never rested on the
rank, it rested on *p*=0.0015 against the tightest available control and 34-of-34 leave-one-out
stability, both of which survive a wide rank CI. What it says is that **every bare rank in this
series — August's TLT/IEF/AGG 1/1/1, October's IEF 12/12, February's SPY 1/12 — should be read as a
description of the sample, and the significance test beside it is what carries the call.** Where a
lane has only the rank, as this one does for March, there is no call to make.

### 6. This print's own runway is the inverse of February's — REFUTED, and the contrast is the point

The March payroll→March opex runway is **bimodal, and mechanically so**: the rule can emit a release
date anywhere from the 5th to the 12th, so the gap to the third Friday is either **10 sessions
(21 of 34 years)** or **5 sessions (13 of 34)**. **2027-03-05 is in the long, 10-session mode** —
the same ordinary runway February had.

Measured over a **length-matched 11-bar window on both sides** (the control opens at a non-payroll
March session and runs the same 11 bars, so a share cannot rise by division):

| Instrument | Long-runway March print | 11-session March null | Ratio | *p* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SPY | **3.1%** (n=21) | 4.2% | 0.74× | 0.657 |
| QQQ | 2.5% (n=16) | 4.3% | 0.59× | 0.457 |
| HYG | 3.5% (n=12) | 4.3% | 0.81× | 0.808 |
| IEF | 9.7% (n=15) | 4.5% | **2.15×** | **0.046** |

**This is the direct inverse of the February result**, which found SPY at 12.9% vs 5.4%
(**2.40×**, *p*=0.034) on the identical construction. Two consecutive prints, one statistic,
opposite answers — which is the strongest single reason not to treat "payrolls concentrate their
expiry window's variance" as a general property.

**The short-runway number is arithmetic, not a finding, and is reported as such**: 5-session March
windows show a 22.0% SPY share, which is roughly the 1/5 an equal-weight day would take. It is
excluded from the call for exactly the reason [`jobs-2027-10-08`](jobs-2027-10-08.md) excluded its
own.

**The one nominal hit in this document is IEF at *p*=0.046**, and it is left unacted-on: it is one
of 18+ tests run here, it appears on no other instrument, and IEF's own headline comparison in leg 3
is *p*=0.945. It carries a falsifier in the ledger rather than a call.

releases. **Projection-round-vs-not is a different variable**, so it was tested rather than assumed

| SPY | QQQ | TLT | IEF | HYG |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.96× (*p*=0.943) | 0.77× (*p*=0.340) | 1.03× (*p*=0.904) | 1.21× (*p*=0.254) | 0.94× (*p*=0.897) |

### 8. The one MIXED leg — QQQ's move compresses relative to SPY's on March payrolls

The one asymmetry in leg 3's table that is not obviously noise: SPY is rank 4 while **QQQ is rank
10**, which is unusual — the two normally track. Tested pairwise, on the 27 March payrolls where
both trade:

- Median **QQQ/SPY** absolute move on March payrolls: **1.055**
- Median on every other payroll (n=288): **1.383**
- Ratio **0.76×**, ***p*=0.094**

**Not significant, and labelled that way.** It is registered as a forward test because it is cheap
to score and because a paired ratio is the one construction here immune to the month-baseline
problem — not because it is believed. At *p*=0.094 with one nominal hit already claimed elsewhere in
this document, the honest status is **MIXED**.

### Honest limits

- **The date is derived, not published.** BLS has published no 2027 schedule; if the shutdown-style
  displacement of 2025-26 recurs, every leg keyed to 2027-03-05 dies with the date.
- **Four archive filenames from 1994–1999 are unresolved.** They encode non-Friday dates, so they

- **This document runs 18+ tests and reports one nominal hit (IEF, *p*=0.046) and one marginal
