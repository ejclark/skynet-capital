**TL;DR.** Stand aside — and this print's *usual* reason for mattering is about to be cut away. PPI's
standing claim on attention is that a handful of its components (portfolio management, health-care

## Initial research

### The question

What will the September-2026 PPI show, how should we read it given that it lands the morning *after*
CPI (the reverse of the September pair), and — with a chair who argues from PCE and an FOMC 13 days
later carrying neither dots nor forward guidance — how much rate-path signal does this print actually
carry for our nine tracked AI-infra / mega-cap names?

### One-line verdict

A medium-impact wholesale print whose conventional claim to relevance — that it previews core PCE — is
**structurally narrowed 15 days before it prints**, because BEA's 2026-09-30 annual update retires the
PPI-based portfolio-management deflator; what remains is a confirmation read on the prior morning's
CPI inside a three-day corridor, and the honest posture is guard-shaped and unchanged: stand aside.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The date and time are right — SUPPORTED, with a weaker chain than usual.** The calendar entry
reads 2026-10-15, 08:30 ET, `confirmed`, sourced `BLS: bls.gov/schedule/news_release/ppi.htm — checked
2026-08-31`. That check was made yesterday by the `cpi-2026-10-14` pulse, which read the BLS schedule
page directly and proposed this event off it. This session could **not** independently re-read that
primary (403 from this runner, three endpoints tried). Corroboration is secondary: an aggregated 2026
US economic calendar (fedratecalc.com, fetched today) lists "Thu, Oct 15, 2026 8:30 AM ET" for
September 2026 PPI. Status stays `confirmed` — the primary check is one day old and was made by this
same lane — but the honest label on *today's* verification is corroborated-not-re-read.

**2. The ordering is reversed versus September, and that inverts the print's function — SUPPORTED.**
For the August data cycle the pair is PPI 09-10 → CPI 09-11 (PPI first: the "whipsaw setup" the
[`ppi-2026-09-10`](ppi-2026-09-10.md) doc is built around). For the September data cycle it flips:
**CPI 10-14 → PPI 10-15**. So this print cannot set up CPI-day positioning; it can only **confirm or
contradict** a reaction the tape has already had. That is a lower-leverage job — the surprise has
already been spent 24 hours earlier — and it argues *against*, not for, treating 10-15 as its own
event. Add **monthly opex 10-16** (OCC third-Friday, `confirmed`) and the corridor is three
consecutive mornings: number, confirming number, expiry.

**3. THE MATERIAL FINDING — the PPI→core-PCE channel is severed on 2026-09-30, retroactively —
SUPPORTED.** Two PCE components have historically been drawn straight from PPI: health-care services
and financial services (portfolio management fees). Beginning with the **2026-09-30 annual update of
the National Economic Accounts**, and applied retroactively, **BEA stops deflating PCE portfolio
management with the PPI for portfolio management and investment advice, and instead extrapolates
quantity from a BLS Current Employment Statistics hours-based indicator** (BEA *Survey of Current
Business*, "Preview of the 2026 Annual Update", June 2026; corroborated by contemporaneous sell-side
coverage). Sourced size of the effect on core PCE: **~−13bp** on a May-2026 example, **~−20bp** per
Goldman/JPMorgan — consistent with the −10 to −30bp range the sibling [`pce-2026-09-30`](pce-2026-09-30.md)
entry already carries for the same update (which also restates legal services and computer software).

Why that lands specifically on *this* event: the July PPI's headline was flat (0.0% m/m), but
**core-ex-food-energy-trade-services rose 0.4% — four times June's pace — driven by a 6.5% jump in
portfolio management fees**. That line was the entire "hot underneath" story of the last print, and it
was tradeable precisely *because* it fed PCE. From 09-30 it does not. **2026-10-15 is the first PPI
release published after the change is in force**, so any desk still reading its portfolio-management
line as a core-PCE nowcast input is using a map BEA retired 15 days earlier.

**4. And the severed channel had a feedback loop worth naming — SUPPORTED.** PPI's portfolio-management
index treats a rise in assets under management driven by a rising market as a *price* increase (fees
are levied as a percent of portfolio value; BLS PPI factsheet, NAICS 523940). So an equity rally
mechanically manufactures "fee inflation" that then propagated into the Fed's preferred gauge — the
market's own strength inflating the index the chair uses to argue for hiking against it. BEA's change
breaks that loop deliberately; the wage/hours indicator has far lower correlation to asset prices.
This is a *mechanism* claim, not a quantified one, and it cuts in the disinflationary direction for
published core PCE without any change in underlying prices.

**5. The July baseline — soft headline, sticky-but-now-differently-read internals — SUPPORTED/MIXED.**
July PPI (released 2026-08-13): final demand **unchanged, 0.0% m/m** against a +0.2% consensus, its
lowest in four months; final-demand goods **−0.7%** (energy-led), final-demand services **+0.2%**,
construction **+2.2%**; core **+0.2% m/m**; **12-month final demand +4.7%**. The market bought it —
S&P 500 to a record close, yields down, September hike odds cut below 40% (contemporaneous press,
2026-08-13). The re-read leg 3 forces: the +0.4% core-ex-trade "surge" that made that print look hot
underneath was overwhelmingly one portfolio-management line, and that line's rate-path transmission is
the exact thing being retired. It was a *wholesale* signal, and stays one; it is no longer a PCE
signal.

**6. Existence risk — a 10-01 lapse delays this print rather than deleting it — SUPPORTED, and it
differs from CPI.** The FY2027 funding deadline is **2026-09-30**; the sibling
[`government-funding-deadline-2026-09-30`](government-funding-deadline-2026-09-30.md) doc's base case
is that it is averted (both chambers have passed a CR; the Senate's cleared 90–6 on 08-08, to Dec 11,
against the House's Dec 4 — that doc was written 08-29 and its "one-week expiry gap" framing is
slightly understated: press coverage also puts an OMB grant-rule delay and ICE/CBP funding
restrictions in the Senate text, i.e. riders, not only a date. Press-grade, unreconciled, logged not
relied on). What matters here is the **branch**, and the 2025 precedent separates PPI from CPI
cleanly: BLS *completed PPI collection* before the 2025 lapse and could only not process it, so
**September-2025 PPI was delayed more than five weeks and published 2025-11-25**, and the
**October-2025 PPI news release was cancelled and folded into the November release, published
2026-01-14**. CPI's loss was permanent (October-2025 CPS household data was never collected and cannot
be reconstructed). So the sibling doc's blackout list — jobs 10-02, CPI 10-14 — should be read as
**deletions**, with this print appended as a **delay**: still a real cost, because a delayed 10-15
print arrives *after* the 10-28 FOMC.

**7. The corridor into 10-28 is unusually dark — SUPPORTED.** Three blindfolds compound at the October
meeting: Warsh **formally abandoned forward guidance** on 2026-08-28 ("a discipline, not a decision"),
the **October FOMC carries no SEP**, and September **PCE publishes 10-29 — the morning *after* the
statement**. So the Committee votes without the print of its own preferred gauge, and CPI 10-14 +
PPI 10-15 are the last inflation reads it sees. That is the one argument *for* this print carrying
more weight than its medium tier implies — and leg 3 is the argument against: the specific bridge
from PPI to that gauge is narrower than it was, leaving health-care services as the surviving
PPI→PCE line worth reading. Net: informationally more interesting than a routine PPI, still not
tradeable.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `confirmed`; BLS primary checked 2026-08-31, not independently re-read today).** Stand
aside on 2026-10-15 and treat it as the *second* morning of a three-morning corridor (CPI 10-14 → PPI
10-15 → opex 10-16), not as a standalone catalyst. No directional macro position; no new
rate-duration-sensitive paper initiated into the corridor; any position held across it sized as if all
three mornings are live. The substantive stance is a **downgrade of a channel we already relied on**:
from 2026-09-30, PPI portfolio management no longer feeds core PCE, so the loudest line in the July
print stops carrying rate-path signal — read **health-care services** as the surviving PPI→PCE line,
and read the headline and core-ex-trade as wholesale-pressure measures in their own right. Base case
for the print itself (**estimate-labeled**: trend extrapolation, no consensus exists at D-45): a
contained headline with the 12-month rate holding elevated near the July 4.7%, with energy the swing
factor in goods, and core-ex-trade **decelerating from +0.4%** precisely because the portfolio-management
spike that produced it is not a repeatable monthly event.

**Kill switches:**

- **BEA's 2026-09-30 annual update does not carry the portfolio-management methodology change** its own
  June SCB preview describes → leg 3 collapses, this doc's central finding is void, and the old
  "PPI previews PCE" map is restored in full. Check on 09-30, the day it publishes.
- **The 10-15 tape reacts to the portfolio-management line anyway** → the channel is severed in the
  accounts but not in the market's reflexes; that reframes the finding from "ignore the line" to
  "a dated mispricing to fade", and is a stance change, not a refutation.
- **A hot September PPI** — headline at or above ~0.3% m/m, or core-ex-trade re-accelerating on
  something *other* than portfolio management (energy pass-through, health care, airfares) → the
  wholesale-pressure read is live on its own terms and the deceleration base case is dead.
- **No CR signed by 2026-09-30** → this print is expected **delayed**, not cancelled, and expected to
  land *after* the 10-28 FOMC (2025 precedent: >5-week slip). Re-plan the corridor around CPI 10-14's
  own fate, which under a lapse is deletion rather than delay.
- **The CPI on 10-14 and this print disagree in direction** → the reversed ordering makes 10-15 the
  scoring morning; a contradiction is exactly the case where a medium-impact print gets outsized
  attention, and the corridor framing should be re-derived rather than assumed.
- **A September-data consensus publishes before 10-15** → resolves this doc's largest limit; re-run the
  surprise framing against it instead of the July-trend extrapolation above.
