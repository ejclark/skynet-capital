## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Five blackout ledgers precede this one and no two reached the same conclusion —
[September](fomc-blackout-start-2026-09-05.md) called a no-short-vol window because top-tier US data
was trapped inside the gate, [October](fomc-blackout-start-2026-10-17.md) refused that rule because
October's prints landed *before* the gate, [November](fomc-blackout-start-2026-11-28.md) found the
gate outlives the decision by a session with CPI in that overhang,
[January](fomc-blackout-start-2027-01-16.md) turned the same geometry into a **sizing rule** on an
earnings cluster, and [March](fomc-blackout-start-2027-03-06.md) **refused January's rule** and went
further, reporting the gate's own boundary as inert (0.64% in-gate vs 0.61% post-gate). Meanwhile
[`fomc-2027-06-09`](fomc-2027-06-09.md) owns this meeting's corridor, its calendar anomaly and its
Kalshi book, and reports June as the quietest FOMC month on record.

So the question is narrow and adversarial: **March says the gate boundary does not matter and the
decision ledger says June is quiet — does either survive being measured on June, and does this gate
add anything the decision ledger does not already own?**

**One-line verdict.** Neither survives intact, and the two failures are the same fact seen twice.
**June's quiet is one session deep.** The decision day is the quietest of any month (0.522%,
reproduced exactly) and the *next* session — this gate's last day — is **1.145%**, 1.65× the June
baseline and 2.77× the session after it, the widest overhang-to-post-gate gap of the twelve months.
So March's boundary refusal does not transfer, January's overhang framing does, and the decision
ledger's "quiet month" needs the words *"on the decision day."* Around that sit a rare trapped
print (May payrolls, derived to 2027-06-04, inside the gate in 3 of 21 years) and a calendar first
that is measurably inert (the only Memorial Day gate in 166). Date `estimate`; no directional call.

### Honest limits

**The headline is a base rate with an unestablished cause, and the confound is named rather than
buried.** The "overhang" session is also, always, the day after an FOMC decision — so leg 3 cannot
separate "the Fed is muted for one more session" from "post-decision repricing happens in June on
day two." The pooled table is the argument that it is *not* mainly the gate: if muting the Fed
carried a premium, the effect should be uniform across months, and it is not — over/post runs 2.77×

mechanism. **The n is small where it matters:** 18–19 June observations across four chairs, and the
