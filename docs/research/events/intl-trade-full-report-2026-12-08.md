## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This edition sits three days from a funding cliff, one day from an FOMC decision, and inherits a
failure-mode claim from two sibling ledgers that tells its reader what a lapse would do to it.
**Is that claim true, does the 12-11 expiry touch this edition at all, and does anything about the
12-08 session or the 12-08 nowcast vintage differ from the class the siblings measured?**

**One-line verdict:** the inherited failure-mode claim is misattributed and the cliff misses this
edition entirely — but the two things that *are* distinctive here both cut against the siblings'
generalizations, because 12-08 is the first FT-900 to land on an FOMC meeting's opening day and the
first in this ledger family to be a month-1 nowcast vintage, the loudest position class in the archive.

### Leg 4 — the full report is a rounding correction for the nowcast · **MIXED**, and this edition sits in the loud class

Joining `ContribArchives` to the release-date archive rather than to free text, over the
2020-01-07 → 2026-07-28 overlap:

| Vintage class | n | median \|Δ net exports\| | P(<0.10pp) | p90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FT-900 full report (same-day vintage) | 79 | **0.070pp** | 57.0% | 0.369pp |
| Advance Economic Indicators (2025–26 rows only) | 11 | 0.708pp | 0.0% | 2.091pp |
| Every other vintage | 896 | 0.002pp | 93.2% | 0.058pp |

That reproduces the sibling ordering. But the full-report column is a **mixture**, and the variable
that splits it is *which month of the nowcast quarter the reference data covers* — not, as the 11-04
ledger classified it, where the vintage sits in the quarter's calendar:

| Reference month's position in the nowcast quarter | n | median \|Δ net exports\| | P(<0.10pp) | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Last month of the **prior** quarter (the 11-04 shape) | 24 | **0.035pp** | 83.3% | 0.125pp |
| **Month 1 of the nowcast quarter (the 12-08 shape)** | 26 | **0.167pp** | **38.5%** | 2.280pp |
| Month 2 (the 10-06 shape) | 27 | 0.096pp | 55.6% | 1.232pp |
| Month 3 | 2 | 0.375pp | 0.0% | 0.596pp |

Month 1 against every other position: permutation p=**0.0003** (20,000 iterations). It survives
dropping the pandemic year (**p=0.0041**, restricted to 2021+) and the year after it (**p=0.0135**,
2022+). The mechanism is plain — a month-1 full report replaces a purely model-based forecast for the
first month of a quarter with hard data, where a prior-quarter-month report touches the nowcast
quarter only through carryover.

Graded MIXED rather than supported, because the December sub-sample argues the other way and is not
dismissible:

| December edition | Reference month | Δ net exports |
|---|---|---|
| 2020-12-04 | Oct 2020 | −0.004pp |
| 2021-12-07 | Oct 2021 | −0.049pp |
| 2022-12-06 | Oct 2022 | +0.237pp |
| 2023-12-06 | Oct 2023 | −0.063pp |
| 2024-12-05 | Oct 2024 | −0.008pp |

Median **0.049pp**, **80%** under 0.10pp — against **38.5%** for the month-1 class as a whole. December
vs. other month-1 editions: p=**0.14** on n=5, which is neither a confirmation of a December effect nor
a licence to ignore one. So the honest output is a registered test that adjudicates between the two
priors (`-1`), not an assertion.

One outlier is worth carrying forward rather than winsorizing: the largest month-1 move in the archive
is **2026-01-08, +2.280pp** — the first full report after the lapse, delivering October-2025 data into
a nowcast that had gone months without any trade observation. That is what a *broken* schedule does to
this line, and it is the quantitative form of Leg 3's warning about the successor edition.

### Leg 5 — the 12-08 session is a place to act on an 08:30 print · **REFUTED**, and the reason is specific to this date

Cross-referencing the 84-release archive against the Fed's meeting panels: of the **72** FT-900
releases since 2021-01-01, exactly **3** land on an FOMC meeting day — 2022-05-04 (the decision day),
2025-05-06 (day 1, no SEP), and **2026-12-08 (day 1 of an SEP meeting, the first in the archive)**.
One further instance sits outside the structured window: 2020-11-04, day 1 of the meeting whose
statement is `monetary20201105a1` — the election-aftermath session the 11-04 ledger attributes to the
election, and which was an FOMC opening day as well.

Two prior analogues is not a sample, so the argument is structural: measure the classes that contain
this date and let them say what dominates the session. SPY, 1,425 sessions since 2021-01-01:

| Class | n | median \|overnight gap\| | p | median session range | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FOMC meeting day 1 (two-day) | 45 | 0.265% | 0.382 | **0.926%** | 0.718 |
| FOMC decision day | 45 | 0.161% | **0.012** | **1.358%** | **0.001** |
| FT-900 release day (no FOMC) | 67 | 0.341% | 0.637 | 0.992% | 0.764 |
| Baseline (neither) | 1,268 | 0.316% | — | 0.966% | — |

QQQ reproduces the structure: day 1 0.460%/1.384% (p=0.71/0.93), decision day 0.256%/**1.684%**
(p=0.045/**0.019**), FT-900 0.427%/1.393% (p=0.995/0.852) against a 0.427%/1.370% baseline.

Three things follow. The FT-900 class is **inert** — an independent reproduction of the 10-06 ledger's
null on a differently-constructed sample (67 releases from eight Census calendars, FOMC days excluded;
theirs was 32 solo days from the posted GDPNow map). The **opening day of an FOMC meeting is a waiting
room**, statistically indistinguishable from an ordinary session and, on the point estimate, quieter.
And the **expansion is the next day**: decision-day range runs **+41%** over baseline while the
overnight gap runs *smaller*, which is the signature of a move that happens after 14:00 rather than at
the open. Pairing them directly, SPY's day-1 range is smaller than the same meeting's decision-day
range in **82.2%** of 45 two-day meetings (median ratio **0.62**), and **86.4%** of the 22 SEP meetings
(median ratio 0.56); QQQ reads 77.8% and 86.4%. That is `-2`.

### Honest limits

- **The archive is release *dates*, not release *contents*.** Legs 2 and 3 measure whether and when
  the FT-900 published. They say nothing about whether a delayed edition's data quality suffered, and
  a close-out reading the successor's numbers should not treat "it published" as "it published
  normally."
- **n=5 on the December sub-sample, and it is the reason `-1` exists.** The month-1 position effect
  is strong (p=0.0003, robust to two exclusions); the December counter-evidence is weak (p=0.14). A
  reader is entitled to weight them differently, which is precisely why the disagreement is registered
  as a test rather than resolved in prose.
- **Two prior analogues for the FT-900 × FOMC-day-1 configuration.** Leg 5's numbers are the
  *containing* classes, not the intersection. The intersection has no usable sample and this ledger
  does not pretend otherwise.
- **All the nowcast work measures a model, not a market.** Every Δ describes the Atlanta Fed's
  estimate. The only price claims here are the session-class studies, and both are reasons *not* to act.
- **`ContribArchives` ends 2026-07-28** and carries no Q4-2026 vintages, so `-1`'s class priors are
  out-of-sample for the quarter being nowcast. The live GDPNow readings carried by the sibling ledgers
  (Q3 **4.749%**, net exports **−1.456pp**, 09-03 vintage) are cited as theirs, not re-fetched here.
- **No October consensus exists at D-94**, and none will before the **11-27** advance goods report
  sets the headline. Every October-content statement here is a base rate, never a forecast.
- **`symbols: []` is doing real work.** Even were every measurement twice as strong, this event has no
  instrument attached and no house playbook keyed to it.
