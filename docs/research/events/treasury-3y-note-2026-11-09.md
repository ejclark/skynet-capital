## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Stand aside — and stop looking at the calendar oddity, because it measures to nothing.**
The eye-catching fact is that this 3-Year sells on a **Monday**, pulled a day forward because the bond
market shuts Wednesday 11-11 for Veterans Day. Measured, that is a non-event twice over: Monday is
ordinary for this security (**44 of 200** auctions since 2010, **8 of 16** November seats), and in the
$58B constant-size era Monday and Tuesday auctions are **indistinguishable on demand** — bid-to-cover
**2.6113** (n=8) versus **2.6123** (n=22), Welch **t = −0.02**. Nor is a refunding leg a different
animal from a mid-quarter one: **2.638** (n=10) versus **2.599** (n=20), **t = 0.96**. So the two 3-Year
ledgers already on the shelf transfer here intact, and the slot deserves a sentence, not a thesis.
**What actually distinguishes 2026-11-09 is its corridor, and it is the densest this series has
faced:** midterm elections **11-03** → the quarterly refunding statement **11-04** → jobs **11-06** →
**this auction 11-09** → **CPI and the US–China tariff-truce expiry both 11-10** → bond market shut
**11-11** → 30-Year **11-12**. The 3-Year stops at 1:00pm ET with an unhedgeable inflation print
**the next morning**, which is the reverse of September (CPI three days after) and of October (CPI eight
days after). **Two things this doc adds to the corpus.** First, yesterday's 09-08 print carried the
series' real news and it was not the cover: the fourth straight month of **domestic** rotation —

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The November 3-Year note auction (1:00pm ET) opens the November refunding block and sells on a
**Monday** rather than the block's usual Tuesday, because the bond market is closed Wednesday 11-11 for
Veterans Day. Its two siblings on the shelf — [September](treasury-3y-note-2026-09-08.md) and
[October](treasury-3y-note-2026-10-06.md) — have already established the reframe (a 3-Year is a
**policy-path** auction, not a term-premium one) and the base-rate machinery. So the question for this
one is narrow and three-part: **does the unusual slot actually change anything measurable; does being a
refunding leg rather than a mid-quarter one change anything measurable; and what in this corridor is
genuinely different?**

**One-line verdict:** the two structural questions both measure to **nothing** (Monday-vs-Tuesday
t = −0.02, refunding-vs-mid t = 0.96), which is a useful result because it lets the siblings' base rates
transfer untouched — and what is left is a corridor, not a curiosity: this is the only 3-Year in the
series that stops with **CPI the next morning**, inside a week that also carries the midterms, the
quarterly refunding statement, jobs, a tariff-truce expiry and a full bond-market closure.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date, the settlement and the new-issue status are right — SUPPORTED, primary, and extracted
   more carefully than last time.** The page-2 row reads `3-Year NOTE` with **no `R`** and **no `T`**,
   announce **Wednesday, November 04, 2026**, auction **Monday, November 09, 2026**, settle **Monday,
   November 16, 2026**. Its block-mates on the same page: 10-Year **Tuesday 11-10**, 30-Year **Thursday
   11-12**, both also unmarked, all three settling **11-16** (the 15th is a Sunday). `Holiday -
   Wednesday, November 11, 2026 - Veterans Day` appears between them. New-issue status is corroborated
   structurally: **all 200** 3-Year rows carry `reopening: "No"`, each with its own CUSIP, so the CUSIP
   is unknowable before 11-04 — a fact about the instrument, not a gap in the work. Status stays
   **`estimate`**: the schedule is tentative and this lane may not self-confirm.

2. **The Monday slot has no measurable demand consequence — REFUTED as a story, and this is the leg
   that saves the reader a thesis.** The proposal that seeded this event framed the Monday slot as the
   headline. Three measurements say it is not. **(a) Monday is ordinary for this security:** 44 of 200
   auctions since 2010 (22%), 3 on a Wednesday, the rest Tuesday; among the 16 November seats,
   **8 were Mondays**. **(b) Monday and Tuesday auctions are indistinguishable in the constant-size
   era:** bid-to-cover mean **2.6113** (n=8, σ 0.120) vs **2.6123** (n=22, σ 0.093), difference
   **−0.001**, Welch **t = −0.02**; indirect share 64.06% vs 64.74%. **(c) It is not even a Veterans-Day
   artifact:** the same schedule puts the **December 3-Year on Monday 12-07** and the **January one on
   Monday 2027-01-11**. What *is* Veterans-Day-specific is the block *shape*, and the
   [30-Year sibling](treasury-30y-bond-2026-11-12.md) has already established that as Treasury's
   standing answer — Mon / Tue / [shut] / Thu, three consecutive **bond sessions**, nothing compressed.
   One narrowing for the record: that sibling cites **3 for 3** (2009, 2015, 2020); `auctions_query`'s
   3-Year series begins **2010-02-09**, so this lane independently confirms **2015-11-09** and
   **2020-11-09** — 2 of 2 in range — and cannot see 2009 either way. Corroboration, not contradiction.

3. **A refunding leg is not a distinct demand regime — REFUTED, which is what lets the siblings'
   numbers transfer.** Both prior 3-Year ledgers were mid-quarter auctions; this is a refunding leg
   announced by a quarterly statement, so the transfer needed testing rather than assuming. In the
   constant-size era, refunding-month 3-Years (Feb/May/Aug/Nov, n=10) ran bid-to-cover mean **2.638**
   (σ 0.111) against mid-quarter **2.599** (n=20, σ 0.092): difference **+0.039**, SE 0.041,
   **Welch t = 0.96** — inside noise. Indirect share 63.8% vs 64.9%, dealer 14.1% vs 14.5%; neither
   moves. Widening to the whole 2018+ sample (n=105) gives 2.586 vs 2.559, the same non-result. **The
   one asymmetry worth carrying:** refunding months hold the era's **two highest** prints — 2.85
   (2025-11-10, the November seat) and 2.79 (2025-02-11) — and a wider top range (2.53–2.85 vs
   2.43–2.73). A fat right tail on n=10 is an observation, not a finding, and is logged for a future row.

4. **The base rate, restated as a distribution — SUPPORTED, and it is the successor to a test that
   just died.** All 30 constant-size auctions (2024-04-09 → 2026-09-08, $58,000,000,000 every time,
   `reopening: "No"` every time):

   The methodological point is the one the 09-08 close-out paid for in a killed test. FT-20's fence was
   the **observed range of n=8 draws**; under exchangeability a ninth draw sets a new extreme with
   probability 2/9 ≈ 22%, so it carried a ~22% false-kill rate before any economics — and that is
   exactly how it died, at 2.72, one tick out, **on the strong side**. A **mean ± 1.5σ** band on the
   30-auction era is **2.464–2.760** and holds **26 of 30** (87%). This doc quotes the distribution and
   registers its own tests on composition instead, because the cover has now been measured twice and
   the interesting variance is not there.

5. **The 09-08 print's real news was the composition, and it is a run rather than a spike — SUPPORTED,
   and this is the genuinely new measurement.** The September close-out named the shift from one
   auction (*"the marginal buyer changed and no bid-to-cover band of any width can detect that"*).
   Measured across the era, that reading strengthens. Direct share, last five auctions: **20.1 → 21.0 →
   24.8 → 24.0 → 26.9%**, against an era mean of **21.04%** (σ 6.13). Dealer takedown over the same
   run: 16.9 → 15.3 → 7.7 → 11.7 → **10.9%**, against **14.40%** (σ 3.91). Indirect: 63.0 → 63.7 → 67.5
   → 64.2 → **62.1%**, against **64.56%** (σ 6.25). **Direct share ≥ 24% happened in 10 of 30 auctions
   in the era (33%) — but in 3 of the last 3.** The honest caveat is the run's length: three prints is
   short, the series is noisy (direct swung 31.9% → 11.9% inside four months earlier this year), and no
   significance test survives n=3. That is precisely why it is registered as **FT-1** rather than
   asserted: a fourth straight ≥24% print is one clean observation, and its absence is another.

Nothing directional, for the ordinary reasons: `symbols: []`, the date is `estimate`, and no house
playbook is macro-keyed. What the conditions support is a **reading discipline**, four items of it.
