## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This event exists on the calendar for one reason, recorded in its own `notes`: it is the **28-day
pre-announcement** of the national December CPI print that lands 2027-01-22, and the sibling ledger
[`japan-cpi-2027-01-22`](japan-cpi-2027-01-22.md) built its central "this print is ~80%
pre-published" claim on that role. That sibling owns the *national* side of the pair and is not
re-litigated here.

The question left for this ledger is the one asked from the flash's own side: **how much information
does the 2026-12-25 release itself carry, and against what alternative?** "Tokyo predicts the nation
to 0.20pp" is only impressive if the alternative is nothing. Nobody had checked what the alternative
actually is.

**One-line verdict: the flash's incremental value is marginal and its second headline is empty —
simply reusing the national November print released seven days earlier predicts the national
December core almost as well (0.231pp vs 0.201pp mean absolute error, and better at the ±0.5pp bar),
while the 2026 Tokyo yearly average is 11/12 fixed on 2026-11-27 — and the release lands on the one
date in this 205-event calendar when the NYSE is shut.** The stance is stand aside, as it was always
going to be at `impact: low` with `symbols: []`. What changes is that the next session can price the
2026-12-25 pulse as screenable rather than material.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The flash's lead is only marginally better than doing nothing — MIXED, and it is this
   document's central finding.** The sibling ledger measures the Tokyo-minus-national same-month gap
   and reports mean absolute **0.201pp** on the BoJ's core measure, 78% within ±0.3pp. That number
   reproduces exactly here. What it does not answer is *compared to what*. The obvious alternative
   costs nothing and is available **seven days earlier**: the national November print releases
   **2026-12-18**, so on 2026-12-25 a reader already holds last month's national y/y. Racing the two
   over the identical **103 months** (2018-01 → 2026-07):

   | Predictor of national core y/y | Mean | Mean abs | sd | Within ±0.3pp | Within ±0.5pp |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | **Tokyo same-month flash** | −0.073pp | **0.201pp** | 0.258 | **78%** | 92% |
   | **Naive: last month's national** | −0.009pp | 0.231pp | 0.299 | 66% | **94%** |
   | **50/50 blend of the two** | −0.041pp | **0.175pp** | 0.216 | **84%** | **98%** |

   All items gives the same shape (Tokyo 0.194pp vs naive 0.260pp). So the flash **does** win on
   mean absolute error and on the tight ±0.3pp bar — but by **0.03pp**, and it **loses** at ±0.5pp,
   where a reader who ignored it entirely would have been right 94% of the time. The honest reading
   is not that the flash is worthless: the blend beating both by a clear margin says it carries real
   independent signal. The reading is that **"~80% pre-published on 2026-12-25" credits this release
   with information most of which was already public on 2026-12-18**, and that a session budgeting
   attention should treat 2026-12-25 as a confirmation, not an arrival. Registered as
   **FT-japan-cpi-tokyo-flash-2026-12-25-2**.

2. **The 2026-12-25 release lands on the only NYSE holiday this calendar tracks — SUPPORTED,
   primary, and mechanically checked.** nyse.com's own hours page states *"All NYSE markets observe
   U.S. holidays as listed below for 2026, 2027, and 2028"*, and its Christmas Day row reads
   **2026: Friday, December 25****, **2027: Friday, December 24 (Christmas Day observed)**,
   **2028: Monday, December 25**. Footnote **** reads verbatim: *"Each market will close early at
   1:00 p.m. (1:15 p.m. for eligible options) on Thursday, December 24, 2026."* Taking the 2026 and
   2027 holiday rows off that same table and intersecting them with every `date` field in
   `src/domain/market-events/`, **exactly one of 205 events matches: this one.** Tokyo, meanwhile, is
   open — JPX's calendar page lists 21 market holidays for 2026 (Jan 1, Jan 2, Jan 3, Jan 12, Feb 11,
   Feb 23, Mar 20, Apr 29, May 3, May 4, May 5, May 6, Jul 20, Aug 11, Sep 21, Sep 22, Sep 23,
   Oct 12, Nov 3, Nov 23, Dec 31) and **December 25 is not among them**. The consequence is the
   opposite of convenient: rather than giving a clean read on a Japanese print uncontaminated by US
   tape, it means **there is no US tape at all**, the first US session is **Monday 2026-12-28**, and
   that session carries `advance-economic-indicators-2026-12-28`. The sibling's channel-kill test
   ("a tracked name moving >2% attributed to a Japanese CPI headline") is therefore unobservable
   across this event's own window and is stated below with that limit attached.

   a sharper version of the sibling's finding rather than a copy of it.** The Remarks cell for this
   row reads `2026 yearly average of Ku-area of Tokyo` (the *Japan* 2026 annual average is a
   different row, 2027-01-22). That figure is the simple mean of twelve monthly Tokyo index levels.
   Eleven are public once the **Tokyo November flash releases 2026-11-27** — and the twelfth arrives
   in **this same release**, so the annual headline adds *strictly nothing* beyond the flash printed
   alongside it. Computed on the actual Tokyo series:

   | December y/y surprise | Index points | Move in the 2026 Tokyo annual rate |

   The Bureau publishes the annual rate to one decimal, so a December print would have to miss by
   well over a full percentage point to move it a single rounding step.

4. **December is not an unusually predictable month for Tokyo — REFUTED against the hypothesis this

   side too. On the long window that looks strongly true; on the recent window it does not:

   | Window | n | Mean abs change in Tokyo core y/y, Nov → Dec | Within ±0.3pp |
