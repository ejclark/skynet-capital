## Initial research

### The question, plainly

On Friday **2028-11-10** the Federal Reserve **Board of Governors** is closed while the Reserve Banks
operate and the NYSE holds a full session. The [`fed-board-closure-2027-12-31`](fed-board-closure-2027-12-31.md)
initial research discovered the date on 2026-09-09 while building the census its own uniqueness claim
needed, and proposed it as
[`proposals/fed-board-closure-2028-11-10.from-fed-board-closure-2027-12-31.json`](../../../src/domain/market-events/proposals/fed-board-closure-2028-11-10.from-fed-board-closure-2027-12-31.json)
with an unusually explicit brief: this is *"the cleaner member of the class"* because *"unlike 2027-12-31 it
is not a year-end or quarter-end session, so it isolates the board-only-dark mechanism from the year-end
pile-up its sibling carries"*; it is *"the out-of-sample test"* of the sibling's H.4.1/H.8 release-slot
finding; and it carries **two gaps the proposer could not close** — the bond side (*"do not assume either
an open tape or a closure"*) and any expiration coincidence (the OCC file *"stops before 2028"*).

So: **does the release-slot finding replicate out of sample, what does the bond market actually do, and is
this really a context-free Friday?** And behind all three, the question the row exists to answer for a
member: **is there anything to do on 2028-11-10?**

**One-line verdict:** stand aside — the release-slot finding replicates exactly, both gaps close toward
"nothing happens" (bonds trade, nothing monthly expires), and the proposal's *isolation* premise is the one
thing that fails, because this date is **D+3 of a presidential election by arithmetic identity**, which is
a far larger fact about the session than the Board's dark desk.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date and the split are quadruple-sourced, and K.8 names 2028-11-10 outright.** SUPPORTED. The
   page header states the rule verbatim — *"For holidays falling on Saturday, Federal Reserve Banks and
   Branches will be open the preceding Friday; however, the Board of Governors will be closed"* — and its
   first footnote enumerates the affected dates, ending *"… and November 10, 2028."* The 2028 column reads
   Veterans Day `November 11*` against that footnote, and 2028-11-11 is a Saturday by computation. The NYSE
   side is stronger here than in the sibling's case, because the exchange does not merely carve this
   holiday out — **its ten-row table has no Veterans Day row in any of 2026, 2027 or 2028**, and the only
   2028 early closes it names are Monday July 3 and Friday November 24. So 2028-11-10 is a full
   9:30–16:00 session with no footnote attached.

2. **The bond side — the proposal's first open gap — is CLOSED, and the tape trades.** SUPPORTED, and it
   is a primary. SIFMA's own **US Holiday Archive** records, in the two prior Saturday-Veterans-Day years:

   Thanksgiving. Against that, 2016 and 2022 — Veterans Day *on* a Friday — read *"Friday, November 11"*, a
   full close. So SIFMA does **not** roll Veterans Day onto the preceding Friday, unlike Saturday
   Independence Day and Saturday Christmas, which it does. Corroborated independently by Treasury's own
   daily par yield curve, which is only computed from a priced session:

### What the conditions support (date `estimate`)
