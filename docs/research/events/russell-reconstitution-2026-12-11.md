## Initial research

### The question

`russell-reconstitution-2026-12-11` reached this calendar as a proposal from the
[`russell-reconstitution-2027-06-25`](russell-reconstitution-2027-06-25.md) initial research, which filed

registered a scored forward test on this date and framed it on IWM whole-day volume.

Two questions follow, and neither is the sibling's. **What actually happens on 2026-12-11 — is the
December leg the same reconstitution as June's, or a different one?** And **can the test the sibling
registered on this date actually be scored?**

**One-line verdict:** December is a *structurally reduced* reconstitution, not a smaller June — the
ground rules say so in a comparison table, line by line — and the volume test registered on it is
unscoreable in the corridor it lands in, which is why this session proposes the **2026-11-13 preliminary
lists** as the measurement date instead.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is right, and it is now over-determined rather than merely derived — SUPPORTED.** The
   sibling had two statements (the page, plus §4.2.3's second-Friday rule). This session adds two more
   independent ones. §9.3.1 restates the quarterly-implementation schedule as *"third Friday of March,
   fourth Friday in June …, third Friday in September and **second Friday in December**."* And the FAQ
   PDF states it a fourth time — *"Implementation: Semi-annual reconstitution changes are implemented
   after the close on the last Friday in June and the second Friday in December"* — while publishing a
   dated December 2026 column that names the outcome directly: **Review Effective Date (open of), 14
   December 2026**, which is the Monday after 12-11. December 2026's Fridays are 12-04 / **12-11** /
   12-18 / 12-25. Four statements, one of them naming the literal effective date, and the rule
   reproduces it. *(Note in passing: the FAQ's "last Friday in June" is a **third** phrasing of the June
   clause, alongside §4.2.3's "fourth Friday" and §9.3.1's carve-out version. That corroborates the
   sibling's 36-of-36 equivalence proof rather than threatening it — a five-Friday June's
   Friday-prior-to-last is its fourth Friday — but it is the sibling's leg, not this one's.)*

2. **December is a structurally REDUCED reconstitution, not a smaller June — SUPPORTED, and this is the
   session's central finding.** Appendix F carries a **second** table, *"Reconstitution updates"*, which
   neither the sibling nor the proposal quotes. It compares the two legs line by line:

   | Update | June | December |
   |---|---|---|
   | Total market cap — movement between market-cap indexes | ✓ | **✓ (full)** |
   | Nationality / country assignment changes | ✓ | **✓ (full)** |
   | Total shares outstanding — used for ranking | ✓ | **✓ (full)** |
   | Float adjustments (large private holders) | ✓ | **"Applied with a 3% buffer"** |
   | Style probability changes | ✓ (whole universe) | **"New Adds to R3000E, including IPOs added between rank dates" + movements R1000↔R2000 and R2000↔Microcap only** |
   | Voting rights changes | ✓ | **"No adjustments or further research on existing members"** |
   | UBTI | ✓ | ✓ |

   Three corroborations, all primary. §9.3.2: *"For the June review, no buffers will be observed for
   ranking total capitalization or implementation of free float changes (i.e. float updates will be
   implemented regardless of size as buffers will not be applied)."* §9.3.3: *"For the December review,
   no buffers will be observed for ranking total capitalization. However, free float updates will be
   implemented with observation of the 3% buffers."* And the owner's public page states the style half
   verbatim: *"During the December reconstitution, style probabilities are updated for new additions or
   membership movements … as well as IPOs"*, against *"Companies are also reevaluated during the June
   reconstitution to determine where they lie along the investment styles spectrum."* **So the
   membership-migration leg lands in full, and the two legs that re-weight existing holdings — style
   re-scoring across the universe, and unbuffered float updates — largely do not.** Every prior about
   Russell reconstitution flow is built on June, where all six lines fire.

3. **The scale is the owner's own number, and it now splits across a six-month window — SUPPORTED.**
   *The Impacts of the Russell US Reconstitution* reports Russell 2000 reconstitution turnover — *"the
   aggregate market value of index additions and the aggregate market value of index deletions by the
   index's base market value"* — averaging **12.8% across 252 stocks for 2004–2026**, down from **32.0%
   across 534 stocks for 1996–2003** (methodology changes from 2003 onward, chiefly 2007's
   capitalization banding, account for the drop). That annual figure is the quantity now being divided.
   **The divisor is time, and it is exactly half:** June 2026's rank day was 2026-04-30 and the previous
   reconstitution was June 2025, so its drift window was twelve months; December 2026's rank day is
   2026-10-30, six months later. Under a random walk of relative capitalizations, the count of names
   crossing a breakpoint scales with dispersion, ≈√t → **0.71×**; under linear accumulation → **0.50×**.
   So a first-principles range for December is roughly **125–180 names**, against the 252 annual average,
   *before* leg 2's scope reduction is applied to the traded notional. **That range is this session's
   model, not the owner's** — stated as such, and it is exactly what 2026-11-13 will settle.

4. **The sibling's forward test cannot be scored as framed — SUPPORTED (negative result), and it is the
   most useful thing here.** `FT-russell-reconstitution-2027-06-25-1` predicts 2026-12-11's IWM whole-day
   volume ratio at ≥1.33× against a Dec-second-Friday baseline, with a kill at <1.20×. Reproducing that
   baseline independently: across **20** December second Fridays 2006–2025 — all under the annual regime,
   so all with *no* reconstitution — IWM's ratio to its own trailing-60-session median volume ran

   | Statistic | IWM | SPY |
   |---|---|---|
   | mean | 1.07 | 1.12 |
   | **median** | **1.00** | 1.05 |
   | max | 1.57 (2025) | 1.89 |
   | min | 0.78 | 0.64 |

   The 2016–2025 sub-window reproduces the sibling's figures exactly (mean 1.14, median 1.08), so this is
   the same measurement on a longer sample — and the longer sample says the December second Friday is
   **exactly an ordinary session**, IWM printing its own trailing median. The problem is the tail.
   **5 of those 20 no-reconstitution years already cleared 1.20×** (2015 1.56, 2016 1.23, 2019 1.52, 2020
   1.24, 2025 1.57) and **3 of 20 cleared 1.33×**. A single 2026-12-11 print therefore carries a **25%
   false-positive rate at the sibling's kill threshold and 15% at its pass threshold** — before the fact
   that this particular December second Friday carries a government-funding deadline, a CR expiry, CPI-1
   and FOMC-2 is counted at all. The sibling named the collision and framed the test low-side to dodge it;
   the measurement says the low side is not safe either, because *four* of the 20 quiet years printed
   ≥1.19×.

5. **A better instrument exists, works at population scale, and still fails at n=1 — MIXED, and the
   failure is the finding.** The corridor's confound is *market-wide*: a funding deadline, a CPI and an
   FOMC drive SPY volume as much as IWM's. Reconstitution does not — it is Russell-specific. So divide:
   **IWM's volume ratio ÷ SPY's volume ratio**, which cancels any day that is simply busy.

   | Sample | n | mean | median | sd | p90 | max |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | December 2nd Friday, no recon (2006–2025) | 20 | 0.994 | 0.984 | 0.222 | 1.250 | 1.491 |
   | June recon day (2006–2026) | 21 | **1.205** | 1.145 | 0.439 | 1.694 | 2.482 |
   | Every Friday (2006–2026) | 1,042 | 0.984 | 0.952 | 0.255 | 1.292 | 3.382 |

   The separation is real: **June-minus-December = +0.211, one-sided permutation p = 2.8%** (200,000
   relabelings). It also confirms the December second Friday is unremarkable — 0.984, indistinguishable
   from any Friday. **But a population-scale separation is not a single-observation test.** At a
   threshold of **1.30** the no-recon years trip **1 of 20 (5% false positive)** while the recon years
   trip only **4 of 21 (19% power)**. At **1.20**: 20% false positive, 33% power. **A one-shot volume test
   on 2026-12-11 is underpowered by roughly five to one against the effect it is looking for**, and no
   choice of threshold on this instrument fixes that — the recon distribution's own sd (0.439) is twice
   the baseline's.

### Honest limits

**The 125–180-name range in leg 3 is a model, not a measurement, and the model's weakest assumption is
named:** breakpoint migrations do not arise only from *relative* dispersion between names. A six-month
market-wide advance lifts the R1000/R2000 breakpoint itself and forces migrations that a random-walk
√t argument does not capture, so the range could understate December badly in a trending tape. It is
registered as a forward test precisely because it is a guess with a date on it. **The 3% float buffer and
the partial style re-scoring reduce *re-weighting* flow, and this ledger does not know what fraction of
reconstitution notional that is** — the owner publishes turnover as one number for the whole event, so
leg 2's asymmetry is directionally certain and quantitatively unsized. **Every volume figure here is
whole-day consolidated ETF volume**, which is the wrong instrument for a closing auction; the owner's own
June 2026 figure is **$219.9bn on NYSE and $334.0bn on Nasdaq** *"in the closing moments"*, and nothing in
this repo measures auction notional or MOC imbalance. Legs 4–7 therefore constrain what our instruments
can see, not what actually trades. **IWM is an ETF tracking the Russell 2000, not the reconstitution** —
the flow is in the underlying names migrating, and an index-level proxy sees the net of two-sided flow
that largely cancels. **Leg 5's permutation test uses 21 overlapping-regime June events against 20
December ones; the two samples are not drawn from identical volatility regimes** (Junes include 2016
Brexit and 2020 COVID), and I ran the relative measure at four thresholds after seeing the data, so the
19%-power figure is a description of this sample rather than an out-of-sample guarantee. **The relative
instrument's own premise — that a funding deadline moves SPY and IWM proportionally — is untested and
