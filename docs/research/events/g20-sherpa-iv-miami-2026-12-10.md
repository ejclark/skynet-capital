## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** the calendar carries 2026-12-10 as G20 Sherpa Meeting IV in Miami, the
final drafting session before the Doral leaders' summit four days later. Is the date real, does a
sherpa meeting move anything, what is actually observable there, and does the entry's claim that this
is "the earliest observable tell on whether the Doral summit has content" survive contact with the
host's own calendar?

**One-line verdict:** the date is primary-verified and the meeting is a document event with a
*measured* price null behind it (n=11 sessions, 4 windows, indistinguishable from ordinary sessions);
its one real content — whether the sherpas produce a consensus leaders' declaration or the chair's
statement this presidency already fell back to once — is worth reading and impossible to trade, and
the entry's "earliest tell" claim is **wrong** and corrected here, because Sherpa III (10-01/02)
comes first.

**Method:** sourced web research plus one price measurement — geopolitical kind, no symbols keyed, so
no earnings/intraday instruments were run (per
[`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md)); the instrument cache was busted before the

host government's own G20 calendar, a state.gov-run `.gov` site — HTTP 200 with a full browser header
set), **treasury.gov `sb0620`** (the Asheville G20 Chair's Statement, footnote read verbatim), and

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is primary-verified — independently, this session — and the `estimate` label still
   cannot move.** SUPPORTED on the fact, blocked on the schema. `g20.org/events-calendar/` returned
   **HTTP 200** to a full browser header set and lists verbatim *"Sherpa Meeting IV (Miami, Florida) /
   December 10 – December 12, 2026"*, directly above *"Leaders' Summit (Miami, Florida) / December 14
   – December 15, 2026"*. That is the host government's own calendar, read direct, and it is a second
   independent read of the page the duplicate-resolution pass used to file this entry hours earlier.
   The label stays `estimate` because `CONFIRMED_PREFIX` in `scripts/event-scan-validation.mjs` has no
   slot for a host-government **diplomatic** calendar — the same schema gap
   [`g20-miami-2026-12-14`](g20-miami-2026-12-14.md),

   the meeting as "December 11-12". The host page says **10–12**; the primary wins, and the entry's

2. **A sherpa meeting has no measured price channel — and this is the first time the house has
   measured it rather than asserted it.** SUPPORTED. Four dateable windows, S&P close-to-close from
   re-run adjusted bars: **India's fourth and final sherpa meeting** (Mewat, Haryana, 2023-09-03→07 —
   newsonair.gov.in, 2023-09-03) gave −0.419 / −0.697 / −0.321 %; **Brazil's fourth and final**
   (Rio, 2024-11-12→17 — UAE MoFA, Indico.UN) gave −0.289 / +0.023 / −0.605 / −1.320 %; **this
   presidency's Sherpa I** (Washington, 2025-12-15/16, host calendar) gave −0.160 / −0.238 %; **Sherpa
   II** (Washington, 2026-06-29/30, host calendar) gave +1.175 / +0.792 %. Against each session's own
   year: **5 of 11 above the year median** (null 5.5) and **1 of 11 above the year p90** (null ≈1.1);
   the pooled window median |move| is **0.419%** versus year medians of 0.531 / 0.452 / 0.507 /
   0.509 %. The single p90 breach — 2024-11-15, −1.32% — sits in the post-election Powell week and is
   attributable elsewhere. Honest form: the null cannot be rejected, and eleven sherpa-week sessions
   look, if anything, slightly *quieter* than ordinary ones. This is a measured "no effect," not a
   measured zero.

3. **The document is the whole deliverable — and this presidency has already failed to produce one.**
   SUPPORTED, primary, and it is the freshest fact in this doc. Treasury's Asheville output
   (`sb0620`, read direct) is titled **"G20 Chair's Statement"**, not a communiqué, and its footnote 1
   reads verbatim: *"The statement was agreed by all G20 members present except China, which objected
   to paragraphs 4, 10, 11, and 13."* Press reporting the same day (Bloomberg, CNBC, Benzinga,
   2026-09-01/02) supplies the mechanism and the content: China's dissent blocked the joint
   communiqué and forced the chair's-statement fallback, over language on eliminating non-market
   policies that exacerbate imbalances, on free navigation through the Strait of Hormuz, on the IMF's
   surveillance of global imbalances, and on external debt owed to G20 members. Bessent, at the
   presser: *"I'd hoped to be able to announce a unanimous joint communiqué today."* Ten weeks before
   the sherpas must produce the *leaders'* text, the fallback has already been exercised once at
   ministerial level, by this chair, against this dissenter.

4. **The leaders' track's consensus record broke last year, so a broken 2026 text would be the second
   in a row — not an unprecedented event.** SUPPORTED. Johannesburg (2025-11-22) adopted a 122-point
   leaders' declaration **at the start** of the summit, unanimously among those present, while the US
   boycotted and stated it would reject any outcome document presented as G20 consensus without its
   consent; Argentina walked out of the envoy negotiation immediately before adoption (CNBC, Reuters,
   newsonair.gov.in). The pre-2025 record was a long unbroken run of consensus declarations; it is
   unbroken no longer. This is what makes leg 3's precedent load-bearing rather than anecdotal, and it
   is the evidentiary base for the forward test registered below.

5. **The US narrowing cuts the other way, and honestly so.** MIXED — the one leg that argues against
   the fracture read. The state.gov media note of **2025-12-18** records the presidency's opening
   position verbatim: priorities are *"unleashing economic prosperity by limiting regulatory burdens,
   unlocking reliable and affordable energy options, and pioneering new innovative technologies,"*
   with working groups launched for each *"and also … an additional working group to identify areas of
   consensus on trade issues"* — four in total. CFR (2025-12-19) records what was removed: climate
   change, debt, development, inequality, sustainability. A deliberately narrow text is *easier* to
   agree, not harder, and narrowing is the standard host tactic for guaranteeing a deliverable. So the
   fracture case is not one-way: the same chair that took a chair's statement at Asheville has spent
   twelve months engineering a leaders' text small enough to pass. Graded MIXED, and it is the main
   reason the forward test below is registered at ~coin-flip rather than as a confident call.

6. **This is NOT "the earliest observable tell" — the calendar entry overstates it, and the entry is
   amended in this PR.** REFUTED as written. The same host calendar that dates this meeting also
   dates **Sherpa Meeting III (Washington, 2026-10-01/02)** and the **Foreign Ministers' Meeting
   (Atlanta, 2026-10-30/31)**, both earlier points at which the state of the leaders' text — and any
   Chinese objection to it — is observable. Sherpa IV is the **last** drafting session, not the first
   tell; its distinctive property is finality, not primacy. The practical consequence is a cadence
   one, and it is already satisfied: at `low` impact and 97 days out this ledger's band is
   `low:15+` → every 30 days, putting the next pulse at **2026-10-04**, two days after Sherpa III. The
   earlier tell is covered without adding a calendar entry (see the adjacency note below).

7. **The date can never attribute, and that is structural.** SUPPORTED, and it is what kills any

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date: estimate — on a schema gap, not on doubt).** No position, and no mechanism by which
one could exist: a drafting session's output is a document, its price signature is measured at
indistinguishable-from-ordinary across 11 sessions, and its two market days are owned by CPI and the
CR expiry. Treat 2026-12-10/12 as a **read**: does the US chair emerge from Miami with a consensus
Leaders' Declaration, or with the chair's-statement fallback it already used at Asheville on
2026-09-01? Read the earlier tells first — Sherpa III on 10-01/02, the Atlanta foreign ministers'
meeting on 10-30/31 — because this meeting is the *last* drafting session, not the first observable
one, contrary to the calendar entry's original wording (amended in this PR). `low` impact is affirmed,
not raised. Estimate status widens caution only; nothing here is date-keyed action.

**Kill switches.**

- *"A sherpa meeting is an ordinary session"* dies if an S&P close-to-close |move| on **2026-10-01,
  10-02, 12-10 or 12-11** exceeds 2026's p90 (**1.479%** as measured 2026-09-04) **and** no larger
  dated catalyst on that session explains it. Stated plainly: every one of those four dates already
  carries a bigger catalyst, so this switch is close to unfireable — which is the finding, not a
  loophole.
- *"A drafting session is not a decision"* dies on a named, dated deliverable published out of Miami
  before 2026-12-14 — a signed product list, an export-control carve-out, an extension of China's
  tariff-exclusion process. That would trip the sibling summit ledger's upgrade flag here first, and
  warrant an impact-tier review of both entries.
- *"The Miami leaders' text will not be a clean all-member consensus declaration"* (the registered
  forward test) dies on a consensus Leaders' Declaration adopted with China's assent and no recorded
  dissent or reservation.
- *"This is not the earliest tell"* dies if Sherpa III (10-01/02) **and** the Atlanta FMM (10-30/31)
  both conclude with no published statement on the leaders' text — in which case Sherpa IV really is
  the first observable point and leg 6's correction was itself too strong.
- *"The date holds"* dies on any change to `g20.org/events-calendar/`; the same fetch is the flip
  trigger if `CONFIRMED_PREFIX` ever gains a slot for a host-government diplomatic schedule.
- *"No tracked symbol carries exposure"* dies if AVGO's Q4 FY26 print is registered in
  `earnings-calendar.ts` at 2026-12-10 — this window then contains a tracked-name catalyst and the
  attribution hazard sharpens.
