## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Q3 is mostly already known, and it is strong.** ONS has published July (**+0.4% m/m**) and
August (**+0.1%**); with September flat, Q3 2026 GDP prints about **+0.7% q/q** — September would have to
fall roughly **2.2% m/m** for the quarter to print 0.0%. That is well above the **0.1%** the Bank of
England's July Report projected for Q3 and above the **0.4%** its staff nowcast implied in September, so
the live question on 11-12 is not growth versus recession but **how far above the Bank** the quarter lands
and what that does to the **11-05** rate decision's afterglow (Bank Rate **3.75%**, three of nine members
voted to *raise* in September). **None of this is ours to trade:** `symbols: []`, no house playbook is

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is firm and corroborated three ways.** SUPPORTED, primary. The ONS release page reads
   *"Release date: 12 November 2026 7:00am"* and *"This release is not yet published"*; the ONS August
   bulletin names *"Next release: 12 November 2026"*; the gov.uk register returns
   `release_timestamp 2026-11-12T07:00:00Z`, state `confirmed`. The entry stays `estimate` (taxonomy,
   no self-confirm), not on doubt about the date. One web-search snippet listed 11 December for "Q3
   data" — that is the *monthly* October edition's neighbourhood and not this release; the primaries
   above govern.

2. **Q3 is mechanically strong already.** SUPPORTED, arithmetic on ONS primaries. The August bulletin
   (published 2026-10-15): July **+0.4% m/m** (unrevised), August **+0.1%**, June **+0.4%** (revised up
   from 0.3%); three months to August **+0.7%** versus the three months to May. Setting June = 100 and
   taking April **−0.1%** and May **0.0%** from the Q2 first-estimate bulletin (published 2026-08-13;
   later revisions to April/May are not itemised in the August bulletin, so the sensitivity below
   varies May ±0.1): quarterly average Q3 / Q2 −1 is **+0.73%** with September flat, **+0.57%** with
   September −0.5%, **+0.40%** with September −1.0%, and **0.0%** only at about **−2.2%**.
   The check on the method: the same levels reproduce the published 3m/3m **+0.67% ≈ +0.7%**. Varying
   May by ±0.1pp moves the flat-September answer by about ±0.04pp (**+0.70% to +0.77%**). Not in the
   arithmetic: revisions to July and August in the 11-12 release itself, and the Q2 base (revised to
   **+0.5%** in the 09-30 quarterly accounts, Blue Book 2026 basis).

3. **The Bank's published Q3 numbers are far below this, and have been revised up once already.**
   SUPPORTED, primary. The Bank's **September 2026** minutes: *"UK GDP had increased by 0.4% in 2026 Q2,
   0.1 percentage points higher than had been expected in the July Report"*; a staff update *"implied
   GDP growth of 0.4% in 2026 Q3, higher than the 0.1% rate projected at the time of the July Report"*,
   while *"business surveys had continued to point to somewhat weaker growth"*; the MPC *"voted by a
   majority of 6–3 to maintain Bank Rate at 3.75%"* with three votes to raise to 4%. So a **+0.7%**
   print would beat both the July projection by about 0.6pp and the September nowcast by about
   0.3pp. That nowcast pre-dates the August print (**+0.1%**, published a month later); this ledger
   does not know what the Bank's November Report (**11-05**) will say, and treats that publication as
   the next information event, not as something to forecast.

4. **September is the swing month and is flagged weak, but not weak enough.** MIXED, primary plus
   inference. The ONS August bulletin states *"Indicators of consumer demand weakened in September
   2026"* and *"Housing indicators pointed to weaker activity in September 2026."* UK flash PMIs for
   September (composite **51.7** from **52.5**, services 51.7, NEWS via the 10-07 sibling pulse) cooled
   but stayed above 50. Against the **−2.2%** hurdle for a zero quarter, a September that weak would
   be extreme for a monthly UK GDP series (this ledger did not pull the series history to put a number
   on how rare; stated as a judgment). No published
   **consensus** exists for the Q3 first estimate or September (searched 2026-10-30: a calendar source
   says none yet from data providers; the rest of the results were prior years) — so the base case here
   is arithmetic, not versus-consensus, and a surprise measure has to wait until consensus appears.
