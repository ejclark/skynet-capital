## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This id reached the calendar as a proposal from
[`treasury-coupon-announcement-2027-01-07`](treasury-coupon-announcement-2027-01-07.md)'s own initial
research, filed because the payroll lands the morning *after* that announcement and the Friday
*before* the 3-Year / 10-Year / 30-Year block. The proposal handed forward three things: a derived
date it labelled honestly as derived, an explanation for that date (*"every prior structural twin …
slipped EXACTLY ONE WEEK to January 8, 3 for 3"*, attributed to New Year's Day), and a channel it
cared about (*"a jobs print … can and does reach the YIELD at which the block clears"*).

Four questions follow. **Does the date survive an independent re-derivation, and is the explanation
for it correct?** **What is January's seasonal profile, given that the three payroll ledgers already
on the shelf found three different ones?** **Does the auction corridor do anything measurable?** And,
because January 8 is a *second* Friday: **does [`jobs-2027-10-08`](jobs-2027-10-08.md)'s runway
finding transfer — given that ledger explicitly declared a second-Friday January impossible?**

**One-line verdict:** the date reproduces and is *better* supported than the proposal claimed, but
for a different reason — a December-reference boundary at January 4, 14-for-14, that fires without
any holiday; January is a **fourth** profile and it is the flat one, so no neighbouring month's
instruction transfers; the auction channel is **0 of 4** on the literal shape; and the sibling's
second-Friday population turns out to be **missing 31% of its members**, most of them Januaries,
which deflates its headline from 3.76× to 2.74× while strengthening its "arithmetic, not behaviour"
conclusion.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The plain rule reproduces against BLS's own release dates — SUPPORTED, and validated far past
   what any sibling could check.** The rule (release on the third Friday following the end of the
   Sun–Sat reference week containing the 12th) was coded from its definition and run over
   1993–2028. Against the **96** reference months BLS's historical table covers in 1993–2000 it is
   exact **89 of 96**, and all seven misses are named, not waved at: three are the December boundary
   (leg 2), two are the July pull-forward (leg 3), one is the 1995–96 government shutdown
   (Dec 1995 → **1996-01-19**, +14) and one is an **unexplained** one-day pull-forward
   (Oct 1998 → **1998-11-05** instead of 11-06). Adding the corrections and re-testing against every
   primary-dated month available — the 1993–2000 table plus BLS's current schedule page for
   2025-11 … 2026-08 — gives **102 of 106**, with the four residuals being the two shutdown
   displacements and the 2026 catch-up schedule.

2. **The January move is a DECEMBER-REFERENCE BOUNDARY, not a holiday slip — SUPPORTED, and this is
   the leg that corrects the proposal.** The proposal attributed 2027-01-08 to New Year's Day and
   rested it on three twins. The rule's December output and the actual release, every year the
   primary or a direct archive probe covers:

   | Rule output lands | Reference month | Actual release | Verified by |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | **Jan 3** | Dec 1996 | **1997-01-10** (+7) | BLS historical table |

   | **Jan 1** *(holiday)* | Dec 1998 | **1999-01-08** (+7) | BLS historical table |
   | **Jan 1** *(holiday)* | Dec 2020 | **2021-01-08** (+7) | `empsit_01082021.htm`, HTTP 200 |

   **Three of the six slips came off a Friday that was not a holiday**, and the boundary sits between
   January 3 and January 4 — a date that *is* an ordinary Friday in the one case that pins it. Across

   ten Januaries.

3. **The same holiday moves the release the OTHER WAY in July — SUPPORTED, and it is why "holiday
   slip" was never one mechanism.** When the rule lands on Independence Day the release moves **one
   day earlier**, to the Thursday: **1997-07-03** and **1998-07-02** in BLS's own table, and
   **2026-07-02** on BLS's current schedule page — three for three, all primary. December moves a
   week later; July moves a day earlier. The plausible reason December cannot move earlier is that
   the alternative would publish December data on **2026-12-31**, inside the reference month itself
   — **stated as an inference, not sourced**, because no BLS text explaining the practice was
   retrievable.

4. **BLS has published no 2027 schedule, so this stays `estimate` — SUPPORTED.**
   `/schedule/news_release/2027_sched.htm` returned **HTTP 404**, and the per-year archive route
   does not exist for any year (2016 and 2021 404 identically). A derived date is not a published
   one and this lane does not self-confirm.

### What the conditions support

Nothing directional. Three instructions fall out and all three are refusals:

- **Retire the "New Year's slip" explanation, keep the date.** A later lane deriving a December
  payroll from "the holiday moved it" will get Dec 2013, Dec 2024 and Dec 2025 wrong and will get
  July backwards. The rule to carry forward is the **January-4 boundary**, 14-for-14, plus the
  separate **July pull-forward**.
- **Do not carry any neighbouring month's instruction into January.**
  [`jobs-2027-08-06`](jobs-2027-08-06.md)'s "size duration for twice an ordinary session" is keyed
  to a month ranking 1-of-12 in TLT/IEF/AGG; [`jobs-2027-10-08`](jobs-2027-10-08.md)'s equity-side
  reading is keyed to a month where QQQ ranks 4-of-12. January is neither: **eight of nine
  instruments at or below their own median month, TLT 11-of-12.** Both would over-size a flat print.
- **Flat is not the same as safe, and the difference is where premium lives.** The median January
  payroll is unremarkable (leg 8) while the equity and credit tail is not (leg 9, SPY 4/32 vs 1.6,
  QQQ 4/27 vs 1.4, TLT 0/24). The instruction is a refusal to *sell* the print's premium on the
  strength of a quiet median — not a reason to buy it, which the same *p*-values do not support
  either.

And one constraint, which is arithmetic rather than an edge: **an January-expiry contract carries
this print with 5 sessions of life left, not 10** ([`opex-2027-01-15`](opex-2027-01-15.md) at D+7),
so it cannot be rolled away from inside the front-month cycle. All of it is `estimate`-labelled and
none of it is an entry.
