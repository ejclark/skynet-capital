## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This id existed only as a proposal, filed by the Q4 **Survey of Professional Forecasters** lane on
2026-09-09 on the narrowest possible grounds: it is *"a hole in a series this repo already
tracks"* — `philly-fed-mfg-2026-09-17`, `-2026-10-15` and `-2026-12-17` all exist with researched
ledgers, and the November edition between October's and December's simply was not on the shelf. That
proposal assigned the writer of the canonical file two jobs: **(a)** re-verify the date and the
8:30 a.m. time against an MBOS-specific surface rather than the shared calendar row, and **(b)**
decide whether the November edition inherits its siblings' `low` tier *"or whether landing inside
the pre-blackout window changes it."* It also flagged that the [December ledger](philly-fed-mfg-2026-12-17.md)
had already considered and **declined** this exact event, carrying it as a dated kill switch instead
— so the live question underneath is sharper than the filing: **is there anything about November
that is not already covered by the three siblings, and if so is it worth a row?**

**One-line verdict:** job (a) discharges from the primary but **not** from an MBOS-specific page
(there isn't one that reaches November); job (b)'s **premise is false** — 11-19 sits nine days clear
of a blackout the bank publishes verbatim — and the tier stays `low`; what genuinely distinguishes
November is **timing**, which is an *information* property and not an impact one: it is the last MBOS
the December FOMC can see, December's fully-funded pre-cliff control month, and the manufacturing
input to the 11-25 Q4 PIES. **Three plausible stories that would have made that timing tradable were
measured on the bank's own 700-month file and all three are nulls**, and the one number that survives
says November's value is as an *input* — it carries 3.9× the information about December that August
does.
