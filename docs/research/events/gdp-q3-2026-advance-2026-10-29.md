## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Will the Q3 2026 advance GDP estimate actually be published on 2026-10-29, what is it likely to say,
and — since it lands at the same 08:30 tick as PCE, the morning after an FOMC and in the middle of
five mega-cap prints — does anything in it change how we read the AI capex cycle we actually hold?

**One-line verdict.** The dominant risk is not the number but the release's *existence* (an exact
one-year precedent had BEA cancel this very report outright); conditional on it printing, the
headline is a poor guide — the two live anchors sit ~2pp apart and the divergence is composition —
so the house stance is a reading protocol, not a trade.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **Date, time and payload are right — SUPPORTED (primary).** bea.gov/news/schedule, fetched
   2026-08-29, lists "GDP (Advance Estimate), 3rd Quarter 2026" for **October 29, 8:30 AM** —
   matching the calendar entry's citation exactly. The same page lists **September 30, 8:30 AM** for
   "GDP (Third Estimate), Industries, Corporate Profits, State GDP, and State Personal Income, 2nd
   Quarter 2026" — a dated BEA release not previously on this calendar, proposed in this PR.
2. **The release can be *deleted*, not delayed — SUPPORTED, and it is the load-bearing finding.**
   One release-cycle ago the identical report died: BEA's own schedule-update blog (2025-11-24)
   states "**Gross Domestic Product, 3rd Quarter 2025 (Advance Estimate) is cancelled**" after the
   43-day FY2026 shutdown; the follow-up (2025-12-10) confirms BEA then published "two estimates of
   third-quarter GDP, instead of the usual three" — an initial/merged estimate on **2025-12-23** and
   an updated one on **2026-01-22** — explicitly "because the advance estimate … was canceled". The
   structural analogy is close to exact: a lapse from 2026-10-01 puts 10-29 at roughly day 29 of a
   shutdown, deeper into it than 2025-10-30 was. Conditional on `government-funding-deadline-
   2026-09-30` (**estimate**), whose own ledger's base case is aversion — both chambers have passed
   a CR separated only by a one-week expiry date (House Dec 4 / Senate Dec 11, 90–6). So: unlikely,
   but total when it happens, which is exactly the asymmetry CLAUDE.md's safety rule is about.
3. **The two live anchors disagree by ~2pp — MIXED, and unresolved at D-61.** The Philadelphia Fed's
   Q3 2026 Survey of Professional Forecasters (32 forecasters, released 2026-08-14) puts Q3 real GDP
   at **+2.5%** annualized (Q4 at +2.3%), revised *up* from +2.2%/+1.6% in the prior survey. The
   Atlanta Fed's GDPNow read **~4.61% on 2026-08-26**, having travelled 6.2% (08-03) → 5.8% (08-06)
   → 4.31% (08-14) → 4.03% (08-18) → 4.61% (08-26). Against a Q2 base of **+1.5%** (BEA second
   estimate, 2026-08-26 — see the sibling `gdp-q2-2026-second-2026-08-26` close-out), both anchors
   imply acceleration; they disagree about how much by nearly the width of a normal quarter. Neither
   SPF nor GDPNow figures could be pulled from their own sites this pass (both 403) — press/search
   summaries only.
4. **GDPNow is the weaker anchor *right now*, by its own published error — SUPPORTED.** The model's
   *final* forecasts (i.e. its most-informed reading, just before the print) carry an average
   absolute error of **0.77pp** and RMSE **1.17pp** since 2011; errors and volatility are widest
   early in the quarter when few hard-data inputs exist — which is precisely where 08-26 sits, two
   months into Q3 with the September data not yet collected. A 4.6% reading two months out is not
   evidence for a 4.6% print; the 2.2pp of travel inside three weeks is the honest measure of its
   current precision.
5. **The composition, not the headline, carries the information — SUPPORTED.** The August nowcast
   decline decomposes into: real PCE cut from **4.1% → 2.5%**, real gross private domestic
   investment from **17.9% → 15.2%**, against an inventory swing (Q2 depletion → Q3 accumulation)
   contributing roughly **+2.0pp** to the headline, and a July goods trade deficit of **$118.8B** —
   the largest since March 2025 — driven by capital-goods imports **+11.3%** on AI hardware.
   Inventories and imports are the two lowest-quality lines in the accounts: one reverses next
   quarter, the other subtracts mechanically. A headline flattered by an inventory rebuild while the
   consumer decelerates is a *worse* read than a lower headline built on final demand.
6. **The AI capex cycle sits on both sides of the identity, and the micro read lands within 48
   hours — SUPPORTED.** Epoch AI's quarterly series (BEA + Census construction + proprietary
   networking estimates, updated 2026-06-05) puts computing-infrastructure investment at **1.14%
   (Q3-25) → 1.29% (Q4-25) → 1.49% (Q1-26)** of US GDP, with the AI-specific component rising
   **0.10% → 0.56%** year-over-year; sell-side estimates put AI capex at roughly **+140bp** of 2026
   growth (press-sourced, not reconciled). The same hardware shows up as *investment* (adds) and as
   *imports* (subtracts) — which is why leg 5 matters more than the headline. This print then lands
   the same day as **AMZN and AAPL** (after close), one day after **GOOG/META**, two after **MSFT** —
   all `estimate` dates from 8-K cadence. Macro measurement of the capex cycle and five first-party
   capex guides inside 72 hours is the single most information-dense window on this calendar; the
   honest use is calibration, not a trade.
7. **The Fed decides without it, so the print aims at December — SUPPORTED.** The FOMC statement is
   14:00 ET on **2026-10-28** (FED-confirmed), before both this release and Sep PCE. That meeting
   carries **no SEP**, and the sibling ledger records that Warsh formally abandoned forward guidance
   on 2026-08-28 — no dots, no guidance, and now no Q3 GDP in hand. Whatever this print says can
   therefore only reprice the **2026-12-09** meeting, not the one it follows. That same ledger's
   08-28 natural experiment showed this chair's hawkish surprises price into the **2-year, not
   equity vol** (2y +8bp; S&P −0.13% to −0.25%; VIX 14.43, a 2026 low) — so the venue to watch on a
   hot print is the front end.
8. **Base rate for an advance estimate's own market impact — MIXED/contested, deliberately left
   unresolved.** Search summaries of the same academic source the sibling Q2 doc leaned on ("GDP
   announcements and stock prices", ScienceDirect) report that the *initial* announcement's effect
   on stock prices is "tenuous at best" and that the market "responds keenly to the first revision,
   but poorly to the second". That is in tension with the folk model (and with a plain reading of
   the Q2 doc, which cited the paper for the second revision being muted) — the abstract returned
   403 and could not be checked here. Recorded as an open question, not a conviction: it argues the
   advance print is *less* market-moving than its "high" impact tier implies, which would only
   strengthen this doc's stand-aside, so nothing downstream depends on resolving it.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date confirmed, BEA schedule re-fetched 2026-08-29).** Stand aside. Treat 2026-10-29 08:30
ET as an information checkpoint whose **first question is whether it happens at all** — in the
FY2027 funding-lapse branch the 2025 precedent says this exact report is cancelled outright rather
than delayed, and the loss is permanent (**estimate**-labelled: the lapse is conditional and its own
ledger's base case is aversion). Conditional on publication, no position is opened or sized off this
print: the number is anchored between two sources ~2pp apart (**estimate**-grade both), the
divergence is driven by the two lowest-quality lines in the accounts, and the print arrives after
the FOMC that would have used it. What the house does instead is read composition over headline and
carry it into the same-week hyperscaler capex guides. Exposure discipline across Oct 27–29 is
governed by `fomc-2026-10-28`, not by this doc.

**Kill switches:**

- **Existence kill (dominant).** No CR signed by **2026-09-30** → assume this release is deleted,
  not delayed, until BEA says otherwise, and shift weight to the surviving private reads (Conference
  Board 10-27, ISM, ADP, UMich). Conversely a CR signed before 09-30 retires this switch entirely.
- **Anchor-convergence kill.** SPF and GDPNow converging inside ~1pp by the **2026-09-30** Q2 third
  estimate kills the "the headline is unreadable because the anchors disagree" framing — the print
  would then carry a genuine surprise axis and this doc needs re-reading, though still not a trade.
- **Composition kill.** If the 10-29 detail shows BFI equipment/IP decelerating while inventories
  and net-export arithmetic carry the headline, the AI-capex-cycle read turns cautious *regardless*
  of the headline's direction — that is new information for the NVDA/AVGO/MRVL/CRWV pulses, scoped
  as context value, not a trigger.
- **Fed-channel kill.** A hike at the **2026-09-16** FOMC changes what a hot 10-29 print means for
  12-09 (confirming vs. behind-the-curve) and invalidates leg 7's "aims at December" framing as
  written.
- **Not a kill — expected confirmation.** If the 10-29 tape is narrated as a PCE, FOMC-aftermath or
  AMZN/AAPL story with GDP mentioned in one line, that *confirms* the buried-signal pattern the
  sibling Q2 close-out already measured; a future pulse should not read it as a GDP reaction.
