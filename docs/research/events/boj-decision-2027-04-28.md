### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The consumption-tax footnote four sibling ledgers reason from is not in the Outlook Report —
   REFUTED, primary, and it is the most consequential finding in this document.** The
   [01-22 ledger](boj-decision-2027-01-22.md)'s leg 3 states the Bank carries a standing note that
   CPI figures are *"for all items less fresh food, excluding the effects of consumption tax
   hikes"*, flags honestly that it is *"cited from a search-surfaced reading … not from a footnote
   this session extracted verbatim"*, and builds its central fork on it. This session installed
   `pypdf` and extracted the Bank's View PDFs cleanly (24,749 / 30,451 / 30,853 chars for January,
   April and July 2026; the forecast table and its notes both extract as text). **The July 2026
   table's notes are exactly three**, verbatim: *"1. Figures in brackets indicate the medians of the
   Policy Board members' forecasts (point estimates). 2. The forecasts of the majority … are shown
   as a range, with the highest figure and the lowest figure excluded … 3. Each Policy Board member
   makes their forecasts taking into account the effects of past policy decisions and with reference
   to views incorporated in financial markets regarding the future conduct of policy."* A regex for
   `tax` (case-insensitive) over **five Bank's View reports and two full texts** returns **zero
   hits** — including `gor2004a.pdf` (**April 2020**), published six months after the October 2019
   consumption-tax hike, which is precisely where the convention should be loudest. **The
   consequence is not cosmetic.** The siblings' fork was "extend a hikes-only convention, or book
   the drag," with standard practice pointing at extension. If there is no convention in the
   published table, the *default* is that the projections are what they are and the drag is simply
   in them — so **"book the drag" is the more live branch**, and the Bank would have to take a
   positive step to signal otherwise. **Honest limit:** absence in the English *Bank's View* and
   *full text* is not proof of absence everywhere (the Japanese original, the Statement, or a
   chart-image caption could carry it); what is proven is that it is not in the forecast table's
   notes, which is where the siblings placed it.

2. **The April Outlook is where the projection horizon extends, so April 2027 first publishes
   FY2029 — and FY2029 is the year the tax cut unwinds. SUPPORTED, primary, and it is this venue's
   structural signature.** Diffing the extracted tables settles the mechanic outright:

   | Outlook | Fiscal years in the forecast table |
   |---|---|
   | January 2026 (`gor2601a`) | FY2025 · FY2026 · FY2027 |
   | **April 2026** (`gor2604a`) | FY2025 · FY2026 · FY2027 · **FY2028** |
   | July 2026 (`gor2607a`) | FY2026 · FY2027 · FY2028 |

   April 2026's FY2028 row carries **no "Forecasts made in January 2026" comparison line** — the
   mechanical fingerprint of a year appearing for the first time — and the chart x-axis shifts with
   it (January 2026: 2019-2028; April 2026: 2020-**2029**). So **the April 2027 Outlook publishes
   FY2027, FY2028 and, for the first time, FY2029.** Now the fiscal arithmetic. The cut runs *two
   years from April 2027*, i.e. **2027-04-01 → 2029-03-31**; FY2027 is 2027-04-01 → 2028-03-31,
   FY2028 is 2028-04-01 → 2029-03-31, FY2029 is 2029-04-01 → 2030-03-31. A level cut only shows in
   a year-on-year index for twelve months: **FY2027 carries the drag, FY2028 compares cut-to-cut
   and is neutral, FY2029 compares post-expiry prices to cut prices and carries the *boost*.** So
   the April 2027 table must print a **V across three consecutive rows**, in a document where
   FY2029 has never appeared before — and the July 2026 medians it starts from are FY2027 **+2.4**
   (up from +2.3 in April 2026) and FY2028 **+2.0**, both read verbatim off the Bank's own table
   this session, confirming the 01-22 ledger's press-cited figures. **This is the cleanest possible
   place for the Bank to reveal its treatment**, because the unwind year cannot be quietly folded
   into an existing row. **Conditional on the cut being law** — see leg 4.
