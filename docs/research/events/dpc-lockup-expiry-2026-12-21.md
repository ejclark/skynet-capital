## Initial research

### The question
Does the 2026-12-21 DPC lock-up expiry put enough new supply on the market to be worth a position, a hedge, or a stand-aside, and on what date does it actually fire?

**Verdict: the date is firm, the supply is large and ungated, the price premise is absent, the options are unread — stand aside.** One clean 180-day period with no early-release path. The unlock multiple (3.7x float) sits in the study's 3.5–7.6x bucket, the one that fell most (-5.61% into day -1, t -3.22), but the study itself calls size "no dial", and DPC's below-IPO-price run-up bucket fell only -1.65% (t -1.43). Neither licenses a trade.

Read the 424B4 (EDGAR, fetched 2026-11-21; filed 2026-06-26, prospectus dated 2026-06-24 on its cover) for the lock-up clause, "Shares Eligible for Future Sale", Rule 144 text, principal shareholders and the Registration Rights Agreement; the Q3 10-Q (filed 2026-11-12) for the current share count and whether the lock-up changed; the 2026-11-12 8-K and its press release (Exhibit 99.1); the 2026-09-08 8-K (credit agreement); the EDGAR submissions API (CIK 2107018) for filings since the IPO; Yahoo daily bars for DPC, HWM, ATI, CRS, GE, XLB, XLI, SPY and ^VIX (fetched 2026-11-21, last full session 2026-11-20). `earnings-cycle.mjs`, `intraday-edges.mjs` and `research:dossier` were not run (`node_modules` absent and the name has 105 sessions and one report, below what either instrument reads). ### Leg 1 — the date: SUPPORTED
The 424B4 says the company, its officers, directors and "holders of substantially all of our ordinary shares and securities convertible into, or exercisable for, ordinary shares" agreed not to dispose of them "during the period from the date of this prospectus continuing through the date 180 days after the date of this prospectus, except with the prior written consent of Jefferies LLC and Morgan Stanley & Co. LLC. This consent may be given at any time. There are no agreements among Jefferies LLC and Morgan Stanley & Co. LLC, us and any of our security holders or affiliates releasing them from these lock-up agreements prior to the expiration of the 180-day period." 180 days after 2026-06-24 is Monday 2026-12-21; the prospectus's own "Shares Eligible for Future Sale" says the shares become eligible "beginning 181 days after the date of this prospectus", Tuesday 2026-12-22 (day 0 of the lockup study). A text search of the underwriting section for an earnings, price or blackout trigger found none; the carve-outs are transfers to affiliates and estates, equity-plan exercises and tax sales, and tender offers. The Q3 10-Q restates "180 days from June 24, 2026" for the management plan shares. EDGAR lists no 8-K amending any of this. The Registration Rights Agreement lets demands start "180 days following the pricing", the same date.

### Leg 2 — supply: LARGE AND UNGATED (3.7x the float)
Shares outstanding 149,393,016 on 2026-11-02 (10-Q cover). The prospectus counted 145,151,799 after the IPO and both private placements without the underwriters' 4,178,787-share option; the 4.24M difference is consistent with the option having been exercised in full (my inference; neither filing I read states it). The IPO was **all primary: 27,858,585 shares, no selling shareholder**, so the free float is about 32.0M with the option (about $985M at $30.74). Locked: 112,936,894 pre-IPO shares plus 2,083,593 sold to existing holders and directors in the concurrent private placement, about **115.0M**, which is **3.6x the float** (the study's formula gives 3.66x on the current count, 4.21x on the prospectus count; both in its 3.5–7.6x bucket) and about 61 days of the 20-day average volume (1.89M) or 74 days of the 60-day (1.56M). Shares bought in the IPO by management-plan participants are also locked for 180 days (10-Q) and are inside the float figure, which overstates it by an amount I could not read. QIA's 2.27M-share private placement carries **no lock-up** and is a restricted security, saleable under Rule 144 about six months after its 2026-06-26 closing (my reading), roughly 1.2 days of volume: immaterial. Who holds the locked shares (424B4 table, after the offering): J.F. Lehman 23.2M (16.0%, with a board seat, so an affiliate), then Searchlight 10.5M, Hill City 7.5M, UBS Asset Management 5.1M, Lord Abbett 6.8M, Bardin Hill (Man Group) 5.7M, Mudrick 6.4M, Corre 6.4M and Kinetic Partners 6.9M. The prospectus names a director nominee only for J.F. Lehman. **The eight non-board funds hold about 55M shares (1.7x the float, about 29 days of volume), my arithmetic from the table; they are credit and event-driven holders who got their shares in the 2020 restructuring of the company's debt, so none is within Rule 144's one-year holding period and, if not affiliates, none faces a volume cap once the lock-up ends.** J.F. Lehman as an affiliate may sell in any three months the greater of 1% of the shares outstanding (~1.5M) or four-week average weekly volume (~9M, from the 20-day average). A registered offering needs a demand from holders of 10%+ in aggregate for at least $50M (up to two on Form S-1; the company is not S-3 eligible until about June 2027, my reading); holders of about 76.6M shares carry these rights for seven years. This is the opposite of QNT's gated Up-C: here the shares are shares. Nothing public says any holder intends to sell (no 13D/G amendment, Form 144 or S-1 on EDGAR through 2026-11-20; the filings since the IPO are the 10-Q, two 8-Ks, Forms 3/4 and Schedule 13Gs).
