## At a glance

**TL;DR.** The calendar filed this as a second tariff cliff — China's post-March-2025 retaliatory
tariffs and countermeasures snapping back on New Year's Eve. Tested against the sources, that is not
what 2026-12-31 is. The March-2025 agricultural retaliation was **removed outright** on 2025-11-10,
not suspended, so it has no expiry clock at all; the countermeasure suspensions that *do* have a
clock (the 24% reciprocal countertariff, the April-2025 unreliable-entity listings, the Oct-9-2025
rare-earth controls) all run to **2026-11-10** and are already tracked by
[`us-china-tariff-truce-expiry-2026-11-10`](us-china-tariff-truce-expiry-2026-11-10.md). The White
House fact sheet attaches December 31 2026 to **exactly one** commitment — China's *market-based
tariff-exclusion process for US imports*. So this is not a second cliff; it is the narrowest leg of
the same one, 51 days later. It is still worth watching for one reason: it is the only Chinese
tariff-relief mechanism whose clock runs past 11/10, and it expires in the middle of an unfinished
**$30bn reciprocal tariff-reduction negotiation**. And where the 11/10 leg's discomfort was that its
extension venue sits *after* the deadline, this leg has the opposite geometry — **three leader-level
venues fall before it** (Washington 2026-09-24, APEC Shenzhen 11/18–19, G20 Miami 12/14–15). No
symbol this calendar tracks (AAPL, AMZN, AVGO, CRWV, MRVL) carries the exposure; the exposed complex
is agriculture, and it has *already* run 10–17% in a month on drivers that are mostly not this. Watch
it; do not trade it, and specifically do not chase the ag tape as if it were a bet on this date.

## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** the calendar carries 2026-12-31 as the day China's suspension of its
post-March-2025 retaliatory tariffs and non-tariff countermeasures expires. Is that what that date
actually governs, what — if anything — is genuinely on that clock, and which of the names this
research tracks carry the exposure?

**One-line verdict:** the date is real but its stated scope is wrong — 2026-12-31 governs China's
market-based tariff-exclusion process and nothing else, because the retaliatory tariffs it names were
*removed* rather than suspended and every clocked countermeasure runs to 2026-11-10, so this is the
tail of an already-tracked cliff rather than a second one, and its renewal geometry is favourable
where 11/10's is not.

**Method:** sourced web research only — no price/options instruments run (geopolitical kind, no
symbols keyed, per the macro-print/geopolitical method in
[`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md)). Primary read: the whitehouse.gov fact sheet
"President Donald J. Trump Strikes Deal on Economic and Trade Relations with China" (Nov 2025),
fetched direct and read clause-by-clause for which commitment carries which end-date. Cross-checked
against China Briefing (quoting the State Council Tariff Commission's 2025-11-05 announcement),
Global Times/MOFCOM readouts for the May-2026 and August-2026 developments, Skadden and Wiley for the
$30bn arrangement and USTR dockets, CSIS and CNBC for the September-summit expectation set, and
AgWeb/Farm Progress for the grain tape. Quotes for VIX, SPX and the ag complex pulled live
2026-09-04 (2026-09-03 closes). All figures as-checked 2026-09-04 and drift.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **December 31 2026 attaches to exactly one commitment, and it is not the one in the title.**
   SUPPORTED, and this is the load-bearing finding. Read clause-by-clause, the White House fact
   sheet's China commitments carry these end-dates: the retaliatory-tariff sentence ("China will
   suspend all of the retaliatory tariffs that it has announced since March 4, 2025") — **no
   end-date stated**; the non-tariff sentence ("China will suspend or remove all of the retaliatory
   non-tariff countermeasures taken against the United States since March 4, 2025, including China's
   listing of certain American companies on its end user and unreliable entity lists") — **no
   end-date stated**; the rare-earth sentence — **no end-date stated**; and the exclusion sentence,
   verbatim: **"China will further extend the expiration of its market-based tariff exclusion process
   for imports from the United States and exclusions will remain valid until December 31, 2026."**
   That is the only December-31 clause in the document. The event's own `source` field cites this
   fact sheet, so the title's scope was inferred from adjacency within the same document, not from
   the dated clause.

2. **The March-2025 agricultural retaliation was removed, not suspended — it has no clock.**
   SUPPORTED. On **2025-11-05** China's State Council Tariff Commission confirmed it was *removing*
   the 10–15% retaliatory tariffs on US agricultural products announced in March 2025 — roughly 740
   commodity lines covering chicken, wheat, corn, cotton, sorghum, soybeans, pork, beef, aquatic
   products, fruit, vegetables and dairy — effective **2025-11-10** (China Briefing, quoting the SCTC
   announcement; USDA FAS GAIN report CH2025-0209 covers the same action). A removal cannot expire.
   The separate 24% reciprocal countertariff *was* suspended, for one year from 2025-11-10, with a
   10% levy retained — that is the 11/10 clock, not this one.

3. **Every clocked countermeasure runs to 2026-11-10, and is already tracked elsewhere.**
   SUPPORTED. The unreliable-entity-list measures split by announcement date: the **March-4-2025**
   listings were *removed* effective 2025-11-10; the **April-4-2025** listings were *suspended for
   one year* from 2025-11-10, i.e. to **2026-11-10**. MOFCOM/GAC Announcement No. 70 suspended the
   six Oct-9-2025 export-control announcements to the same date. MOFCOM's own framing of the Kuala
   Lumpur package is that the US 24% reciprocal tariff and China's corresponding countermeasures, the
   US Affiliates Rule and China's corresponding export controls, and the US Section 301
   maritime/logistics/shipbuilding measures and China's corresponding countermeasures, are all
   suspended **until 2026-11-10**, with both sides to discuss the arrangement after the one-year
   pause. That entire set is the
   [`us-china-tariff-truce-expiry-2026-11-10`](us-china-tariff-truce-expiry-2026-11-10.md) ledger's
   subject. This event, read correctly, does not duplicate it — it is its 51-day tail.

4. **The exclusion process is small but is the only Chinese tariff-relief mechanism running past
   11/10 — and it expires mid-negotiation.** MIXED. At the Trump state visit to China of
   **2026-05-13/15**, MOFCOM's readout listed eight preliminary outcomes, including a "reciprocal
   tariff reduction arrangement covering products of comparable scale — starting at $30 billion or
   more on each side," with agreed categories expected to receive MFN rates or lower, and an
   extension of the Kuala Lumpur framework for ongoing consultations. USTR opened dockets
   **USTR-2026-0430** (comments due **2026-07-10**) and **USTR-2026-0431** (rebuttals due
   **2026-07-27**); MOFCOM ran its own consultation with domestic firms, associations, local
   governments and US chambers in China, both sides saying in July 2026 they were working to finalise
   product-level measures "as soon as possible." The MIXED grade is deliberate: Skadden's read is
   that the $30bn relief is to be negotiated through a bilateral Board of Trade / Trade Council
   mechanism **rather than** a market-based exclusion process, so the claim that the 12/31 exclusion
   expiry is the delivery vehicle for the $30bn arrangement is *not* established. What is
   established is only the timing collision: an unfinished tariff-reduction negotiation and the
   expiry of China's standing exclusion channel fall in the same quarter.

5. **The renewal geometry is favourable — the exact inverse of the 11/10 leg's problem.** SUPPORTED,
   and it is the reason this is a watch rather than a hedge. Bessent said in May 2026 the
   administration was "not in a hurry" to extend, naming three venues: the September Washington
   summit, APEC in November, and a December G20 in Florida. For the 11/10 leg, two of those three
   post-date the deadline. For **this** leg all three pre-date it: Xi's Washington state visit
   **2026-09-24** (estimate; China had not officially confirmed as of 2026-09-02, and CSIS lists a
   2026-09-17 press-briefing preview), **APEC Shenzhen 2026-11-18/19**, and the **21st G20 leaders'
   summit at Trump National Doral, Miami, 2026-12-14/15**, which Trump hosts — 17 days before this
   deadline. Reporting as of 2026-09-02 describes Trump and Xi as scheduled to meet at all three.
   *Proposed to `market-events.ts` this PR as an `estimate`:* `g20-miami-2026-12-14`.

6. **The truce is holding on its named legs while both sides add new measures outside them.**
   SUPPORTED. On **2026-08-05** MOFCOM tightened export controls on UAVs and related
   components/technologies to the US (case-by-case strict review, immediate effect), sanctioned six
   US entities — Applied DNA Sciences, Stratum Reservoir, Altana Technologies, the Responsible
   Business Alliance, Verite Group and Human Rights in China — over Xinjiang-related sanctions
   support, added Compliance Testing LLC over FCC assistance, had CNCA suspend US certification-body
   factory inspections for China Compulsory Certification, and opened a national-security
   investigation into imported office printing and copying equipment, all in response to US
   restrictions on Chinese cotton, drone and robotics businesses. None of it touched the Kuala Lumpur
   suspensions. Same pattern the 11/10 ledger recorded for China's 2026-06-22 listing of MP Materials
   and USA Rare Earth: the arrangement is a narrow, load-bearing truce, not a détente, and the levers
   outside it stay live throughout.

7. **The arrangement survived the SCOTUS ruling that voided the US side's legal basis.** SUPPORTED
   with a sourcing caveat. The Supreme Court held on **2026-02-20** that IEEPA does not authorize
   tariffs; the President terminated the IEEPA duties the same day and CBP stopped collection
   **2026-02-24**, eliminating the 10% "fentanyl" and reciprocal tariffs on Chinese goods. The US
   wall was rebuilt on Section 122 and then Section 301. A MOFCOM press release is reported to state
   that US tariffs on Chinese goods will remain at or below the Kuala Lumpur level regardless of
   which legal mechanism substitutes — i.e. China treats the arrangement as intact through the
   authority swap. That matters here because it is the precondition for the 12/31 date meaning
   anything at all. Caveat in Honest limits: no MOFCOM primary was read.

8. **No symbol this calendar tracks carries the exposure, and the complex that does has already
   run.** SUPPORTED. The calendar's symbol-keyed events cover **AAPL, AMZN, AVGO, CRWV, MRVL** —
   zero agricultural exposure, and the AI-semis supply-chain channel belongs to the 11/10 rare-earth
   leg, not to this one. The exposed complex is agriculture, and to the **2026-09-03** close it had
   already moved over one month: **WEAT +16.8%, BG +15.1%, CTVA +14.9%, CORN +13.9%, MOS +13.5%,
   DE +13.1%, SOYB +10.3%, ADM +6.9%** against **SPX +0.14%** (SPX 7,747.71; VIX **14.32**, down from
   15.86 a month earlier). Attribution is genuinely mixed: USDA reported a 44% weekly jump in
   new-crop US soybean export sales on China buying, with outstanding 2026-27 sales to China at
   **6.79 MMT as of 2026-08-20** — 27% of the 25-MMT annual target, with StoneX's Arlan Suderman
   seeing 50–60% by late September — but the same reporting credits damage to a Black Sea port
   slowing Russian wheat exports (December SRW wheat closed **$7.84** on 2026-08-28, the
   highest most-active close since February 2023) and a hot, dry Northwest Corn Belt, with corn at
   three-year highs. Three drivers, one tape.

**What the conditions support:** nothing directional, and — as with the 11/10 leg — specifically not
the trade the event's own title implies. There is no retaliatory-tariff snapback on this clock to
position for. The one genuine question, whether China's exclusion channel is renewed or folded into
the $30bn arrangement, is a foreign-administrative outcome 118 days out with three leader-level
venues in front of it and no tracked symbol attached. The ag complex is the only place the exposure
lives, and it has run 10–17% in a month on drivers that are mostly weather and the Black Sea — which
makes it a bad vehicle for this thesis in both directions: too crowded to buy, too
multiply-caused to short. The correct posture is a watch with dated triggers at the three summits,
and no exposure taken on the date itself.
