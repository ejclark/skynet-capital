### The question, plainly

This event existed only as `proposals/fhfa-hpi-2027-05-25.from-fhfa-hpi-2027-11-30.json`, one of five 2027
rows the November sibling proposed in a single PR to close out FHFA's own posted 2027 table. That proposal
left three numbered instructions, and two of them close doors rather than open them:

> **(1) BLACKOUT MEMBERSHIP IS ALREADY DERIVED AND IT IS NO.** … **Re-derive rather than trusting this note.**
>
> **(2) THE SEASONAL HYPOTHESIS IS CLOSED — DO NOT RE-OPEN IT.** … A further refutation is a confirmation of
> a closed hypothesis, not a finding.
>
> **(3) THE INTERESTING CONTENT QUESTION IS THE RESTATEMENT, AND ITS BASE RATES ARE ALREADY MEASURED.**

So the proposer handed this lane a date with its two obvious angles pre-closed. **The question this ledger
actually asks is the one the proposer could not have asked, because it is invisible from November: what is
structurally true of a *May* last Tuesday, and does the May release sample transfer to a year that breaks
that structure?**

**One-line verdict:** the structure is real and rare — 2027 is one of eleven years in seventy-one where the
May release is not the session after Memorial Day — and, unlike the blackout dimension the sibling found,
**it costs the sample nothing**, because the post-holiday session is not a regime; the genuinely new finding
is a **control trap** in the pre-blackout window that turns a p=0.0142 into a p=0.4833 under the right
comparison.

### Leg 3 — **THE FINDING: 2027-05-25 is one of eleven years in seventy-one where the May release is NOT the post-Memorial-Day session** · **SUPPORTED**

Memorial Day is the **last Monday of May**; FHFA publishes on the **last Tuesday**. Those two rules collide
in a way no sibling has had reason to look at:

- If Memorial Day falls on **May 25–30**, the last Tuesday is the **very next day** — the first session of a
  holiday-shortened week.
- If Memorial Day falls on **May 31**, the last Tuesday is **May 25**, six days *before* the holiday.

Across **1990–2060** the first case holds in **60 of 71 years**. The eleven exceptions are **1993, 1999,
2004, 2010, 2021, 2027, 2032, 2038, 2049, 2055 and 2060**. In the post-policy panel:

| Year | Memorial Day | Last Tuesday (the release) | Offset |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 2021-05-31 | **2021-05-25** | **−6d — before the holiday** |
| 2022 | 2022-05-30 | 2022-05-31 | +1d — the session after |
| 2023 | 2023-05-29 | 2023-05-30 | +1d |
| 2024 | 2024-05-27 | 2024-05-28 | +1d |
| 2025 | 2025-05-26 | 2025-05-27 | +1d |
| 2026 | 2026-05-25 | 2026-05-26 | +1d |
| **2027** | **2027-05-31** | **2027-05-25** | **−6d — before the holiday** |

So **five of the six** post-policy May releases sit on a returning-from-holiday session and **this one does
not**. That is exactly the shape of problem the `2027-11-30` sibling documented one dimension over: a
historical sample whose defining feature the target date does not share.
