## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Never a trade, read it for one number, and do not try to measure it on the tape.** This is
the second consecutive **calendar-clean** monthly deficit comparison — and the first clean at *zero
versus zero*. November 2026 and November 2025 both carry **no** first-of-month military/VA/SSI/
Medicare-drug benefit batch (Nov 1 is a **Sunday** in 2026 and was a **Saturday** in 2025, so each
year's own batch pays out in the prior October; Dec 1 is a **Tuesday** in 2026 and was a **Monday**
in 2025, so neither year pulls the next month's batch back). That matters because the artifact is
enormous when it is *not* matched: November 2024 ran **two** batches and printed a **$366.76B**
deficit against November 2025's **$173.28B** on zero — a **$193B** swing that is almost entirely
payment-calendar arithmetic. Base case for this print, on a `confirmed` date but a model-derived
number: **a deficit near $205B, honest band $180–230B**, against **$173.28B** a year earlier — a
genuine y/y deterioration of roughly **$32B** on a matched month. The swing factor is not the
tariff line this time but **interest**: November is a heavy coupon month (**$77.54B** of cash
interest in Nov-25, against $430.67B of everything-else outlays), and the recent y/y run-rate on
heavy months is **+14% to +47%**. **The thing this ledger adds that its siblings could not.**
2026-12-10 is the **anti-11-12**: **19** tracked events sit within ±5 days, against **6** for
[`mts-october-2026-11-12`](mts-october-2026-11-12.md) — CPI at **08:30 the same morning on the same
reference month**, an FOMC decision **with SEP and dot plot at 14:00 the day before**, and a funding
cliff at midnight **the day after**. But the reason that kills a market-impact test here is
**attribution, not variance**: this session measured 721 sessions of TLT and found a loud morning
raises the 13:30→close σ only **0.242% → 0.287%**, non-monotonically — a *far* weaker effect than
the VIX-regime split the October sibling measured (0.20% → 0.359%). The window will not blow out;
you simply will not be able to say the MTS caused anything in it. **And one inherited claim is
wrong.** The proposal that filed this date says a funding lapse "would hit this release's schedule
before any other MTS in the window." It cannot: the CR funds through **2026-12-11**, so this release
lands one day *inside* the funded window and November's data is entirely within it. The exposed
edition is the **December-data statement on 2027-01-13**.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

anything — and given that this date collides with CPI, sits a day after an FOMC with a dot plot and
a day before a funding cliff, can anything about this release be measured on the tape at all?

**One-line verdict:** the date is primary-`confirmed` at 2:00 p.m. ET and is **promoted from
`estimate` this session** on a re-read of Treasury's own release table plus two independent

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is real, the time is 2:00 p.m. ET, and the entry is PROMOTED to `confirmed` —
   SUPPORTED by one Treasury primary read direct in this session plus two independent
   corroborations.** The **November 2025 MTS**, page 40, downloaded and text-extracted here, reads
   verbatim *"Listed below are the scheduled release dates for the Monthly Treasury Statement. The
   Statement is released at 2:00 p.m."* with the accounting-month row **November 2026 → December 10,
   2026** (and, usefully, **December 2026 → January 13, 2027**). Corroboration one: the
   **8th-federal-workday rule** — validated 20-of-20 against the published 2024 and 2026 calendars by
   the August sibling — computes December 2026's 8th federal workday as **Dec 10** exactly (Dec 1 is
   a Tuesday and no federal holiday falls before the 10th; Christmas is the 25th). Corroboration
   two: Treasury's `services/calendar/release` endpoint (965 rows, HTTP 200) carries dataset
   `015-BFS-2014Q1-13` with exactly three forward releases — **2026-09-11, 2026-11-12, 2026-12-10** —
   matching the published table row for row *including the September gap*, at timestamps decoding to
   **14:00 ET on both sides of the DST boundary** (18:00 UTC on 09-11; 19:00 UTC on 11-12 and
   12-10). That dataset is still **not named** on any endpoint this session could reach, so it is
   offered as a fingerprint match — independently reproduced from the October sibling's — and not as
   a second named primary. The entry's `estimate` grounds — the lane's no-self-confirm limit on an
   event discovered in the `mts-august-2026-09-11` sweep — are cleared: this is the owning event's
   own session, the primary has been re-fetched here, and `TSY:` is an authorized confirmed-tier
   prefix (the identical promotion `mts-october-2026-11-12` made on 2026-09-05 from this same PDF).

2. **November 2026 and November 2025 both carry ZERO first-of-month benefit batches, so this is the
   second consecutive calendar-clean comparison and the first clean at zero-vs-zero — SUPPORTED, and
   it is the load-bearing structural finding.** Treasury's rule is that the military/VA/SSI/
   Medicare-drug batch nominally paid on the 1st moves to the prior business day when the 1st is a
   weekend or holiday. For a November, two dates decide the count — whether Nov 1 falls in the month,
   and whether Dec 1 falls on a weekend and pulls December's batch back:

3. **A block-ordering trap in `mts_table_1` will corrupt any November series read the obvious way —
   SUPPORTED, and it is worth a leg because the next pulse will hit it.** Each MTS publication
   carries two fiscal-year blocks, and **`record_fiscal_year` labels the publication, not the
   block**: in the 2024-11-30 statement, line codes 10–140 are FY2024 (so its "November" row is
   **Nov 2023, $314.01B**) and 150–280 are FY2025 (its "November" row is **Nov 2024, $366.76B**),
   *both stamped `record_fiscal_year: 2025`*. The API's row order is not stable, so filtering by
   `record_fiscal_year` and taking the first match silently returns a value one year off — this
   session produced exactly that error before catching it against a direct dump. **The correct read
   is the highest `line_code_nbr` matching the month name within the publication for that month's
   end.** Every November figure in leg 2 was pulled that way.

4. **The market impact is a null, inherited and not re-measured — SUPPORTED.** The August sibling
