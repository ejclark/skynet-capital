   lane flagged — filing `confirmed` for a date it had discovered in-sweep — is now moot on the
   substance: two lanes, two BLS documents, same date, same slot.

2. **The y/y productivity headline falls on base effects alone, and the drop is quantified —
   SUPPORTED, computed from the published index.** Nonfarm business productivity index
   (`PRS85006093`, 2017=100): 2025Q2 **117.385** → 2025Q3 **118.884** → 2025Q4 **119.350** → 2026Q1
   **119.594** → 2026Q2 **120.017**. The Q2 y/y checks out at 120.017/117.385 − 1 = **2.24%**, i.e. the
   published 2.2%. The base quarter is the problem: 2025Q3 rose **+1.28% q/q = +5.2% SAAR**, the
   strongest quarter since 2023. Against that base:

   | Q3-2026 q/q SAAR | Q3 index | Printed y/y |
   |---|---|---|
   | 0.0% | 120.017 | 0.95% |
   | **+0.19%** | 120.073 | **1.00%** ← ECI doc's lower trigger |
   | +1.4% (Q2 repeat) | 120.435 | 1.30% |
   | **+3.3%** (central) | 120.956 | **~1.8%** |
   | **+4.21%** | 121.262 | **2.00%** ← ECI doc's upper trigger |
   | +5.03% | 121.499 | 2.20% (Q2 repeat) |

   Read the last row first: **holding the current y/y requires matching the best quarter in three
   years.** The mechanical consequence is that a y/y decline from 2.2% is close to certain and carries
   no information about Q3 itself — which is exactly how it will be reported.

3. **Unit labor cost is an identity, so the ECI doc's conditional can be solved — SUPPORTED, and this
   is the load-bearing leg.** ULC index = hourly-compensation index ÷ productivity index × 100:
   148.796 ÷ 120.017 × 100 = **123.979**, matching the published 2026Q2 ULC index to three decimals.
   The identity is exact, so a grid of (productivity, compensation) outcomes maps to ULC y/y with no
   model in between. Against the 2025Q3 ULC base of **122.570**:

   | prod q/q SAAR ↓ / comp q/q SAAR → | 2.0% | 3.0% | 4.5% | 6.2% | 8.0% |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | 0.0% | 1.65 | 1.90 | 2.27 | 2.68 | 3.11 |
   | +1.4% | 1.30 | 1.55 | 1.91 | 2.33 | 2.76 |
   | **+3.3% (central)** | **0.83** | **1.08** | **1.44** | 1.85 | 2.28 |
   | +4.21% | 0.61 | 0.85 | 1.22 | 1.63 | 2.05 |

   Hourly compensation's last ten quarters (`PRS85006102`, SAAR): 2.6 · 2.1 · 3.7 · 6.2 · 1.2 · 6.3 ·
   4.4 · 4.8 · 4.8 · 5.8 — volatile, and 6.2/6.3 are the observed ceiling. Inverting the identity for
   the ECI doc's **ULC y/y ≥ 2.5%** trigger: it needs compensation at **5.45% SAAR** if productivity
   is flat, **6.92%** at a Q2-repeat 1.4%, and **8.93%** in the central case. **Only the top-right
   corner of the grid reaches 2.5%, and only when productivity simultaneously prints near zero** —
   a joint tail, not a scenario. Meanwhile ULC y/y ≥ **2.0%** (the Fed's objective, the line that
   actually matters) is a live risk if productivity disappoints: it needs comp at **3.41%** SAAR when
   productivity is flat, which three of the last ten quarters cleared.

4. **The central case is anchored on a real nowcast, and the nowcast is loud and unstable —
   SUPPORTED with a named caution, Atlanta Fed primary.** GDPNow for Q3 2026 is **4.7 percent on
   September 3**, verbatim from the commentary archive fetched today, down from 4.8 (09-01). Its path
   this quarter: **5.0** (07-30 initial) → 6.2 (08-03) → 5.9 (08-04) → 5.8 (08-06) → 4.3 (08-14) →
   **4.0** (08-18) → 4.6 (08-26) → 4.8 (09-01) → **4.7** (09-03). A **2.2-point range** in five weeks,
   with an investment nowcast that touched **17.9%** annualized on 08-14 before collapsing. Against
   Q2's actual advance GDP of **1.5%** this is an enormous acceleration. Two credibility points, in
   both directions: the model's **final** Q2 nowcast matched the 1.5% advance print exactly after
   rounding (its own commentary, 07-30) — but that is the *final* nowcast, and today's is a
   two-months-of-data reading with the widest error band it will ever have. Nonfarm business output
   ran **+1.70% SAAR** in Q2 against GDP's 1.5%, so the sector-to-GDP spread is small and positive;
   applying it, output near **4.9%** against hours near **1.4%** gives productivity ≈ **3.4% SAAR**.
   That is the number the whole central case rests on and it is the weakest link in this doc.

5. **Q3 hours are already two-thirds published, and the third month lands 10-02 — SUPPORTED, but the
   estimate is rounding-limited.** BLS's own technical note, fetched today, states verbatim: *"The
   primary source of hours and employment data is the BLS Current Employment Statistics (CES)
   program"*, with *"Data from the CPS ... used to estimate hours worked for farm labor, nonfarm
   proprietors, and nonfarm unpaid family workers."* Total private employment (`CES0500000001`) ran
   Apr **135,467k** · May **135,528k** · Jun **135,554k** · Jul **135,625k** · Aug **135,752k**, with
   average weekly hours (`CES0500000002`) at 34.3 through July and **34.4** in August. Employment ×
   AWH against the Q2 average gives **+1.36% to +1.56% SAAR** for Q3 across a ±100k range for

   is **0.29%** — the entire quarterly move. So treat "hours ~1.4% SAAR" as a plausible centre with
   a ±0.5pp band, not a measurement. For reference, the published Q2 hours figure was **+0.28% SAAR**

7. **The labor share is the release's real headline and it is on a five-quarter slide — SUPPORTED,
