## Initial research

**The question.** What is the 2027-01-25 deliverable, does it move US Treasuries or anything in our
book, and does it deserve the weight the `treasury-30y-bond-2026-09-10` ledger's D4 row gave it when

**One-line verdict:** it is a standing advisory council's routine annual report, commissioned in
parallel with the Norges Bank analysis it will comment on, decided by nobody, landing between a BoJ
meeting and an FOMC — and the flow it concerns is smaller than the swing Norway's own reported

### Leg 1 — "the expert group appointed by the Ministry of Finance in 2025": what is it? → **MIXED**

The calendar entry inherited NBIM's own phrasing, which is accurate but unrevealing. Pinned down:

- The body is the **Ekspertrådet for Statens pensjonsfond utland**, established **spring 2025** and
  chaired by professor **Karen Helene Ulltveit-Moe** (Innst. 418 S (2025–2026), stortinget.no,
  fetched direct 2026-09-06: "Rådets oppdrag for 2025 var å vurdere grunnlaget for
  investeringsstrategien", and it "la frem sin rapport på et åpent seminar 26. januar 2026").
- It is **standing, not ad hoc**: it "will annually receive an assignment and shall submit a concise
  report to the Ministry of Finance" (regjeringen.no id3150446, via search index — see the method
  note above).
- Its **first report is already public**, dated **23. januar 2026** (the PDF's own title block reads
  "23. januar 2026 … Ekspertrådet for Statens pensjonsfond utland RAPPORT 2025") and presented at an
  open seminar on **26 January 2026**. Both **2026-01-26 and 2027-01-25 are Mondays** — the same
  late-January slot.
- Its **2026 assignment is the bond question specifically**: the council "shall review the basis for
  the investment strategy with particular emphasis on bond investments," and is asked to assess "what
  is an appropriate regulation of bond investments in the management mandate of Norges Bank," with
  the report requested **by 25 January 2027** (id3150446, via search index).

**MIXED rather than SUPPORTED** because the entry's framing survives in one half and fails in the
other. It *is* a real dated node in the bond-benchmark process, and its 2026 remit is squarely the
bond index — that part holds. But "expert group appointed … in 2025" reads, in English, as a body
convened *for* this question; it is instead a routine annual advisory report from a council whose
prior edition is already on the record. That distinction is the whole reason the impact tier stays
**low**.

### Leg 2 — is 2027-01-25 a decision point? → **REFUTED**

It is the second of two advisory inputs, both commissioned on the same day.

The Ministry's **2026-02-27** follow-up to the council's 2025 report did two things at once: it asked
Norges Bank for "analyses and assessments of the bond investment strategy," and it gave the
Ekspertrådet its new 2026 assignment (id3150446, via search index). The Norges Bank half is the
letter this calendar already cites — NBIM's submission of **2026-09-01**, which states verbatim that
it answers "the Ministry of Finance's letter of **25 February 2026**." So the January report does not
*react* to NBIM's proposal from outside the process; it is the parallel second opinion the Ministry
ordered before NBIM answered.

What follows it is not a decision either. Press expectation is the annual GPF white paper in **spring
2027** (2026's was presented **2026-03-27**, and the Storting's finance committee reported it as
Innst. 418 S), then a parliamentary hearing and decision. NBIM itself defers twice over: it will
"present a proposal for an implementation plan **once the Ministry has taken a position** on the
advice," and any adjustment "should be made **gradually** out of consideration for market impact and
transaction costs."

### Leg 3 — is ~$80B a material Treasury demand shock? → **REFUTED at the flow level**

This is where primary data does the most work, and it cuts hard against the headline.

**Scale, from Treasury's own TIC table** (`ticdata.treasury.gov/Publish/mfhhis01.txt`, fetched direct
2026-09-06; the series available on that date ends **Dec 2025**, and that limit is stated rather than
papered over). Norway's reported holdings, in $B, Jan→Dec 2025:

| Jan | Feb | Mar | Apr | May | Jun | Jul | Aug | Sep | Oct | Nov | Dec |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 173.1 | 161.8 | 200.1 | 195.9 | 187.3 | 195.1 | 208.0 | 214.9 | 215.9 | 193.7 | 218.9 | 208.1 |

- **Intra-year range: $57.1B** ($161.8B → $218.9B), i.e. **71% of the entire proposed reduction**,
  inside twelve months, with no market event attributed to it.
- **Largest single month: +$38.3B** (Feb→Mar 2025). **Largest single-month decline: −$22.2B**
  (Sep→Oct 2025) — that one month alone is **28% of the $80B**, and it passed unremarked.
- **Norway is 2.24%** of the **$9,269.5B** foreign-held total (Dec 2025) — 4th-decimal-place
  arithmetic next to Japan's $1,185.5B and the UK's $863.1B.

**Absorption.** SIFMA puts Q2-2026 US Treasury average daily trading volume at **$1,170B/day**. The
proposed ~$80B is **6.8% of a single day's volume**. Executed "gradually" over three years (~756
sessions) it is **~$106M/day — 0.009% of ADV**.

**The measured base rate, from this repo's own tape.** The letter is dated **2026-09-01** and was made
public **2026-09-04**. Treasury's daily par-yield curve (home.treasury.gov, fetched direct
2026-09-06) across that window: 30Y **5.27 (9/1) → 5.27 (9/2) → 5.25 (9/3) → 5.24 (9/4)**; 10Y 4.79 →
4.79 → 4.77 → **4.78**; 20Y 5.27 → 5.27 → 5.25 → **5.25**. The long end **fell 3bp** across the
publication of the actual $80B number — on a session that also carried a **~3x payroll beat**. A
January 2027 advisory report that contains no new dollar figure and makes no decision has a weaker
claim on the tape than that did.

**One honest caveat, stated rather than resolved:** TIC country attribution is custodial, so Norway's
line is not identically the GPFG's Treasury book. It is nonetheless the only monthly primary
available, and NBIM's own "~$215B at end-June 2026" sits comfortably inside the 2025 range above,
which is the cross-check that matters.

### Leg 4 — can any of it execute inside 2026, or early 2027? → **REFUTED**

Two dated precedents bound the process, and they disagree only about how *slow* it is.

- **The bond index, 2017–2019 (the closest analogue).** Norges Bank advised on bond guidelines by
  letter of **2017-09-01**, the Ministry asked for more on **2017-10-26**, and NBIM's fuller
  submission followed on **2017-12-14**. The Ministry's position landed in **Meld. St. 20
  (2018–2019)** — roughly **18 months** after the first letter, and two annual white papers on.
- **The equity share, 2016–2017 (the faster path).** The Mork committee reported **Oct 2016**, the
  Ministry's white paper came **2017-03-31**, and the Storting approved 70% equities around **June
  2017** — about **8 months** from expert report to parliamentary approval, with phase-in over years
  after that.

Applied from **2027-01-25**: the fast path is a white paper in spring 2027 (only ~2 months after the
report — tighter than the Mork precedent) and a Storting decision **~June 2027**; the slow path is the
2028 white paper, ~**spring 2028**. Either way, a mandate change is followed by NBIM's
*then*-to-be-written implementation plan and a deliberately gradual transition. **Nothing in this
proposal can move a dollar in 2026**, which is the practical point for any US long-end ledger citing
it — and it is why `treasury-30y-bond-2026-09-10`'s D4 refinement was right to walk back D5's framing.

### Leg 6 — what residual risk is real? → **UNRESOLVED, and recorded as such**

Not flow: **signalling**. The GPFG is the largest sovereign wealth fund in the world (~$2.3T;
NOK 22,683bn at H1 2026), and a Ministry endorsement of "cut the government subindex from 70% to 50%"
is a template other official holders can cite in their own reviews. That is the one channel through
which a Norwegian advisory report could matter to US yields out of proportion to $80B.

This session could not date it, size it, or find a primary that even attempts to. It is therefore left
open with **no trigger attached** rather than converted into a plausible-sounding one — an honest
