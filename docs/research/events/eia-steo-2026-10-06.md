## Initial research

**The question.** What will the October 2026 STEO say, does its release move anything, and does any
of it change how we sit in the tracked names (NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL AMZN)?

**One-line verdict:** the September sibling proved the release hour is a non-event for crude; this
session proves it is a non-event for *our names* too, kills two risk legs this edition carried a
priori (the date, and the funding cliff), and re-points the document at the only thing it can
honestly do for the book — measure two numbers that belong to other ledgers.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md)
(macro-print mode; the house instruments are equity/earnings-keyed and cover no commodity or
publication). Primaries fetched direct **2026-09-08**: `eia.gov/outlooks/steo/release_schedule.php`

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is primary-sourced and doubly derived — SUPPORTED, and the September ledger's flagged
   date risk is REFUTED.** `release_schedule.php` (fetched 2026-09-08) lists **10/06/2026** for the
   October edition, with the release window stated as **12:00–12:15 pm ET**. The September sibling
   flagged a live risk here: the same page's rule ("release of the STEO on the first Tuesday
   following the first Thursday of each month") appeared to yield Tue **09-08** while the table said
   Wed **09-09**, and it logged the one-day gap as unresolved. **It is resolved, and the resolution
   is on the same page**: the rule carries a footnote that *if a Federal holiday falls on the Monday
   before the normal release date, the release is delayed until Wednesday*. September's normal
   Tuesday was 09-08; the Monday before it is **2026-09-07, Labor Day** — an event this calendar
   independently tracks as `labor-day-market-closure-2026-09-07`. The rule with its own footnote
   produces 09-09 exactly. There was never a table-vs-rule conflict, only an incompletely read rule.
   **For October the rule applies with no adjustment:** 2026-10-01 is a Thursday, the following
   Tuesday is **10-06**, and 10-05 is not a Federal holiday (Columbus Day 2026 falls 10-12). The
   same check clears the rest of 2026 — **11/10** (Nov 5 Thu → Tue Nov 10; Nov 9 not a holiday) and
   **12/08** (Dec 3 Thu → Tue Dec 8; Dec 7 not a holiday) both reproduce from the rule. Filed
   **estimate** on prefix grounds only — this calendar defines no `confirmed` prefix for the EIA —
   not on date doubt. **Consequence: kill switch #2 of the September ledger ("the date slips … every
   EIA date this calendar carries needs re-derivation from the rule rather than the table") is
   already answered for October, November and December before it can fire.**

2. **The 2026-09-30 funding cliff does not threaten this publication — REFUTED as a risk, twice
   over.** A priori this was the one genuinely new hazard the October edition carried that September's
   did not: `government-funding-deadline-2026-09-30` is filed **`high`** impact and sits six days
   before this release, and an appropriations lapse is the standard way a scheduled federal
   publication slips. It is dead on the primary. This repo's own ledger for that event records that
   **H.R. 6500, the *Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027*, was signed 2026-09-02 as
   Public Law 119-103**, its **section 106 funding agencies through 2026-12-11** — so **2026-10-06
   sits comfortably inside the funded window** and there is no lapse to delay anything. The
   belt-and-suspenders precedent points the same way: EIA published the **October 2025 STEO on
   2025-10-07**, seven days into the October 2025 lapse (`eia.gov/outlooks/steo/pdf/steo_charts.pdf`
   is dated "October 7, 2025"), consistent with EIA's standing announcement language that
   *"publications will continue to be released according to established schedules"* during a lapse.
   **Two honest qualifications, neither of which revives the risk for 10-06.** First, the version of
   `eia.gov/about/announcement.php` served on 2026-09-08 carries a statement dated **January 22,
   2018** — it is archived boilerplate, so it is cited as precedent language and *not* as a live
   2026 commitment. Second, the risk is not abolished, only moved: the **December 8 edition lands
   three days before the 2026-12-11 CR expiry**, and [`cr-expiry-2026-12-11`](cr-expiry-2026-12-11.md)
   explicitly declines to inherit September's aversion base case. That is why the December edition is
   proposed to the calendar in this PR with the exposure written into its own notes.

**Limits, honestly.**
- **This research is written one day before the September edition publishes, which is its single
  biggest limitation.** The 2026-09-09 STEO is the most informative thing that will happen to this
  ledger before the event, and it does not exist yet — so every content claim here reads the

  in both windows) is robust to all of that and is the only claim made. A precise effect size is
  not, and nothing should be sized off it.
- **A null is not proof of absence.** Eight editions cannot rule out a STEO large enough to matter

- **The electricity numbers remain secondary and are inherited, not re-derived.** The Texas
