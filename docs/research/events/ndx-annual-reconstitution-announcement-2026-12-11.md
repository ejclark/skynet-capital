## Initial research

### The question

This id reached the calendar as one proposal from the [`sp-quarterly-rebalance-effective-2026-12-21`](sp-quarterly-rebalance-effective-2026-12-21.md)
initial research (2026-09-09), read in full first per the *"your own event was proposed by others"* rule; the canonical file
is written by this session and the proposal is now inert. The [2027 sibling](ndx-annual-reconstitution-announcement-2027-12-10.md)
was researched the same day and is read in full here too.

That sibling did the heavy work on the mechanism: 19 cohorts, a null addition leg, a removal leg real pooled and — on its
reading — dead since 2016, and one live turnover number. It also wrote down, twice, what it could **not** do:

> *"**The cross-sectional control is missing.** … It is not a matched control group of similar Nasdaq names that were
> *not* reconstituted … a properly matched cohort would be better and needs a historical Nasdaq market-cap panel this
> house does not have."*

and registered the corresponding kill switch:

> *"A later lane builds a matched cross-sectional control — similar Nasdaq names not reconstituted, same sessions — and
> the removal effect disappears in the pre-2016 era too."*

**So: can this house build that control, and does the removal effect survive it?** Repeating the sibling's measurement on
the nearer cycle would have added a date and nothing else.

**One-line verdict:** the control does not need a market-cap panel — it is the index's **own continuing members**,
reachable by rolling the change table backwards — and the effect not only survives it but **sharpens**, un-killing the
modern era the sibling had written off, while cutting the sibling's one live turnover number roughly in half by giving
it the baseline it was missing.

   established, re-run here rather than inherited.** The methodology carries two schedules in one table:

   > *"Reconstitution Frequency: **Annually** … Reconstitution Reference Dates: **Last trading day of November** …
   > Reconstitution Announcement Dates: **After the close on the sixth trading day prior to the Reconstitution Effective
   > Date** … Reconstitution Effective Dates: **At market open on the first trading day following the third Friday in
   > December**"*

   > *"Rebalance Frequency: **Quarterly** … Rebalance Effective Dates: **At market open on the first trading day
   > following the third Friday in March, June, September, and December**"*

   In December the two coincide, so **one announcement carries both** — the point the proposal named first.

   | Check | Result |
   |---|---|
   | Third Friday of December 2026 → effective open → six sessions back | **2026-12-18** → **2026-12-21** → **2026-12-11**. The seven sessions in the window are 12-11, 12-14, 12-15, 12-16, 12-17, 12-18, 12-21; no holiday displaces any of them |
   | The count-back always lands on December's **second Friday** | **21 of 21** cycles 2005–2025, recomputed on SPY's session calendar. December 2026's second Friday is **2026-12-11** |
   | The **effective-date** half against **observed** reconstitutions (secondary change table) | **19 of 19** December blocks 2007–2025 land on the date the rule computes — 2007-12-24 through 2025-12-22, zero misses |

   The proposal's third `estimate` count — *"a forward application of a standing rule rather than a fetched schedule"* —
   is retired by the third row. What remains is the **prefix taxonomy** and the **unpublished 2026 release**.

2. **HEADLINE (a) — the matched cross-sectional control exists, it needs no market-cap panel, and it validates the
   benchmark the sibling used — SUPPORTED.** The sibling's control was a *time*-dimension one (the same names on other
   sessions in the same December window), which kills momentum and tax-loss stories but cannot answer "compared to
   what other names?". Rolling the change table backwards supplies the missing dimension: on each cycle's reaction
   session, the **continuing members** — in the index both before and after that reconstitution — are the same family,
   the same size band, the same session and the same survivorship attrition as the treated cohort.

   | Group, reaction session, excess vs QQQ, 2007–2025 | n | mean | median | signed | sign p |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | **Continuing members (the control)** | **1,369** | **−0.023%** | +0.009% | 681 / 1369 neg | **87.1%** |
   | Additions | 82 | −0.035% | +0.213% | 46 / 82 pos | 32.0% |
   | Removals | 73 | −0.564% | −0.639% | 48 / 73 neg | **1.0%** |

   The control row is the load-bearing one: **an unreconstituted NDX member's excess over QQQ on this session is a
   clean null.** So QQQ was an unbiased benchmark and the sibling's headline numbers are not an artefact of vehicle
   choice — a result that had to be established before the control could be used to *correct* anything. The removal row
   also independently reproduces the sibling's −0.579% / 49-of-75 / p=1.1% from a separately-built panel.

3. **HEADLINE (b) — demeaning by the matched control un-kills the modern-era removal effect — SUPPORTED, and it
   contradicts the sibling's "dead modern" reading.** Each name's excess minus its own cycle's continuing-member mean
   removes the cycle-common component, which is pure noise for a cross-sectional comparison:

   | Removal leg, reaction session | n | vs QQQ (the sibling's frame) | **demeaned by continuing members** |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | **All, 2007–2025** | 73 | −0.564%, 48/73, **p=1.0%** | **−0.591%, 50/73, p=0.2%** |
   | **2007–2015** | 30 | −1.070%, 23/30, **p=0.5%** | **−0.877%, 22/30, p=1.6%** |
   | **2016–2025** | 43 | −0.211%, 25/43, **p=36.0%** | **−0.392%, 28/43, p=6.6%** |
   | **2019–2025** | 35 | −0.161%, 19/35, **p=73.6%** | **−0.358%, 22/35, p=17.5%** |

   Mann-Whitney removals against the control directly: **p=0.5%** pooled, **p=0.2%** pre-2016, **p=29.1%** since 2016.
   **The sibling's kill switch is scored and does not fire** — the matched control was supposed to be the test that
   could dissolve the effect, and it does the opposite in every era. The modern era moves from **p=36.0%** ("dead") to
   **p=6.6%** ("marginal"), and 2019–2025 from p=73.6% to p=17.5%. The mechanism is unglamorous and worth stating so
   nobody reads it as a discovery: **variance reduction.** A common December shock hits treated and untreated names
   alike; subtracting it shrinks the noise without touching the signal.

   At **cycle level** the two eras stop separating at all:

   | Cycle-level matched diff (removal mean − continuing mean) | n | mean | median | sd | range | neg | sign p |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | 2007–2015 | 9 | −0.723% | −0.747% | 0.690pp | −1.570% … +0.585% | 8 / 9 | **3.9%** |
   | 2016–2025 | 10 | −0.716% | −0.493% | **1.823pp** | −4.435% … +1.583% | 7 / 10 | 34.4% |
   | **All** | 19 | **−0.720%** | −0.696% | — | — | **15 / 19** | **1.9%** |

   The era **means are identical** (MW pre vs post **p=56.8%**); what grew is the **spread**, by a factor of 2.6 in
   standard deviation. The dispersion test is only **MW p=12.1%** on 19 observations, so this is offered as the honest
   alternative reading rather than as a result: **name-weighted, the effect still looks like it is decaying; cycle-weighted,
   it looks flat with a wider spread.** At 19 cycles neither view wins. What does not survive either view is the
   confident claim that the effect is *gone*.

4. **HEADLINE (c) — the addition leg is a null on the matched control too, and the S&P magnitude still does not
   transfer — SUPPORTED, confirming the sibling.** Demeaned by continuing members: **−0.004%, 45/82 positive, sign
   p=44.0%**; Mann-Whitney additions against the control **p=68.4%**. Per cycle, the addition cohort's mean excess
   reached the S&P panel's **+2.31%** in **0 of 19** — the maximum was **+1.358%** (2014, n=4), the median cycle
   **+0.001%**, the worst **−3.296%** (2007). Demeaned, **0 of 19** as well. The one thing separating the legs is each
   other: MW additions vs removals **p=2.3%** pooled, **p=2.0%** pre-2016, **p=23.3%** since.

5. **HEADLINE (d) — the sibling's one live number is roughly halved once it has a baseline — SUPPORTED, and this is a
   correction rather than an addition.** That ledger read the implementation Friday as *"the flow is enormous in the
   names and nearly invisible in the vehicle"* — ~4.8× in the cohort against QQQ's 1.30×. But the implementation Friday
   is **quadruple witching**, which lifts every NDX member:

   | Implementation-Friday relative turnover (own trailing-60 median) | n | mean | median | above 1.0× |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | Additions | 82 | 6.241× | 4.897× | 82 / 82 |
   | Removals | 72 | 5.471× | 4.967× | 71 / 72 |
   | **Continuing members (the control)** | **1,368** | **2.144×** | **1.924×** | **1282 / 1368** |

   Mann-Whitney cohort vs control **p=0.0%** on both legs, so the reconstitution flow is unambiguously real. But the
   flow-specific quantity is the **ratio**, not the level: **cohort median over continuing-member median is 2.403×
   (mean 2.506×, minimum 1.419×, 18 of 19 cycles ≥ 1.5×)**. And the sibling's framing of the vehicle needs one word
   changed: QQQ at 1.30× is not blind to *index flow* so much as blind to **ordinary NDX-member witching**, since its
   own continuing members print 1.92× on the same session. The reaction session tells the same story at smaller size —
   cohort **1.269×** (adds) / **1.277×** (removes) against continuing members' **1.047×**, MW **p=0.0%** both — so the
   announcement's volume footprint is real and about **half** the size it appears against a 0.876× QQQ reading.

6. **A regime hypothesis that inverts the decay story — RAISED, NOT ESTABLISHED.** Splitting the 19 cycles at the

### Honest limits

**The roster source is secondary, and now doubly load-bearing.** The Wikipedia change table supplies both the treated
cohort *and*, through the roll-back, the control — so an error in it corrupts both sides rather than one. Three things
bound that risk and none eliminates it: the roll-back keeps the roster between 100 and 106 members across 19 years, all
117 December removals resolve inside the reconstructed roster, and the effective dates the methodology computes match
the table's own December blocks 19 of 19. A systematic omission of *intra-year* changes would still bias the control
membership, and nothing here would catch it. **Coverage is incomplete and asymmetric.** 82 of 116 additions (71%) and 73
of 119 removals (61%) had usable bars, against 1,369 of 1,821 control rows (75%); 61 tickers were delisted or acquired
and 74 rows were dropped by a deliberately strict name guard. Attrition is heaviest on removals that were later acquired,
so the removal panel is **survivor-tilted** — but the control is tilted the *same* way by the *same* filters, which is
the one respect in which this control beats the sibling's time-dimension one: to first order the bias differences out.
**The control is same-index, not never-a-member.** Continuing NDX members are the tightest matched group available
without a market-cap panel, and they are matched on family, size band, session and survivorship. They are *not* matched
on the characteristic that got a name removed — removals are small, recent losers by construction, and continuing
members on average are not. If mid-December systematically punishes the smallest NDX names, this control does not remove
it; the sibling's same-window time control does, and the two are complementary rather than redundant. A
market-cap-matched non-member cohort would beat both, and is the next lane's job. **The era and VIX splits are
underpowered and confounded with each other.** 9 versus 10 cycles; the dispersion test is p=12.1%, the direct VIX
comparison p=21.4%, and pre-2016 is also the high-VIX era at p=3.4%. Leg 6 is a hypothesis with a number attached, not a
result. **Two outlier cycles carry a lot of the modern spread** — 2023 (−2.942%) and 2024 (−4.435%, on a two-name
cohort) — and dropping them would flip the era comparison; they are kept because dropping outliers after seeing them is
the failure mode this house names in every ledger. **December 2026's sessions are projected, not observed**, since bar
data ends 2026-09-08; the projection is weekday arithmetic minus US market holidays, and the only holiday within a
fortnight of the window is Christmas Day 2026-12-25, a Friday, after it. **The date is `estimate`** and every
trading-adjacent statement above carries that label; Nasdaq's 2026 release does not exist yet, `symbols` is empty by
design, shorting is blocked house-wide, and no house playbook is index-flow-keyed.
