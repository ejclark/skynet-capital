### The question, plainly

### Leg 2 — the cross-ledger correction: the denominators are **45/23**, not 48/20 · **SUPPORTED**

The `fhfa-hpi-2026-10-27` ledger's headline finding is that FHFA release days can be FOMC **day 1** and
never day 2, at **17 of 68** since 2021, split **17 of 48 monthly / 0 of 20 quarterly**. Re-deriving it
from a fresh fetch of both calendars:

- **The seventeen dates reproduce exactly** — `2021-01-26 · 2021-04-27 · 2021-07-27 · 2022-01-25 ·
  2022-07-26 · 2023-01-31 · 2023-07-25 · 2023-10-31 · 2024-01-30 · 2024-04-30 · 2024-07-30 ·

- **The split does not.** FHFA's own release-type labels — read off both the live 2026/2027 tables and
  the archive's 271 rows — put every **quarterly** edition in **February, May, August and November**.
  Between 2021-01-01 and 2026-08-25 that is **23 quarterly slots** (four a year 2021–2025, three in
  2026), leaving **45 monthly**.

|---|---|---|---|

**The correction makes the sibling's point stronger, not weaker** — a third becomes 37.8%, and the
quarterly zero widens from 20 observations to 23. But it also has a consequence the sibling did not

the meeting and outside the October blackout.

### Leg 3 — **this ledger's own finding: how soft is the base this print sets** · **SUPPORTED**

Both siblings deferred their content question to this edition. Neither could answer the obvious
follow-up — *how much does a published FHFA monthly figure move afterwards?* — because the revision
tables live in PDFs and `pdftotext` is unavailable on this runner. **ALFRED, the clean route to
vintages, returned 404** (recorded in `probe-ref.blocked`). The route that worked is the plainest one:
**FHFA states the revision in prose, in the first paragraph of every monthly report**, e.g.

> U.S. house prices rose nationwide in May, up 0.3 percent from the previous month … **The previously
> reported 0.1 percent price decline in April remained unchanged.** *(2026-07-28 report)*

Thirty monthly report landing pages were fetched, 2023 → 2026; **28 carry a revision sentence** (the
two that do not are 2024-12-31 and the 2025-08-26 quarterly). Reading every one:

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| Reports stating **"remained unchanged"** | **10 of 28 (36%)** |
| Reports stating **a revision** | **18 of 28 (64%)** |
| Revisions of exactly **0.1 pp** | **17 of 18** |
| Largest revision ever observed | **0.2 pp** — 2026-03-31, December 2025 revised `+0.1% → +0.3%` |
| Direction | **14 upward · 4 downward** |
| Revisions ≤ 0.2 pp | **18 of 18 · 28 of 28 reports** |

The four downward revisions are 2023-04-25, 2023-06-27, 2024-06-25 and 2025-12-30. The three most
recent revisions are all upward (2026-03-31 +0.2, 2026-04-28 +0.1, 2026-06-30 +0.1), and the most
recent report of all (2026-07-28) left April unchanged.

**What this means for the print, stated narrowly.** The 09-29 report carries **July 2026** data and
will revise the previously-published **June** figure, which the 2026Q2 report put at **unchanged from
May** (index **442.53**). On these base rates the June figure has roughly a **1-in-3** chance of
standing, a **2-in-3** chance of moving **one tick**, and — on 28 of 28 observations — essentially no
chance of moving more than **0.2 pp**. That is the honest measure of how firm a base the two sibling
ledgers are building on: firm to about a tenth of a point, with an upward tilt.

**It licenses nothing.** A 0.1 pp revision to a two-month-old index that no model and no regulator
reads is not tradeable; it is a note about the *quality of the base*, and it is registered as
`FT-fhfa-hpi-2026-09-29-2` precisely so it is checkable rather than merely interesting.
