## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The [12-03 sibling](ism-services-2026-12-03.md) discovered this event was missing from the calendar,
filed it, and left it unresearched. So: is the **2026-10-05** date right, and — given that three
sibling ledgers have already measured this release's market reaction to nil — **does this slot have
any distinguishing property at all**, or is it the fourth copy of the same finding? And one question
none of the siblings could ask: their null was measured against *prints*. Does it survive a
**quantified surprise**?

**One-line verdict:** the date holds, and the slot does have a distinguishing property — it is the
**weakest** of the four, the precise inverse of 12-03 on the informational axis, while the
tradeability null gets substantially *harder* evidence here than it has had anywhere: the year's
largest hawkish Prices beat moved rates in the wrong direction, at the 14th percentile of their own
distribution.

### Method

**(a) A post-payrolls base-rate study — this session's new instrument.** Yahoo dividend-adjusted daily
closes for **TLT, ^TNX, SPY, QQQ, IWM**, 2015-01-01 → 2026-09-01 (n≈2,933 bars each), against every

session's |move| are percentile-ranked against that same calendar year's own distribution, and the
sign relationship between them is recorded. **n=129 pairs.** The point is attribution: if payroll
shocks bleed into the next session, no 10-05 move is readable as ISM at all.

**(b) Release-day measurement, re-run first-hand rather than carried.** The eight 2026 release dates

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is 2026-10-05 — SUPPORTED, from a primary fetched this session rather than carried.**
   The July-data PRNewswire release was fetched direct today; its rule paragraph reads verbatim:
   *"ISM® then compiles the report for release on the third business day of the following month…The
   one exception is in January, the report is released on the fourth business day of the month.
   \*Unless the New York Stock Exchange is closed."* Implemented holiday-aware and run over 2026, that
   rule reproduces **all nine** on-record release dates with nothing left over. October 2026 opens on a
   **Thursday**, so its first three business days are Thu **10-01** · Fri **10-02** · Mon **10-05**,
   with no NYSE closure in the window (Columbus Day is not an NYSE closure). **10-05 stands**, and the
   [11-04 sibling's](ism-services-2026-11-04.md) "10-06" remains the fourth business day and wrong.

2. **ismworld.org is SSO-gated across ROB content generally, not merely on its calendar — SUPPORTED,
   and it retires a search path.** Both siblings recorded that *the ROB calendar* 302s. This session
