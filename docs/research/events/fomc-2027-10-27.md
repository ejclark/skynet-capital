## Initial research

**The question, plainly.** The scanner handed this session `fomc-2027-10-27` as `never-assessed`,
and the process says to read every proposal for the id and then write the canonical file. Should
it? And if not, what is actually wrong here and how big is it?

**The verdict in one line.** Same meeting, so no second canonical file — but the duplicate is live
today regardless of that choice, it is one of four, and the detector that would have caught it does
not work as it was specified.

### Method

Primaries re-fetched in-session, never inherited from the proposal or the sibling. The Board's
calendar page was pulled direct (`federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm`, HTTP 200,
164,831 bytes, page stamp "Last Update: August 19, 2026") and the 2027 panel re-parsed from the raw
HTML. Calendar state was read through `scripts/market-events-read.mjs` — the same reader
`event-scan.mjs` uses — so what is reported as "live" is what the scanner itself sees, not a
directory listing. The alias audit is an all-pairs sweep over the 363 live events with three
candidate discriminators scored against a hand-verified truth set. VIX is the 2026-09-04 close
(14.53), the most recent the fetch returned on a Sunday.

### Leg 1 — are 2027-10-26 and 2027-10-27 the same meeting? SUPPORTED (same meeting)

The 2027 panel reads verbatim, from this session's own fetch:

> `January 26-27  March 16-17*  April 27-28  June 8-9*  July 27-28  September 14-15*  October 26-27  December 7-8*`

under the same panel's footnote `* Meeting associated with a Summary of Economic Projections`.
**October 26-27 is one entry and carries no asterisk** — one two-day meeting, no SEP, no dot plot.
Weekdays were computed rather than assumed: 2027-10-26 is a **Tuesday** and 2027-10-27 a
**Wednesday**, the Committee's standard Tuesday-Wednesday shape, so the decision lands on day two.
The sibling's phantom-meeting trap reproduced exactly — the panel's footer carries
`Note: A two-day meeting is scheduled for January 25-26, 2028.`, which a year-scoped parse returns
as a spurious ninth 2027 meeting — and was avoided by cutting the panel at its footer.

The page also states verbatim `Each meeting date is tentative until confirmed at the meeting
immediately preceding it`, which is why `estimate` is correct and why the promotion trigger is the
**2027-09-14/15 meeting**, not a better fetch. This lane flips no status without an `IR:`/`BLS:`/
`FED:` primary, and a page that disclaims its own dates is not that.

So the two ids name one meeting. That much of the sibling's note holds, independently re-derived.

### Leg 4 — does the detector the sibling named work? REFUTED as specified; a corrected spec is measured here

The sibling proposed, and explicitly left unbuilt: *"warn on any two entries of the same kind whose
dates are within two days and whose titles share a distinctive token."* Implemented literally and
run over the live calendar, it flags **42 pairs to find 4 — about 10% precision**. The noise is
structural, not tunable: consecutive Treasury auctions dominate it (`treasury-3y-note` vs
`treasury-10y-note`, and so on for most of a page), and those are genuinely distinct events. Ship
that as named and it is a gate firing on the wrong thing — the exact DX defect `CLAUDE.md` calls
out — and the first response would be carve-outs.

A shared-primary-URL rule alone is worse, not better: at a two-day window it flags **106 pairs at
~4%**, because citing the same publisher's *calendar* page (`treasury.gov`,
`census.gov/economic-indicators/calendar-listview.html`) is the norm for this corpus.

The **AND of three cheap signals** is what works — same `kind`, dates within **1** day, a shared
*deep* primary URL (host **plus path**, so a bare domain does not count), and title-token Jaccard
≥ 0.30:

| Spec | Flagged | True found | Precision |
|---|---|---|---|
| As named (kind + ≤2d + shared title token) | 42 | 4 / 4 | 10% |
| Shared deep URL alone (≤2d) | 106 | 4 / 4 | 4% |
| **kind + ≤1d + shared deep URL + Jaccard ≥ 0.30** | **6** | **4 / 4** | **67%** |
| Same, Jaccard ≥ 0.40 | 2 | 2 / 4 | 100% |

Jaccard ≥ 0.30 is the operating point: full recall at six items a human can eyeball. Raising the
threshold buys perfect precision by dropping the presidents-day pair (0.36) and this one (0.33) —
recall is what the warning is for. The two false positives are the informative ones and both are
same-day-different-event, which any such rule must tolerate: `sifma-japan-early-close` vs
`sifma-uk-bond-market-closure` (two markets, one holiday date) and `consumer-confidence-2027-10-26`
vs `fhfa-hpi-2027-10-26` (two prints, one morning). A warning, never a red gate.

### Honest limits

- **The truth set is hand-verified and small (n=4).** The precision figures in leg 4 are measured against duplicates this session found by eye; a fifth alias the sweep missed would lower recall for every spec in the table equally, but the *ranking* — 67% against 10% and 4% — is what the recommendation rests on, and that is robust to one more true pair.
- **`--validate` was read, not modified.** No detector was built. This lane is not the owner of a scanner gate, and shipping one from a research PR is exactly the out-of-scope move the sibling declined for the same reason.
- **The duplicate is not fixed.** It is measured, named and escalated as **#1969** (`bottleneck`), carrying all four pairs, the detector table and the two open questions. Every path that actually removes it — deleting a proposal, or deleting a canonical file plus its ledger — is forbidden to this lane verbatim, which is why the escalation is the deliverable rather than a diff.
- **Nothing here is tradeable.** `symbols` is empty, no house playbook is rates-keyed, the event is `estimate`, and the date is 416 days out. Three independent reasons the calls are all stand-aside.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance.** `fomc-2027-10-27` is an **alias key, not an event**. The meeting it names — FOMC
Oct 26-27 2027, no SEP, decision Wednesday 2027-10-27 at 14:00 ET, `estimate` until the
2027-09-14/15 meeting confirms it — is researched at
[`fomc-2027-10-26.md`](fomc-2027-10-26.md), and that ledger carries whatever this meeting is worth.
This session therefore writes **no** `src/domain/market-events/fomc-2027-10-27.json`, on the ground
that a second canonical file entrenches a duplicate rather than resolving one; the id is left
tracked by the standing-in proposal, which is the cheaper of the two things to prune later. Every
horizon is stand-aside, and that is a statement about this id, not a view on the meeting.

The correction this stance rests on: the duplicate is **live, not inert**, and it is one of **four**
live alias pairs — so the gap is a class the calendar will keep producing, not a naming quibble
between two lanes. `estimate` widens caution and licenses no entry.

**Kill switches.**

- **The whole document dies** if Oct 26 and Oct 27 2027 turn out to be two meetings rather than one two-day meeting — i.e. the Board publishing a 2027 calendar whose October entry is not the single range `October 26-27`.
- **The "decline the canonical" call dies** if the sibling `fomc-2027-10-26.json` is ever deleted or renamed, which would leave the meeting untracked canonically and make writing this file correct rather than duplicative.
- **The detector recommendation dies** if a fifth or sixth live alias pair is found that the Jaccard ≥ 0.30 spec does not flag, since the spec is recommended for its recall and nothing else.
- **The "class, not a one-off" claim dies** if the three both-canonical pairs in leg 3 are resolved and no new pair appears for a quarter — that would make aliasing a burst artifact of the 2026-09-05/06 fan-out rather than a standing gap.

Two predictions carry score-by dates and are registered in
