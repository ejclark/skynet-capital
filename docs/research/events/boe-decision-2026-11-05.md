itself asks disagrees with the price.** November is priced **54.2% hike / 45.9% hold / 0.0% cut**

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The September ledger established that the 09-17 meeting's rate decision is dead information and its
payload is a once-a-year balance-sheet number. **November is the inverse.** There is no balance-sheet
decision, and the rate decision is the most genuinely contested one on the UK curve. So the question
is: **is the strip's 54.2% right, and does anything about the answer reach a US-only,
equity-and-earnings-keyed book?**

**One-line verdict:** the strip is pricing a hike that neither the Bank's own surveyed participants,
nor the professional forecasting community, nor the Bank's own published modal projection expects —
and November is a **forecast round**, which means the Bank has to publish the arithmetic that
adjudicates the disagreement rather than merely voting on it.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **November is the only two-sided meeting on the strip, and December is conditional on it.**
   SUPPORTED, from a fetched strip. centralbank.watch (BoE page, fetched today, data stamped
   **2026-09-08**), against a Bank Rate of **3.75%**:

   | Meeting | Cut | Hold | Hike | Implied |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 2026-09-17 | 0.0% | **93.1%** | 6.9% | 3.77% |
   | **2026-11-05** | 0.0% | 45.9% | **54.2%** | **3.89%** |
   | 2026-12-17 | 0.0% | 5.4% | **94.6%** | 4.11% |

   The December column is **cumulative** — "higher by 12-17" — so the ~37bp the strip carries across
   the three meetings is really one question asked twice: *November, or December?* September answers
   nothing (a 93.1% hold), and December's 94.6% is not an independent forecast. **This meeting is
   where the strip's conviction is actually tested**, and it is the only one of the three whose
   outcome a coin flip would describe. Note the **0.0% cut** column at every horizon: the
   distribution is one-sided, which matters for leg 5.

2. **The Bank's own surveyed market participants expect no hike in 2026 at all — and then cuts.**
   SUPPORTED, **BoE primary, fetched**. The **Market Participants Survey, July 2026** (survey dates
   **15–17 July**, **78 respondents**) medians: **30 July MPC 3.75% · end-2026 3.75% · one year ahead
   3.50% · two years 3.25% · three years 3.25%**, with **over 90%** probability on 3.75% after the
   July meeting. Read plainly: the same institutions that trade the strip, asked directly by the Bank,
   put the end-2026 rate exactly where it is now and then have it **falling**. The same survey's
   median for the annual APF gilt reduction is **£50bn** — which independently corroborates, from a
   BoE primary, the figure the [`boe-decision-2026-09-17`](boe-decision-2026-09-17.md) ledger could
   only cite to press.

3. **The professional forecasting community says the same thing, from a different sample.**
   SUPPORTED, press-relayed but specific and dated. A **Reuters poll conducted 13–18 August 2026**,
   **64 economists**: **56 of 64 (87.5%)** expect Bank Rate unchanged at **3.75%** for the rest of
   2026 — *up from 83% the prior month* — with **six** expecting a hike by year-end and **two** a cut,
   and **no economist** forecasting a change at the September meeting. Converted to a probability of
   *any* hike by December, the profession is at roughly **9%**; the strip is at **94.6%**. Two
   differently-constructed surveys (one the Bank's own market-side panel, one a sell-side/academic
   economist panel) landing in the same place is the strongest form this evidence takes — they are
   wrong together only if the *tape* has information neither has, which is exactly leg 6's objection.

4. **The Bank's own published modal projection undershoots the target while conditioned on a curve
   that hikes — which is the Bank's standard way of saying the curve is too high.** SUPPORTED on the
   numbers, with a provenance caveat named in *Honest limits*. The **July 2026** MPR's central
   projection: CPI **peaks at 3.2% in Q4 2026**, falls to **1.7% in Q1 2028** (the two-year horizon)
   and is **1.9% in Q3 2029** (three years); GDP growth **1.1%** at Q3 2026 and Q3 2027, then **1.7%**
   and **1.6%**. The conditioning assumption is the market path, described in the same source as
   factoring in *"a high chance of two rate hikes by Q3 2027 as priced by financial markets."* An MPR
   projection is a conditional forecast, so a central path that sits **below 2%** at both the
   two- and three-year horizons *given* those hikes is the Committee stating, in its own arithmetic,
   that delivering the priced path overshoots the tightening the target needs. **And the curve has
   moved up since**: the July conditioning path carried ~two hikes by **Q3 2027**; the strip now
   carries ~1.5 by **December 2026**. Conditioned on today's curve, the November round should
   undershoot **more**, not less — unless the energy shock has done real second-round damage in the
   intervening quarter. That is a testable claim with a published venue: registered as
   **FT-boe-decision-2026-11-05-2**.

5. **The rate side's live question is arithmetic, not sentiment: a hike needs two switchers.**
   SUPPORTED on the count, MIXED on the direction. The **30 July 2026** minutes (fetched): **6–3** to
   hold at 3.75%, with **Megan Greene, Catherine L Mann and Huw Pill** each preferring **+25bp to
   4.00%**. The majority's premise is stated verbatim — *"There is little evidence so far to suggest
   such effects, and there have continued to be clear signs of underlying disinflation in recent
   data"* — alongside a hawkish risk assessment: *"the risks to the inflation outlook are tilted to
   the upside relative to the central projection."* So the strip's 54.2% is a bet that **two** of six
   majority members switch within one forecast round. The dissent has grown at three consecutive
   meetings, which is the honest counterweight and the reason this is registered at **medium**; but
   the domestic data since July has run the majority's way (July headline **2.9%** was almost entirely
   energy — housing/household services **4.1%** on a **14.7%** gas rise after an Ofgem cap change —
   over **core flat at 2.6%** and **services falling 3.6% → 3.4%**, with regular pay **3.5%**,
   unemployment **4.9%** and vacancies **707k** falling). Registered as
   **FT-boe-decision-2026-11-05-3**, and deliberately scoped to the *dissent count*, not the rate, so
   it can be scored independently of FT-…-1: a **5–4 hold** kills this test while the rate test passes.

6. **The honest objection: both surveys are older than the tape they are being used to contradict.**
   This is the leg that keeps the call at medium. The MaPS was taken **15–17 July**; the Reuters poll
   closed **18 August**; the strip is **08 September**. Between those dates the UK long end made a
   **28-year high** — the 30-year gilt at **5.89%**, and a **2026-09-08** DMO sale of £4.25bn of the
   5.375% 2056 at **5.8168%**, the highest yield at any DMO auction since the office was founded in
   1998 (press-cited, per the September ledger's leg 5). A strip that has repriced *after* both
   surveys closed is not straightforwardly "wrong"; it may simply be the only one of the three reads
   that has seen September. The mitigation is a dated one rather than an argument: the **next Market
   Participants Survey**, published around the 09-17 round, is a like-for-like refresh, and its
   end-2026 median is the single cleanest update to this ledger's central call. It is written into the
   signals list as such.

7. **The Budget lands eight days before the vote and pushes the same way — with the name of the
   Chancellor genuinely unresolved in the sources.** MIXED, and the mixedness is about provenance,
   not direction. The **28 October 2026** Budget carries an OBR Economic and Fiscal Outlook the same
   day (statutory), and is previewed across UK professional publishers as **tax-raising** — Labour's
   third such Budget in three years after **£41.5bn** (2024) and **£26bn** (November 2025) — with the
   OBR expected to show real GDP growth around **1.4%**. Fiscal tightening delivered eight days before
   a forecast round is a **disinflationary input to that forecast round**, which cuts against the
   hike. **Two independent press sources contradict each other on who delivers it** — several name
   **John Healey** as the new Chancellor (and the September lane filed the event on that reading),
   while at least one 2026-09-07 piece still names **Rachel Reeves**. No gov.uk or obr.uk page was
   fetched this session, so **this ledger carries the date and the direction and does not carry the
   name.** The event stays the September lane's `NEWS:`-prefixed proposal; nothing here upgrades it.

8. **The run-in is unusually clean, fully dated, and primary-sourced.** SUPPORTED, both dates fetched
   today. **UK labour market: 2026-10-20 07:00 London** — gov.uk statistics announcement, verbatim
   *"Release date: 20 October 2026 7:00am (confirmed)"*. **UK CPI (September data): 2026-10-21 07:00
   London** — ONS release page, *"Release date: 21 October 2026 7:00am"*, *"This release is not yet
   published."* Then the Budget 10-28, then the vote 11-05. Two contrasts with September matter.
   First, **timing**: September's committee got its labour print on 09-15 and its CPI on 09-16 and
   voted on 09-17 — a 48-hour window; November's gets both **sixteen days out** and a fiscal
   set-piece in between, which is enough room for a data surprise to actually change a forecast round
   rather than merely a vote. Second, **coverage**: the November labour-market release is **2026-11-10**
   and the October-data CPI **2026-11-18**, both *after* the vote, so 10-20 and 10-21 are genuinely
   the last reads. Both proposed to the calendar in this PR.

9. **September's payload is not repeated here, and a pulse should not go looking for it.**
   SUPPORTED, primary, and inherited. The **19 June 2026** APF gilt-sales Market Notice (fetched by
   the September lane) puts the **annual QT review** at *"the MPC meeting on 17 September 2026."* The
   BoE dates page (fetched today) confirms **November is a Monetary Policy Report month** and
   September is not. So November's document set is: summary, minutes, vote, **full MPR**, press
   conference — and the MPR is the payload. The symmetry is worth stating once: **September is a
   settled rate with an unpriced balance-sheet number; November is a contested rate with a published
   forecast.** A quarterly gilt-sales *schedule* may accompany the meeting as an operational notice;
   that is a schedule, not a decision, and no ledger should treat it as one.

10. **No tracked symbol carries a sterling channel — and no play is proposed.** SUPPORTED, and this
    is where the honesty sits rather than a hedge. `symbols: []`. The house playbooks (S1/S2/E1/S3/S4

11. **The attribution problem is worse than September's, and the reason is political, not monetary.**

### What plays the conditions support (date estimate)

**None.** No entry, exit, hedge or size is keyed to 2026-11-05. Three rules and one branch:

- **Read-only, and at the arithmetic.** Unlike September, this meeting publishes a forecast and holds
  a press conference, so there is real content — but the content that adjudicates anything is the
  **projection table**, not the summary's adjectives.
- **Search order when it lands** — (1) the **modal CPI at the two-year horizon** against July's
  **1.7%**; (2) the **conditioning market path** it was run on, since a lower undershoot on a higher
  path is not the same signal as a lower undershoot on the same path; (3) the **vote split** against
  6–3; (4) whether the minutes tie the energy shock to **wages**, the sentence that converts a supply
  shock into a cycle.
- **The anti-attribution rule.** Any read of the 11-05 US tape that has not first accounted for the
  **11-03 midterms**, the **11-04 refunding** and the **11-06 jobs** print is not entitled to its
  attribution. Leg 11 is the whole of it.
- **The branch to pre-decide** — if the MPR's two-year modal CPI comes in **at or above 2.0%** on a
  path no higher than July's, the Bank has revised its own disinflation story and legs 2–4 lose their
  spine together. The consequence for this book is not a sterling trade; it is that the **global
  term-premium** leg of the `fomc-2026-09-16` tiering gets *heavier*, and that is where it should be
  written.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `estimate`):** **stand aside completely, and take the under on the strip.** This book
holds nothing with a sterling-rates channel and none is proposed. The ledger's substantive positions
are **analytical**, not positional. First and centrally, it expects a **hold at 3.75% on 2026-11-05**
against a price of **54.2% higher** — because the Bank's own market panel (end-2026 median 3.75%,
then cuts), a 64-economist poll (56 of 64 for no change in 2026) and the Bank's own July modal
projection (1.7% at two years on a path that hikes) all point the same way, and because the strip's
alternative requires **two of six** majority members to switch inside one forecast round. Second, it
expects the **November MPR's two-year modal CPI to print below 2.0%** — the arithmetic by which a
forecast round tells a curve it is too high, and the reason this meeting's payload is the projection
rather than the vote. Third, it expects the **hawkish bloc to stay at three or fewer**, which is
scored separately from the rate so that a 5–4 hold registers as the warning it would be. Fourth, it
commits the calendar to an **anti-attribution discipline** stronger than September's: on a date two
days after the **US midterms**, one day after the **quarterly refunding** and one day before the
**October jobs report**, no US rates or risk move may be credited to the Bank of England until those
three have been ruled out. The call is **medium**, not high, for one stated reason: **both surveys
closed before the September gilt blow-out that the strip has seen.** Estimates widen caution and
license nothing.
