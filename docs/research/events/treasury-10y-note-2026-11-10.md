## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **This auction's setup has never happened before, and every piece of it is ordinary — the
research value is in having decomposed it rather than in the auction.** At 1:00pm ET on Tuesday
2026-11-10 Treasury sells the November refunding's **new** 10-Year note (expected **$42B**, not
published until **2026-11-04**), four and a half hours after the **October CPI print** at 08:30 ET,
on the **last bond session before the market shuts for Veterans Day**. In the 18 November 10-Year
auctions on record (2009–2025) the October-data CPI has **never** shared the auction's session — it
landed a median 7–8 days later. **2026 is the first collision**, and it is a calendar accident: BLS
put October CPI on Tuesday 11-10 (**confirmed**) and Veterans Day falling on a Wednesday pushes the
10-Year leg to Tuesday. Each component was measured this session against primaries and none of them
bites. **CPI-day auctions:** 30 since 2018; against each one's four nearest non-CPI 10-Year auctions
the bid-to-cover delta is **+0.004** and the indirect share is **+2.75pp** — no penalty, a mild tilt
toward end investors. **The Tuesday-Nov-10 slot with a shut Wednesday behind it:** **3/3** (2009,
2015, 2020 — all 10-Year new issues on exactly this date), clearing bid-to-cover **2.570** against
**2.506** for the other twelve Novembers. **The closure gap:** those three moved **−5 / 0 / −10bp**
from the auction close to the next session, mean absolute **5.7bp** against **4.6bp** for ordinary
next-day rolls, **max 10bp against 30bp**. What is left is a routine $42B new issue whose size is
over-determined (**11/11 at $42B since 2024-02-07**) and whose only unpublished number arrives
**2026-11-04**. Date is `estimate`, `symbols: []`, and nothing here is a trade.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Three sibling ledgers already own most of what a 10-Year auction doc could say — the September
reopening ([`treasury-10y-note-2026-09-09.md`](treasury-10y-note-2026-09-09.md)), the October one
([`treasury-10y-note-2026-10-07.md`](treasury-10y-note-2026-10-07.md)), and, for the corridor,
[`treasury-30y-bond-2026-11-12.md`](treasury-30y-bond-2026-11-12.md), which settled a day ago that
the Veterans Day closure does **not** disrupt the November refunding block. Repeating any of them
would be a non-question, and this doc does not re-litigate the block-shape finding — it inherits it
and tests the 10-Year leg's own distinguishing feature.

That feature is a **session**, not a schedule. On Tuesday 2026-11-10 four things land on one bond
session, and then the market shuts:

- **08:30 ET** — the October-data **CPI** print (`cpi-2026-11-10`, **confirmed**, high impact).
- **during the day** — the **US–China tariff truce expiry** (`us-china-tariff-truce-expiry-2026-11-10`,
  estimate, high impact) and a **CRWV** earnings print (estimate, from the earnings calendar).
- **1:00pm ET** — this auction: the quarter's **new** 10-Year benchmark, expected $42B.
- **Wednesday 2026-11-11** — the bond market is **closed** for Veterans Day, so the session's own
  close is the last cash mark for two calendar days.

So the question is: **does selling the quarter's benchmark 10-Year into a CPI session, with no
next-day cash market to digest it, do anything measurable to the auction?** Three intuitive,
testable readings:

1. A high-impact macro print four hours earlier should show up as **weaker or more erratic demand**
   at 1:00pm.
2. With no next-day session, dealers cannot lay off inventory, so the auction should **price with a
   larger concession** and the tape should **gap** across the closure.
3. This combination is **unprecedented**, so the prior record cannot speak to it.

**One-line verdict.** Reading 3 is true and readings 1 and 2 are refuted. The combination has never
occurred in the 2009–2026 record; each of its components has, repeatedly, and none of them moves
demand. What remains is an ordinary $42B refunding new issue whose only unpublished number lands
**2026-11-04**.

- **The schedule** — `home.treasury.gov/system/files/221/Tentative-Auction-Schedule.pdf`, plain

  Re-fetched independently of the proposal that discovered this event.
- **Auction history** — `api.fiscaldata.treasury.gov` `auctions_query`,
  `original_security_term:eq:10-Year, security_type:eq:Note, auction_date:gte:2009-01-01`, **318
  rows**, of which **103 are 10-Year TIPS** and were dropped, leaving **215 nominal** (**214** with

- **The tape** — `home.treasury.gov` daily par yield curve CSVs, one file per year **2009–2026** (18

- **CPI release dates** — `bls.gov`'s own schedule and archived-release pages, **2009–2026**.

**Two source-reliability notes, both recorded rather than papered over.**

*TIPS contamination.* `auctions_query`'s `security_type` does **not** separate 10-Year TIPS from
nominal notes — both come back as `Note`, and the TIPS rows ($21–25B at ~2% yields) corrupt any
naive average. Filtering on a non-null `ref_cpi_on_issue_date` separates them; every statistic below
is **nominal-only**. This is the same trap the 30-Year sibling recorded for its own series, and it
was reproduced independently here rather than inherited.

*bls.gov is blocked from this runner.* Plain `curl` against `www.bls.gov` and `download.bls.gov`
returned **403** on every attempt (recorded in `probe-ref.blocked`) — the documented blind spot
[`ppi-2026-11-13.md`](ppi-2026-11-13.md) already carries for this lane. The CPI dates below were
read from the same BLS pages through the harness's own fetcher, which is not subject to that block.
They are therefore a **BLS primary read through a second-hand renderer**, not a locally parsed one:
the 2026 schedule it returned agrees exactly with the independently sourced `cpi-2026-11-10` calendar
entry (checked 2026-08-15), and the 2025 series it returned reproduces the shutdown distortion the
CPI ledger documents (September data delayed to **10/24/2025**, October data **absent entirely**),
which is a strong internal consistency check but not a substitute for parsing the page here.

**Bidder shares are competitive-only.** Every indirect/direct/dealer percentage below is a share of
**competitive** awards, with SOMA add-ons excluded from the denominator. Dividing by
`total_accepted` instead makes the three shares sum to ~81% and understates each one, because the
add-on (leg 10) is a fifth of the line.

**Cycle vocabulary.** A nominal 10-Year is sold three times — a **NEW** issue in the refunding month
(February, May, August, November), then reopened in each of the two following months. **2026-11-10
is a NEW issue**: the tentative schedule's row carries no `R`, and **17 of the 18** November 10-Year
auctions since 2009 were new issues (2019 ran an extra reopening on 11-05 alongside its 11-06 new
issue).

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The date, security and settlement are what the calendar says — SUPPORTED, primary.** The
tentative schedule's row reads verbatim `10-Year NOTE / Wednesday, November 04, 2026 / Tuesday,
November 10, 2026 / Monday, November 16, 2026`, with no `R` and the PDF's own legend confirming
`R --denotes reopening`. Settlement is Monday 11-16 rather than the customary 15th because
2026-11-15 is a Sunday; the note will be dated 2026-11-15 and back-dated, and its first interest
payment date will be **2027-05-15**. 1:00pm ET is Treasury's standing coupon convention and is
**not** separately sourced. Status correctly stays `estimate`: a tentative schedule is tentative by
construction, and the 11-04 refunding statement is what fixes it. **This ledger deliberately
predicts no CUSIP.** The October sibling could predict one because a reopening's CUSIP already
exists; a new issue's does not, and guessing at the 912828/91282C sequence would be inference
dressed as a reading.

**2. This is announced by the refunding statement on 11-04 — SUPPORTED.** The schedule's own
announcement column puts this note at **Wednesday, November 04, 2026**, the quarterly refunding
([`treasury-refunding-2026-11-04.md`](treasury-refunding-2026-11-04.md)), not at any nearer coupon
announcement. Nothing between today and 11-04 publishes this auction's size, coupon or CUSIP.

**3. The 10-Year is the only auction on 11-10 — SUPPORTED, and it is a detail worth having.**
Reading the tentative schedule's whole November block by column: **Monday 11-09** carries the 3-Year
note plus 6-, 13- and 26-week bills; **Tuesday 11-10** carries *only* this auction — its 4-, 8- and
17-week bill lines are **announcements** (those bills auction 11-12) and **settlements** (of the
11-03/11-05 sales); **Thursday 11-12** carries the 30-Year plus three bill auctions plus a coupon
announcement. So the 1:00pm print is the **sole Treasury supply event of its session**, which is the
opposite of the crowding a reader might assume from the corridor's density. The crowding on 11-10 is
macro, not supply.

**4. The CPI collision is genuinely unprecedented for this leg — SUPPORTED, and it is the finding
that makes this event worth its own document.** Pairing every November 10-Year auction with that
year's October-data CPI release date:

The gap has been closing for a decade as BLS moved the release earlier in the month, and 2021 and
2022 came within a day of it. **2026 is the first same-session pairing**, and it is an accident of
two independent calendars: BLS's Tuesday 11-10 release date (confirmed) and Veterans Day landing on
a Wednesday, which pushes the 10-Year leg from its usual Wednesday to Tuesday. Neither mechanism
knows about the other.

**5. A CPI print in the same session does not weaken 10-Year demand — REFUTED as a hypothesis, on a
matched control.** Since 2018 there have been **30** nominal 10-Year auctions on a CPI release day
(none before 2018-04-11 — the two calendars simply did not overlap in the earlier regime). Raw
pooled means are misleading here, because CPI-day auctions all sit in the post-2018 window when
indirect shares were structurally higher, so this session used a **matched control**: each CPI-day
auction against the mean of its **four nearest non-CPI 10-Year auctions** in the same series.

| Comparison | n | Bid-to-cover | Indirect share |
|---|---|---|---|
| CPI-day auctions vs their 4 nearest non-CPI neighbours | 30 | **+0.004** | **+2.75pp** |
| (sign test: above its own control) | | 11/30 | 18/30 |
| Pooled 2017+, CPI-day | 30 | 2.493 | 67.3% |
| Pooled 2017+, non-CPI | 88 | 2.480 | 64.1% |

**What this establishes:** the effect on bid-to-cover is **indistinguishable from zero** — a +0.004
mean with only 11 of 30 above their own control's mean is a distribution centred on nothing, with a
few strong prints offsetting a mild tendency to sit just under. The indirect tilt (**+2.75pp**,
18/30 positive) is the more consistent reading and points the *safe* way: on CPI days a slightly
larger share goes to end investors and less is left with dealers. **What it does not establish:** any
directional edge, and nothing about the *tail* (stop vs. when-issued), which is the sharper demand
metric and is not in Treasury's published dataset.

**6. The session is more volatile, and that is the entire measurable effect — SUPPORTED, and it is
small.** The 10-Year par yield's move from the prior close to the auction-day close:

| Auction-day class | n | Mean absolute move |
|---|---|---|
| CPI same session | 30 | **4.7 bp** |
| No CPI | 184 | **3.8 bp** |

A **0.9bp** difference — real, in the expected direction, and far too small to be a position. It is
a reason to be flat into an 08:30 print, which is the standing rule anyway
([`cpi-2026-11-10.md`](cpi-2026-11-10.md): *"Never — hold an unhedged paper position across the
print"*), not a reason to have a view on the 1:00pm auction.

**7. The Tuesday-November-10 slot is precedented 3/3 and shows no demand penalty — SUPPORTED.** The
30-Year sibling established the block shape from its own end; this leg tests the same identity from
the 10-Year's, independently. In every year Veterans Day fell on a Wednesday, the 10-Year new issue
auctioned on **exactly 2026's date**:

| Class | n | Years | Bid-to-cover | σ | Indirect | Dealer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Veterans-Day-Wednesday (10Y on Tue Nov 10)** | 3 | 2009, 2015, 2020 | **2.570** | 0.200 | 54.2% | 35.1% |
| Holiday inside the block (any weekday) | 5 | 2009, 2014, 2015, 2020, 2025 | 2.532 | 0.164 | 54.9% | 31.6% |
| No holiday inside the block | 12 | the rest | 2.506 | 0.169 | 58.7% | 28.7% |

Demand on the exact-shape years ran **higher**, not lower (2.570 vs 2.506). At n=3 that is not an
effect and is not claimed as one; what it supports is the null — **there is no measured penalty for
selling the benchmark 10-Year the day before the market shuts.** November as a whole is likewise not
a weak refunding month: new-issue bid-to-cover by month, 2009–2026, is February **2.570** / May
**2.594** / August **2.538** / November **2.514** — a 8bp-of-cover spread across 18 years.

**8. There is no precedent for a large adverse gap across the closure — SUPPORTED directionally,
small-n by construction.** Leg 7 covers the auction; this covers what happens after it, which is the
reading that actually worries a holder. Across **4,424** par-curve sessions, only **four** 10-Year
auctions have had a weekday closure immediately behind them — and three of them are the exact-shape
Novembers:

| Auction | Next session | 10Y move |
|---|---|---|
| 2009-04-09 (Good Friday) | 2009-04-13 | **−8.0 bp** |
| 2009-11-10 (Veterans Day) | 2009-11-12 | **−5.0 bp** |
| 2015-11-10 (Veterans Day) | 2015-11-12 | **0.0 bp** |
| 2020-11-10 (Veterans Day) | 2020-11-12 | **−10.0 bp** |
| *All four* | | mean abs **5.7 bp**, max **10.0 bp** |
| *Ordinary next-session roll* | 210 | mean abs **4.6 bp**, max **30.0 bp** |

All four were rallies. **This does not establish that closures damp the 10-Year** — four
observations, three of them from the same calendar quirk, cannot separate an effect from three quiet
Novembers, and the signed direction is certainly not a forecast. What it establishes is the only
form of the claim a stance can rest on: **no precedent for a large adverse gap into or across this
closure.** It agrees with two prior independent measurements — the 30-Year sibling's par-curve read
on its own leg, and [`sifma-bond-market-closure-2026-11-11`](sifma-bond-market-closure-2026-11-11.md)'s
ETF-side finding that the session after a bonds-shut day moves **0.946×** its trailing-20 median
across 42 closures. Three instruments, three series, same conclusion.

**9. The size is over-determined at $42B — SUPPORTED, and the proposal understated it.** Eleven
consecutive nominal 10-Year new issues have printed **$42B**, from **2024-02-07** through
**2026-08-12**, across clearing yields **3.960% → 4.683%** — a 72bp span the size did not respond
to. The proposal that discovered this event dated the streak from 2025-02-12 and counted seven; the
full pull says eleven, and the correction runs in the *conservative* direction (a longer flat run is
a stronger null). Reopenings corroborate from the other side: **20 consecutive $39B** reopenings
since 2024-03-12. Both sit inside the fact the coupon-announcement siblings established — **no
nominal coupon or FRN size has changed anywhere since April 2024**, ~29 months and ten quarterly
refundings, each carrying *"maintaining … for at least the next several quarters."* The number is
**not published today** (`sb0590`'s anticipated-size table ends at Oct-26) and becomes published
**2026-11-04**. The eleven give the usable demand band: bid-to-cover mean **2.466**, σ **0.091**,
range **2.32 – 2.60**; competitive shares indirect **67.6%** / direct **19.2%** / dealer **13.2%**.

**10. The headline supply number understates the line by about $11B — SUPPORTED, and it is the
number a reader is most likely to have wrong.** Total accepted has exceeded the offering amount at
**15 of 15** nominal 10-Year new issues since 2023, because Treasury adds SOMA roll-in awards on top
of the announced size. On the $42B era the add-on has run **$6.54B – $14.83B**, mean **$11.38B**,
with accepted/offering averaging **1.271×**. The most recent new issue (2026-08-12) offered $42B and
accepted **$52.62B**. The last nominal 10-Year new issue with a zero add-on was **2015-02-11**. The
10-Year's add-on is roughly **double the 30-Year's** in dollar terms ($11.38B vs $5.75B) — worth
carrying, because "the November refunding sells $42B of 10s" is off by a quarter.

**11. The corridor is dense, and its density is macro rather than supply — SUPPORTED.** Eighteen
tracked entries sit within ±5 days. The auction's own session (**11-10**) carries **CPI** (confirmed,
high), the **US–China tariff truce expiry** (estimate, high), a **CRWV** earnings print (estimate),
the BoJ Summary of Opinions and the EIA STEO — but, per leg 3, no other Treasury auction. The
run-up carries **jobs 11-06** (confirmed, high) and the **3-Year 11-09**; the aftermath carries the
**30-Year 11-12**, the coupon announcement, October MTS and **PPI 11-13**. The sequence that can
actually move this event's numbers is earlier and shorter: **borrowing estimates 11-02 → midterms
11-03 → refunding 11-04**. No dated adjacent event was discovered this session that the calendar does
not already carry, so this PR proposes none.

**12. The print carries existence risk, inherited and dated — SUPPORTED.** `cpi-2026-11-10` records
that October is precisely the collection month a federal funding lapse destroys, and that a 2025
shutdown caused BLS to **cancel the October-data CPI outright** — visible in this session's own BLS
pull, which shows September-2025 data delayed to **10/24/2025** and October-2025 data absent. The
base case is that the FY2027 lapse is averted (`government-funding-deadline-2026-09-30`). If it is
