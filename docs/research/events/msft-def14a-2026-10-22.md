## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The calendar carries this entry only because a sibling lane inferred it: researching the 2026 annual
meeting, that lane measured a 47-day filing-to-meeting lag and proposed a proxy filing at
`2026-10-22`. Two questions follow, and this lane owns both. **Is that date right**, tested against
an anchor the sibling did not use? And **is the filing tradable** — the sibling's own leg 4 called
it "MIXED: the premise is right, the trade is not," which is an invitation to look harder rather
than a finished answer.

### One-line verdict

The date survives an independent test — a second anchor sharing none of the first's inputs lands
within one day of it — and the trade does not survive at all: correcting the event study's anchor
to the first session that could actually price the filing *strengthens* the refusal rather than
weakening it, and the one cell that looked tradable turns out to be an earnings window mislabelled.

### Legs tested

**1. "2026-10-22 is the right estimate" — SUPPORTED by an independent anchor; status stays `estimate`.**

The proposal's anchor was the filing-to-meeting lag. This lane built a second one that shares no
input with it — the **Form 10-K to DEF 14A** lag, which never touches the meeting date:

| Anchor | Sample | Median lag | sd | 2026 point estimate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A — DEF 14A → annual meeting | 2018-2025, n=8 (43, 49, 44, 47, 47, 49, 47, 45) | **47 d** | **2.20** | 2026-12-08 − 47 = **2026-10-22** (Thu) |
| B — Form 10-K → DEF 14A | last five years, n=5 | **84 d** | 5.07 | 2026-07-29 + 84 = **2026-10-21** (Wed) |
| B — same, wider | 2008-2025, n=18 | 76.5 d | 9.15 | 2026-10-13 |

Two things fall out. **The anchors agree to one day**, which is meaningful precisely because they
are independent — B is anchored on a filing that already happened and knows nothing about December.
And **A is the better estimator**, by a factor of 2.3 on dispersion (sd 2.20 vs 5.07); B's
sixteen-year version is worse still, because Microsoft's proxy calendar date wandered by four weeks
across that span while the *lag to the meeting* never did. So the entry keeps 2026-10-22 as the
point estimate, with A's full n=8 range as the honest window: **2026-10-19..2026-10-26**.

A third, weaker anchor is worth recording because it cuts the right way. The proxy is filed a median
**7.5 days before** the FY-Q1 earnings print (last eight years: −8, −7, −8, −12, +2, −5, −6, −8 days;
**17 of 18** years the proxy came first, 2022 the lone inversion). Read off the **estimate**
2026-10-27 print, that anchor says 2026-10-19/20 — earlier than the point estimate. It is not used to
move the date, because reading an estimate off another estimate is how a calendar fools itself; per
this repo's honesty rule an estimate may only *widen caution*, so it is recorded as pressure on the
window's left edge and nothing more.

The status stays **estimate** permanently until the document exists. This is not the sibling lane's
situation — there, a signed 10-K stated the meeting date and could be checked 8-for-8 against
history. Here **no primary source states this date at all**, because the SEC does not schedule proxy
filings and Microsoft does not pre-announce them. Verified this session that no 2026 DEF 14A has been
filed: the most recent on the feed is 2025-10-21, and the index runs through 2026-09-08.

Every prior look at this event — including the sibling lane's leg 4 — measured MSFT minus QQQ on the
filing *date*. The `acceptanceDateTime` field says that is the wrong session:

unpublished. Measuring it measures noise by construction — which is exactly why the naive series
looks so implausibly quiet (sd 0.64pp): it is largely a study of ordinary sessions.

The whole apparent drift is two contaminated observations (2015's +10.14pp window holds the

average slightly negative and win a third of the time. There is nothing here, and 2026 would be a
particularly poor year to try it: the point estimate's own D+5 window runs into **2026-10-27**

**5. "The proxy carries information that could reprice MSFT" — MIXED: it carries real information, none of it financial.**

Read from the 2025 document itself rather than from a description of it, the slate was four
management proposals — election of 12 directors, say-on-pay, Deloitte ratification, and the 2026
Stock Plan — followed by **six shareholder proposals**: a European Security Program censorship-risk

on data operations in human-rights hotspots, a report on human-rights due diligence, and a report on
AI/ML tools for oil and gas development. Every one is an advocacy filer asking for a *report*.

That matters for a specific reason. The tempting 2026 story is that the first proxy after the
two-segment recast will frame AI capex for shareholders — and the proxy is indeed where the
AI-governance *headlines* come from. But the AI content in a Microsoft proxy is other people's
resolutions, and the financial content is compensation tables. Guidance, capex and segment
disclosure live in the 10-Q and the print, six days later on the current estimate. The premise
survives; its trading implication does not.

### Honest limits

- **n=18 is small and the study is hand-built.** It carries no controls beyond the ones stated and is not a repo instrument. It is usable because it is read as a **null** — supporting "nothing is here" on eighteen observations is far cheaper than supporting "something is."
- **The anchor correction is inferred from acceptance timestamps, not from tick data.** A filing accepted at 16:16 ET is assumed unpriced at the 16:00 close. That is almost certainly right, but no intraday print was examined to prove no leakage in the final minutes.
- **`acceptanceDateTime` is treated as EDT throughout.** Every filing in the sample falls in late September or October, so EDT holds for all eighteen; a filing that ever slipped past the DST boundary would need re-checking.
- **Ex-2008 is a judgement, and it is disclosed rather than hidden.** Both the with- and without- figures are printed above; the case for excluding it is mechanical (a market-wide crash rebound, not a proxy reaction), not statistical convenience — and note the exclusion makes the *point estimate* flatter while making the *dispersion* honest.
- **The date remains an estimate and cannot become anything else before the fact.** Both anchors are cadences, and a board can move a filing for reasons no cadence sees. The window is honest; the point inside it is a convenience.
- **2026 is the first proxy under the two-segment structure**, so the content prior is drawn from a disclosure regime that has just changed. That cuts toward more caution about the null, not less — and it is why leg 5 is graded MIXED rather than REFUTED.
