## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Four questions. (1) This id existed only as
`proposals/dallas-fed-mfg-2026-12-28.from-treasury-2y-note-2026-12-28.json`, and the
[11-30 sibling](dallas-fed-mfg-2026-11-30.md) had explicitly **declined** to file it. Which lane was
right? (2) Does the sibling's measured stand-aside — and specifically its registered kill switch —
transfer to a release sitting between Christmas and New Year? (3) What, if anything, is specific to
*December's* edition rather than to the series? (4) Is there anything here that moves the calendar's
reading of **2026-12-28** as a session?

**One-line verdict:** filing was right and the sibling's stated reason for declining was wrong in
kind; the stance transfers but **the instrument does not** — November's clean-morning precondition is
absent and the Dec-26-31 tape runs a measured **0.68-0.76×** its own trailing baseline, so this doc
refuses the attribution test rather than inheriting an unfalsifiable one; what December *does* carry
is a **first-post-FOMC field window (Dec 15-23, derived twice)** and the quarter-end
**wages/prices/outlook-concerns** special-question slot; and as a session, 2026-12-28's value to the
calendar is precisely that this row makes its crowding visible.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is quotable verbatim, and the cadence rule is now validated on 32 editions —
   SUPPORTED, and it stays `estimate`.** The TMOS page's table headed **"2026 release dates"** carries
   the column header `Release (9:30 a.m. CT)` and the row **`December | Monday, December 28`**, with
   the note *"The publishing process begins at 9:30 a.m. CT. While we strive to ensure the
   information is published to our website in a timely manner, we cannot guarantee the exact timing
   all survey content becomes available online."* 9:30 a.m. CT = **10:30 ET** (CST in December). The
   sibling ledgers validated the last-Monday rule on 2025-26 only; the 32 mastheads fetched here
   validate it across **Jan-2024 → Aug-2026 with one exception** — **2026-05-26**, a Tuesday, because
   Memorial Day fell on May's last Monday. December 2026 has no such collision (Christmas is Friday
   12-25; the last Monday is 12-28). It stays `estimate` for the reason the sibling identified and
   this doc confirms: `market-events-data.ts`'s prefix table has **no confirmed tier for a reserve-bank
   District survey page** (`FED:` is scoped to federalreserve.gov's FOMC calendar), so this series is
   **structurally unpromotable from any lane** regardless of source quality. That is a governance
   observation, not a research finding, and is left as one — the same way the sibling left it.

2. **The proposing lane was right to file, and the sibling's reason for declining does not survive —
   MIXED, and this is the doc's governance contribution.** The [11-30 ledger](dallas-fed-mfg-2026-11-30.md)
   declined `dallas-fed-mfg-2026-12-28` on the ground that *"filing further `low` rows for a series
   measured as market-neutral would contradict the finding."* The
   [treasury-2y-note-2026-12-28](treasury-2y-note-2026-12-28.md) lane filed it four days later
   because that auction session **is not attributable at all** and this survey at 10:30 is one of the
   reasons. Both statements are true; the inference is not. A calendar row does two jobs — *this
   might move the tape* and *this contaminates the tape* — and a rule that files only the first
   removes confounder markers from exactly the sessions where attribution is hardest. The sibling's
   own leg 8 proves the point in the opposite direction: it could claim a clean November test **only
   because BEA, Census and BLS were checked and found empty**. That check is only possible when the
   confounders are tracked. So the row is upheld, on the proposing lane's reason rather than on a
   claim that this print matters.

3. **The sibling's registered kill switch does not transfer to this date — SUPPORTED, and this is
   the main finding.** `FT-dallas-fed-mfg-2026-11-30-3` tests whether the 10:30-11:30 ET window
   exceeds the 75th (kill: 90th, both tickers) percentile of its own trailing-60 distribution, and it
   is honest **because November's session has an empty 08:30 slot and only Chicago's 09:45 print
   ahead of it**. 2026-12-28 has, from this repo's own calendar: `advance-economic-indicators-2026-12-28`
   at **08:30** (medium), this survey at **10:30**, the bill auctions at **11:30**, and
   `treasury-2y-note-2026-12-28` **and** `treasury-5y-note-2026-12-28` together at **13:00** (medium
   each) — with `fomc-minutes-2026-12-30` two sessions later. A percentile crossing on that morning
   has at least two better explanations than a Texas diffusion survey. Re-registering the test here
   would manufacture an observation that cannot be assigned, which is worse than declining it.

4. **And the tape is structurally quieter, which mis-calibrates the threshold independently —
   SUPPORTED, measured.** Every **Dec 26-31** session from 2004 through 2025, ranked against its own
   trailing-60-session distribution:

   | Metric (SPY, n=91 sessions) | Dec 26-31 median percentile | All other sessions | Median ratio to trailing-60 median |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | \|close-to-close\| | **40.7th** | 49.2nd | **0.76×** |
   | High-low range | **22.0th** | 49.2nd | **0.68×** |
   | Volume | **11.9th** | 49.2nd | **0.68×** |

   QQQ reproduces it: **39.0th / 23.7th / 8.5th**, ratios **0.75× / 0.73× / 0.66×**. The consequence
   for a percentile trigger is direct — sessions reaching the **90th percentile of their own
   trailing-60** are **4/91 = 4.4%** (SPY) and **2/91 = 2.2%** (QQQ) inside the window, against
   **10.7%** and **10.4%** outside it. A threshold calibrated on normal sessions is roughly **half to
   a quarter** as likely to fire here, purely from liquidity.

5. **At the direct unit the discount is milder, and the sample contains a false positive — MIXED,
   and stated because it cuts against leg 4's neatness.** Yahoo hourly bars reach only 730 days, so
   the **10:30-11:30 ET** version of leg 4 has **n=8** (Dec 26-31, 2024 and 2025): median
   \|open→close\| percentile **44th** (SPY) / **53rd** (QQQ), range percentile **38th / 39th**, range

   demonstration of leg 3's objection, not a counter-example to it.

6. **The daily-unit null holds for December releases specifically — SUPPORTED, n=22.** Taking the

   holiday, which is 6 of the 22), SPY's close-to-close move on that session sits at a **median 39.8th

### What the conditions support

Nothing directional, and one deliberate subtraction. The subtraction is the point: **the 11-30
ledger's attribution test is not inherited here**, because the precondition that made it honest —
an empty 08:30 slot — is absent, and because the Dec-26-31 tape runs **0.68-0.76×** its own
baseline, so the same threshold means something different. What replaces it is three things a
December print can actually settle: the **field window** (Dec 15-23, derived twice), the **panel
size** (the shrink trend, which governs how much of any move is sampling noise), and the
**amplitude claim itself** (does the release hour on 2026-12-28 come in below its own trailing
median, as the holiday-week ratios say it should). Beyond the tape, December's value is content:
the **first field window opening after the year's last FOMC**, and the quarter-end **wages, prices
and outlook concerns** block that is the qualitative half of the prices-first reading order. And as
a calendar entry, this row's job is to be a **confounder marker on 2026-12-28** — so that the 2Y and
5Y auction lanes, which have already flagged that session as unattributable, can see *why* in the
data rather than infer it.
