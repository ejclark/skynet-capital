## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This id reached the calendar as a proposal from [`jobs-2027-08-06`](jobs-2027-08-06.md)'s own initial
research, filed at D+28 to make that lane's "first of exactly two payrolls the September meeting
sees" leg checkable. It arrives carrying two inherited stories and a neighbour's striking fact. The
inherited stories: *August is the payroll that moves duration* (the sibling's measured finding) and
*a payroll before a long weekend lifts the reopen* ([`jobs-2027-07-02`](jobs-2027-07-02.md)'s). The
neighbour's fact: [`fomc-blackout-start-2027-09-04`](fomc-blackout-start-2027-09-04.md) established
that September 2027 is the **only second-Tuesday September FOMC in 2007–2027**, which pulls the whole
corridor a week early and drops this print on the eve of the blackout.

Four questions follow. **Does the derived date survive an independent re-derivation, and is the
primary retrievable yet?** **Does the August seasonal instruction transfer to September, or is it
month-keyed?** **Does the long-weekend reopen finding survive on this date's own subset — and if it
does, *where in the four days* does the move actually happen?** And: **does landing on the eve of a
communications blackout do anything measurable at all?**

**One-line verdict:** the date reproduces 7-for-7 and stays `estimate` because every BLS route 403s;
the August instruction **does not transfer** and September is close to its mirror image; the
long-weekend lift **does** replicate on the pure pre-Labor-Day subset and resolves to something
narrower and more useful than the pooled version — it lives **entirely in the weekend gap**, not the
reopen session; and the blackout framing, like the cycle-position framing the sibling killed, is
**descriptively true and predictively empty**.

### Method

Two prior findings are **cited rather than re-run**, and both are named as such below.

- **Attempted and SUCCEEDED.** `federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm` returned

  pages `fomchistorical1993.htm … fomchistorical2020.htm` all returned 200. That is the `FED:`
  primary for every FOMC date and every blackout window this document derives.
- **Measured, with Yahoo split/dividend-adjusted daily bars** (the source
  `scripts/research/market-data.mjs` uses, re-fetched this session with **open** and **volume**,

  (20,000 relabelings, fixed seed) for magnitude; two-sided binomial against a 50% null for
  direction. Robustness by leave-one-out and by era split, both reported.

- **Deliberately NOT re-run:** the sibling's refutation of *days-to-FOMC-decision* over 392 releases

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date reproduces independently — SUPPORTED, and it stays `estimate`.** The rule (release on
   the third Friday following the end of the Sun–Sat reference week containing the 12th) was coded
   from its definition and run over 1993–2027 before being applied. It reproduces **7 of 7** knowns:
   the four `confirmed` entries on this calendar (2026-09-04, 2026-10-02, 2026-11-06, 2026-12-04)
   and the three historical Sunday-July-4 releases (2004-07-02, 2010-07-02, 2021-07-02). August
   2027's reference week is Sun 08-08 → Sat 08-14; the third Friday after 08-14 is **2027-09-03**.
   It stays `estimate` on two counts: no primary was retrievable (five blocked fetches above), and
   this lane does not self-confirm.

2. **The rule's documented failure mode cannot fire here — SUPPORTED.** Re-run against every
   trading calendar 1993–2026 the rule leaves **24 derived dates that were not trading days** (New
   Year's, a run of Good Fridays, a run of Independence-Day collisions) — the failure mode is a
   holiday landing on the rule's *own Friday*. **2027-09-03 is an ordinary NYSE trading day.** The
   nearest holiday is Labor Day, Mon **2027-09-06**, which *follows* the print and never displaces
   it; the rule produced the identical shape for 2026-09-04, one of the four `confirmed` knowns.

3. **The August instruction does not transfer — SUPPORTED, and this is the leg that matters most for
   a reader arriving from the sibling.** Same statistic, same bars, same code path: the payroll-day
   **amplification ratio** (a month's payroll-session median \|move\| over *that same month's*
   non-payroll-session median), which divides the month out by construction.

   different month.

7. **The base rate it inverts is decisive, which is why leg 6 reads as a finding — SUPPORTED.** How
   often does the reopen out-move the print itself? After an **ordinary** payroll the print
   dominates roughly 2-to-1: SPY **141/355** (40%, *p*=0.0001), TLT **89/255** (35%, *p*<0.0001),
   IEF **81/255** (32%, *p*<0.0001), LQD 89/255 (35%, *p*<0.0001), AGG 81/242 (33%, *p*<0.0001) —
   five instruments, every one decisively below half. After a **pre-Labor-Day** payroll it flips to
   a coin flip or better: SPY 12/20 (60%), IEF 9/14 (64%), LQD 9/14 (64%), AGG **10/14** (71%), TLT
   7/14 (50%). None of the P counts is individually significant at n=14–20 and none is claimed to
   be; the evidence is the **contrast with a base rate established at n=242–355 and *p*<0.0001**.

8. **The equity version fails its own control; the rates version mostly survives it — MIXED, and
   reported split.** P versus **S** (September payrolls with an ordinary weekend) isolates the
   holiday from the month. Reopen \|move\|: **SPY 1.07× (*p*=0.87)** and QQQ 1.05× (0.90) — the
   equity lift is entirely a September effect and **is not carried**, exactly as
   `jobs-2027-07-02` found by a different route. Rates and credit hold up but thinly: LQD **1.80×
   (*p*=0.048)**, ^VIX 2.78× (0.051), IEF 2.53× (0.088), TLT 1.88× (0.17), AGG 1.13× (0.87). On
   n=14 vs 11 this control is underpowered and AGG's collapse is a real crack in the story.

9. **The finding lives in the modern half — SUPPORTED, and that is the right way round for a
   forward test.** Split at 2010, P-vs-O reopen \|move\|: **2010–2026** gives TLT **2.31×**
   (*p*=0.0048), LQD **2.38×** (0.0058), AGG **2.06×** (0.036), IEF **1.92×** (0.030) on n=10;
   **≤2009** gives 2.01× (0.054), 1.35× (0.48), 1.24× (0.69), 1.37× (0.50) on n=4. The pre-2010
   bond sample barely exists (these ETFs start 2002), so the early half is reported rather than
   tested. SPY is null in both eras (1.53×/1.66×, *p*=0.22/0.16), consistent with leg 8.

10. **Leave-one-out says TLT is robust and the rest are fragile — SUPPORTED as a limit.** Dropping
    each P session in turn from the gap test: **TLT keeps *p*<0.05 in 14 of 14** drops (range
    0.0024–0.0198). **IEF, LQD and AGG keep it in only 7 of 14** (IEF worst case *p*=0.104 dropping
    2015-09-04; LQD 0.068 dropping 2023-09-01; AGG 0.055 dropping 2015-09-04). One instrument's
    finding is sturdy at this n; three are single-observation-sensitive and must be read that way.

11. **Direction is not predictable, and two apparent exceptions are impostors — SUPPORTED as a
    refusal.** Whether the reopen continues the print-day sign, P set: SPY **9/20** (*p*=0.82), QQQ
    7/16 (0.80), AGG 6/14 (0.79), IEF 5/14 (0.42), LQD 4/14 (0.18), TLT 4/14 (0.18). Two results
    *look* significant — **^VIX reverses 16 of 19** (*p*=0.0044) and SHY reverses 10 of 12
    (*p*=0.039) — and **both are general payroll facts, not this set's**: on ordinary payrolls ^VIX
    reverses **220 of 353** (*p*<0.0001) and SHY **152 of 241** (*p*=0.0001). Against the right
    control (S, September ordinary weekends) neither separates: ^VIX 5/14 (*p*=0.42), SHY 6/11
    (*p*=1.00). **Killed here rather than banked.**

12. **The blackout framing predicts nothing — REFUTED, and it is a different mechanism from the one
    the sibling killed.** The sibling refuted *days-to-decision*. The framing this date invites is
    narrower and, on its face, more plausible: the print lands the day before
    `fomc-blackout-start-2027-09-04`, so **no Fed official may publicly react to it before the
    meeting**. Tested by splitting September payrolls on exactly that: blackout opening at **D+1**
    (n=11) versus **D+8** (n=13), print-day \|move\| and forward 10-session realized vol across six
    instruments. **Twelve tests, one nominal hit** (AGG forward RV, 1.35×, *p*=0.044) — precisely
    the false-positive count twelve tests at α=0.05 produce, and it reproduces in nothing else. SPY
    print-day \|move\| is **1.00×** (0.449% vs 0.451%, *p*=1.00). The speech-availability story is
    descriptively true and predictively empty.

13. **A neighbour's fact is right and its natural inference is wrong — SUPPORTED, and worth banking

    the gate* — is **false**. Payroll-to-blackout gap across the 26 Septembers 2000–2026: **+1 day

    Both dates move together with the month's weekday alignment, so an early meeting does not
    produce an early-relative payroll. **Unique meeting, entirely ordinary payroll placement.**

### Honest limits

D-359, and the first limit is in the date field: **the primary is unretrievable**, so a 7-for-7
derivation stands in for a published schedule. **The small-n problem is real and named rather than
smoothed:** the object of study is **20 sessions** (14 for the bond ETFs, 11 for the control, 3 for

have** — five instruments significant on the gap leg and zero on the session leg (leg 6), against a
