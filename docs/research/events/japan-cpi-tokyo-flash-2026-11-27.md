### Conviction legs, tested

1. **November is an ordinary month, so the promotion has no news-content case — REFUTED, and this is
   the finding that reframes the document.** The sibling measured one month pair (November→December)
   and reported it as ordinary. Nobody had computed the whole seasonal profile, which is what decides
   whether *this* date is special. Mean absolute month-on-month change in Tokyo's own core y/y, by
   calendar month:

   | Month | 2018-2025 (n=8) | within ±0.3pp | 2000-2025 (n=26) | within ±0.3pp |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | **November** | **0.190pp** | 75% | **0.149pp** | 85% |
   | December | 0.201pp | 75% | 0.127pp | 88% |
   | *Any month* | 0.239pp | 66% | 0.172pp | 82% |
   | Quietest (Jul / Mar) | 0.113pp | 88% | 0.095pp | 96% |
   | **Noisiest (April)** | **0.596pp** | 25% | **0.428pp** | 54% |

   On the recent window November ranks **4th quietest of twelve** and December **5th** — a 0.011pp
   separation on n=8, which is nothing. On the long window December is *quieter* than November. The
   raw November changes 2018-2025 are **0.00 / +0.11 / −0.22 / +0.22 / +0.22 / −0.33 / +0.42 / −0.01**.
   **Conclusion: "watch 2026-11-27, not 2026-12-25" cannot be justified by what the November print
   says.** Registered as **FT-japan-cpi-tokyo-flash-2026-11-27-3**.

   The table carries a second finding worth banking: **April is the noisiest month by a wide margin**
   (0.596pp recent, 5× November). The food consumption-tax cut is effective **2027-04-01**, so its
   one-off drag will land in the single month where the Tokyo flash is least readable anyway. Any
   future session trying to measure that drag off an April flash should expect the worst
   signal-to-noise of the year.

2. **The annual-average lock is arithmetically real and substantively empty — MIXED.** The sibling's
   mechanism is correct: eleven of twelve monthly levels are public once this print lands. This
   session quantified what that buys, by backtesting the actual procedure a reader would run on the
   November flash date — take the eleven published months, assume December repeats November's y/y,
   compute the annual all-items rate — against the figure the Bureau eventually published:

   | 2000-2025 backtest (n=26) | Result |
   |---|---|
   | Mean absolute error in the annual rate | **0.024pp** |
   | Maximum absolute error, 26 years | **0.066pp** |
   | Years within 0.07pp | **26 / 26 = 100%** |
   | Years where the **rounded one-decimal** headline matched | **19 / 26 = 73%** |

   So the underlying number is pinned on 2026-11-27 to a tolerance far tighter than anything anyone
   would trade. The published headline is *not* pinned, because a residual of 0.02-0.07pp still flips
   a value sitting near a rounding boundary a bit over a quarter of the time. **Both halves matter:**
   the lock is stronger than the sibling implied at the level of the number, and weaker at the level
   of the headline. And the headline is the one the sibling itself called the emptiest on this
   calendar. Registered as **FT-japan-cpi-tokyo-flash-2026-11-27-1**.
