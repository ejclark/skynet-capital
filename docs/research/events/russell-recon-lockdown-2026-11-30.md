3. **The lock-down boundary is administrative, not a trading-session boundary — SUPPORTED, and it

### Honest limits

**The seasonal is a slot, not a mechanism.** Nothing here explains *why* the Monday after Thanksgiving
drags small caps; four controls rule out the obvious families, which is not the same as an explanation,
and an unexplained seasonal is exactly the kind of result that dies out of sample. **n=21, one slot.** The
slot was not chosen by search — it is the date the event lands on, and the panel in leg 6 is the
calibration — but a single 21-observation calendar test is a fragile foundation for an argument this
sheet leans on hard. **The 2016–2025 10-of-10 is the strongest and the most suspect number here**, since a
strengthening seasonal with no mechanism is as easily a crowding artefact as a real one. **IWM-vs-SPY is
an index-level proxy for a single-name mechanic** — inherited from both siblings and undiminished; adds
and deletes largely cancel at index level, so every effect measured here is a floor. **Leg 7's baselines
are geometry-sensitive**, and the two window definitions give 15-of-21 and 13-of-21 — the ledger reports
both rather than picking the convenient one, but a scoring session must state which it used. **Leg 8 is
n=1.** **The corridor is heavy** (21 events within five days, three `high`), so the 2026 print itself will
be weak evidence whatever it says. **The date is `estimate`** and every trading-adjacent statement above
carries that label; shorting is blocked house-wide, `symbols` is empty by design, and no house playbook is
index-flow-keyed.

**Stance (date `estimate`, `EST:` owner-published in the July 2026 FAQ's dated schedule table, prefix-gapped
rather than unpublished).** Treat 2026-11-30 as **a certain, mechanically real, entirely untradeable
