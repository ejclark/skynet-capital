## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **The HMI can only ever collide with a dot-plot meeting, and 2026-09-16 is the sixth
time it has.** Across **63** scheduled FOMC decision days 2020–2027 read off the Fed's own
calendars, SEP meetings sit on day-of-month **8–22** while non-SEP meetings sit on **1–7 and
26–31** — **zero** inside 15–20. All **84** publisher-authored HMI dates fall on day-of-month
**15–20**. The two calendars intersect on the quarterly dot-plot Wednesday and nowhere else, and
all five elapsed collisions (2020-09-16, 2020-12-16, 2021-12-15, 2022-03-16, 2022-06-15) are SEP
meetings. That is the whole call: an FOMC day multiplies precisely the window a 10:00 print would
have to occupy — ITB open→close **1.5570% vs 0.9522%** clean (n=52, **p=0.0011**), XHB 1.4926% vs
0.8569% (p=0.0004), ITB range 2.9747% vs 2.0761% (p<0.0001) — while the HMI's own contribution
reproduces the sibling ledger's null on a re-derived date set, **0.9514% vs 0.9522%** (n=73,
**p=1.00**). There is nothing to attribute and no power to attribute it with: an HMI effect would
have to **double** the collision-day median before n=5 could separate it from an ordinary decision
day. Date `confirmed` on the publisher's own calendar; the call is stand aside on every horizon.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This id reached the calendar as **two** `EST:` proposals filed the same day by two sibling lanes,
and they named two different reasons. [`new-home-sales-2026-09-24`](new-home-sales-2026-09-24.md)
filed the collision:

> 2026-09-16 is the September FOMC decision day (fomc-2026-09-16, tracked), so the
> builder-sentiment read lands on the loudest session of its own corridor and any tape reaction to
> it is unattributable by construction — the same stacked-event problem the
> new-home-sales-2026-10-27 lane measured on FOMC-eve, one rung worse.

[`housing-starts-2026-09-17`](housing-starts-2026-09-17.md) filed the pairing — that 09-16 → 09-17
is the September HMI → New Residential Construction pair, and that 09-16's close is the denominator
of 09-17's overnight gap.

The sibling [`nahb-hmi-2026-11-17`](nahb-hmi-2026-11-17.md) already measured the HMI's own channel
and refuted it. So the question left for **this** lane is narrower and sharper: **is the FOMC
collision an accident of the 2026 calendar or a structural property of the two schedules, and does
it leave any window in which the September print could be read at all?**

**One-line verdict:** the collision is **structural** — the HMI's date window and the non-SEP FOMC
window do not overlap anywhere in eight years, so an HMI can only ever collide with a dot-plot
meeting — and **no window survives**: the session is 1.6× louder than baseline and the pre-open is
*quieter* than baseline, so there is neither a signal to read nor a place to read it.

### Method

**Dates.** NAHB's "2026 HMI Schedule" page was fetched direct (HTTP 200, **47,131** bytes,
2026-09-06) and the same page pulled from seven Wayback snapshots (20200927, 20211021, 20220928,
20230906, 20240909, 20250821, 20260105), re-parsed independently of the sibling lane to **84**
publisher-authored dates 2020-01-16 → 2026-12-16; **78** of the elapsed rows are US trading
sessions. FOMC decision days come from `federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm` (HTTP
200, **164,831** bytes) for 2021–2027 and `/monetarypolicy/fomchistorical2020.htm` (HTTP 200,
**98,511** bytes) for 2020 — **63** scheduled meetings, decision day taken as the last day of each
range, the page's own `*` marking a Summary of Economic Projections. Unscheduled and notation-vote
actions (2020-03-03, 2020-03-15, 2020-03-19/23/31, 2020-08-27, 2025-08-22) are **excluded** —
they have no scheduled 14:00 statement, and including them would smuggle the March-2020 tape into
a baseline about pre-announced events.

**Prices.** ITB, XHB and SPY daily OHLC from the Nasdaq API, **2,514** sessions 2016-09-06 →
2026-09-04. The closes reproduce the sibling ledgers to the cent — **ITB 93.91 · XHB 103.25 · SPY
770.19** on 2026-09-04; VIX **14.53** from CBOE's delayed-quote endpoint. No fetch failed this
session, so `probe-ref.blocked` is empty.

**Metrics, pre-specified before any test was run.** The HMI publishes **10:00 ET** and the FOMC
statement **14:00 ET** — both inside the session, so the window that can contain either is
**open→close**, `|close ÷ open − 1|`; the **overnight gap**, `|open ÷ prior close − 1|`, is the
placebo neither can reach; range is `(high − low) ÷ low`. Four disjoint classes over the matched
window 2020-01-02 → 2026-09-04 (**1,678** sessions): **collision** (n=5), **HMI-only** (n=73),
**FOMC-only** (n=47), **clean** (n=1,553). Two-sided permutation tests on medians, 20,000
iterations, seed 20260906.

### Leg 1 — the date, and the collision, are both primary — **SUPPORTED**

NAHB's table reads `Sept. 2026 | September 16, 2026` under the standing note *"Normal release
time: 10:00 AM Eastern Time"*. The Fed's own 2026 calendar reads `September 15-16*`, the asterisk
its key defines as *"Meeting associated with a Summary of Economic Projections"* — so 2026-09-16
carries the statement, the presser and the dot plot. NAHB's live HMI page (HTTP 200, 61,528 bytes)
reports **"HMI Key Findings: August 2026"** — the index up one point to **35**, current sales 39,
six-month expectations 43, buyer traffic 23, with 35% of builders cutting prices at an average 6%
and incentives at 63% — i.e. the edition its own schedule dates 2026-08-17, so publisher schedule
and publisher output agree. That is the same source class the sibling promoted on, so this file
promotes to **`confirmed`/`IR:`** on the [`existing-home-sales-2026-11-12`](existing-home-sales-2026-11-12.md)
precedent. Caution kept rather than smoothed: a scheduled date is NAHB's commitment, not a promise
the tape is open — 2 of 80 elapsed rows (2023-06-19, 2024-06-19) were Juneteenth.

**One correction to the sibling ledger.** [`nahb-hmi-2026-11-17`](nahb-hmi-2026-11-17.md) recorded
*"an unbroken mid-month Monday/Tuesday convention"* across the same 84 dates. Re-parsed here the
split is **Mon 27 · Tue 24 · Wed 20 · Thu 12 · Fri 1** — Mon+Tue is **51 of 84 (60.7%)**, and the
one Friday is 2026-01-16. The correction is not cosmetic: the **20 Wednesdays** are the entire
mechanism of this ledger, because **58 of 60** parsed FOMC decision days are Wednesdays (the two
exceptions, 2020-11-05 and 2024-11-07, are the election-week meetings). Had the convention been
unbroken, no collision could exist.

### Leg 2 — the collision is structural, not a 2026 accident — **SUPPORTED**

Split the 63 scheduled decision days by whether the Fed's own calendar marks them SEP:

| Class | n | Day-of-month occupied | Inside the HMI's 15–20 window |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SEP** (Mar · Jun · Sep · Dec) | 31 | **8–22** | **19** |
| **non-SEP** (Jan · Feb · Apr · May · Jul · Oct · Nov) | 32 | **1–7 and 26–31** | **0** |
| NAHB HMI, all 84 published dates | 84 | **15–20** | 84 |

The non-SEP calendar has a clean gap exactly where the HMI lives. So the intersection of the two
schedules is the quarterly dot-plot Wednesday and nothing else — and the record agrees: **all five**
elapsed collisions are SEP meetings, and 2026-09-16 is the sixth consecutive one. This is not a
statistical claim needing a p-value; it is a property of two published calendars, and it means the
collision recurs on a knowable quarterly cadence rather than by luck.

It also picks the *loudest* variety. SEP decision days move ITB more than non-SEP ones (median
open→close **1.6252%** vs 1.3755%, against 0.9522% clean; SPY 0.7529% vs 0.5360% vs 0.4658%).

### Leg 3 — an FOMC day owns the only window the HMI has — **SUPPORTED**

n=52 in-window decision days (the 47 FOMC-only plus the 5 collisions) against the 1,553 clean
sessions:

| Metric | ITB | XHB | SPY |
|---|---|---|---|
| open→close (**the live window**) | **1.5570% vs 0.9522%, p=0.0011** | **1.4926% vs 0.8569%, p=0.0004** | **0.7130% vs 0.4658%, p=0.0058** |
| overnight gap (**the placebo**) | 0.3457% vs 0.5123%, p=0.0497 | 0.3810% vs 0.5037%, p=0.14 | **0.2047% vs 0.3339%, p=0.0290** |
| session range | **2.9747% vs 2.0761%, p<0.0001** | **2.5871% vs 1.8931%, p<0.0001** | **1.3001% vs 1.0210%, p=0.0082** |

The gap row is the finding this lane adds, and it is the one that closes the escape hatch. The
natural fallback for a 10:00 print on a 14:00 day is *"read it before the statement"* — but the
decision-day pre-open is **narrower** than baseline in both ITB and SPY, i.e. the tape holds still
overnight and then does all its work inside the session. There is no quiet corner of 2026-09-16 in
which a builder-sentiment print gets its own reading.

### Leg 4 — and there is nothing to attribute anyway — **REFUTED (the HMI channel), REPRODUCED**

The HMI-only class reproduces the sibling's null on an independently re-derived date set and a
recomputed baseline:

| Metric | ITB | XHB | SPY |
|---|---|---|---|
| open→close | **0.9514% vs 0.9522%, p=1.00** | 0.8940% vs 0.8569%, p=0.77 | 0.4403% vs 0.4658%, p=0.71 |
| overnight gap | 0.5049% vs 0.5123%, p=0.94 | 0.4961% vs 0.5037%, p=0.84 | 0.4261% vs 0.3339%, p=0.069 |
| session range | 2.2203% vs 2.0761%, p=0.30 | 1.9594% vs 1.8931%, p=0.57 | 0.9323% vs 1.0210%, p=0.34 |

SPY's marginal gap cell (p=0.069) is the same one the sibling attributed to the **09:15 G.17**
release rather than the 10:00 HMI; nothing here disturbs that attribution and this lane did not
re-test it.

The collision days themselves confirm the reading. Their ITB open→close values are `2.094 · 0.018 ·
2.093 · 0.718 · 1.491` — median **1.4914%**, statistically indistinguishable from the FOMC-only
median of 1.5579% (**p=0.90**; XHB p=0.32, SPY p=0.84). A collision day looks like a decision day.

**And the power arithmetic is the honest closing argument.** The FOMC-only ITB open→close
distribution spans p10–p90 of **0.3501–3.3994%**; the entire HMI-only distribution (median 0.9514%,
IQR 0.5278–1.5314%) sits inside the FOMC-day IQR of 0.6681–2.4516%. Shifting the five observed
collision medians upward until they separate from FOMC-only at p<0.05 requires ~**2.0×** — ITB
1.4914% → 2.9828%, SPY 0.7877% → 1.5754%. So even an HMI effect twice the size of anything this
series has ever shown would be invisible on a decision day at n=5. *Unattributable by construction*
was the proposing lane's phrase; this is the number behind it.

### Honest limits

- **n=5 is five.** Every claim about the collision *class* rests on five sessions, four of them in
  a 21-month window (2020-09 → 2022-06) that contains the COVID recovery and the 2022 liftoff. The
  Leg 3 result (n=52 decision days) and the Leg 2 result (a calendar fact) do not depend on it; the
  Leg 4 comparison and the D+1 median do, and are stated at medium confidence for that reason.
- **The structural claim is 8 years, not forever.** Nothing binds the FOMC to keep its non-SEP
  meetings at month ends. The claim is that it has never scheduled one inside day-of-month 15–20
  across 63 meetings 2020–2027, and the falsifier is written as such.
- **Unscheduled FOMC actions are excluded by choice**, which is a judgment call, not a neutral one:
  including 2020-03-03 and 2020-03-15 would roughly double the decision-day medians and make Leg 3
  look stronger than it honestly is. The narrower definition is the conservative one here.
- **Magnitude only, never direction.** Every metric is absolute. Whether an HMI *surprise* moves
  builders directionally is untested — the same residual the sibling recorded, for the same reason
  (no machine-readable HMI consensus history was reachable).
- **The September G.17 date is unknown and not claimed.** `federalreserve.gov/feeds/g17.xml` (HTTP
  200, 577,293 bytes, 189 monthly items) carries no entry later than the July-2026 data release,
  and one item's `dc:date` reads **2006**-08-21 for "G.17 Data for July 2026" — a publisher typo,
  recorded rather than silently repaired. So whether industrial production shares the 2026-09-16
  morning is not established here, and no stacking claim rests on it.
- **12 permutation tests were run in the primary pass** (Legs 3 and 4, three names × three
  metrics, minus the cells reported without a test). Leg 3's cells clear p<0.05 as a block across
  two independent homebuilder ETFs and SPY, which is not the shape of a multiple-comparisons
  artefact; Leg 4's are null throughout, which cannot be one.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stand aside on every horizon, at `confirmed`.** This is a `low`-impact, `symbols: []` macro print
whose own channel the sibling ledger already refuted, landing on a session whose variance belongs
to the Federal Reserve. The reason it earns a ledger rather than a footnote is what the structural
leg buys the calendar: the HMI–FOMC collision is **quarterly and predictable**, so this repo can
say in advance which builder-sentiment prints are readable (2026-10-19) and which are not
(2026-09-16), instead of discovering it after the tape.

**What kills this stance:**

- **The session** — ITB's 2026-09-16 open→close coming in **at or below 0.9522%**, the clean
  median. A decision day that is *quieter* than an ordinary session would undercut Leg 3, which is
  what the whole unattributability argument stands on. Base rate 63.8% the other way. Registered
  as `-1`.
- **The pre-open** — SPY's 2026-09-16 overnight gap **exceeding 0.3339%**, the clean median.
  Decision days sit at or below it 70.2% of the time; a wide one on the most-anticipated morning of
  the quarter says the narrowing is noise and the escape hatch reopens. Registered as `-2`.
- **The structure** — the September edition not publishing 2026-09-16 at 10:00, or the FOMC
  statement not landing 2026-09-16, or a **non-SEP** FOMC meeting being scheduled inside
  day-of-month 15–20 before 2027-12-31. Any of the three costs Leg 2 its footing. Registered
  as `-3`.
- **The playbook** — a macro- or housing-keyed entry landing in `docs/plans/trade-playbooks.md`
  before 2026-09-16, which would give a `symbols: []` print something to attach to for the first
  time.

Estimates only widen caution; `confirmed` licenses nothing on its own. The predictions above are
registered in [`forward-tests/nahb-hmi-2026-09-16.md`](../forward-tests/nahb-hmi-2026-09-16.md).
