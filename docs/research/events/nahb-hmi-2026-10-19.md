## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This id reached the calendar as one `EST:` proposal from the
[`nahb-hmi-2026-11-17`](nahb-hmi-2026-11-17.md) adjacency sweep, and that proposal named a *control*,
not a hypothesis:

> it is the CONTROL for the one caution the November edition cannot clear… this calendar currently
> tracks NOTHING on 2026-10-19, and the tracked October stack has cleared by then… So an unstacked
> HMI day is where the sentiment channel, if it existed, would be visible without a 09:15 print in
> front of it.

And it left one explicit, honest limit for this session to clear:

> the October 2026 G.17 date was NOT verified this session (federalreserve.gov/releases/g17/
> g17_release_dates.htm 404s as of 2026-09-06 and the g17.xml feed carries only elapsed releases),
> so whichever lane researches this id should confirm it before relying on the unstacked framing.

**Is the date the publisher's own; is 2026-10-19 actually unstacked; does the sentiment null survive
on the unstacked class specifically; and can the one residual the sibling declared untestable — the
*directional* channel — be tested after all?**

**One-line verdict:** the date is NAHB's own on four checks and promotes to `confirmed`; the
unstacked premise is **SUPPORTED** by a Fed primary the sibling could not find; the sentiment null is
**SUPPORTED on the unstacked class specifically**; and the directional residual is now **MEASURED —
non-null, homebuilder-specific, and still not tradeable.**

### Method

**Dates.** NAHB's HMI schedule was fetched direct (HTTP 200, **47,131** bytes, 2026-09-06) and the
same page recovered from the Wayback CDX index. Three of the sibling's snapshots
(20240909, 20250821, 20260105) return a page with no schedule table for this runner, so a different
snapshot set was used — 20211021, 20220928, 20230906, 20240227, 20240616, 20241023, 20250322,
20250617, 20251012 — and it yields **the same 84 publisher-authored dates for 2020–2026**. That is an
independent reproduction of the sibling's date series, not an inheritance of it. Of the **80** already
elapsed, **78** are US trading sessions; the two exceptions are **2023-06-19** and **2024-06-19**,
both Juneteenth.

**The G.17 dates are a better source than the sibling had.** It parsed 185 monthly items out of
`feeds/g17.xml`, which carries only elapsed releases. The Board also publishes a maintained
release-date **table** at `releases/g17/release_dates.htm` — the underscore is the difference from the
404ing `g17_release_dates.htm` — fetched this session at HTTP 200, **117,098** bytes: **334** dates
**2000-01-14 → 2027-12-16**, footer *"Last Update: August 18, 2026"*, and still carrying
**"October 2025 NA"** and **"November 2025 NA"** from the funding lapse, i.e. maintained rather than
stale. Census dates come from `census.gov/construction/xls/historic_release_dates.xls` (HTTP 200,
**55,808** bytes, **510** unique New Residential Construction dates 1984-02-16 → 2026-12-17, New
Residential Sales in its own column). FOMC meeting dates from `monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm`.

**Prices.** ITB, XHB and SPY daily OHLC from the Nasdaq API, **2,514** sessions 2016-09-06 →
2026-09-04. Closes reproduce both sibling ledgers to the cent — **ITB 93.91 · XHB 103.25 · SPY
770.19** on 2026-09-04 — and VIX **14.53** from CBOE's delayed-quote endpoint. Yahoo and stooq were
not needed and not called.

**Metrics, pre-specified before any test was run**, and inherited deliberately from the sibling so the
reproduction is like-for-like. The HMI publishes **10:00 ET**, thirty minutes after the open, so the
window that can contain it is **open→close**, `|close ÷ open − 1|`; the **overnight gap**,
`|open ÷ prior close − 1|`, is a **placebo**; range is `(high − low) ÷ low`. Comparisons are two-sided
permutation tests on medians, 20,000 iterations, seed 20260906, over the matched window **2020-01-02 →
2026-09-04** (**1,678** sessions). Paired comparisons use an exact two-sided sign test. The directional
leg (Leg 5) is the one metric added this session, and it is **signed** rather than absolute.

### Leg 1 — the date is the publisher's own — **SUPPORTED**

NAHB's "2026 HMI Schedule" table reads `Oct. 2026 | October 19, 2026` under the standing note
*"Normal release time: 10:00 AM Eastern Time"*. Three corroborations, all primary:

1. NAHB's live HMI page (`/indices/housing-market-index`, HTTP 200, **61,518** bytes) carries
   **"HMI Key Findings: August 2026"** — the edition its own schedule dates 2026-08-17 — reporting
   the index **up one point to 35**, current sales **39**, six-month expectations **43**, buyer
   traffic **23**, with **35%** of builders cutting prices at an average **6%** reduction and
   incentives at **63%**.
2. Ten snapshots of the same table across 2020–2026 show an unbroken mid-month Monday/Tuesday
   convention. It is a forward calendar the producer maintains, not an announcement.
3. 78 of the 80 elapsed rows are trading sessions.

Promotion runs on the precedent [`nahb-hmi-2026-11-17`](nahb-hmi-2026-11-17.md) set on this identical
table, and [`existing-home-sales-2026-11-12`](existing-home-sales-2026-11-12.md) before it. The
Juneteenth caution carries forward unchanged: a scheduled date is NAHB's commitment, not a promise the
tape is open. Note for the record that **2020-10-19** is the only other October-19 HMI in the archive,
and it was likewise a post-opex Monday.

### Leg 3 — the sentiment null survives on the unstacked class — **SUPPORTED**

First the reproduction, on an independently assembled date set (**1,678** sessions, **78** HMI days,
**1,600** baseline). It matches the sibling to three decimals across the board: ITB open→close
**0.9559% vs 0.9627% (p=0.96)**, the placebo gap 0.5011% vs 0.5034% (p=0.97), XHB 0.9031% vs 0.8695%
(p=0.79), SPY's marginal gap 0.4184% vs 0.3283% (**p=0.067**). Two vendors, two date parses, one
answer.

Then the test this event exists for. Splitting the 78 by whether the G.17 shares the morning gives
**34** stacked and **44** unstacked days, and comparing the unstacked class against a **clean
baseline** (n=**1,493** sessions carrying neither an HMI, a G.17 nor a New Residential Construction
print):

| Metric | ITB | XHB | SPY |
|---|---|---|---|
| open→close (**the live window**) | 0.9894% vs 0.9689%, **p=0.91** | 0.9437% vs 0.8696%, p=0.65 | 0.4394% vs 0.4703%, p=0.73 |
| overnight gap (**the placebo**) | 0.4787% vs 0.4993%, p=0.81 | 0.4814% vs 0.5012%, p=0.77 | **0.3391% vs 0.3275%, p=0.85** |
| session range | 2.3290% vs 2.0917%, p=0.18 | 2.0448% vs 1.9080%, p=0.40 | 0.9698% vs 1.0352%, p=0.62 |

The stacked class run against the same clean baseline reads SPY gap **0.4828% (p=0.0365)** — the
sibling's Leg 4 attribution, reproduced at p=0.0365 against its p=0.0385 on a separately parsed G.17
series. The lift is the 09:15 print's; an unstacked morning is exactly baseline.

**The paired test, split by stacking — new this session.** The sibling ran its decisive
consecutive-session design on all 71 HMI→starts pairs and got 35/71 = 49.3%. That pooled stacked and
unstacked days together, which is precisely the confound this event was proposed to remove:

| Pair set | n | Sentiment day smaller | Medians (sentiment / data) | Sign test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ITB, **all** pairs | 71 | 35 (49.3%) | 0.9604% / 0.9866% | p=1.00 |
| ITB, **unstacked** pairs | **39** | **18 (46.2%)** | 1.0089% / 1.0319% | **p=0.75** |
| ITB, stacked pairs | 32 | 17 (53.1%) | 0.8503% / 0.8527% | p=0.86 |
| XHB, unstacked pairs | 39 | 22 (56.4%) | 0.9730% / 1.2331% | p=0.52 |

Give the sentiment print the morning entirely to itself and it is *still* a coin flip against the data
print the next day. That is the control returning its verdict **before** 2026-10-19 arrives, which is
the whole value of having filed it.

### Leg 4 — the instance confounds — **inert, and worth stating so no one adjusts for them**

2026-10-19 is a Monday, and the first session after October monthly opex (10-16). Both were checked
rather than assumed away:

- **Monday HMI days**, n=25 of 78 (day-of-week: Mon 25 · Tue 23 · Wed 17 · Thu 12 · Fri 1): ITB
  open→close **0.9514% vs 0.9604%**, **p=1.00**; range 2.1335% vs 2.2715%, p=0.65.
- **Post-opex HMI days**, n=14 — and **2020-10-19**, the only other October-19 HMI, is one of them:
  ITB open→close 1.0742% vs 0.8654%, **p=0.35**.
- **October editions**, n=6 (2020-10-19 … 2025-10-16): ITB open→close 1.0004% vs 0.9559%, p=0.91. No
  October seasonal, on a sample far too small to have found one anyway.
- **FOMC blackout**, 2021 forward only (the 2020 historical calendar page served no meeting table to
  this runner): 22 of 66 HMI days fall inside a blackout, ITB open→close 0.9248% vs 0.8654%, p=0.83.

None of them moves a threshold. That is the useful result: the registration below can use the class
p90 unadjusted.

### Leg 5 — the directional residual, which the sibling called untestable — **MEASURED, non-null, not tradeable**

The sibling closed with this honest limit:

> Whether the HMI's *surprise* moves builders directionally is untested, because no machine-readable
> history of HMI prints and consensus was reachable from this runner.

Half of it is now reachable. NAHB's live HMI page links **Table 2: National HMI – History** as a
workbook (`t2-national-hmi-history-202608.xls`, HTTP 200, **35,840** bytes, last saved 2026-08-14),
carrying **500** seasonally-adjusted monthly readings **1985-01 → 2026-08**. The HMI is a same-month
release, so each of the 78 release days maps to its own printed level and to a month-over-month
change. Across the 78, against the same-day **signed** open→close return:

| Name | r | Permutation p (20,000 iters) | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ITB** | **+0.2557** | **0.0292** | the homebuilder ETF moves with the print's own delta |
| XHB | +0.2193 | 0.0569 | the sister homebuilder ETF corroborates |
| **SPY** | **−0.0587** | **0.59** | the market-wide placebo is flat, as a real channel requires |

That is the correct signature: present in both homebuilder names, absent in the index. It is also the
first non-null this event's research has produced, and everything about how to read it is a caution:

- **Sign agreement is 54.8%** — 40 of the 73 release days with a non-zero delta (p=0.48). The
  relationship is in *magnitude*, not in a hit rate anyone could size. XHB reads 58.9% (43/73,
  p=0.16).
- **On this event's own class it does not reach significance**: unstacked days give r=+0.2389
  (p=0.117, n=44) and sign agreement **48.8%** (20/41, **p=1.00**).
- **A month-over-month change is not a surprise-versus-consensus.** No consensus series was reachable,
  so what is measured could be co-movement with the conditions the survey itself measured rather than
  a reaction to the 10:00 release.
- **The timing cannot be checked.** Separating "moved after 10:00" from "moved all session" needs
  intraday bars this runner does not have. Until that exists, the finding is a description of a
  daily-bar association and nothing more.

2026-10-19 is the cleanest out-of-sample instance the series offers for it — unstacked, blackout,
nothing else on the tape — which is why it is registered rather than merely reported.

### Honest limits

- **Multiple comparisons, stated up front.** **44** permutation tests were run this session. **Three**
  cells crossed p<0.05 against an expectation of ~2.2. One is a *reproduction* of the sibling's
  existing finding (SPY's stacked-morning gap, p=0.0365), so it is not a fresh discovery. One is Leg
  5's ITB correlation (p=0.0292), which earns its place only because XHB corroborates and SPY does
  not. The third is noise and is named as such below.
- **The one cell reported as noise.** ITB's session *range* on the 13 days that are both unstacked and
  inside an FOMC blackout reads 2.7617% vs a 2.0189% clean baseline, **p=0.0237** — and 2.0015%,
  p=0.0203, against a baseline restricted to the same calendar years, so the obvious era explanation
  does not dissolve it. It is still reported as noise, for four reasons: n=13; XHB does not
  corroborate (2.2068% vs 1.8491%, p=0.17); the **pre-specified live window** on the same 13 days is
  null (ITB open→close p=0.59); and all HMI days in those same years are null on range (p=0.41). It is
  the third split of a small sample and nothing in the stance rests on it.
- **The window is 2020–2026, not a decade.** NAHB's schedule page is archived from 2020 forward, so 78
  releases across the COVID boom, the 2022 rate shock and the 2023–26 malaise — a full cycle, but a
  fraction of the 510-date history the Census ledgers work from. The HMI *level* history reaches 1985;
  the *release-date* history does not.
- **The blackout leg is 2021 forward only.** `monetarypolicy/fomchistorical2020.htm` returned HTTP 200
  but served no meeting table to this runner, so 2020's twelve HMI days are excluded from Leg 4's
  blackout row and from the 13-day cell above. Stated rather than patched with dates from memory.
- **Blocked sources, recorded not substituted.** `federalreserve.gov/releases/g17/g17_release_dates.htm`
  returned **404**, exactly as the sibling recorded — the working path is `release_dates.htm`, and that
  one underscore is the whole reason the October date went unverified for a session. Three Wayback
  snapshots (20240909, 20250821, 20260105) returned HTTP 200 with no schedule table; they are recorded
  here as unusable rather than as failures, and substitute snapshots reproduced the same 84 dates.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stand aside on every horizon, at `confirmed`.** This is a `low`-impact, `symbols: []` macro print
whose control premise is now verified and whose sentiment hypothesis fails again on exactly the class
the control was built to isolate. It earns its row for three reasons, none of them a trade: it is the
**D-1 read** of the starts print; it is the corridor's only session clean enough to score a hypothesis
on, which is what makes it the natural home for Leg 5's out-of-sample test; and its verification
turned up two untracked G.17 releases the calendar was missing.

**What kills this stance:**

- **The direction** — ITB's 2026-10-19 open→close closing *against* the October HMI's month-over-month
  move. Registered as `-1`. One observation on a 54.8%/48.8% base rate: a single result settles
  nothing on its own and the registration says so; the value is that it is written down before the
  print rather than fitted after it.
- **The magnitude** — ITB's 2026-10-19 open→close at or above **2.5739%**, the p90 of the 44 unstacked
  HMI days. Registered as `-2`. That the unstacked p90 and the all-78 p90 (2.5734%) agree to five
  ten-thousandths is itself a small piece of evidence that stacking does nothing to this window.
- **The structure** — the October edition not publishing 2026-10-19 at 10:00, New Residential
  Construction not publishing 2026-10-20, or any 08:30/09:15 federal release appearing on 2026-10-19.
  Registered as `-3`. The last clause is the one that specifically kills the "unstacked control"
  framing rather than the schedule.
- **The playbook** — a macro- or housing-keyed entry landing in `docs/plans/trade-playbooks.md` before
  2026-10-19, which would give a `symbols: []` print something to attach to for the first time.

Estimates only widen caution; `confirmed` licenses nothing on its own. The predictions above are
