## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Three questions, in the order they earn their keep. (1) Does the Chicago Business Barometer move the
tape *at all* — a question both sibling ledgers declared confounded and shelved? (2) What should a
paper-trading book expect from the **2026-10-30** print, and what is knowable about it 55 days out?
(3) What is different about *October's* instance specifically, versus the September one already
researched in depth?

**One-line verdict:** the attribution question was never unknowable — it was being asked of the wrong
window, and measured on the 5-minute bar that actually contains the 09:45 release the answer across
all three available prints is a **null**, including on the year's largest miss; everything else about
10-30 is a stand-aside, because the print's own corridor (FOMC, PCE, GDP, ECI, five mega-cap reports,
the midterms) owns every session around it and its October base case is a function of a September
number that does not exist yet.

   the strongest available accuracy check. The [09-30 sibling](chicago-pmi-2026-09-30.md) named the

3. **The confounding is a permanent property of the release slot, not three unlucky months —

   confound every session-level read of this series indefinitely. The three-print record:
   **08-28** — Warsh's Jackson Hole keynote (September hike odds ~35% → 56–59%). **09-30** — ADP

   ministerial. **10-30** — see leg 4. Any research programme that waits for a "clean session" for
   this indicator waits forever. Leg 2 is the way out.

   Two structural notes fall straight out. **The rate decision is already made when this prints** —
   not merely by the 10-28 statement two days earlier, but earlier still: the [FOMC
   sibling](fomc-2026-10-28.md) establishes that October's *own* hike conditional is near **15–20%**,

   decided regime rather than inform one. **And the survey's own confounder is a shade lighter than
   September's**: 10-30 is month-end but **not** quarter-end, so the index-rebalance flows that the

5. **The ISM lead compresses to one business day in October — SUPPORTED, and it makes the cleanest
   available test of this series' whole reason for existing.** The Barometer's reputation rests on a
   reported **68%** correlation with the national ISM Manufacturing PMI it precedes, and ~**60%**
   with q/q real GDP over 15 years (secondary-sourced summary statistics, carried from the 09-30
   sibling; this repo has computed neither, and no house instrument can, since both research scripts
   are symbol-keyed). The lead is usually two business days — **08-28 Fri → 09-01 Tue** was two. In
   October it is **one**: **Fri 2026-10-30 → Mon 2026-11-02**, with no session in between for the
   market to digest Chicago before the national print lands. That is the shortest lead of the year
   and the tightest natural experiment the pair will offer: if the 68% relationship is real, the
   two prints' month-over-month directions should agree with essentially nothing else intervening.
   Registered as a forward test rather than asserted.

6. **The October field period is the pre-midterm run-up, and that is a genuine confound in the
   survey itself — SUPPORTED, and it is new to this instance.** The Barometer polls ~200+ Chicagoland
   purchasing professionals (~50% monthly response) for *relative* assessments — better / worse /
   unchanged. That is a sentiment instrument, and the October field period runs directly into the
   **2026-11-03** midterms. The [midterms sibling](midterm-elections-2026-11-03.md) establishes that
   the campaign's live economic issue is the AI data-center backlash — 64% of Americans oppose rapid
   data-center construction and 77% expect it to raise their power bills (Reuters/Ipsos, Jun 2026);
   **H.R. 9340** (data centers pay the full cost of grid upgrades) cleared House Energy & Commerce
   **52-0** — and that the result may not be known on election night. Cost allocation and tariff
   policy land on Chicagoland manufacturers' input costs directly. The honest inference is not
   directional; it is a **discount**: a soft October headline is as consistent with election
   uncertainty depressing sentiment as with orders actually falling, and the two are not separable
   from a diffusion index. Read the subcomponents, not the headline.

### What the conditions support

Nothing directional, and less than the September instance supported. What this document adds is a
**measurement discipline** that replaces a shelved question: when the 10-30 print lands, measure the
**09:45–09:50 five-minute bar** against its own trailing distribution, not the session — the session
belongs to ECI at 08:30, and before that to the FOMC, PCE, GDP and five mega-cap reports. Read
**Prices Paid before the headline** for as long as this Fed's case is pinned to inflation. Treat the
headline as a one-business-day probe on the **11-02** national ISM rather than as information in
itself, and discount a soft reading by the pre-midterm field period. And take no position off any
of it.

### Honest limits

**n = 3.** That is the binding constraint on leg 2 and the reason its derived call is graded Medium
rather than High: Yahoo's 5-minute history reaches 60 sessions, which caps the sample at three
releases, and two of those three (06-30, 07-31) were *small-surprise* prints where a null is close to
uninformative. The one large-surprise observation (08-28) is a single data point, and it is the one
carrying almost all the weight. The percentile baseline is drawn from the same 60 sessions that
contain the releases, so the comparison is in-sample by construction. The 09:45 window isolates a
09:45 release from an 08:30 release imperfectly, not perfectly — sustained drift from an 08:30 shock
still runs through it, which is why the 09:40 placebo bar is reported alongside. Beyond the
measurement: the date is `estimate` and structurally cannot be promoted from inside this lane (leg 1);
no October consensus exists at D-55 and none is expected until late October; the 68% ISM correlation
and 60% GDP correlation remain secondary-sourced summary statistics this repo has not computed;
Order Backlog was not separately reported in August, so one of the five weighted legs is missing from
any subcomponent read; and the entire October base case is downstream of a September print that has
not happened.
