## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **This is not a scheduled tax rise. It is a scheduled tax rise's *default setting*, and HM
Treasury says so on its own publication.** The 3p/l step is real, dated and already in secondary
legislation — SI 2026/164 as amended by SI 2026/555 expires the 5p rebate on **2026-12-31** and
switches on the 55.95p tier for **1 Jan – 28 Feb 2027**, so it needs **no positive act to happen and a
fresh SI to stop**. But the same gov.uk paper that sets those rates writes, verbatim, *"The
legislative default will be for rates to return to the levels set at Budget 2025 from 1 January 2027,
**but the government will confirm final rates at Budget 2026**"* — the decision is reserved to the
**2026-10-28 Autumn Budget**, 65 days before the step. **The base rate on that decision is 5 for 5, and
it is measured, not asserted:** DESNZ's own weekly fuel series carries the duty rate as a column across
**1,214 weeks since 2003**, and UK fuel duty **last rose on 2011-01-03** — **815 consecutive weeks**
without an increase, with the 5p cut's annual self-expiry overridden by SI every single year
(2022/365 → 2023/329 → 2024/300 → 2025/228 → 2026/164 → 2026/555). The stated reason for the last
override — *"elevated fuel prices arising from the Iran war"* — has **intensified**, not resolved:
petrol **164.40p** (+22.9% y/y, rank 25 of 1,214 weeks), diesel **186.36p** (+31.6%), Brent **$100.62**
(+50.9%). **Size, if it does land: about half a tenth.** HMT's own line is *"reduce CPI by 0.04
percentage points"*; ONS's 2026 weight (27.184/1000) times 3.6p on a 164.40p pump gives ~0.05pp —
**0.04–0.07pp** on the January-2027 headline, published ~2027-02-17 beside the January Ofgem step.
**And it will not be visible even then:** the 2022 cut was worth 6.0p at the pump and showed only
**−3.70p at four weeks, −0.28p at eight**, because crude ate it. **Nothing here is ours.** `symbols: []`,
no UK-listed name in this book, and 2027-01-01 is a **full market closure on both sides of the
Atlantic** — there is no session in which this could be priced. Date is **estimate**; it widens caution
and licenses nothing.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

Does the UK's 3p/l fuel duty rise actually happen on 1 January 2027, how big is it if it does, and is
any of it ours? The id reached this lane as **one** proposal,
`from-uk-cpi-2026-10-21` (filed 2026-09-09), read in full before a line of this was written — it is now
shadowed by the canonical `src/domain/market-events/uk-fuel-duty-rise-2027-01-01.json` written in this
PR. Its central correction is confirmed and carried forward: the 1p step a sibling lane had put into
the September 2026 CPI collection **was cancelled**, so no duty step sits in either the August or
September 2026 data.

**One-line verdict: the step is real and pre-armed, the decision is not, and the decision has gone the
same way five times running — none of which is tradeable here.**

quantitative legs computed from the publishers' own machine-readable series rather than from
commentary:

papered over.

### Leg 1 — is the 1 January step law, or an announcement? **SUPPORTED: it is law, and the default runs by inaction.**

This is the leg the proposal did not have, and it inverts the naive read. SI **2026/164** (made
2026-02-25) article 1 sets the effect windows; SI **2026/555** (made 2026-05-21, laid 2026-05-22)
article 2 amends them, substituting *"31st December 2026"* for *"31st August 2026"* and *"1st January
2027"* for *"1st December 2026"*, and omitting articles 4–7 entirely — the 1p-from-September tier.
What remains is a rebate that **expires by its own terms on 2026-12-31** and a 55.95p tier with effect
*"in the period beginning with 1st January 2027 and ending with 28th February 2027."*

So nobody has to do anything for the rise to occur. Somebody has to make a new SI for it not to.

### Leg 2 — but does the default actually run? **REFUTED, five times for five, and the publisher agrees.**

Two independent findings, one legal and one statistical.

**The publisher reserves the decision.** The gov.uk paper's own words: *"The legislative default will
be for rates to return to the levels set at Budget 2025 from 1 January 2027, but the government will
confirm final rates at Budget 2026."* It repeats the point a second time in the background section
(*"return to their previous trajectory from 1 January 2027, but the government will confirm final
rates at Budget 2026"*). HM Treasury is telling readers not to treat its own rate table as settled.

**The default has never run.** SI 2026/164's explanatory note quotes the statutory reason the question
recurs annually — the Excise Duties (Surcharges or Rebates) Act 1979 s.2(2): an order *"shall cease to
be in force at the expiration of a period of one year from the date on which it takes effect, unless
continued in force by a further order."* The cut is structurally self-expiring. It has been continued
**every year without exception**: 2022/365 → 2023/329 → 2024/300 → 2025/228 → 2026/164 → 2026/555.
"The legislative default is a rise" has therefore been true five times, and the rise has happened zero
times. The structure is not evidence against the base rate; it *is* the base rate's setting.

**Computed here from DESNZ's own duty column, 1,214 weekly rows, 2003-06-09 → 2026-09-07:**

| Finding | Value |
|---|---|
| Last week UK fuel duty **rose** | **2011-01-03**, 58.19p → 58.95p |
| Consecutive weeks with no increase | **815** (15 years 8 months) |
| Duty changes since | two, both **cuts**: 2011-03-28 → 57.95p, 2022-03-28 → 52.95p |
| Duty increases in the series before 2011 | ten, 2003–2011 — the pattern stopped, it did not thin out |

### Leg 3 — has the justification for deferring weakened? **REFUTED: it has intensified.**

The gov.uk paper states the policy objective as support *"in response to elevated fuel prices arising
from the Iran war."* Measured against the same DESNZ series, that condition is worse now than at the
deferral:

| Decision date | Week read | Petrol | Diesel | Petrol y/y | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spring Statement 2022-03-23 | 2022-03-21 | 165.37p | 177.47p | +32.8% | cut introduced |
| Spring Budget 2023-03-15 | 2023-03-13 | 146.97p | 167.04p | −8.1% | extended |
| Spring Budget 2024-03-06 | 2024-03-04 | 144.73p | 154.53p | −1.6% | extended |
| Autumn Budget 2024-10-30 | 2024-10-28 | 134.41p | 139.71p | −12.7% | extended |
| Budget 2025-11-26 | 2025-11-24 | 136.07p | 145.40p | +0.5% | extended (staged unwind announced) |
| Amendment 2026-05-21 | 2026-05-18 | 157.39p | 186.56p | +19.2% | extended, September step **cancelled** |
| **Autumn Budget 2026-10-28** | **2026-09-07 (latest)** | **164.40p** | **186.36p** | **+22.9%** | **pending** |

Note what this table does *not* say: deferral is **not** conditional on high prices — it happened in
2024 with pump prices falling 13% y/y. The base rate is unconditional, which makes it stronger, not
weaker. The 2026 reading simply removes the one argument that could have broken it. Corroborating
context: Brent **$100.62** on 2026-09-09, **+50.9% y/y**, against a two-year range of $60.47–$112.57;
current petrol ranks **25th of 1,214 weeks** and diesel **22nd**.

### Leg 4 — how big is it, if it lands? **SUPPORTED at ~half a tenth, by two independent methods.**

- **HM Treasury's own number.** The paper's macroeconomic-impact line: extending the 5p cut through
  Q4 2026 *"is expected to reduce CPI by 0.04 percentage points in quarter 4 2026"* — against a
  previous trajectory that had removed 3p by December.
- **Computed here.** 3p duty + 20% VAT = **3.6p** at the pump. On 164.40p petrol that is **+2.19%**
  (on 186.36p diesel, +1.93%). ONS's 2026 CPI weight for liquid fuels, vehicle fuels and lubricants
  (series **A9FS**) is **27.1840 parts per 1000**, so the contribution is ≈ **0.056pp** — and that is
  an **upper bound**, because the ONS class also carries heating oil and lubricants, which the duty
  step does not move proportionally.

Two methods, different inputs, same order of magnitude: **0.04–0.07pp** on the January-2027 headline
rate. For scale, headline CPI printed **2.9%** in July 2026 (ONS `D7G7`); this step is worth roughly
half of one rounding increment.

### Leg 5 — will it be observable? **REFUTED at pump-price resolution.**

Measured on the one clean natural experiment the series contains — the 2022-03-23 cut, worth **6.0p**
at the pump once VAT is applied, against the pre-cut week of 2022-03-21 (165.37p):

| Weeks after | Observed petrol change | % of the theoretical 6.0p |
|---|---|---|
| +1 (2022-03-28) | −2.72p | 45% |
| +2 | −3.46p | 58% |
| +4 (2022-04-18) | **−3.70p** | **62%** |
| +6 | −2.89p | 48% |
| +8 (2022-05-16) | **−0.28p** | **5%** |

The honest reading is **not** "pass-through is 62%" — that window is Ukraine-invasion crude chaos and
the two effects are not separable. The honest reading is the stronger one: **a duty step of this size
is not separable from crude at weekly resolution, and is fully swamped within two months.** Anyone
scoring this event off the pump price rather than the duty column will get a wrong answer.

### What plays the conditions support

**None.** `symbols: []`, this repo tracks no UK-listed instrument, and no house playbook (S1/S2/E1/
S3/S4, G1) is energy-, rates- or sterling-keyed. Two structural facts close the question rather than
merely discouraging it: the step takes effect on **2027-01-01**, a full closure of both the US equity
market (`new-years-day-market-closure-2027-01-01`) and the UK bond market
(`sifma-uk-bond-market-closure-2027-01-01`) — there is no session in which it could be priced — and
the information content was already released on **2026-05-22**, seven months earlier. This entry's
operational job is to be **ruled out** as an explanation for a January move, and to hold the base rate
so a future session does not mistake a legislated default for a forecast.

### Honest limits

- **The fiscal leg is reasoned, not fetched.** obr.uk and the Commons Library briefing CBP-10340 both
  returned **403** to this lane, with and without browser headers, and are recorded in
  `probe-ref.blocked` rather than substituted. So the Exchequer cost of a further deferral — the one
  argument that genuinely cuts *against* the base rate, in a Budget the Chancellor has framed on
  "fiscal discipline" — is unquantified here. That is the main reason the quarter call is graded
  **medium** and not high.
- **n = 5 is a small base rate on a political decision**, made by a Chancellor this lane has no read
  on, and the gov.uk paper itself notes the Budget-2025 plan resumes **RPI uprating in future years**
  — the freeze has an announced end even in the deferral scenario.
- **A partial resolution is live.** Budget 2025's own unwind was staged across three dates, so
  "defer January, keep March" has direct precedent. That is why the second step is proposed as its
  own event and why `FT-uk-fuel-duty-rise-2027-01-01-2` tests the two together.
- **The step is `estimate`, and both halves of that label are doing work** — the rate schedule is
  taxonomy-blocked from `confirmed` (no prefix covers HM Treasury), *and* the outcome is genuinely
  open by the publisher's own admission. Nothing date-keyed may act on either.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (estimate).** **Stand aside at every horizon — permanently, not pending information.** There
is no instrument, no session, and no playbook; the step's entire informational content was published
on 2026-05-22 and its resolution is a UK fiscal decision with no channel into this book. Separately,
and as a forecast rather than a position: **expect the 2026-10-28 Autumn Budget to defer, reduce or
cancel the 1 January 2027 step**, on a measured 5-for-5 base rate, 815 weeks without a UK duty
increase, and a stated policy justification that has intensified since the last deferral. Confidence
**medium** — the fiscal counter-argument is unquantified here because both sources carrying it were
blocked. Because the confidence is medium and the instrument set is empty, this changes **no** sizing:
a forecast is not a position.

**Kill switches:**

1. **The base rate breaks** — the DESNZ weekly road fuel prices row for **w/c 2027-01-04** prints a
   duty rate of **55.95p**. The default ran; 5-for-5 becomes 5-for-6 and every future UK duty ledger
   re-weights. (`FT-uk-fuel-duty-rise-2027-01-01-1`)
2. **The decision comes early** — HM Treasury publishes a fuel-duty measure, a written ministerial
   statement or a further SI **before 2026-10-28**. The "one venue, one date" model is wrong and the
   watch window moves forward.
3. **A price channel appears** — any tracked name (NVDA/AVGO/MRVL/CRWV) moves **>2%** on a session
   attributable to a UK fuel-duty headline before **2027-01-01**. The `symbols: []` premise is false
   and this document is rebuilt from scratch, not amended.
4. **The size is wrong by an order of magnitude** — the January-2027 CPI print (~2027-02-17) attributes
   **>0.15pp** of the monthly headline move to motor fuels. The ONS-weight arithmetic and HMT's own
   0.04pp are both wrong and Leg 4 needs redoing.
