## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** a weekly US petroleum inventory report lands at 10:30 ET on 2026-09-16,
in the middle of the loudest oil tape of the year and 3½ hours before an FOMC statement. What is
likely in it, does it move anything, and is there anything here for us to do?

**One-line verdict:** nothing to do — but the print is an **attribution hazard** worth a written
rule, because the one prior observation in this regime shows the tape ignoring the print completely.

**Method:** primary sources first, per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md). EIA's
own WPSR landing page, release-schedule page and Tables 1/4/9 CSVs were fetched directly
2026-09-15 (all HTTP 200, no blocked sources this session); prices are Yahoo daily bars and live
quotes pulled the same session (`^VIX`, `CL=F`, `BZ=F`, `RB=F`, `HO=F`, `XLE`, `USO`) at ~14:08 ET;
the September 2026 STEO supplies the structural forecast; press sources are cited with dates where
no primary exists. No instrument scripts were run — this is a macro/sector print with no symbol
attached, and `earnings-cycle`/`intraday-edges` have nothing to say about it. Every
trading-adjacent line below carries the event's **estimate** date label.

WTI front-month closed **96.05 → 102.48, +6.69%** that session, and Brent **101.21 → 107.63,
+6.34%**. Every number in the report argued for lower prices and crude had its largest single-day
gain of the window. The cause is not mysterious — drones launched from Iraqi territory damaged Saudi
Arabia's **7 mb/d East-West pipeline** the same day, forcing it shut (CNBC 2026-09-13; Bloomberg
2026-09-14), Saudi Arabia cancelled September-loading European cargoes, and Hormuz traffic sits near
**~10 ships/day**. A tanker was struck again on **09-13**.

**The honest weight of this:** one observation, in one regime, on a holiday-shifted release. It is
enough to justify a written attribution rule and a forward test; it is **not** enough to claim the
print is permanently inert, and the test is registered precisely so the next session scores it
rather than retrofits it.

### Leg 3 — what is mechanically likely in tomorrow's numbers? SUPPORTED on runs, MIXED on crude

**Refining is pinned at the top of its range.** Percent utilization printed **97.8** (prior week
98.0; **94.9** a year ago), with Gulf Coast **98.3**, Midwest **101.6** and only the East Coast soft
at **85.3**. Operable capacity has *shrunk* y/y, 18,027 kb/d against 18,160. The incentive is
extreme: heating oil at **$5.0174/gal** against WTI at **$105.98** is a crack of roughly
**$2.49/gal (~$104.75/bbl)**, at the very top of the September STEO's own ">$2/gal from August
through November" forecast; the gasoline crack is ~**$0.73/gal**. Refiners paid that much do not
voluntarily slow down. This is the basis of `FT-…-1`.

**Crude direction is genuinely two-sided, so no directional claim is made.** Pulling toward a draw:
17,586 kb/d of crude inputs at near-max runs, Cushing already thin at **21.824 Mbbl** (−8.5% y/y),
and the September STEO's view that US crude inventories sit below the 5-year low through end-2026.
Pulling toward a build: the flow mix flipped hard last week — crude **exports fell to 3,417 kb/d
from 4,483** and **net imports jumped to 3,407 from 2,287** — and domestic production printed
**13,947 kb/d** (including a **+65 kb/d** STEO re-benchmark EIA disclosed on the page). Those two
forces are the same order of magnitude. A ledger that guessed between them would be manufacturing
confidence it does not have.

**The line nobody prices weekly.** The SPR stands at **285.360 Mbbl**, down **29.6% y/y** from
405.224 and still drawing **1.244/wk**. Total crude including SPR is **−14.5% y/y**. Commercial
crude looks flat y/y (**−0.1%**) only because the strategic reserve absorbed the difference.

### Honest limits

- **No consensus figure for the week ending 09-11 was findable** from this lane's sources today, so both forward tests are keyed to the *prior print* and to observable levels rather than to a surprise-vs-consensus measure.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (event date `estimate`).** **Stand aside.** No position, no hedge and no watch-list entry
is keyed to this print. The deliverable of this ledger is a written **attribution rule** for our own
post-mortems, not a trade: *no 2026-09-16 price move is credited to the 10:30 ET petroleum report
unless the crude change's sign matches the session's close direction.* On the single prior
observation in this regime it did not, by a wide margin.

**Kill switches:**

1. **The print reasserts itself as the price-setter** — the 2026-09-16 commercial-crude w/w change and WTI's 09-16 close move in the textbook-matched directions (draw + up, or build + down). `FT-…-2` dies, the attribution rule loses its evidence, and the next session rebuilds Leg 2 from scratch.
2. **The runs thesis breaks** — percent utilization prints **below 96.0** on 2026-09-16, which would mean turnaround season or a Gulf Coast outage is already biting despite a ~$2.49/gal distillate crack. `FT-…-1` dies and Leg 3's one-sided half goes with it.
3. **We acquire a vehicle** — any house playbook gains an energy leg, or a tracked symbol gains direct crude exposure, before **2026-12-31**. Leg 4 flips from REFUTED to live and this stops being a refusal.
4. **The shock resolves** — the Saudi East-West pipeline returns to service and Hormuz traffic normalizes toward its ~20 mb/d baseline. The regime Leg 2 is scoped to ends, and the n=1 observation stops generalizing forward.
5. **Date failure (void, not killed)** — no WPSR publishes by **13:00 ET 2026-09-16**. That falsifies the date, not the analysis; both forward tests void rather than score.
