## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This event arrived as a proposal from the [`ecb-decision-2026-10-29`](ecb-decision-2026-10-29.md) D-16 pulse
(2026-10-13), which needed a dated scoring point for its supply-side kill. Its two siblings answered the
same shape of question differently: [`eurostat-hicp-final-2026-09-17`](eurostat-hicp-final-2026-09-17.md)
was a receipt on a sixteen-day-old number, while [`eurostat-hicp-flash-2026-10-01`](eurostat-hicp-flash-2026-10-01.md)
arrived before the decision it could inform. This one is the third kind: **a receipt that happens to
carry a pre-registered kill.** So the question is: *does the complete release change the flash's story, and
is there any version of this event worth a position?*

**One-line verdict:** the headline almost certainly holds at 3.8%, the services cell is what matters and is
very likely to stay at or above 3.1%, and none of it is a trade.

dissemination API (`prc_hicp_minr`, index `2025=100`; geo `EA`, `EA20`, `EA21`). Secondary search for the

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is 2026-10-16, 12:00 CEST (06:00 EDT).** SUPPORTED, two primaries. Eurostat's flash release
   says the full September data is "scheduled for October 16, 2026"; the ECB calendar row reads
   "16/10/2026 12:00". This is the same convention the two siblings used and both scored on it
   (FT-…-09-17-1 and FT-…-10-01-1 passed). Both zones are on summer time until 2026-10-25, so 12:00 CEST
   is 06:00 EDT. Registered as FT-1 anyway: three tested months is a short run, and the convention is
   reused monthly.

2. **The complete release is usually a receipt, and the one revision on record was downward.** SUPPORTED
   from the sibling ledger's table: April 3.0→3.0, May 3.2→3.2, June 2.8→2.8, July 2.9→2.9 (flash →
   complete), then August **3.3→3.2**. Four of five unrevised; the one revision was −0.1pp. No upward
   revision appears in the run.

3. **The headline is computable from the database, and it sits on a rounding edge.** SUPPORTED, registered
   rather than claimed. The euro-area `EA` all-items index is **100.52** for September 2025 and **103.69**
   for August 2026 (fetched today); the flash gave **+0.6% m/m**. So September y/y is
   `103.69 × 1.006 ÷ 100.52 − 1` = **3.77%**, and across the flash's rounding band (m/m 0.55–0.65) it spans
   **3.72–3.82%**. Intersected with the flash's own rounding (a published 3.8 means unrounded ≥3.75), the
   unrounded flash sits in roughly **3.75–3.82**: still 3.8 on a clean confirm, with a downward revision of
   as little as 0.03pp enough to print **3.7**, and an upward one needing at least +0.03pp. Leans toward
   "3.8 or 3.7, not 3.9". The `EA20` series gives 103.67 and a similar answer; the headline aggregate
   uses the 21-member area (Bulgaria joined 2026-01-01), hence `EA` here. The sibling flash ledger's caveat
   still applies: database rates and press-release rates can differ by up to 0.1pp.

4. **Services is the cell that matters, and it is a larger revision than the series has seen to unwind.**
   SUPPORTED. [`ecb-decision-2026-10-29`](ecb-decision-2026-10-29.md)'s supply-side kill names "services HICP
   re-accelerating above its August 3.0%" and says to score it at this release. The flash printed **3.2%**.
   From the database, services index was **100.85** (Sep 2025) and **104.82** (Aug 2026 → 2.997% = 3.0%);
   a 3.2% reading means September's services index sits near **104.1** (m/m ≈ −0.7%, against the base's −0.9%
   package-holiday roll-off). Getting back to ≤3.0% needs an index near 103.9 or lower — a **~0.15–0.2pp**
   downward revision to one component. The only revision on record in this series is 0.1pp on the headline,
   and Eurostat's food revision in August (1.2→1.1) was the same size on a smaller component. Rating:
   services ≥3.1% in the complete release is **likely (~85–90%)**, which means the ECB ledger's kill is more
   likely confirmed than un-fired. That is a statement about the *ECB ledger's* inputs and is routed there.

5. **The contribution table is precomputable, as the sibling showed.** SUPPORTED. Using the sibling's implied
   weights (energy 9.1%, services 47.0%, NEIG 25.6%, food 19.2%), which reproduced its own August table
   within 0.02pp (energy +1.29pp vs 1.30 predicted; services +1.43pp vs 1.41): energy 18.8% → **+1.71pp**,
   services 3.2% → **+1.50pp**, NEIG 1.1% → **+0.28pp**, food 1.4% → **+0.27pp**, summing to **3.76pp**
   against a 3.8% headline. Registered as FT-4.

6. **It is the last full euro inflation datum before the ECB votes, and the window to react is short.**
   SUPPORTED from this calendar. Between this release and [`ecb-decision-2026-10-29`](ecb-decision-2026-10-29.md)
   the euro area publishes only the 10-27 monetary developments and bank lending survey; the October
   flash (11-04) lands *after* the decision. 10-20 is the last session before
   [`ecb-quiet-period-start-2026-10-21`](ecb-quiet-period-start-2026-10-21.md) on which Governing Council
   members may speak freely on policy. So if this print is read as a change, the on-record reaction falls on
   10-16 → 10-20. Recorded as calendar visibility, not a price channel.

7. **No tracked symbol carries a channel, and the argument is the siblings'.** SUPPORTED. `symbols: []`;

   decisions at 0.97x–1.17x an ordinary day for the Euro Stoxx 50 and DAX. Tier stays **low**.
