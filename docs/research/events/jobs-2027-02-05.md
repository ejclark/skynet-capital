## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This id reached the calendar as a proposal from
[`treasury-refunding-2027-02-03`](treasury-refunding-2027-02-03.md)'s own initial research, filed
because a payroll on the refunding Friday means no bond move in that week can be attributed to the
supply announcement on the tape alone. That proposal handed forward one **explicitly unverified**
claim, and it deserves credit for labelling it as such:

> CARRIED, NOT VERIFIED THIS SESSION: the January Employment Situation is also the edition that
> normally carries the CES annual benchmark revision and updated household-survey population
> controls, which would make it the highest-content payroll print of the year… it is not
> load-bearing for the proposal, which stands on the date rule and the corridor position alone.

So: **is the highest-content-print claim true where it can actually be tested — in the price?** Three
questions follow. **Does the derived date survive an independent re-derivation, and is the primary
retrievable yet?** **What is February's seasonal profile, given that three sibling lanes have now
measured three different ones?** And: **the proposing lane's own concern was an attribution problem
for the refunding ledger — does the payroll side of that corridor carry any effect the sibling
[`jobs-2027-05-07`](jobs-2027-05-07.md) has not already refuted?**

**One-line verdict:** the date reproduces 7-for-7 and stays `estimate` because every BLS route 403s;
the mechanism is **still unsourced** but its market-side consequence is **real and large** — February
is the **rank-1 equity-and-credit payroll month of twelve**, robust to leave-one-out, era splits and
the tightest local control available; the effect is a **center shift, not a tail** and is confined to
the print session; and the sibling's refunding-month null is **decomposed rather than overturned** —
it is the average of this month and three ordinary ones.

### Method

Macro-print mode per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md) — no symbol-keyed
instrument applies (`symbols: []`). Nothing was inherited from the proposing lane: the scheduling
rule, the FOMC dates and the payroll series were rebuilt from their own definitions or from primary

- **Attempted and failed, recorded rather than worked around.** `bls.gov` under a browser
  user-agent, four routes, **HTTP 403 on all four** (`/schedule/news_release/2027_sched.htm`,
  `/schedule/news_release/empsit.htm`, `/schedule/home.htm`, `/bls/news-release/empsit.htm`). All

- **Attempted and SUCCEEDED.** `federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm` returned
  **HTTP 200** (164,831 bytes, page-stated *"Last Update: August 19, 2026"*). That is the `FED:`
  primary for every 2027 meeting date this document uses.
- **Measured, with Yahoo split/dividend-adjusted daily bars**, on **SPY, QQQ, TLT, IEF, SHY, LQD,
  AGG, HYG, ^VIX**, 1993–2026 (**380** usable payroll sessions, 1993-02-05 … 2026-09-04, of which
  **34** are February-released). Payroll dates were *generated by the rule*, not looked up, then
  intersected with each series' own trading days. February opex was computed from its definition
  (third Friday) and cross-checked against [`opex-2027-02-19`](opex-2027-02-19.md).
- **Statistics:** medians throughout; two-sided permutation tests on the difference of medians
  (20,000 relabelings, fixed seed; 8,000 inside the leave-one-out loop) for magnitude; two-sided
  binomial against a 50% null for direction. Robustness by leave-one-out, by three era splits, and by
  a **tight local control** — the payroll session against the ten nearest *other February* sessions —
  all reported, including where a control weakens a leg.
- **Re-grepped, not cited:** `docs/plans/trade-playbooks.md`, `docs/research/multi-symbol-sweep.md`.
- **Deliberately NOT re-run:** [`jobs-2027-09-03`](jobs-2027-09-03.md)'s refutations of the
  *days-to-FOMC-decision* framing (392 releases) and of the *communications-blackout* framing (12
  tests, one nominal hit). Both are cited as settled; this print sits **D+9 after** a decision, on
  the far side of the same null. [`jobs-2027-10-08`](jobs-2027-10-08.md)'s inter-print-gap refutation
  is likewise cited, not re-run.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date reproduces independently — SUPPORTED, and it stays `estimate`.** The rule (release on
   the third Friday following the end of the Sun–Sat reference week containing the 12th) was coded
   from its definition and run over 1993–2028 before being applied. It reproduces **7 of 7** knowns:
   the four `confirmed` entries on this calendar (2026-09-04, 2026-10-02, 2026-11-06, 2026-12-04) and
   the three historical Sunday-July-4 releases (2004-07-02, 2010-07-02, 2021-07-02). For January 2027
   the reference week is Sun 2027-01-10 → Sat 2027-01-16 and the third Friday after 01-16 is
   **2027-02-05**. It stays `estimate` on two counts: no primary was retrievable (four blocked
   fetches above), and this lane does not self-confirm.

2. **The rule's failure mode is quantified for the first time, and it fires on the print BEFORE this
   one — SUPPORTED, and it is why that neighbour is not proposed here.** Run against SPY's own
   trading calendar, the rule emits a **non-session date 24 times in 404** releases 1993–2026 (Good
   Fridays, July 4ths, and four New Year's Days). Across the whole forward window 2026-10 → 2028-01
   it fires **exactly once**: **2027-01-01**, the December 2026 reference month — the payroll
   immediately preceding this one. That is not this lane's to fix, and the fix already exists:
   [`proposals/jobs-2027-01-08.from-treasury-coupon-announcement-2027-01-07.json`] derives a
   one-week slip to **2027-01-08**, evidenced 3-for-3 by BLS archive filenames
   (`empsit_01082010.pdf`, `empsit_01082016.pdf`, `empsit_01082021.pdf`, surfaced by search, not
   fetched — bls.gov 403s). **One honest discrepancy in the count, banked not resolved:** that
   proposal describes its three as *"the only years since 1998 where December 12 falls on a
   Saturday,"* but December 12 **1998** was itself a Saturday and the rule duly emits **1999-01-01**,
   making the candidate set **four**. No archive evidence for January 1999 was obtainable this
   session, so this is recorded as an unresolved count, **not** as a correction to that lane's
   conclusion. It matters here only through the inter-print gap, and that turns out not to matter at
   all: excluding all four affected Februaries (1999, 2010, 2016, 2021) leaves this document's
   headline result unchanged at **1.73×** (*p*=0.0191, n=30).

4. **The amplification survives every control that could kill it — SUPPORTED.** Four tests:

   | Test | SPY | HYG | QQQ |

   | vs every other payroll | **1.73×** *p*=0.0138 | **1.82×** *p*=0.0322 | 1.28× *p*=0.301 |

   The local control is the important one: it compares the print to February sessions *five days
   either side of it*, so no month-level seasonality, no January-effect residue and no
   volatility-regime drift can produce it. **SPY strengthens under it** (1.73× → 1.78×, *p* 0.0138 →
   0.0015). The era splits **agree in sign in all three** without any one reaching significance
   alone (n=10, 7, 17) — read that as consistency, not as three confirmations. QQQ passes the local
   control and fails the pooled and leave-one-out tests, so **QQQ is carried as MIXED**, not as part
   of the claim.

5. **It is a wider middle, not a fatter tail — SUPPORTED, and this is what the stance is allowed to
   say.** Three checks all point the same way. (a) **Tails are ordinary**: February payrolls clear
   the all-payroll 95th-percentile \|move\| **1 of 34** in SPY (2.563%; 1.7 expected), 1 of 27 in
   QQQ, **0 of 24** in TLT, 1 of 24 in IEF, 1 of 19 in HYG. (b) **The next session is flat**: D+1
   \|move\| SPY 1.04× (*p*=0.90), QQQ 1.06× (0.81), TLT 0.99×, AGG 0.70× (0.21) — nothing
   significant in either direction. (c) **The fortnight after is if anything calmer**: forward
   10-session realized vol SPY **0.88×** (*p*=0.31), QQQ 0.79× (0.23), TLT 0.97×, IEF 0.93×, ^VIX
   0.93×. The whole effect lives inside one session and does not spill.

6. **The variance-share result is the exact inverse of the October sibling's, and that contrast is
   the point — SUPPORTED.** [`jobs-2027-10-08`](jobs-2027-10-08.md) found a print taking 21.5% of its
   front-month window's realized variance and correctly deflated it: the window was **half as long**,
   so the share rose by division and per-session vol was unchanged. Here the window is the **ordinary
   10 sessions** (leg 7), the measurement is a **fixed 11-bar window on both sides**, and the share
   still roughly doubles:

   | | SPY | HYG | QQQ | TLT |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | February share | **12.9%** | **11.6%** | **12.9%** | 15.5% |
   | other-payroll share | 5.4% | 3.4% | 4.6% | 8.7% |

   Length is matched by construction, so this cannot be arithmetic; and leg 5 shows the denominator
   is flat-to-lower, so it is the **numerator** — the print itself — doing the work. Two lanes, one
   statistic, two genuinely different causes.

7. **The runway is ordinary, and saying so is the useful part — SUPPORTED.** 2027-02-05 is a
   first-Friday release, so [`opex-2027-02-19`](opex-2027-02-19.md) sits **14 calendar days** later;
   across all 34 February payrolls the trading-session distance is **9 or 10, median 10**, with
   nothing else in the distribution. A reader arriving from the October ledger should **not** import
   its half-runway constraint: February is the plain-vanilla configuration, and everything unusual
   about this date is in the print, not in the calendar around it.

8. **The proposing lane's corridor concern is real but belongs to the refunding ledger, not this one
   — SUPPORTED as a boundary.** The proposal's stated worry was attribution: with
   [`ism-manufacturing-2027-02-01`](ism-manufacturing-2027-02-01.md) on the Monday,
   [`ism-services-2027-02-03`](ism-services-2027-02-03.md) ninety minutes after the 08:30 ET
   announcement on the Wednesday and a payroll on the Friday, **no bond move anywhere in that week
   can be attributed to supply on the tape alone**. That is a correct and useful warning, and it is
   an instruction for [`treasury-refunding-2027-02-03`](treasury-refunding-2027-02-03.md)'s ledger,
   which owns the supply question. It implies nothing about how this print behaves, and this document
   makes no claim about the refunding announcement.

9. **…but the sibling's refunding-month null needed decomposing, and February is the whole of it —
   SUPPORTED, and this is a refinement of an existing result rather than a contradiction.**
   [`jobs-2027-05-07`](jobs-2027-05-07.md) measured refunding-week payrolls at **0.96–1.09×** an
   ordinary payroll Friday across seven instruments and concluded, correctly, that looming supply
   does not amplify the print. That number **reproduces here**: all four refunding months pooled
   against the other eight give SPY **1.07×** (*p*=0.76). But the pool is not homogeneous:

   So the pooled 1.07× is the average of a **rank-1 month** and **three ordinary ones**. The
   supply-overhang hypothesis stays **refuted** — the three refunding months that are *not* February
   are indistinguishable from the eight that are not refunding months at all (0.94×, *p*=0.74). What
   the pooled number concealed is a **calendar** effect that happens to share a month with the
   refunding cycle. Both lanes are right; only the framing needed splitting.

10. **The benchmark-revision mechanism gets no support from the one test available to price data —
    MIXED, and honestly so.** If February's amplification came from the annual benchmark revision's
    *surprise* content, it should have weakened once BLS began publishing a **preliminary** benchmark
    estimate ahead of the February release each year (from 2003). It did not: SPY 1993–2003 **1.68×**
    (*p*=0.205, n=11) against 2004–2026 **1.78×** (*p*=0.0209, n=23). No decay, if anything a slight
    strengthening. That does not refute the mechanism — a revision can move markets while being
    pre-announced, and the January print also carries refreshed population controls, new seasonal
    factors and the year's noisiest post-holiday adjustment, none separable from price data alone.
    **The effect is measured; the cause is not identified, and this document does not claim one.**

11. **Direction is not predictable — SUPPORTED as a refusal.** Whether the print-day sign continues
    over the following five sessions, February set: SPY **21/34** (*p*=0.229), QQQ 14/27 (1.00),
    TLT 13/24 (0.839), IEF 13/22 (0.523), LQD 16/24 (0.152), AGG 14/23 (0.405), HYG 11/19 (0.648),
    ^VIX 16/34 (0.864). Nothing here fixes a sign, and a bigger median move with an unfixed sign is a
    *sizing* fact, never a directional one.

12. **The structural claims, from a `FED:` primary — SUPPORTED.**
    `federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm` (HTTP 200, page-stated *"Last Update:
    August 19, 2026"*) lists 2027 as January 26-27, March 16-17\*, April 27-28, June 8-9\*, July
    27-28, September 14-15\*, October 26-27, December 7-8\*, the asterisk marking a Summary of
    Economic Projections. So this print is the **first payroll after the January meeting** (D+9) and
