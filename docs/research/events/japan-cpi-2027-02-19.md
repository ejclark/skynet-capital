## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This event was discovered in the [japan-cpi-2027-03-19](japan-cpi-2027-03-19.md) initial-research
adjacency sweep and filed with an unusually specific claim already attached to it, recorded in its
own `notes`: that **this** print, not the Tokyo February flash released a week later, is the better
free estimate of the February core number the Bank of Japan misses by one day when it decides on
2027-03-18. The sibling measured that on 27 Februaries and moved on. Two questions are left for
this ledger, and neither is the sibling's.

**First: does the claim survive being scored on January prints rather than February ones?** The
sibling's benchmark asks how well month *m−1* predicts month *m*, tested at *m* = February. This
event *is* the *m−1* term. Nobody has asked how well this print is itself anticipated, or whether
January is a normal month to be doing this arithmetic in.

**Second: is January special?** The obvious prior — and this session's opening hypothesis — is that
it should be. Japanese firms reset prices at the calendar-year turn, and a New-Year effect would
mean the board is carrying a stale number across an unusually noisy hop.

**One-line verdict: the claim survives and is stronger at January than at February, but the
hypothesis behind the second question is refuted — January is a dead-average month, Japanese core
y/y is uniformly persistent, and the one month where that uniformity breaks is April, which is
exactly where 2027's food consumption-tax cut lands.**

The stance is unchanged and was never in doubt — stand aside, `impact: low`, `symbols: []`. What
changes is that the corridor now has a measured boundary rather than an assumed one, and that two
US-side events sharing this print's date have been found and proposed.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **January is not a special month — REFUTES this session's own opening hypothesis.** The New-Year
   price-reset prior predicts that national core y/y should move more from December to January than
   in a typical month. Ranking all twelve calendar months by mean absolute change in national core
   y/y from the prior month, 2000-01 → 2026-07 (n=26-27 per month):

   | Rank (noisiest first) | Month | meanAbs | median | Rank 2018+ |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 1 | **April** | **0.366** | 0.230 | 1 (0.440) |
   | 2 | November | 0.204 | 0.116 | 4 |
   | 3 | May | 0.201 | 0.116 | 11 |
   | 4 | December | 0.197 | 0.115 | 3 |
   | 5 | July | 0.189 | 0.117 | 10 |
   | 6 | February | 0.178 | 0.116 | 2 |
   | 7 | **January** | **0.166** | 0.115 | **7** (0.217) |
   | 8 | October | 0.160 | 0.115 | 9 |
   | 9 | September | 0.138 | 0.113 | 6 |
   | 10 | June | 0.134 | 0.112 | 12 |
   | 11 | March | 0.129 | 0.115 | 8 |
   | 12 | August | 0.125 | 0.114 | 5 |

   January sits at **rank 7 of 12 on both samples**, below the all-month mean of 0.182pp. The
   hypothesis is refuted cleanly: whatever New-Year repricing happens in Japan does not show up as
   extra movement in the year-on-year *rate*. This print carries an ordinary amount of new
   information, and the sibling's persistence logic is not being applied across an unusually
   noisy hop.

2. **The Tokyo flash loses to persistence at every January window — SUPPORTS this event's own
   calendar note, more strongly than the sibling supports its own.** Replicating the
   [japan-cpi-2027-03-19](japan-cpi-2027-03-19.md) benchmark on the same e-Stat files and pointing
   it at January: predict national core y/y for month *m* from the **Tokyo flash for month *m***
   versus from the **national print for month *m−1***, both published before the national *m*
   release.

   | Sample | Tokyo flash meanAbs / median | Persistence meanAbs / median | Tokyo win rate |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | All months 2000-01 → 2026-07 (n=319) | 0.250 / 0.219 | **0.182 / 0.115** | 114/319 = 36% |
   | All months 2018-01 → 2026-07 (n=103) | **0.201 / 0.135** | 0.231 / 0.218 | 54/103 = 52% |
   | **Januaries 2000 → 2026 (n=27)** | 0.273 / 0.227 | **0.166 / 0.115** | **9/27 = 33%** |
   | **Januaries 2018 → 2026 (n=9)** | 0.205 / 0.118 | **0.217 / 0.115** | **4/9 = 44%** |
   | Februaries 2000 → 2026 (n=27), sibling replication | 0.288 / 0.225 | **0.178 / 0.116** | 9/27 = 33% |
   | Februaries 2018 → 2026 (n=9) | **0.253 / 0.118** | 0.353 / 0.227 | 6/9 = 67% |

   The sibling's numbers replicate exactly. The new row that matters is the fourth: **at January,
   the Tokyo flash loses head-to-head even inside the 2018+ window** — 44%, the only sub-coin-flip
   2018+ cell in the matrix, against 52% for all months and 67% for February. So the one defence
   the flash had at this event's sibling is unavailable here. Note the honest wrinkle: on Januaries
   2018+ the flash has the *lower mean* (0.205 vs 0.217) while losing the head-to-head, the
   signature of an estimator with fewer large misses and more small ones. Both are stated; neither
   supports "skilled."

3. **The April cliff — SUPPORTED, new, and the most valuable thing this session found.** Leg 1's
   table has one genuine outlier. **March→April is 0.366pp mean absolute**, rank 1 of 12 on the
   full sample and rank 1 again post-2018 at 0.440pp. It is **1.8× the second-worst month** and
   **2.0× the all-month average**; the other eleven months sit inside a narrow 0.125-0.204pp band.
   That is not a marginal ranking artefact of the kind leg 1's rank-7 placement would be — it is a
   single month separated from the pack on both samples.

   The mechanism is **inferred, not proven**: Japan's fiscal year starts 1 April and it is the
   conventional date for scheduled price and fee revisions. What the finding licenses is a
   boundary, not a story. Every "the stale print is nearly as good" argument in this corridor —
   this event's own calendar note, the sibling's leg 2, the BoJ ledger's reading of its March
   meeting — rests on Japanese core y/y being persistent. Leg 1 shows that persistence is
   remarkably uniform across eleven months. Leg 3 shows it **breaks at exactly one boundary**, and
   2027's instance of that boundary carries the [food consumption-tax
   cut](japan-food-tax-cut-2027-04-01.md) effective **2027-04-01**, which that ledger sizes at
   **−1.19pp on core** from the Bureau's own 2025-base weight table.

   **This finding is handed to `japan-food-tax-cut-2027-04-01` and `boj-decision-2027-04-28`, not
   acted on here** — those are other lanes' events and one file per event (issue #1449) means this
   PR does not touch them. **It is also deliberately NOT registered as a forward test**, and the
   reason is the honest one: 2027's April hop is confounded by a −1.19pp policy shock, so a
   prediction that it exceeds every other hop would pass almost automatically and would test the
   tax cut rather than the seasonal. A confounded test that cannot fail is worse than no test.
