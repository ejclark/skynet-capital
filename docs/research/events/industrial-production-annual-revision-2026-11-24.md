## Initial research

### The question, plainly

On 2026-11-24 the Federal Reserve Board plans to issue the annual revision to its industrial production
and capacity utilization indexes. Does the date hold, does it come with a market footprint, and is there
any instrument in this repo it should attach to? The proposal (`proposals/…from-industrial-production-2026-11-17.json`)
asked a fourth question: is a low-impact restatement worth its own session? This one is, narrowly: it
turns a proposal into a confirmed date and registers two scoreable tests, and nothing more.

### Method

Primary sources only, fetched direct 2026-11-09: the Board's announcements feed
(`feeds/g17.html`, 619,956 bytes), the G.17 release-date table (`releases/g17/release_dates.htm`, 117,274
bytes) and the current release page (`releases/g17/current/default.htm`, 116,335 bytes), all HTTP 200.
The feed was parsed for every annual-revision notice and every "Annual Revision Released" posting, and
each notice date was matched to its release date. VIX from Yahoo's chart endpoint. No symbol-keyed
instrument was run: `symbols: []`, so `earnings-cycle` and `intraday-edges` have nothing to attach to.

### Leg 1: the date holds (SUPPORTED, high)

The Board's feed carries the notice dated 2026-09-18 and the current release page still shows it, 52 days
later. Across the feed's record, every date-specific notice was followed by the revision on exactly the
date it named: 2017-03-31, 2018-03-23, 2019-03-27, 2021-05-28, 2022-06-28, 2023-03-28, 2024-06-28 and
2025-11-24, **8 of 8**. The quarter-only placeholders ("tentatively plans … in the fourth quarter of
2025", "in the autumn of 2026") are a different class and are not counted. The one caveat is lead
time: first date-specific notices ran 11 days (2023) to 43 days (2024) ahead; this one was 67, longer
than any, so the sample is silent on a notice this early. That is why the status moved on a record and
not on a posted time. The proposal suggested a time as the promotion bar; none is posted, and that
departure is stated rather than hidden.

### Leg 2: what gets revised and by how much (MIXED, medium)

Scope per the notice: base year 2022; 2023 and 2024 Census manufacturing benchmarks; per-industry
seasonal factors and monthly indicators; some estimation methods; capacity through Q4-2025 (Census
Quarterly Survey of Plant Capacity Utilization) plus USGS, DOE and other capacity data. Size record, from
the Board's own "Released" summaries: 2022, little revision to manufacturing growth and higher
utilization; 2023, no yearly total-IP growth rate revised by more than 0.6pp and utilization little
changed; 2024, no new benchmark and two years revised by 0.1pp; 2025, manufacturing output revised
from a rise to a decline of more than 1-1/2% net (Feb 2020 to Aug 2025) and Aug 2025 manufacturing
utilization 1-1/4pp below its previous estimate. Two benchmark years and a base-year change put this
revision nearer 2025 than 2024. MIXED because the Board states no total-industry level change for any
year; FT-2 exists to produce that observation.

### Leg 3: a market footprint (REFUTED for tradable purposes, medium)

The parent ledger measured the monthly G.17: its own channel (the GDPNow Equipment contribution) moves a
median 0.0159pp and a retail-free G.17 vintage is not distinguishable from an ordinary day (p=0.342,
n=84). An annual revision has no GDPNow vintage in the Atlanta Fed schedule this session could find to
test, and the sibling's 11-17 Equipment test (`FT-industrial-production-2026-11-17-1`) does not
cover it. This session did not re-run the sibling's study and did not measure a revision-day tape
effect; it carries no claim of one. Honest limit: the repo has no revision-day sample.
