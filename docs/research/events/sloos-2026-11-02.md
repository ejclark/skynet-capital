## Initial research

### The question, plainly

The Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey publishes at 14:00 ET on **2026-11-02**, the Monday after the
October 27–28 FOMC and the day before the midterms. It was proposed to this calendar four days ago by
the [`fomc-minutes-2026-11-18`](fomc-minutes-2026-11-18.md) initial research, off the same November
calendar page — filed `low`, with the honest note that it *"has no measured reaction function in this
book."* So the question is exactly that: **does SLOOS move anything, and if not, what is this
document for?**

**One-line verdict:** it does not move anything measurable — KRE's excess return vs SPY on all three
2026 releases sits at the 73rd, 55th and 9th percentile of its own daily distribution — but its
**response window closes in early October**, which makes the November edition the first available
read on how a September hike (if one comes) transmits into bank credit standards. Date **confirmed**;
no directional call.

**Method:** sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md) —
federalreserve.gov primaries fetched and parsed directly today (2026-08-31), plus a price measurement

earnings instruments run: `symbols: []`, no issuer. Genre model:

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date is FED-primary twice over — flipped `estimate` → `confirmed`.** SUPPORTED. Two
   independent federalreserve.gov pages, both fetched today. The November 2026 calendar page lists
   **"Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey on Bank Lending Practices (SLOOS)" at 2:00 p.m. on November
   2**. The SLOOS landing page states the convention verbatim — *"The Federal Reserve generally
   conducts the survey quarterly, timing it so that results are available for the January/February,
   April/May, August, and October/November meetings of the Federal Open Market Committee"* — and 2026
   obeys it three for three, each release the Monday after an FOMC: **02-02** (after Jan 27–28),
   **05-04** (after Apr 28–29), **08-03** (after Jul 28–29). Oct 27–28 → Monday **2026-11-02**. The
   published row and the published rule agree, so the derivation is the source's, not mine. The
   calendar entry's standing `EST:` note — filed estimate purely because the proposing sweep would
   have been self-confirming — is now closed by an independent session. `FED:` prefix, **confirmed**.

2. **The reaction function is measurably absent — on the instrument that should carry it.** SUPPORTED,
   and this is the leg the sibling minutes ledger could not build. That document proved its refusal by
   *index move size*; SLOOS days are not quiet by that standard (reported S&P moves of −0.84%, +0.30%,
   +1.79%), so size alone would prove nothing. The right instrument is the regional-bank complex, and
   measured from daily closes this session:

   | SLOOS release | KRE | XLF | KRE excess vs SPY | Percentile of KRE's own 2026 \|excess\| |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 2026-02-02 | +1.79% | +1.10% | **+1.29%** | 73rd |
   | 2026-05-04 | −1.19% | −0.65% | **−0.82%** | 55th |
   | 2026-08-03 | +1.31% | +0.77% | **−0.11%** | 9th |

   Reference distribution, same pull: KRE |excess vs SPY| **median 0.75%**, p75 1.36%, **p90 2.09%**,
   p95 2.72% (n=164 sessions, 2026-01-01 → 08-28). Nothing crosses p90, the signs do not agree, and
   the mean excess is **+0.12%**. The attribution evidence points the same way: contemporaneous wraps
   for all three sessions name a driver, and it is never SLOOS — tech rotation out of the leaders
   (02-02), an AAPL earnings beat alongside a UAE/Iran missile-interception headline (05-04), and
   Amazon crossing $3T with the Dow at a record (08-03).

3. **The response window closes in early October — this is the finding that earns the row.**
   SUPPORTED, by derivation that is labelled as such. The July 2026 edition publishes its own
   collection dates: respondent banks **received the survey 2026-06-17**, responses were **due
   2026-07-02**, release **2026-08-03** — i.e. sent ~47 days before release, due ~32 days before.
   Applied to a **11-02** release that is **sent ≈ 2026-09-16, due ≈ 2026-10-02**. Two consequences,
   pulling opposite ways: the survey captures bank views formed in roughly the **two weeks after the
   09-16 FOMC**, making it the first structured read on that decision's credit transmission; and it is
   **~four weeks stale** relative to the 10-28 meeting whose Monday it follows. *This arithmetic is
   mine, off one edition's published dates — the Fed does not publish a forward collection calendar,
   and the next pulse should re-derive it when the October edition's dates appear.*

4. **What the last three editions actually say: a fading tightening impulse, on standards that are
   still historically tight.** SUPPORTED. January 2026 (Q4-2025 data): tighter standards for
   commercial loans. April 2026 (Q1): **tighter** C&I standards, basically unchanged C&I demand;
   unchanged CRE standards with weaker-or-unchanged demand. July 2026 (Q2, 56 domestic banks + 18
   foreign branches): C&I standards **basically unchanged** with **stronger** demand from large and
   middle-market firms; **easier** CRE standards; mixed household standards with weaker RRE demand;
   a modest net share tightening **credit cards**. So the direction of change has gone tighter →
   tighter → flat. But the July **special questions** — the levels rather than the changes — report
   *"the levels of standards are currently at the tighter end of the range"* since 2005 **for all loan
   categories except C&I**, where standards remain *"generally easier than their midpoints."* Flat
   changes on top of a tight level is not the same story as easy credit, and that distinction is the
   single most misread thing about this release.

5. **Low market impact is not low informational content — the Fed's own research says so.**
   SUPPORTED. A February 2026 FEDS Note (Baylor, Keane, Morgan and Zlate, published **2026-02-23**)
   isolates the component of banks' *expected* standards that observable macro and financial
   conditions do not explain — an Expected Credit Supply Index — and finds it carries predictive power
   for subsequent lending-standard changes, *"more persistent, especially for business loans."* That
   is the same scarcity-not-power shape the minutes ledger argues for its own release: a document
   worth reading precisely because the tape does not price it on the day.

6. **Our exposure is inherited and thin, with one non-obvious link that stops short of a position.**
   MIXED. `symbols: []`. This calendar's names are mega-cap tech; bank credit standards transmit to
   regional banks, CRE and small caps, which is why leg 2 measures KRE rather than QQQ. The one link
   worth naming is **CRWV** — a debt-financed datacenter builder in a book that already tracks
   [`crwv-2026-11-10-print`](crwv-2026-11-10-print.md) eight days later — but its financing runs
   through private credit and asset-backed markets, **not** the surveyed bank channel, so SLOOS is at
   best a sentiment read on that theme. Explicitly not a position, and not a reason to add symbols to
   this entry.

7. **It publishes through a funding lapse.** SUPPORTED. The Federal Reserve funds itself from its own
   earnings rather than appropriations, so a **2026-09-30** lapse does not stop it; the measured 2025
   precedent carried by the sibling ledgers is BLS cancellations and delays (Oct-2025 PPI *Canceled*,
   Sep-2025 import/export prices delayed 47 days), with no Fed analogue. In a lapse scenario SLOOS and
   the Beige Book become a larger share of the available macro record — which raises what it is worth
   *reading*, and changes nothing about what it is worth trading.

8. **Its signal is buried by construction — the corridor is the worst-timed on this calendar.**
   SUPPORTED. Within five days of 11-02 this book tracks **FOMC 10-28**, **GDP Q3 advance 10-29**,
   **PCE 10-29**, **midterms 11-03** (`estimate`, `high`) and **jobs 11-06** — and this session adds
   **ISM Manufacturing 11-02** (`estimate`, 10:00 ET, proposed in this PR), which lands on the same
   morning four hours ahead of it. A `low`-impact survey released 24 hours before a national election
   has no window in which to be the story, whatever it says.

### Honest limits

**n=3**, all in one calendar year and one regime. The KRE measurement is a single-instrument read on
one ETF and cannot detect a repricing that happened in credit spreads or in individual regional-bank
names rather than in the index. **A data conflict I could not resolve and will not paper over:** the
same daily-bar pull returns SPY moves that disagree in *sign* with the contemporaneous press wraps on
two of the three dates (SPY **+0.50%** vs a reported S&P **−0.84%** on 02-02; SPY **−0.37%** vs
**+0.3%** on 05-04) — so index levels in this document are carried from the press and ETF returns
from the API, **never mixed inside one comparison**, and the leg-2 table is internally consistent
because every number in it comes from the same pull. Reconciling those two sources is the first job
of the next pulse. Leg 3's collection dates are my arithmetic off a single edition. Leg 5 rests on one
FEDS Note. And the 09-16 and 10-28 meetings have not happened: if September hikes, leg 3's branch
fires and this document's informational claim gets re-derived rather than patched.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date confirmed):** **read it, do not trade it.** The channel is measurably absent on the one
instrument that should carry it — KRE's excess vs SPY on all three 2026 releases landed at the 73rd,
55th and 9th percentile of its own 2026 daily distribution, no sign consistency, mean **+0.12%** —
and no session wrap names SLOOS as a driver. What earns it a calendar slot is **timing**: its response
window closes in **early October** (derived), so the November edition is the first structured read on
how a September hike transmits into bank credit standards, published on a day already owned by ISM
Manufacturing (`estimate`) in the morning and the midterms (`estimate`) the next. No directional call,
no size.

**Kill switches:**

- **Date kill:** federalreserve.gov publishing a SLOOS date other than **2026-11-02**, or announcing
  one of its occasional additional surveys before then. Either breaks leg 1 and returns this entry to
  `estimate`. Score by **2026-11-02**.
- **Channel kill (deliberately NOT registered as a forward test):** KRE's close-to-close excess vs SPY
  on **2026-11-02** landing outside **±2.09%** (the measured p90) with session wraps naming SLOOS.
  Leg 2 would die. It stays a kill switch rather than an FT because the window is confounded by
  construction — ISM the same morning, the midterms the next day — and a test that cannot attribute
  its own result is not a test.
- **Transmission kill:** the **2026-11-02** edition reporting a **net tightening** of C&I standards
  for large and middle-market firms. Leg 4's fading-impulse read dies and the credit channel becomes
  a live thing to track rather than a quarterly curiosity. Registered as **FT-32**, score by
  **2026-11-03**.
- **Staleness kill:** the October edition publishing collection dates that put its response deadline
  *after* **2026-10-28**. Leg 3's whole "first post-hike read, but pre-meeting stale" framing needs
  re-deriving. Re-check when those dates appear.
- **Regime kill:** a hike delivered on **2026-09-16** or **2026-10-28**. Leg 3's branch fires; the
  informational value gets re-derived at the next pulse (the tradability call does not move).
- **Relevance kill:** a bank-credit-stress event — a failure, an FDIC action, an emergency facility —
  before **2026-11-02**. SLOOS stops being a quarterly technical and becomes the release everyone
  reads, exactly as it was in 2023; this document's low-impact framing would not survive it.

**Forward test registered:** **FT-32** in [`forward-tests.md`](../forward-tests.md) — the transmission
kill above, stated as a prediction with its anchors, its stated low information value on a pass, and a
**2026-11-03** score-by date. It is registered because it is the one claim here the release itself
adjudicates without the tape's help; the stance takes no position and is sized at zero.
