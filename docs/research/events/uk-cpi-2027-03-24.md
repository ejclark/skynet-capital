### Leg 1 — "The date is 2027-03-24, at 02:00 ET." **SUPPORTED on the date. REFUTED on the time, by exactly one hour.**

The date re-verifies cleanly. ONS's release page (HTTP 200, 42,073 bytes, 2026-09-10) reads "Consumer
price inflation, UK: **February 2027**", "**Provisional release date: 24 March 2027 7:00am**", "This
release is not yet published". 07:00 London, a **Wednesday**, consistent with ONS's standing CPI slot.

The **time conversion both proposals carry is wrong**, and it is wrong for a reason that is specific to
this one entry. US clocks advance on the **second Sunday in March** (2027-03-14); UK clocks not until the
**last Sunday in March** (2027-03-28). Between those two dates the Atlantic gap is **four hours, not
five**. Converting 07:00 `Europe/London` for every `uk-cpi-*` entry on this calendar:

### Leg 2 — "This print carries ONS's second weights update." **SUPPORTED, and from methodology rather than from an annual announcement.**

The `uk-cpi-2027-02-17` lane established this from the 2026 *updating weights* article plus a 6-of-6
empirical match on `CJXA`. That is a strong read, and its author graded it honestly as *"identification
from 6 discriminating years, not a statement of ONS policy"*.

It can be upgraded. ONS's **technical manual** — a methodology document, not a yearly release — states
the design directly (fetched direct 2026-09-10):

> "Weights are currently updated in **two stages every year** for both the [CPIH] and the [CPI]: **once
> with the January index** to take account of the new annual COICOP weights, and **once in the following
> month** to take account of the changes to the basket of representative items."

and, in §8.5 on price updating:

> "At the **second update of weights, published with the February index**, the same underlying [year
> *y*−2] expenditure is updated to **January of year *y***."

That is ONS saying, in its own standing methodology, both that the second update exists and that it
publishes **with the February index** — which is this print. The sibling's inference is now a citation.

Note the technical manual's own stated staleness, recorded rather than buried: it warns that "from March
2025, we have introduced 'consumption segments' into the aggregation structure … The information in this
section has not been updated." That caveat attaches to the **aggregation** section, not to §8.5's price
updating or §9.5.2's collection, but it is an honest limit on the document as a whole.

### Leg 6 — "The reweighting should show up in the February headline." **REFUTED by this lane's own test.**

This was this lane's own hypothesis, not an inherited one, and it is worth recording because it looked
right. If the February print mechanically carries a new weight vector *and* a new basket every year, the
February year-on-year step should be systematically larger than other months'. From `D7G7`, 2010–2026:

| Statistic | February (n=17) | All other months (n=182) |
|---|---|---|
| mean \|step\| | 0.2824pp | 0.2868pp |
| **median \|step\|** | **0.300pp** | **0.200pp** |
| max \|step\| | 0.700pp | 2.100pp |
| share ≥ 0.3pp | 52.9% | 41.8% |
| share ≤ 0.1pp | 29.4% | 39.0% |
| share ≥ 0.6pp | 11.8% | 12.6% |

The shape is exactly the one the hypothesis predicts: a **wider middle without a fatter tail** — the
median is the **second highest of all twelve months** (behind April's 0.500pp) while the mean is dead on
the all-month baseline and the maximum is one of the smallest. A small, always-present mechanical
perturbation added to every February would look precisely like that.

**It does not survive its own test.** Drawing 17 months at random from the all-month pool reproduces a
median of ≥ 0.300pp in **26.5%** of 20,000 permutations. At n = 17 the gap is indistinguishable from
sampling noise. The regime split points the same way but is no better powered: pre-2017 (before the
double update) February's median was 0.200pp on n = 7, 2017 onward 0.300pp on n = 10 — directionally
consistent, and worthless at those counts.

**Graded REFUTED and not carried into the stance.** No forward test is registered on it, because
registering a test this lane has already failed to support would be manufacturing an n rather than
earning one. If a future pulse wants it, the honest form is a pre-stated 10-more-years horizon, not a
re-slice of the same 17 observations.

### Leg 7 — "Whether the VAT step smears across 31 March is unresolved." **RESOLVED — the sibling lane's named open uncertainty, closed.**

The `uk-cpi-2027-04-21` lane recorded this explicitly as a **named open uncertainty, not a finding**: the
SI binds supplies *made* in the window, a continuous supply's VAT tax point is normally the earlier of
invoice or payment, so April billing of March consumption could straddle the boundary — and "whether
that smears the step across the two indices depends on whether ONS collects a published tariff rate or a
modelled bill, and the ONS technical-manual page was not located (two guessed URLs, both 404 — an author
error, not a blocked source)."

The page is at
`ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/methodologies/consumerpricesindicestechnicalmanual2019`
(HTTP 200, 1,135,561 bytes, fetched direct 2026-09-10). **§9.5.2, Electricity and gas tariffs:**

> "For each of the major electricity and gas suppliers, we collect **fixed costs (standing charges) and
> prices per unit** of the most popular domestic tariff bands at both day and night rates. **Average
> bills are calculated** for each tariff **using average consumption figures**, and the tariffs for each
> supplier are weighted together using expenditure figures derived from average bills and customer
> numbers."

So it is the **first** of the two: ONS collects the **published tariff rate** — standing charge plus unit
price — and *models* an average bill from standard consumption. It never observes an actual household
bill, an invoice, or a payment date. §6.2.5 confirms VAT rides along in the collected price ("Value Added
Tax (VAT) and compulsory service charges are included").

**Therefore the tax point cannot smear the step.** A rate-based collection registers the VAT change in
the month the *rate* changes, and billing timing has no channel into the index at all. The sibling's
uncertainty is closed in the direction that makes its own `FT-uk-cpi-2027-04-21-1` anchor *more* likely
to pass, not less.

**Stated as an inference, because it is one.** ONS does not write the sentence "the VAT change enters in
the month the tariff rate changes." It writes what it collects, and the conclusion follows from that plus
§6.2.5. The residual — whether suppliers *publish* the reverted rate on 1 April or lag it — is a supplier
behaviour question, not an ONS methodology question, and it is what `FT-uk-cpi-2027-04-21-1` would catch.
