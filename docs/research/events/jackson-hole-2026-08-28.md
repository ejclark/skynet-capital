## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** this is Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote — what's known
about his policy framework and communication style since taking the chair, how has the market
historically reacted to Jackson Hole speeches (especially a new chair's early set-piece remarks), and —
critically — does the date/timing check out against the Kansas City Fed's own primary source, given this
lands directly between NVDA's and MRVL's confirmed earnings prints.

**One-line verdict:** the symposium's Aug 27–29 dates are now primary-confirmed on kansascityfed.org, but
the Friday-keynote timing is still press-reported only; substantively, Warsh has an explicit "framework,
not near-term guidance" playbook, Jackson Hole's own reaction history is fat-tailed with semiconductors
the single most rate-shock-sensitive sector, and this keynote lands inside a three-trading-day corridor
(NVDA 8/26 → MRVL 8/27 → Warsh 8/28) that stacks a new chair's most information-dense speech of the year
directly on top of the AI-earnings names most exposed to a hawkish surprise.

   date range** until KC Fed posts the full agenda — keeping `status: estimate` is correct and required.

3. **Warsh's stated framework: "big picture questions, not near-term guidance."** SUPPORTED. At the
   July 29 post-FOMC press conference (federalreserve.gov transcript; PBS/CNBC same-day coverage), Warsh
   told reporters the Jackson Hole speech would "frame big picture questions" rather than engage in
   "near-sighted debates," and explicitly said the Fed is "not constrained by market prices" in setting
   rates — markets are useful information, not the "definitive source." This sits inside a consistent
   multi-month pattern since taking the chair 2026-05-22: shortened FOMC statements, forward guidance
   stripped from the June statement, no dot submitted in June, deliberately evasive press-conference
   answers, and five internal task forces reportedly stood up to re-examine Fed communication, data
   sources, and inflation-framework methodology (PBS 2026-06-17/07-29; Advantage Gold summary,
   Aug 2026) — a pattern, not a one-off deflection.

4. **Market expectations may be overreading the speech anyway.** MIXED, and itself informative.
   investinglive.com (dated commentary, checked 2026-08-19) argues "Jackson Hole hype outruns Warsh['s]
   playbook of saying as little as possible" — some analysts expect a September hike/hold hint despite
   Warsh's own stated approach arguing against it. The piece flags FOMC committee dissent (three regional
   presidents favoring a hike in July — unusually high dissent this early in a new chair's tenure) as a
   more substantive directional signal than the podium remarks themselves. Read: a hawkish-surprise
   repricing risk exists independent of Warsh's intent, because the market may trade the *absence* of
   guidance as itself informative — the same information-vacuum dynamic the sibling FOMC doc names for
   this chair's shortened statements.

5. **Jackson Hole's historical reaction function is fat-tailed, not average-informative.** SUPPORTED.
   A 10-year Benzinga study (cited via WebSearch, checked 2026-08-19): the S&P 500 over the two symposium
   days averages **−0.4%** (median 0%) — essentially flat on average — but single-day tail events dominate
   the distribution: Powell's 2022 hawkish "there will be pain" speech cut the S&P 500 **−3.37%** in one
   day (and a further −13.14% over the following month), while Powell's dovish 2025 pivot lifted the S&P
   **+1.5%** and the Russell 2000 **+3.92%** in a day. The average outcome and the realistic outcome are
   different things for this event — a new chair's first keynote (maximum information content per legs
   3–4) argues for weighting the tail scenario, not the flat median.

6. **Semiconductors are the most JH-reactive sector historically, and this keynote lands directly on top
   of NVDA/MRVL prints.** SUPPORTED — the load-bearing adjacency finding this research was asked to
   surface. An 11-year sector-reaction dataset (checked 2026-08-19) names semiconductors (AMD, NVDA,
   AVGO, AMAT, MU) as the highest-volatility group around Jackson Hole, with **NVDA specifically down
   −9.23% in a single day** during Powell's 2022 hawkish speech — nearly 3x the S&P's own −3.37% that day.
   The calendar's own confirmed dates stack tightly into a single corridor: **NVDA reports 2026-08-26
   after close, MRVL reports 2026-08-27 after close, Warsh's keynote lands 2026-08-28** — three trading
   days where the sector most sensitive to Fed-framework shocks is also digesting its two biggest own-name
   catalysts of the quarter, on top of PCE and the GDP 2nd estimate (both 8/26, same morning as the NVDA
   print) and 5Y/7Y Treasury auctions (8/26, 8/27) supplying duration risk into the same window — all
   already tracked in `market-events-data.ts`. This is the compounding setup the assignment flagged, and
   the research confirms it: a hawkish surprise Friday morning would hit AI-semis while still repricing
   off two days of fresh earnings, with no calm session between any of the three catalysts to digest one
   independently of the others.
