## At a glance

**TL;DR.** A **read, not a trade** — and the read is that this is the **one Treasury buyback bucket
whose cap actually binds**, which is the opposite of what its two researched siblings found about
theirs. Across all 220 operations the program has ever run, TIPS 1Y-10Y has filled **7 of 8** caps
at 98–100%, a **93.7% mean fill** that ranks **4th of 15** bucket/type categories — against **22.5%
(0 of 4)** for TIPS 10Y-30Y and **10.3% (0 of 9)** for 7-10Y nominals. Then it missed: **2026-07-22
took $405M of $750M**, a 54.0% fill on $3,194M offered. That miss matters more than its size,
because it is the program's **only uncensored measurement of this bucket's price-acceptable
supply** — the other seven prints are censored at the cap and tell you only "at least $750M" — and
it reads **below** the cap. The mechanism is measurable, not inferred: **total par offered has
fallen 51%** ($6,512M → $3,194M) across the eight operations while the window's front rolled a full
year (2026-10-15 → 2027-10-15) and the eligible **sub-1%-coupon line count fell 14 → 10**. Treasury
has retired **$5.62B** here, **67.6%** of it in six CUSIPs, and it is buying the paper it sold at
**negative real yields**: 91282CCM1 (0.125% of 2031) was auctioned July 2021 at **−1.016%** and an
unadjusted **111.990**, and bought back on 07-22 at **91.234** — **20.8 points lower**. Two schedule
firsts on 09-29: it settles **9/30**, the last business day of Q3, which nothing in this bucket has
ever done, and its bucket gets **two** rows this quarter ($1.5B of capacity) against the long
bucket's one ($500M). **sb0607 does not touch it** — the release names "nominal coupon securities"
only — so the $750M cap stands inside the doubled window. Guard-shaped throughout: `estimate`-dated,
no position, and the scale arithmetic (~$45B of TIPS issuance in the same corridor, **60:1**) rules
out any market effect worth acting on.

## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** what does a $750M liquidity-support buyback of 1-to-10-year TIPS on
2026-09-29 actually do; why did this bucket — which had filled its cap almost every time — miss it
on 2026-07-22; is that miss a Treasury policy change or an arithmetic consequence of something
measurable; and what is different about the 09-29 instance specifically?

**One-line verdict:** the program's most reliably-filled TIPS bucket, whose fill is now in genuine
doubt for a reason that has nothing to do with policy — the pool of cheap off-the-run paper it was
built to absorb is draining, measurably — making the 2:15pm ET accepted-par print on 09-29 a cheap,
dated, primary-published test of a mechanism, and worth *reading* rather than *trading* at any size.
