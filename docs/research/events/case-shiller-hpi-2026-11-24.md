### Leg 1 — the November edition is a two-state event, and it can never collide · **SUPPORTED**, and it is arithmetic

The rule is unambiguous and was re-read from the current release today:

> The S&P Cotality Case-Shiller Indices are published on the **last Tuesday of each month at 9:00 am
> ET**.

November 2026's Tuesdays are the 3rd, 10th, 17th and 24th, so the last is **2026-11-24**, and the
two-month lag puts **September 2026** data on it.

**Now apply the rule to November in general.** Thanksgiving is the fourth Thursday, so it falls
Nov 22–28; the last Tuesday of November falls Nov 24–30. Computed for every year 1990–2050, the
offset between them takes **exactly two values and no others**:

| Offset | Meaning | Count, 1990–2050 |
|---|---|---|
| **−2** | the last Tuesday **is** the Tuesday of Thanksgiving week | **26** |
| **+5** | the last Tuesday is the Tuesday **after** Thanksgiving | **35** |

There is no third case. And unlike December, **there is no collision case at all**: Thanksgiving is
a Thursday, and the only fixed-date US market holidays are Jan 1, Jun 19, Jul 4 and Dec 25, none of
which can fall in a November last-Tuesday window. Where the 12-29 lane found that its own edition's
date *"was Christmas Day in 5 of the 37 years 1990–2026"*, this one has never been a closed session
and never can be.

**2026 is a −2 year.** Thanksgiving is **2026-11-26**; the market closes that Thursday
(`thanksgiving-market-closure-2026-11-26`) and half-days the Friday
(`thanksgiving-half-day-2026-11-27`, with `sifma-bond-early-close-2026-11-27`). This print lands on
the Tuesday of that week.

| Year | Last Tue of Nov | Thanksgiving | State |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | Nov 28 | Nov 23 | +5 |
| 2024 | Nov 26 | Nov 28 | **−2** |
| 2025 | Nov 25 | Nov 27 | **−2** |
| **2026** | **Nov 24** | **Nov 26** | **−2** |
| 2027 | Nov 30 | Nov 25 | +5 |
| 2028 | Nov 28 | Nov 23 | +5 |

**The consequence that matters is a mirror of the sibling's, and it is why Leg 2 can be believed.**
The 12-29 lane's central caution was that its release-day sample *"structurally excludes the two
worst year-ends in the VIX9D era"* — 2012 and 2018 — because Christmas fell on those Tuesdays. The
November sample has no such filter. Both years are in it (2012-11-20 and 2018-11-20 are Thanksgiving
Tuesdays), and, as Leg 2 shows, **they are precisely the two observations that break the effect** —
which is the opposite of survivorship and is the strongest thing that can be said for a small sample.
