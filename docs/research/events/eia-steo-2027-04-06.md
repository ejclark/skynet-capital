## Initial research

**The question.** `eia-steo-2027-03-09` did not propose this date for calendar completeness either. It
proposed it as a **discriminator**. That session found the out-year electricity line moved **+0.92%** in
March 2025 and **+0.37%** in March 2026 — 3.0× and 2.1× February, second-largest of the series in both
years — and said two readings survived the measurement: either **March specifically** is elevated (the
first edition whose previous forecast is itself a revision, and the first with two months of winter
actuals), in which case April reverts to the ordinary mean; or **everything after February** is
elevated, in which case the whole stickiness frame is a February artifact. It named 04-06 as the only
edition that separates them. So: **which fork is it?**

**One-line verdict:** **neither** — the fork was false. Extended to four out-year series and measured on
EIA's own unrounded archive files, March is near the **bottom** of its own year in 2023 and 2024, and
ex-2025 the whole early-year block moves the line **less** than the later editions; what genuinely
survives is the *first-print* stickiness, which is now significant on a non-editorial basket and whose
shape is a **decay across roughly three editions**, not a spike in any one of them.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md)
(macro-print mode; the house instruments are equity/earnings-keyed and cover no commodity or
publication). Primaries fetched direct **2026-09-09**:
