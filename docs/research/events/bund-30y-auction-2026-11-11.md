## At a glance

**TL;DR.** Germany sells **EUR 2.5bn of 30-year Bund on 2026-11-11** — the **last long-end auction
of its published year** — on **the one session the US bond market is closed** (Veterans Day).
**Stand aside**, as with both siblings, but for a sharper reason than "the effect is small": the
transatlantic test those ledgers ran **cannot be run on this date at all**. Proven from Treasury's
own primary rather than a holiday calendar — in 1999-2026 the US daily par yield curve has
published a November 11 reading **zero times, across all 14 weekday instances**, while the
Bundesbank quotes the Bund 30Y on **18** of them. The one test that *does* exist here, the **T+1**
version, is **also a null** (**+0.19bp**, SE 0.65, **t = 0.29**) — and its naive form returns a
spurious **t = 2.19** purely by jumping the US CMT's 1,455-day hole, which is the methodological
trap this ledger exists to flag. Two corrections land on the siblings: the "9 dropped dates" were
**7 hole + 2 real US bond holidays**, and **"no December 30Y auction" is neither a cut nor news** —
the debt office announced "each month except December" in **December 2025**, and Germany has
auctioned a 30-year Bund in December **0 times in 218 dates since 1999**. Date **estimate**; it
widens caution and licenses nothing.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This id reached the calendar as a proposal —
`proposals/bund-30y-auction-2026-11-11.from-bund-30y-auction-2026-10-14.json`, filed on 2026-09-09
by its sibling's own initial research. It was read first; this PR writes the canonical file.

Two ledgers already sit in front of this one, and both concluded "stand aside" on a measured null.
A third auction in the same series has to justify itself or it is ceremony. The question is
therefore narrow: **what is true of 2026-11-11 that was not true of 09-16 or 10-14 — and does any
of it change what the entry can measure, rather than merely what it sits beside?**

**One-line verdict:** yes, and decisively — **the test both siblings ran does not exist on this
date**, because the US bond market is closed on 2026-11-11 and publishes no yield to measure; the
substitute T+1 test is a null whose naive form is a **trap**; and two claims inherited from the
siblings turn out to be wrong or dead on arrival.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The date, size and shape — SUPPORTED by three independent Finanzagentur primaries, one of
which is the annual plan itself.** The Q4 2026 calendar carries two lines dated **11.11.2026**:
`Bund 30 Y (R)` at **EUR 1.5bn** and `Bund 30 Y (R)` at **EUR 1.0bn**, ISIN, coupon and maturity all
`-`. The June-2026 H2 outlook carries a single row, `Bund | 30 years | Reopenings (multi-ISIN) |
2500`, with **no accompanying `15/20/30 years` flexible line** — the same full-30Y commitment 10-14
carries, and *unlike* 09-16, whose outlook row pairs `30 years | 1500` with a flexible
`15/20/30 years | 1000`. That distinction is the October sibling's own leg-1 mechanic (the flexible
second line landed in the **15-year** bucket on 2026-08-12 and 2026-08-26), and it holds here:
**EUR 2.5bn of 30-year duration is what both primaries commit to on 11-11.** Only the ISINs are open.

The third primary is stronger than either. The annual plan of **18 December 2025** states the shape
in advance, verbatim: *"In January, April and July, EUR 3.0 billion will be issued via up to three
different Federal bonds in one auction date. In the remaining months, up to two bonds with a volume
of EUR 2.5 billion are planned."* The 2026 tape executes that to the letter — January (01-14, three
lines, EUR 3.0bn), April (04-15, three lines, EUR 3.0bn), July (07-15, three lines, EUR 3.0bn), and
two-line EUR 2.5bn dates in February and March. **November's EUR 2.5bn two-line shape was
published eleven months ahead of the auction.**

Four bank business days back from Wednesday 11.11.2026 — Nov 10, 9, 6, 5, with no German bank
holiday intervening (All Saints, 1 November, falls on a Sunday in 2026 and is a Länder holiday in
any case) — puts the **announcement on 2026-11-05** and the invitation to bid on **2026-11-10**. The
rule is observable, not merely stated: this month's Bund reopening ran announcement **09-03** →
invitation **09-08** → allocation **09-09**.

**2. THE SAME-DATE TRANSATLANTIC TEST DOES NOT EXIST ON THIS DATE — the finding that separates this
ledger from its siblings, and it is proved from Treasury's own data rather than a holiday
calendar.** Both siblings' central leg regresses the German auction's bid-to-offer against the
**same-date** US 30Y CMT change, on the reasoning that bidding closes 05:30 ET and the same US
session is the first that could react. On 2026-11-11 there is no US session.

The proof is mechanical and needs no third-party calendar:

| Check, US Treasury daily par yield curve 1999-2026 | Result |
|---|---|
| November 11 readings published, any year | **0** |
| Of which November 11 fell on a weekday | **14 instances, still 0 readings** |
| Bundesbank Bund 30Y readings on November 11 | **18 years** |

Germany trades and the United States does not, every year, without exception. This corroborates
[`sifma-bond-market-closure-2026-11-11`](sifma-bond-market-closure-2026-11-11.md) from a fully
independent primary — and a **third** one agrees: the Treasury's own Tentative Auction Schedule
PDF, quoted in [`treasury-30y-bond-2026-11-12`](treasury-30y-bond-2026-11-12.md)'s source, carries
the line `Holiday - Wednesday, November 11`.

**3. The T+1 test — the only transatlantic test that exists here — is ALSO a null, and its naive
form is a trap. REFUTED as a tradeable signal.** If the same-date measure is unavailable, the
honest substitute is the next US session's move. Neither sibling ran it. Run on the same 213 dates:

| Split | n | US 30Y **T+1** | vs. same-date (control, identical guard) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soft vs firm bid-to-offer | 104 / 102 | **+0.19bp** (SE 0.65, **t = 0.29**) | −0.04bp (t = −0.06) |
| Undersubscribed vs covered | 47 / 159 | **−0.36bp** (SE 0.66, **t = −0.54**) | +0.25bp (t = 0.30) |

Against an all-day US 30Y baseline of +0.016bp (sd 5.35, n = 5,929). Nothing.

**The trap is worth more than the null.** Run without a gap guard, the same T+1 test returns
**t = 2.19** on undersubscribed-vs-covered — the largest statistic anywhere in this three-ledger
series, and entirely spurious. The cause is the US CMT's **1,455-day hole (2002-02-15 →
2006-02-09)**: for auction dates inside it, "the next US session" is up to four *years* later, and
the resulting "daily change" is a multi-year move attributed to one auction. Requiring the next
session to fall within 5 calendar days collapses it to t = −0.54. Any future pulse extending this
work inherits the guard, not the headline. Registered as **FT-bund-30y-auction-2026-11-11-1**.

**5. The 11-12 print is a confounded number by construction, and that is measurable. SUPPORTED.**
Because 11-11 has no US reading, the first US 30Y observation after this auction is 2026-11-12's,
and its "daily change" spans **two calendar days** (11-10 close → 11-12 close). Measured across all
169 post-holiday US sessions since 1999:

| US 30Y CMT | mean daily \|Δ\| |
|---|---|
| First session after a bond-market holiday | **4.84bp** (n = 169) |
| All sessions | **4.03bp** (n = 5,929) |
| Difference | **+0.81bp**, SE 0.29, **t = 2.74** — a ratio of **1.20x** |

This is arithmetic, not information: two days of news land in one print. Layer on that 2026-11-12
also carries the **US 30-year bond auction result** at 1:00pm ET
([`treasury-30y-bond-2026-11-12`](treasury-30y-bond-2026-11-12.md), a refunding **new issue**), and
the date is triple-confounded — extra day, US supply, plus whatever the Bund auction did. The
practical instruction is the plainest thing in this ledger: **a large 11-12 US long-end move is the
default expectation, not evidence of anything.**

**6. A Bund move on 11-11 is an ordinary Bund day — the closure does not thin the German tape.
SUPPORTED, and it is the one thing that makes the date readable at all.** The natural worry is that
with the US shut, German price action is thin and unrepresentative. It is not:

| Bund 30Y daily \|Δ\|, 2003-2026 | n | mean |
|---|---|---|
| Days the US bond market is **shut** (Germany open) | 170 | **3.35bp** |
| Days the US bond market is open | 5,432 | **3.37bp** |
| Difference | — | **−0.02bp**, SE 0.29, **t = −0.07** (ratio 0.99x) |

The 14 Veterans Days in that set average 3.36bp, range 0 to 8bp. So the German side of 11-11 can be
read normally; it is only the *transatlantic* leg that the closure removes. (Medians differ, 2.0 vs
3.0bp, but that is granularity: the Bundesbank series is quoted to two decimals, so daily changes
are quantised to whole basis points and the median is a coarse statistic here. The means are the
honest read.)

**7. "No December 30-year auction" is neither a cut nor news — and it retires half of the sibling's
own kill switch. REFUTED as evidence of a policy change.** The 10-14 ledger's leg 5 read the Q4
calendar's missing December date as the plateau of Germany's supply tripling, registered it as
**FT-bund-30y-auction-2026-10-14-4**, and attached the kill switch *"a December 2026 30Y auction
date appearing on the Finanzagentur calendar."* Two facts, neither available to that session,
settle it:

- **The debt office pre-announced it.** The annual plan of **18 December 2025** states: *"For
  outstanding 30-year Federal bonds, an auction date is scheduled each month **except December**. A
  total volume of EUR 29.0 billion will be placed via these **eleven taps**."* Its Annex monthly row
  for `Bund (30 years)` reads **3.0 / 2.5 / 2.5 / 3.0 / 2.5 / 2.5 / 3.0 / 2.5 / 2.5 / 2.5 / 2.5**
  across January-November and **nothing** for December, summing to exactly EUR 29.0bn.
- **It has never happened.** Across **218** German 30Y auction-process dates 1999 → 2026, the number
  falling in December is **zero**. The month histogram since 2020 runs 13/13/10/13/12/9/10/11/11/10/9
  for January-November and **0** for December.

So the December limb of that kill switch is **dead on arrival**: it asks for an event with no
precedent in 27 years that the issuer has explicitly excluded in writing. Its other limb — Q4 2026
30Y auction supply exceeding EUR 5.0bn — remains live and is untouched here. The honest amendment
is that **11-11 is the year's last 30-year auction by structural design**, which is a stronger claim
than "the tripling plateaued" and a much weaker one than "Germany is cutting."

**And it is the last long-end auction outright.** After 11-11 the published calendar carries only
17.11 Schatz, 18.11 and 25.11 Bund 10Y, 01.12 Bobl and 08.12 Schatz — **no further date of 15 years
or more in 2026, on either primary.**

workbook counts by the **segment tag on the auction date**, which sweeps in the second leg of

same workbook definition. The *level* is not the issuer's target and must never be quoted as one.

### Honest limits

- **Daily readings, not intraday.** Both yield series are one reading per day, so every leg measures
  the *net* session. Nothing here claims the 11-11 or 11-12 sessions are calm.
- **The Veterans Day precedent is n = 1.** 2020-11-11 is the only prior instance of this exact
  structure, and it sits in the first vaccine week. It is reported, never inferred from.
- **The 1,455-day CMT hole is handled by a 5-calendar-day guard, not by interpolation.** That choice
  is load-bearing for the T+1 leg specifically: without it the leg reports t = 2.19 instead of
  t = −0.54. Any future pulse re-running this must carry the guard.
- **The post-holiday 1.20x is arithmetic, not a finding.** A two-calendar-day change is larger than
  a one-day change for trivial reasons; the number is reported to *disarm* the 11-12 print, not to
  claim a holiday effect exists.
- **Eleven windows were tested across legs 3, 9 and 6.** No result survives a multiple-comparison
  correction, which is the honest reason all of them are registered as nulls.
- **Both ISINs are unpublished.** Every allotment statement is a prior drawn from 23 same-shape
  dates; the identities land 2026-11-05.
- **Retention is a policy variable, not a demand read.** The Finanzagentur chooses it and can use it
  to absorb weak demand or bank paper for market-making, which is why the registered form is a band
  and a direction, never a point forecast.
- **Bid-to-offer is not a US-style tail.** A multi-price auction has no single stop; no
  cross-sovereign comparison of "softness" is attempted, least of all against 11-12's US auction.
- **The supply level is not the issuer's target.** EUR 33.25bn is a workbook-segment figure; the
  plan's EUR 29.0bn counts by residual maturity. Only the year-on-year direction transfers.
- **The 2027 issuance plan is undated here.** The 2026 edition was Press Release No. 10 of
  **2025-12-18**, but one prior observation is not a date, and probing eight plausible 2023-2025 URL
  patterns returned nothing. It is recorded as the deliverable that adjudicates the supply question
  and left for the next pulse to date properly — **not** proposed as a calendar event on a guess.
- **VIX is Yahoo, i.e. secondary**, and 16.44 is the 2026-09-09 close (prior session 15.72, 21-day
  mean 15.18).
- **The date is `estimate`.** Three Finanzagentur primaries agree on date, size and shape, one of
  them published eleven months ahead; the label reflects a gap in `market-events-data.ts`'s prefix
  taxonomy, the same limit both siblings and [`jgb-30y-auction-2026-11-10`](jgb-30y-auction-2026-11-10.md)
  recorded. Estimates widen caution and license nothing.
