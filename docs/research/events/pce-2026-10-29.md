## At a glance

**TL;DR.** Two things make this print different from every other PCE this calendar has tracked, and
neither is the number. **First, it is the first PCE released after the Fed's preferred gauge is
rewritten.** On **2026-09-30** the BEA's annual update restates PCE back to **2021Q1** with new
deflators for portfolio management, legal services and software — Goldman and JPMorgan both put the
mechanical effect at roughly **−0.2pp on core y/y** (Goldman moved its Dec-2026 core call **3.2% →
3.0%**), UBS is on record calling the selection opaque, and BEA's own primary documents describe the
method change while saying nothing about the level effect. **Second, it lands 08:30 ET the morning
_after_ the 10-28 FOMC statement** — the committee decides without it. So this release cannot move
the October decision; it is the first evidence on whether that decision was right, and the first
input to **12-09**. The practical instruction is a discount, not a direction: from 09-30 onward,
treat a cooler core print as **measurement until proven otherwise**. **Third, and correcting this
calendar's inherited framing:** a funding lapse does **not** delete this release. Checked against the
2025 lapse release-by-release (leg 11), September-reference data was *delayed* — PCE by five weeks,
10-31 → 12-05 — while it was the **October** reference month that was destroyed or interpolated. So

[`pce-2026-11-25`](pce-2026-11-25.md). Date **confirmed** (BEA

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

What should a paper book holding NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL AMZN expect from the
September-2026 PCE release on 2026-10-29 — a print that arrives inside the densest 72 hours on this
calendar, the morning after an FOMC that will already have decided, and the first one published
after the BEA rewrites the index itself?

**One-line verdict.** The number is unknowable at D-61 and the release is structurally incapable of
moving the decision it sits next to, so the whole value of this doc is the **measurement break**: a
09-30 methodology change worth ~20bp of core y/y means every inflation read from that date forward
has to be split into "the economy did this" and "the deflator did this," and 10-29 is the first
print where that split can be made cleanly.

2. **The Fed decides *before* this print — SUPPORTED, and it inverts the usual framing.** The
   `fomc-2026-10-28` statement drops **14:00 ET on 10-28**; this releases **08:30 ET on 10-29**. The
   last PCE the October committee actually sees is the **August-data print on 09-30**. Every other
   PCE this calendar has tracked was an *input* to a pending decision; this one is a **verdict on a
   decision already taken**, and the first input to **12-09**. Consequence, stated without a
   position: the transmission channel is not "does this change the October outcome" but "does this
   make the October outcome look early or late," which is a slower, weaker channel than the one the
   sibling PCE doc assumed.

3. **The index itself is being rewritten on 09-30 — SUPPORTED, BEA primary, and this is the
   load-bearing leg.** BEA's Survey of Current Business preview
   ([`0626-nea-preview.htm`](https://apps.bea.gov/scb/issues/2026/06-june/0626-nea-preview.htm),
   fetched today) states the 2026 annual update of the National Economic Accounts begins **"on
   September 30, 2026"** and covers **"the first quarter of 2021 through the first quarter of
   2026."** Three PCE components get new price methodology: **portfolio management and investment
   advice services** (quantity derived from a BLS CES-based extrapolator instead of deflating by the
   PPI), **legal services** (a composite of detailed PPIs replacing the CPI from 2024), and
   **computer software and accessories** (a composite of the CPI plus PPIs for game-software
   publishing and IT-infrastructure services). BEA's own documents describe the *method* and state
   **nothing** about the expected effect on index levels — checked, not assumed.

4. **The street sizes the effect at ~20bp of core, and 10-29 is the first clean read past it —
   SUPPORTED (direction), snippet-fidelity (magnitude).** Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan both estimate
   the revisions cut **May 2026 core PCE y/y by ~20bp, 3.4% → 3.2%**, and Goldman moved its
   **December 2026** core forecast from **3.2% to 3.0%** and 2027 to 2.2% (search-surfaced; Axios
   2026-07-06 and the Confluence 2026-08-05 commentary both 403 on direct fetch — magnitude carried
   at snippet fidelity, and it must be re-verified against the actual 09-30 release). The sequencing
   is what matters and is not snippet-dependent: **09-30 carries both the restatement and the August
   data**, so that print's y/y comparisons are against a restated base and will be genuinely hard to
   read. By **10-29**, current month and prior month sit on the same new basis — this is the first
   September-quarter inflation reading where the deflator change is old news rather than the story.

5. **The revision is contested on grounds that matter for how it will be read — MIXED, one named
   critic.** UBS economist **Alan Detmeister** argues the BEA "chose to lower inflation" by revising
   only components with "a large positive impact on inflation under the current methodology," that
   the approach "clearly lacks transparency, which is a problem in itself," and that opacity around
   the Fed's preferred gauge "would make inflation data more susceptible to manipulation." His worked
   example: portfolio-management services price growth falling from **21.6% to 9.0%** under the new
   method. **This doc takes no view on whether the criticism is right** — its operative consequence
   is one-way and modest: a contested downward revision gives a hawkish chair a ready reason to
   *discount* a cooler print, which weakens the dovish read of any post-09-30 cooling more than it
   strengthens it. Recorded as an asymmetry, not a forecast.

6. **The one component the market is watching for AI signal is one of the three being re-deflated —
   SUPPORTED as a collision, direction unknown.** Goldman's Megan Peters (note dated **2026-07-13**)
   estimates AI-related factors are already adding **>0.2pp** to core PCE y/y, potentially **0.5pp by
   year-end 2026**, through memory/semiconductor prices (8GB DDR5 ~**$148** vs ~$35 a year earlier),
   **software bundling** as AI tools get priced in, and datacenter **electricity** — described as
   largely a US-specific phenomenon (~10bp elsewhere). **"Computer software and accessories" is
   simultaneously one of the three components BEA is re-deflating**, with software/peripherals
   inflation projected to peak near **30% annualized by November 2026**. Whether the new composite
   index amplifies or mutes that signal is **not determinable from BEA's method description**, and
   this doc refuses to guess a sign. What is safe to say: the AI-capex read inside core PCE and the
   methodology change land on overlapping ground, so attributing any software-driven move after 09-30
   to real AI pricing power requires the revision to be netted out first.

7. **Warsh's stated preferred gauge and the gauge he actually argued from disagree by ~100bp —
   SUPPORTED, and it partially resolves the sibling's open leg.** [`pce-2026-08-26`](pce-2026-08-26.md)
   left leg 6 MIXED: this chair called PCE "rough swag" and prefers trimmed mean, so the standard
   reaction function might transmit weaker. At Jackson Hole on **2026-08-28** he argued the hawkish
   case from **headline PCE 3.7% (12-month) and 4.1% (6-month)** — the official gauge, not the
   trimmed mean (full scoring in [`jackson-hole-2026-08-28`](jackson-hole-2026-08-28.md)). Meanwhile

   3.7% and core 3.3% — a **~100bp gap** between the measure he says he prefers and the one he

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date confirmed, BEA primary re-fetched 2026-08-29).** Treat 2026-10-29 08:30 ET as a
**high-impact print with an unusually weak transmission channel and an unusually strong measurement
caveat**. It publishes 18.5 hours *after* the 10-28 FOMC statement, so it cannot inform that
decision — it grades it, and feeds **12-09**. No directional macro position now or planned; the
standing order for the window it sits in is the FOMC doc's **flat into Oct 27–29**, where the risk is
the five estimated mega-cap prints, not this release. **The operative content is a discount rule, not
a level forecast:** from the **09-30** annual update onward, any decline in core PCE must be reported
as containing roughly **20bp of deflator change** (Goldman/JPMorgan, snippet fidelity, unverified
against a published release) before it is read as disinflation — and by symmetry, a core print that
*fails* to fall despite the revision is a genuinely hotter signal than its face value. **No base-case
level is stated**, deliberately: at D-61 there is no consensus, no whisper and no nowcast for
September data, and inventing one would be the kind of confident-but-unsourced number this lane
exists to refuse. The sibling's open question — whether this chair's reaction function runs through
PCE at all — is **not resolved and moves sideways**: on 08-28 he argued from headline PCE (3.7%/4.1%)
while his stated preferred gauge read 2.3%, which says the *measure follows the argument* rather than
that PCE is operative. **The lapse branch changes the date, not the existence:** on the 2025 ladder
(leg 11) a lapse reschedules this print ~5 weeks into early December — four days either side of the
**12-09** FOMC — cancels its GDP twin outright, and puts the real damage on
[`pce-2026-11-25`](pce-2026-11-25.md), whose October price index would be interpolated. That is a
timing and adjacency change, not a reason to hold a position, and the instruction stays flat.

**Kill switches:**

- **The 09-30 release lands with revised core y/y within ~5bp of the old series** — the ~20bp
  paper-cooling estimate is wrong, legs 4/5/8 collapse, and the discount rule is withdrawn
  immediately rather than carried to 10-29.
- **The realized revision materially exceeds ~20bp** (say, ≥40bp on core y/y) — the opposite failure:
  the measurement break is larger than the street sized it, every pre-09-30 inflation comparison in
  this repo's docs needs restating, and UBS's credibility critique becomes the live story.
