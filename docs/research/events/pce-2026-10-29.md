## Initial research

### The question, plainly

What does September 2026 PCE — the gauge Warsh reaffirmed as the Fed's target measure three days
before this research — look likely to show; what does it mean that it publishes **after** the
October FOMC rather than before it; and does the federal-data-blackout branch the sibling docs have
been building toward actually delete this release, as they assert, or something else?

**One-line verdict.** The inherited blackout framing is **off by one reference month** and this doc
corrects it with the 2025 ladder: September-reference data was delayed (PCE by five weeks), October-
reference data was destroyed or interpolated — so in a lapse this print slips toward the 12-09 FOMC
rather than vanishing, while its 10-29 twin (Q3 advance GDP) is cancelled outright on the exact
precedent. On the print itself there is nothing to do: it lands eighteen hours after a decision it
cannot inform, into the densest 72 hours this calendar tracks, where the standing answer is already
flat.

### Leg 2 — this print cannot inform the decision it judges — SUPPORTED, and it is the structural finding

The 10-28 statement drops at **14:00 ET**; this release is **08:30 ET the next morning**. The
committee therefore decides October without September PCE — its own target gauge for the most recent
complete month. That is not unusual scheduling; what makes it matter here is what else is missing
from that meeting, all of it established by siblings and not re-derived: **no SEP** (October is not
a projections meeting — [`fomc-2026-10-28`](fomc-2026-10-28.md) leg 1, FED primary) and **no forward
guidance** (Warsh abolished it on 08-28 — "a discipline, not a decision"). Three blindfolds, and in
the lapse branch the funding sibling adds a fourth. **Consequence, stated without a position:** this
print's function is not input but **adjudication** — it is the first hard inflation number to arrive
after the decision, and it either ratifies or indicts it within one session.

### Leg 3 — there is one 2026 precedent for exactly this configuration, and it relieved — SUPPORTED (n=1)

The July sequence is the analog and it is close: the **2026-07-29** FOMC delivered a hawkish hold
(9–3, three hike dissents) and equities sold — Dow ~**−1.5%**, S&P/Nasdaq ~**−0.6%** (CNBC,
2026-07-29). **June PCE printed the next morning, 2026-07-30**: headline **−0.1% m/m / 3.7% y/y**,
core **+0.1% m/m / 3.3% y/y**, in line on both legs (CNBC/Advisor Perspectives, 2026-07-30). The
tape **rallied** — Dow +135, S&P +26, Nasdaq +289 at time of reporting (recorded in the
[`pce-2026-08-26`](pce-2026-08-26.md) initial research, leg 7). So the one time this exact ordering
has occurred under this chair, an in-line PCE acted as a **relief valve on a decision that had sold
off the day before**. That is a single observation, in the opposite direction from October's
plausible setup (a hike, not a hold), and it is offered as shape rather than expectation.

### Leg 4 — the shutdown branch deletes the *October* reference month, not September — SUPPORTED, and it corrects two sibling docs

This is the leg the doc exists for. The house framing, stated in
[`government-funding-deadline-2026-09-30`](government-funding-deadline-2026-09-30.md) and inherited
verbatim by [`consumer-confidence-2026-10-27`](consumer-confidence-2026-10-27.md) and
`gdp-q3-2026-advance-2026-10-29`'s calendar note, is that a lapse from 10-01 *"deletes jobs 10-02
and CPI 10-14 — 2025 proved they do not come back."* Checked against 2025 release-by-release, that
is **true of the October reference month and false of the September one**:

| 2025 release (reference month) | Scheduled | What actually happened |
|---|---|---|
| CPI (**Sep**) | 10-15 | **Published 10-24** — 9 days late. BLS stated the release "allows the Social Security Administration to meet statutory deadlines"; the COLA is computed from Jul/Aug/**Sep** CPI-W, so September is the one month of the year with a legal forcing function. |
| CPI (**Oct**) | 11-13 | **Full suite never produced.** |

Three consequences follow, and they are the doc's load-bearing output:

1. **This print slips; it does not vanish.** The 2025 analog for a September-reference PCE is a
   five-week reschedule. Mapped onto 2026 that lands it in **early December** — and in 2025 the
   analogous release (12-05) came **four days before** the December FOMC. The 2026 December meeting
   is **12-09**. Knife-edge, and worth naming now rather than discovering in November.
2. **The two 10-29 releases do not share a fate.** Same agency, same 08:30 slot, opposite outcomes
   in 2025: the advance GDP estimate was cancelled outright (an *advance* estimate loses its reason
   to exist once it is late), while the monthly PCE series was rescheduled. The sibling
   `gdp-q3-2026-advance-2026-10-29` doc should be read with that asymmetry in mind.
3. **The genuinely compromised release is `pce-2026-11-25`** — October 2026 data, proposed to the
   calendar in this PR precisely so the exposure is tracked. In the lapse branch that print either
   disappears into a combined January release or arrives with **October's price index interpolated
   from September and November CPI**: a number that exists but is partly synthetic, feeding the very
   gauge Warsh has previously called "rough swag."

**Recorded here, not by editing the siblings** — their rows are append-only and this is a
correction of framing, not of a logged reading.

### Leg 6 — the trend Warsh is reacting to is in the 6-month window, not the headline — SUPPORTED

His own figures, from the prepared remarks: 12-month PCE **3.7%**, **6-month 4.1%**. The 6-month run
rate sitting **40bp above** the 12-month is the arithmetic definition of re-acceleration, and it is
why he said this summer's better-than-expected readings *"do not tell me that underlying trends have
meaningfully improved."* Corroborating the direction from the series itself: core PCE ran **3.0% in
Dec 2025 → 3.3% in Jun 2026** (Advisor Perspectives / CNBC, 2026-07-30), and July core printed
**3.3%** flat with headline **3.7%** flat (BEA primary, scored in the August sibling's close-out).
**What this means for the September print specifically:** the y/y headline can stay pinned at 3.3–3.7
while the momentum measure the chair is actually watching keeps deteriorating — so a "flat, in line"
September core print is **not** the all-clear its own y/y number would suggest. Read the **m/m and
the 6-month annualized**, not the year-over-year, and read them again on **09-30**.

### Honest limits

**No consensus, whisper or nowcast for September 2026 PCE exists at D-61 and none is expected to** —
the September reference month has not begun, the Cleveland Fed nowcast page **403s** on direct fetch,
and core-PCE nowcasts mechanically cannot move until September CPI/PPI data exists. Every forward
statement about the *number* here is trend extrapolation from July, not a sourced expectation; the
one hard figure the doc leans on (Warsh's 6-month 4.1%) is his citation, not an independently
recomputed series. The BLS half of leg 4's ladder rests on **press at search-snippet fidelity**
(bls.gov 403s), though the BEA half is the agency's own blog text and the two halves agree. Leg 3 is
**n=1** and its direction (hold→relief) is the opposite of October's plausible setup. Leg 5 reads
Warsh's recommitment through three press writeups, not the posted text of the remarks — the
`jackson-hole-2026-08-28` close-out filed today is the primary-sourced record and should be
preferred where they differ. The AAPL/AMZN print dates are **`estimate`** (8-K cadence), so the
same-morning collision in leg 7 is estimate-grade. **A judgement call, stated on the record, and it
is an inconsistency this doc surfaces rather than settles.** The two new PCE entries are filed
**`estimate`** with the `EST:` prefix even though their dates come from a verbatim primary
`bea.gov/news/schedule` line, because this lane's own instructions say adjacency proposals are never
`confirmed` — the same reading `chicago-pmi-2026-09-30` took on 2026-08-29, whose note states it
explicitly (*"this lane proposes adjacencies as estimates and never self-promotes them to
confirmed"*). But the **same day**, `consumer-confidence-2026-10-27` filed this very event and its
GDP twin as **`confirmed`**/`BEA:` off the identical fetch, flagged the tension in its own limits,
and nobody has resolved it. The calendar is therefore now visibly incoherent — `pce-2026-10-29`
confirmed, `pce-2026-09-30` and `pce-2026-11-25` estimate, all three from one schedule page. Both
readings are defensible (`market-events-data.ts`'s header does list *"`BEA:` bea.gov release
schedule (GDP, PCE)"* under `confirmed`, and `--validate` enforces that mapping), the practical
difference is nil here (nothing date-keyed acts on any of the three), and it is a one-line change
either way — but it wants a decision from outside the lane, not a third lane doc guessing. The
**"GDP (Third Estimate) … 2nd Quarter 2026"** release on the same

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date confirmed, BEA primary re-fetched 2026-08-29).** Treat 2026-10-29 08:30 ET as a
**high-impact print with no pre-print trade in it**, for a structural reason rather than a
positioning one: it publishes **eighteen hours after** the FOMC statement it comments on, so it can
only adjudicate the decision, never inform it. No directional macro position now or planned; the
standing instruction for the corridor is inherited unchanged from
[`fomc-2026-10-28`](fomc-2026-10-28.md) — **flat into Oct 27–29**, each tracked print by its own
D-1, no new entries into the cluster. Two things this doc adds. **First (leg 4), the lapse branch is
re-specified:** a shutdown from 10-01 does not delete this release, it **slips it ~5 weeks toward
the 12-09 FOMC**, while cancelling its 10-29 GDP twin outright and pushing the genuine deletion /
interpolation risk one reference month forward onto `cpi-2026-11-10`, `jobs-2026-11-06` and
`pce-2026-11-25`. **Second (legs 5–6), the reading order:** Warsh reaffirmed PCE as the 2% gauge on
08-28, so the standard reaction function applies — but he is reacting to a **6-month run rate of
4.1% against a 12-month of 3.7%**, which means the y/y headline is the least informative line in the
release. Read m/m and the 6-month annualized first, starting with the **09-30** August print. On the
day itself, per the 08-28 natural experiment, the informative instrument is the **2-year**, not the
index — and equity-index volatility is not a hedge for this print.

**Kill switches:**

- **A CR is signed before 2026-09-30** — the entire lapse branch (leg 4) becomes historical trivia
  and this reverts to an ordinary high-impact macro print. *The funding sibling's own base case, and
  the most likely kill.*
- **No CR by 2026-09-30 and BEA posts a schedule update** — the 2025 mechanism was a
  `bea.gov/news/blog` post; watch it directly. If that post **cancels** rather than reschedules this
  release, leg 4's central asymmetry is wrong and the September-vs-October framing dies with it.
- **Sep core PCE ≥3.5%** — the 6-month acceleration has become the trend; a 10-28 hold reads as
  behind-the-curve within one session, into the front end. Reassess rate-sensitive exposure that day.
- **Sep core PCE ≤3.0%** — the first sub-3.1 reading since Dec 2025 and beyond anything the July
  trend supports; if the Fed hiked on 10-28 this is a policy-error narrative arriving on AMZN/AAPL's
  print morning, and the two are not cleanly separable.
- **The 10-29 session produces an S&P move ≥1.0% attributable to this print while the 2y moves
  <5bp** — the front-end transmission read inherited from the 08-28 experiment fails on its second
  test, and leg 7's "read the 2y, not the index" instruction is withdrawn.
- **Warsh names a non-PCE gauge (trimmed-mean, a broader basket) before 10-29** — reverses leg 5's
  resolution and re-opens the August sibling's question; his stated inflation-measurement task
  forces, with a possible framework change floated for after January 2027, are the live channel.
- **BLS announces the 10-14 CPI is cancelled rather than delayed in a lapse** — the COLA
  forcing-function argument in leg 4 fails on contact with 2026 rather than 2025, and this print
  loses its principal source input rather than merely its schedule slot.
