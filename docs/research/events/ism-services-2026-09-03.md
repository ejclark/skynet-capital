## At a glance

**TL;DR.** The August ISM Services PMI lands Thursday 2026-09-03 at 10:00 ET — the day *before* the
high-impact August jobs report (9/4) and inside the pre-FOMC corridor. This is the single most
rate-relevant release in the ISM set: services is roughly 70% of the economy, and its **Prices
Index** is the biggest non-CPI mover of the Fed hike/cut path we track. The July print (released
2026-08-05) was the corridor's cleanest hawkish shot — headline **54.1** (a hair light) but the
**Prices Index surged to 70.3** vs a 65.0 estimate, its hottest 12-month average since April 2023,
and it drove the 10-year Treasury yield up ~9bps to its highest since November 2022, pressuring
equities. That print also carried a soft-employment divergence (47.4, in contraction) — a
stagflation tinge the Fed reads carefully. Nothing here is a trade; it is the corridor's most
important rate-path read short of CPI. The asymmetry: a hot August Prices Index lifts yields and
hits our long-duration AI names; a soft print is dovish relief. Warsh **abolished forward guidance
on 2026-08-28** and pinned the hawkish case on *inflation* rather than growth — which promotes this
print to the largest non-CPI lever on the September path. **What is new at D-1 — the forward tell
fired, and it fired hot.** ISM's own **manufacturing** print landed 9/1 from the same institute on the
same survey architecture, and its **Prices Paid index held at 71.1, unchanged from July**, with ISM's
chair attributing the pressure to steel/aluminium, tariffs and "increases in petroleum-based products
as a result of the Middle East conflict." That is the nearest available read on the energy→prices
channel this doc's kill switch names, and it argues **against** the easing-August-cost-environment
lean banked at D-5/D-4 — while staying a different panel from services, which is less energy-intensive
and more labour-driven. The same report's headline **missed** (54.6 vs 55.2 consensus, New Orders -3.0
to 53.7) and the market repriced *hawkishly* anyway: September hike odds **56–58% → 65–68%** (CME
FedWatch), the clearest confirmation yet that growth misses are not moving this Fed and inflation
prints are. The backdrop tightened with it — 10-year **4.79%** intraday (highest since Jan 2025), 30-year
**5.27%**, Nasdaq **-1.4%**, VIX **16.34 (+9.5%)**, its first break out of the sub-15 band held all
month, on a global bond sell-off and Brent back above **$91**. Headline consensus is unchanged at
**54.0** (mass clustered 53–56); the Prices/Employment subcomponent gap is now confirmed at the
calendar level (FXStreet lists Prices Paid consensus as **n/a** at D-1, prior 70.3). **Net: no change
to the base case, a wider asymmetry in both directions, and a hot-Prices branch that just got more
plausible than the last two rows implied.**

## Initial research

**The question.** What is the consensus for the August 2026 ISM Services PMI, what does the July
print and recent trend say, how has the market reacted to this release in each direction during
2026, and which of our tracked names (NVDA MRVL AVGO CRWV MSFT GOOG META AAPL AMZN) carry the most
sensitivity — around 2026-09-03?

**One-line verdict:** the highest-leverage non-CPI rate-path print in the ISM set — services is
~70% of the economy and its Prices Index is the biggest non-CPI mover of the hike/cut path — coming
off a July release whose 70.3 Prices reading (vs 65.0 est) drove yields to a near-three-year high;
no firm August street consensus exists yet at D-15; the market trades it through the yield channel
(hot prices → yields up → long-duration tech pressured), and the house answer is guard-shaped, not
directional — the play is known-date variance discipline the day before jobs, inside the pre-FOMC
corridor.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md)
(macro-print mode: no price instruments). Primary source is ismworld.org (the Report On Business
release calendar and the July Services report); press (PRNewswire, Neil's Newsletter, PNC, CNBC,
Penn Mutual) used only for reaction narration and component detail, each claim dated. The ROB
release-calendar primary sits behind an SSO login (ecommerce.ismworld.org) and was not directly

**Conviction legs, tested:**

1. **The date and time are right — SUPPORTED (with a gated-primary caveat).** ISM publishes the
   Services PMI on the **third business day of the month at 10:00 ET** (ismworld.org ROB
   report-calendar page; cadence stated across the July report and multiple aggregations, fetched
   2026-08-19). September 2026's third business day is **Thursday, Sep 3** (business days: Tue 9/1,
   Wed 9/2, Thu 9/3; Labor Day is Mon 9/7, so no early-month holiday displaces the count) — matching
   the checked-in calendar entry exactly. As with the [`ISM-mfg sibling`](ism-manufacturing-2026-09-01.md),
   the ROB calendar's exact line-item page redirects to an SSO login and could not be fetched
   directly, so this leg rests on the cadence rule + convergent aggregation, not a single fetched
   primary line — a real limit, not an unconfirmed date.

2. **The July Prices Index was the corridor's hottest inflation shot — SUPPORTED.** July Services
   Prices (released 2026-08-05) surged to **70.3** from June's 67.7, **exceeding a 65.0 estimate**
   by a wide margin, its highest 12-month average since April 2023 (ismworld.org / PRNewswire /
   Neil's Newsletter, 2026-08-05). Services inflation is the stickiest, most rate-relevant component
   in the survey set, and a >70 Prices reading is exactly the evidence the hawkish FOMC minority
   (three July hike dissents, per the [`FOMC doc`](fomc-2026-09-16.md)) cites. The calendar's own
   note is precisely right: services is ~70% of the economy and its prices component is "the single
   biggest non-CPI rate-path mover in this set."

3. **The headline was fine but the internals diverged — SUPPORTED.** Headline Services PMI printed
   **54.1** (a touch below a 54.5 estimate, up 0.1 from June's 54.0), the 25th straight month of
   expansion, with Business Activity jumping 3.7 points to **59.1** (second-highest since May 2024)
   and New Orders rising to **57.2** (from 55.1). But Employment fell into contraction at **47.4**
   (from 51.2) — a hot-prices / strong-activity / soft-labor divergence. That combination is
   stagflation-shaped: demand and prices firm while the labor subcomponent softens, the worse mix
   for both our long-duration names and the Fed's decision.

4. **The market trades ISM-services through the yield channel, asymmetrically — SUPPORTED (this-cycle
   precedent).** On the July release day (8/5), the hot Prices reading combined with rising yields to
   keep equities under pressure: the 10-year Treasury yield gained ~9bps to ~4.17%, its highest since
   early November 2022, and the 30-year rose ~10bps (release-day recaps, 2026-08-05 — level figures
   carry aggregator risk, the direction is the reliable part), extending the fresh-2026-high move the
   strong 8/1 manufacturing print had begun (Penn Mutual, 2026-08-03). That hawkish activity/prices
   run reversed only when the 8/7 jobs report collapsed (yields fell, hike odds 67%→44%, per the FOMC
   doc). Pattern: hot-prices ISM-services prints lift yields and pressure long-duration equities; the
   dovish relief comes from the labor and inflation prints. Because services' Prices reading is the
   biggest non-CPI rate mover, this is the ISM release most able to move the tape on its own.

5. **It lands the day before jobs, inside the pre-FOMC corridor — SUPPORTED.** Sep 3 sits directly
   ahead of the high-impact **August jobs report (9/4)** and inside the Sep 1→Sep 16 corridor
   (ISM-mfg + JOLTS 9/1, then PPI 9/10, CPI 9/11, FOMC 9/16 — all confirmed, all on the calendar).
   A hot Prices reading on 9/3 primes the hawkish read going into jobs; the two prints together
   (services prices + payrolls) reset the hike-odds base before the CPI/FOMC leg. Positioning taken
   "for ISM-services" is really a bet on the 9/3→9/4 pair, not one print.

6. **Tracked-name sensitivity — ranked, with mechanism.** `symbols: []` — market-wide; the ranking
   is about *our* risk. All nine trade as long-duration assets on the rate path (the calendar's own
   CPI note). Most sensitive: **CRWV** (debt-financed datacenter build — a hawkish repricing hits

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date confirmed, ISM cadence + convergence re-checked 2026-08-19).** Treat 2026-09-03 10:00
ET as the highest-leverage non-CPI rate-path read in the corridor short of the jobs/CPI prints, and a
high-impact known-date variance event — not a tradeable edge. No directional macro position, no new
rate-sensitive paper entries initiated into the print, and any open position sized as if both outcomes
(hot Prices → yield lift; soft Prices → dovish relief) are live. Base case (**estimate-labeled**:
trend extrapolation, no street consensus exists at D-15): continued expansion near the July level with
the Prices Index holding elevated (>65), a read the market absorbs unless Prices re-accelerate toward
or above 70 again. The asymmetric risk is a hot Prices Index — especially paired with the July
soft-employment divergence — that lifts yields and re-arms the September hike into the CPI/FOMC leg,
hitting CRWV and the high-multiple semis hardest.

**Kill switches:**

- **A soft Prices Index (below ~65) on 9/3** kills the hawkish base case and flips this into a
  dovish-relief input on the same channel the 8/7 jobs miss and 8/12 CPI ran; reassess the corridor
  skew from the actual print.
- **A weak jobs print the next day (9/4)** collapsing hike odds toward zero re-weights this print as
  confirmation rather than a fork — though a hot-Prices-then-weak-jobs sequence is the stagflation
  read, worse for our names even as it lowers hike odds.
- **A published August consensus emerging before 9/3** resolves this doc's biggest limit — re-run the
  "surprise" framing against it rather than the July-trend extrapolation above. **Fired at the
  headline level 2026-08-30** (54.0, prediction-market mass 53–56; see that row) — the surviving
  half of this switch is now a *subcomponent* forecast for Prices or Employment, still not found.
  **Closed as un-fireable 2026-09-02** (see that row): FXStreet's own ISM Services Prices Paid
  calendar entry for the 9/3 release lists consensus **n/a** against a 70.3 prior at D-1, so the
  absence is confirmed at the calendar level rather than merely unfound by search — this half of the
  switch will not fire before the print.
- **Energy de-escalation** ([`CPI doc`](cpi-2026-09-11.md)'s own kill switch — a holding ceasefire,
  Hormuz reopening) would ease the input-cost pass-through feeding the Prices Index; a continuation or
  re-shock is the leg to watch hardest in the Prices subcomponent. **Approached and then reversed
  2026-08-30** (see that row): the 8/27 Iran–Oman agreement and the four-session Brent slide never
  became a reopening, and a US strike on Larak Island on 8/30 put Brent back above $90. This switch
  is un-fired; the de-escalation branch needs a *holding* ceasefire, which no longer exists.
  **Widened 2026-09-01** (see that row): Iran's overnight retaliation against bases in Jordan and the
  UAE made it a two-way exchange with a producer-side dimension, so the re-shock branch — not the
  de-escalation branch — is the one to watch, from the *September* data forward. **Sharpened
  2026-09-02** (see that row): the 9/1 ISM **manufacturing** report — same institute, same survey
  architecture, same August window — held Prices Paid at **71.1** and named "petroleum-based products
  as a result of the Middle East conflict" as one of three drivers, so the energy→prices transmission
  this switch tracks was still running *inside* August, not only from September forward.
