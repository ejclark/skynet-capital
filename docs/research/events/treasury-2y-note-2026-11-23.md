## At a glance

weaker than the calendar has been treating it.** The [10-26 sibling](treasury-2y-note-2026-10-26.md)
measured that 2Y auction days close **−6.07bp** away from FOMC weeks (t = −4.91, n=27) and controlled
it four ways — all four inside the same 2023-2026 window. Extending that study out of sample this
session (all **320** nominal 2Y auctions since 2000 against **6,674** par-curve sessions, both pulled
today): **2000–2022 auction days close +0.38bp, t = +1.34, n=276** — wrong sign, insignificant. The
effect is real *within* its window and demonstrably absent before it, so it is a **regime property
with a start date (2022–2023), not a structural feature of Treasury supply**; the same series has
produced two earlier significant episodes (−2.79bp in 2000-01, **+3.00bp** in 2002-04) and both
decayed. Second finding, a clean negative that matters more for this date: **the Thanksgiving-week
discount does not exist.** In-week auctions look worse on the full 2000+ sample (+2.11bp vs −0.68bp,
permutation p = 0.043) and that entire gap is three pre-2010 prints — 2002 (+16bp), 2003 (+8bp),

## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** this calendar now carries a measured prior that a 2-Year auction day
rallies the front end ~6bp. 2026-11-23 is the first sale where that prior applies cleanly — far from
any FOMC, alone on its own day. Before leaning on it, is the prior actually true, or is it true only
of the window it was measured in? And does the holiday week change anything?

**One-line verdict:** the prior survives as a **regime** claim and dies as a structural one — it is
absent across 22 years of held-out data — and the Thanksgiving-week discount that would have argued
against this particular date turns out to be a pre-2010 artifact, so the honest output is a
distribution to read 11-23 against plus a registered test of whether the regime is still live.

**Method:** sourced research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md), rates mode —
the house instruments are symbol-keyed and this event has `symbols: []`, so no `earnings-cycle` /
`intraday-edges` run applies and no cache bust was required. Every figure below is computed this
session from three Treasury primaries and one exchange primary fetched today: the Fiscal Data
`auctions_query` dataset (all **320** nominal 2-Year note auctions 2000-01-26 → 2026-08-25, FRNs
excluded on the dataset's own `floating_rate` field), the daily par yield curve CSVs for 2000–2026
(**6,674** sessions, 2000-01-03 → 2026-09-08), the Tentative Auction Schedule PDF (HTTP 200, 17,195
bytes, text layer decompressed stream-by-stream), and press release `sb0590` (2026-08-05, read as
text). VIX is CBOE's own `VIX_History.csv`. FOMC meeting end dates 2000–2026 are the published
calendar; the 2026 dates cross-check against three entries this repo already carries
(`fomc-2026-09-16`, `fomc-2026-10-28`, `fomc-2026-12-09`) and against the +21-day minutes cadence.
No fetch failed this session; `probe-ref.blocked` is empty. Each claim dated in line.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The date and terms are right, and the entry stays `estimate` — SUPPORTED.** The tentative
schedule carries the row verbatim: `2-Year NOTE · Thursday, November 19, 2026 · Monday, November 23,
2026 · Monday, November 30, 2026`. No `R`, so a new issue. Read in the same fetch, the **same 11-19
announcement** carries `2-Year FRN R` and `5-Year NOTE` on Tuesday 11-24 and `7-Year NOTE` on
Wednesday 11-25, and the schedule's own holiday line reads `Holiday - Thursday, November 26, 2026 -
Thanksgiving Day`. `TSY:` is an authorised `confirmed` prefix, but this sale is **75 days out with
its announcement 71 days away** — further out than the 10-26 sibling was when it chose `estimate` on
the same reasoning. Holding `estimate` costs nothing: estimates only widen caution and no date-keyed
action follows from this event at any label. **The confirming primary is the 2026-11-19 announcement.**

**2. Nobody has published this auction's size, and the calendar's inherited $69B needs re-labelling —
MIXED, and this corrects the proposal that created the entry.** The proposal filed against this id
carried $69B as "second-hand… carried from a sibling sweep's transcription of the Oct-26 row." Read
direct today, `sb0590`'s anticipated-size table is headed `2-Year 3-Year 5-Year 7-Year 10-Year 20-Year
30-Year FRN` and its rows run **`May-26` `Jun-26` `Jul-26` `Aug-26` `Sep-26` `Oct-26` — and stop**.
There is no November row anywhere in the release. So the transcription question the 10-26 sibling
settled (2-Year is column 1, $69B, not the FRN column) is settled *for October* and simply does not
reach November. What supports $69B here is weaker and should be named as such: **29 consecutive $69B
2Y auctions** (2024-04-23 → 2026-08-25, zero deviations) plus the release's verbatim guidance that
"Treasury anticipates maintaining nominal coupon and FRN auction sizes for at least the next several
quarters." That is a strong expectation, not a published figure. The grid arrives at the **2026-11-04**
refunding; the actual terms at the **11-19** announcement.

**3. The auction-day rally is real in this regime and absent in the 22 years before it — the
sibling's finding survives, its generality does not. MIXED, and this is the load-bearing leg.**
Close-to-close 2Y CMT moves, computed over all 6,674 sessions:

| Population | n | Mean move | t |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2Y auction days, **2023-01 → 2026-09** | 44 | **−4.70bp** | **−4.60** |
| …of those, >7d from any FOMC | 27 | **−6.07bp** | **−4.91** |
| 2Y auction days, **2000-01 → 2022-12** | 276 | **+0.38bp** | +1.34 |
| …of those, >7d from any FOMC | 175 | +0.44bp | +1.20 |
| 2Y auction days, 2010–2019 only | 120 | +0.16bp | +0.65 |

The held-out mean has the **wrong sign** and is insignificant. This is not a power problem — n=276 is
six times the in-sample size. Nor is it a window artifact: the same-window **non-auction** sessions
give a diff-in-diff of **−4.94bp now against +0.43bp then**, and a permutation test of the two
auction-day populations (100,000 draws) returns **p < 0.00001**. So the two regimes genuinely differ,
and the honest statement is that the 10-26 sibling measured something real about 2023-2026 and its
four controls — weekday, month position, outliers, sample halves — could not have caught this,
because every one of them lived inside the window.

**The switch has a date and a precedent.** A rolling 24-auction window puts the turn in **2022-2023**
and shows it stable since: every window ending 2024 or later returns −4.7 to −5.6bp at t between
−2.8 and −6.0, which is the largest and longest episode on the 320-auction record. But it is the
**fourth** episode, not the first:

| Window | Mean auction-day move | t | What happened next |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2000-01 … 2001-12 | **−2.79bp** | −2.96 | decayed to +0.63 within two years |
| 2002-01 … 2004-06 | **+3.00bp** | +2.28 | decayed to +0.71 by 2005-2007 |
| 2015-07 … 2018-06 | **+1.33bp** | +2.82 | decayed to −0.42 by 2018-2020 |
| 2022-07 … 2026-08 | **−5.29bp** | −4.76 | **live** |

Three prior significant episodes, three decays, and two of them the opposite sign. That is the base
rate the stance has to carry. It does not say the current one is noise — it is roughly twice the
magnitude of any predecessor even after scaling by the era's own session volatility — it says the
prior deserves a **falsifier and a review cadence**, not a law's confidence.

**4. The Thanksgiving-week discount is a pre-2010 artifact — REFUTED, and this is the finding that
most changes how 11-23 should be read.** The intuitive worry about selling $69B on the Monday of a
three-session holiday week is thin liquidity and a worse concession. The full sample appears to agree:

| Population (far-from-FOMC 2Y auctions) | n | Mean move | Permutation p |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2000+**, in Thanksgiving week | 18 | **+2.11bp** | **0.043** vs out-of-week (−0.68bp) |
| **2010+**, in Thanksgiving week | 12 | −1.42bp | 0.84 vs out-of-week (−1.06bp) |
| **2013+**, in Thanksgiving week | 10 | −1.20bp | 0.95 vs out-of-week (−1.32bp) |
| **2018+**, in Thanksgiving week | 6 | −2.17bp | 0.94 vs out-of-week (−2.39bp) |

The whole 2000+ gap is **three prints**: 2002-11-27 (+16bp), 2003-11-26 (+8bp) and 2008-11-24
(+22bp) — a $26B auction era, a different settlement convention, and the week after Lehman's quarter.
Trimming just the single highest and lowest observation from each side leaves +2.00 vs −0.61, so it
is not one outlier; it is a pre-2010 cluster, and every cut that excludes it returns a p above 0.8.
**Demand does not deteriorate either**, which is the dimension the discount hypothesis is actually
about: bid-to-cover in-week vs out is **2.790 vs 2.712** (2000+) and **2.632 vs 2.622** (2018+) — the
in-week number is *higher* in both. The two Thanksgiving-week sales inside the $69B era read
**2024-11-25: b/c 2.77, dealer 9.20%, −16bp** and **2025-11-24: b/c 2.68, dealer 11.18%, −5bp**, both
covering above the era median. **Practical output: do not discount 2026-11-23 for the holiday week,
and do not let a soft print be explained away by it either.**

One incidental observation, recorded as such rather than as a claim: across 2000-2026 the **Tuesday**
of Thanksgiving week closes **−2.46bp** (n=26, t = −2.99) against a −0.03bp all-session mean, while
the Monday (−0.54bp), Wednesday (+1.85bp) and half-day Friday (−0.77bp) are unremarkable. That is one
significant result out of four offsets tested and it is not this event's day; it belongs to
`treasury-5y-note-2026-11-24` if anyone wants to pursue it.

**5. 2026-11-23 is far from the FOMC, so the 10-26 sibling's pin does not apply — SUPPORTED.** The
next meeting is [`fomc-2026-12-09`](fomc-2026-12-09.md), **16 days out**, and
`fomc-blackout-start-2026-11-28` opens five days *after* this sale. So where the October auction sat
in the D−2 slot the sibling measured at −2.43bp, this one sits squarely in the far-from-FOMC
population it measured at −6.07bp. The two events therefore test **different halves of the same

test is written to be read alongside that one rather than in place of it.

**Honest limits.** The out-of-sample refutation is a refutation of *generality*, not of the 2023+
measurement — anyone reading it as "the sibling was wrong" has read it wrong. The regime-episode
table is identified by inspection of a rolling window, not by a structural-break test, and the
"three episodes, three decays" base rate is n=3. All moves are **daily close-to-close on a CMT
series rounded to 1bp**, so nothing here can see an intraday concession before the 1:00pm award;
"the auction causes a rally" overstates what is measured, which is that the auction-day close sits
below the prior close with no offset in surrounding days. Causation is not established: an auction
day contains everything else that happened that day. Bid-to-cover's link to the same-day move is
significant in both eras (2023+ corr −0.388, t = −2.73; 2000-2022 −0.213, t = −3.60) but explains
~15% and ~5% of variance respectively. The Thanksgiving-week cuts leave **n=6 to n=12** in-week, so
they establish *absence of evidence at usable power for a large effect*, not proof of no effect —
what they do establish firmly is that the 2000+ result cannot be cited. This auction's size, CUSIP
and demand **do not exist yet**. FOMC odds drift daily. `symbols: []`, `medium` impact, date
`estimate`: nothing here licenses a position in any name.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (estimate-dated event; no standalone play):** watch-only, and the contribution is a
**correctly-labelled prior plus its first clean test**. The 2Y auction-day rally this calendar has
been carrying since 2026-09-05 is a **2022-2023-onward regime**, not a structural feature: it is
−6.07bp away from FOMC weeks in-window (t = −4.91, n=27) and **+0.44bp across the 175 held-out
far-from-FOMC auctions of 2000-2022** (t = +1.20). The regime is the largest and longest of four
episodes on the 320-auction record and the other three decayed, so the prior is used with a
falsifier rather than assumed. For **2026-11-23** specifically, three things line up in the same
direction: it is far from FOMC (D−16 to `fomc-2026-12-09`), so the D−2 pin the
[10-26 sibling](treasury-2y-note-2026-10-26.md) measured does not apply; the Thanksgiving-week
discount that would have cut the other way is **refuted on every post-2010 cut** (p = 0.84 / 0.95 /
0.94, with in-week bid-to-cover *higher*, not lower); and it is the only session in the November
block carrying no other tracked event, so its close-to-close move is actually attributable — unlike
its [20Y sibling](treasury-20y-bond-2026-11-18.md), which shares 11-18 with the FOMC minutes. Base
case is therefore a **rally in the −3 to −9bp band** (the in-regime p25–p75 is −8.5 to −3.0), with
a flat or positive tape counting as real information about the regime rather than about demand.
Grade the print itself on **dealer takedown** (2025-04+ p25 10.2% / median 11.2% / p75 12.3%), never
on indirect share. Size is **not published** and must not be asserted before **2026-11-04**. Nothing
here is directional and no house playbook applies.

**Kill switches (what would change this stance):**

- **The core one — the 2Y CMT move on 2026-11-23 being greater than −3.0bp.** The regime failing on
  its cleanest available test: far from FOMC, alone on its day, in a week proven ordinary. In-regime
  far auctions clear that bar **21 of 27 times (78%)**; the pre-2023 population clears it **23%** of
  the time (41/175) and comparable 2023+ far-from-FOMC *non-auction* sessions **28%** (164/581).
  Registered as **FT-treasury-2y-note-2026-11-23-1**, score by 2026-11-24.
- **The 09-22 or 10-26 predecessor printing at 0bp or higher on the tape.** 2026 has already produced
  two such far-from-FOMC prints (02-24 at 0bp, 03-24 at +7bp); a third before this event sells would
  say the regime is decaying now, and the base case above should be widened toward flat before 11-23
