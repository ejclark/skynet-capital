## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **This one already traded — on 2026-08-18, not on 2026-10-01.** Apple's EU terms change
is not a regulator imposing something on Apple; it is a **negotiated settlement with the European
Commission that Apple announced itself**, and the tape paid for it that day: AAPL **+1.45%** against
QQQ **−1.69%** = **+3.15% excess**, with contemporaneous press naming the scrapping of the
per-install Core Technology Fee as the cause, then another **+2.39% excess** on 08-19 (~**+5.5%**
over the two sessions). October 1 is the **mechanical effective date of a pre-announced deal** — no
decision is made, no number is disclosed, and nothing is revealed that a developer signing the terms
today does not already know. The economics are small in both directions and arguably favour Apple:
the only headline concession is the standard EU App Store commission going **30% → 26%** (a 13.3%
relative cut), applied to an EU slice that Apple itself told analysts is **~7% of global App Store
revenue** — under **1% of global App Store revenue** even before offsets, against a Services segment
running **$30.7B a quarter** (FQ3-2026, +12% y/y). And there *are* offsets: Apple **gains** a 5%
Core Technology Commission on alternative-distribution and link-out transactions where the old
€0.50/install fee collected little, plus a **15%/10%** Store Services Commission on link-outs. John
Gruber calls it "a near-total victory for Apple"; Above Avalon calls it "surprisingly not horrific."
**Stand aside on the date.** Two honest counterweights kept on the record: the effective day is also
**unmeasurable** — 09-30/10-01 carries the FY2027 funding cliff, PCE, GDP-third, Chicago PMI and ISM
Manufacturing, so any AAPL print that session is unattributable — and this settlement **does not**
retire Apple's EU legal overhang: the **€500M** anti-steering fine is still on appeal at the General
Court (filed 2025-07-07), and the Article 6(7) interoperability specification proceedings
(DMA.100203/100204) remain live and separately appealed. The place this event actually shows up is
the **FQ1 Services guide at the est. 2026-10-29 print**, not a date-keyed trade.

**Signals & conditions** — the buy/sell/hold triggers:

- **No buy and no sell signal exists off this event.** The repricing happened on **2026-08-18**;
  buying the effective date is buying a press release that is six weeks old.
- **The date is now `confirmed` (`IR:`), and that changes nothing about the call** — it removes date
  doubt, not the fact that the date carries no new information. A `confirmed` label licenses
  date-keyed action; it does not create a reason for one.
- **Watch (undated, and worth more than 10-01):** any **formal European Commission decision or
  publication** on the Article 6(4) alternative-distribution proceeding · the **General Court ruling
  on Apple's €500M anti-steering appeal** · movement in the **DMA.100203/100204** interoperability
  specification proceedings.
- **Watch (dated):** **2026-10-01** terms effective · est. **2026-10-29** FQ4 print and FQ1 guide —
  the actual venue for any Services read.
- **Do not read this as a headwind by default.** Three of the four rate changes (CTC on alternative
  distribution, the 20% alternative-payment tier, the 15%/10% Store Services Commission on link-outs)
  are Apple **collecting** where it previously collected little or nothing. Net direction is
  genuinely ambiguous and this session could not resolve it — see honest limits.
- **Do not conflate this with the US.** The US link-out regime (post-*Epic* injunction, 2025) charges
  **0%**; the EU link-out now costs **15% + 5% CTC**. A US read-across from these numbers is wrong.

### The question, plainly

The [iPhone-18 launch ledger](aapl-iphone-18-launch-2026-09-09.md)'s 2026-09-02 adjacency sweep found
this date and filed it as the calendar's first **regulatory** AAPL entry — the calendar carried
Apple's product and earnings dates but nothing from Brussels. So: **is 2026-10-01 an information
event, how big is the money actually at stake, and does this settlement retire Apple's EU regulatory
overhang?**

**One-line verdict.** No, small, and no — the information traded on **2026-08-18** at a measured
**+3.15% excess**; the exposure is **under 1% of global App Store revenue** before offsets that may
cancel it entirely; and the €500M fine appeal plus two interoperability proceedings survive

   **revised 2026-08-18**. The prefix table in
   [`market-events-data.ts`](../../../src/domain/market-events-data.ts) defines `IR:` as "company
   primary source" — which is exactly what apple.com is here, so unlike the four standing schema gaps

   prefix obstacle**. The entry was filed `estimate` on 09-02 only because the sweep that *discovered*
   it may not self-confirm; this is an independent session and the promotion is the same move

2. **"October 1 is the information event."** **REFUTED — measured, and this is the load-bearing
   finding.** The information event was the **announcement, 2026-08-18**: AAPL **+1.45%** vs QQQ
   **−1.69%** = **+3.15% excess**, on a session when the rest of large-cap tech was red.
   Contemporaneous press attributes the move to exactly this: GuruFocus reported AAPL "nearing 2%"
   against broad tech declines, and MacDailyNews (08-19, "Why Apple is rallying") named the scrapping
   of the per-install Core Technology Fee as removing "a cloud of legal and financial uncertainty."
   The next session added **+2.19% vs −0.20% = +2.39% excess**, ~**+5.5%** cumulative.
   **Confound named, not resolved:** Rothschild & Co. Redburn upgraded AAPL to **Buy, $400 PT on

   +3.15% is the more defensible number of the two. Either way, an effective date arriving six weeks
   after a fully-specified, developer-signable announcement is a **process day**, not a decision day —
   the same distinction the [FTC-v-Amazon ledger](ftc-v-amazon-antitrust-trial-2027-03-29.md) measured
   at −0.04% excess for a trial opening.
3. **"This is a regulator imposing terms on Apple."** **REFUTED — it is a negotiated settlement Apple
   announced.** Apple's own newsroom language: *"These changes resolve Apple's disagreements with the
   Commission over business terms and alternative distribution."* Two independent readings agree the
   settlement favours Apple. **Daring Fireball** (Gruber): "a near-total victory for Apple" — the
   30%→26% cut is 4 percentage points, and had the DMA simply capped commissions "there's no way the
   rate would have been 26 percent," rather "20 percent at the most"; he adds that the 5% CTC
   surviving at all is a win against widespread predictions it would not. **Above Avalon** calls the
   deal "surprisingly not horrific" and "a dramatic shift from what had been a confusing web of terms
   and conditions." *(Above Avalon's actual impact numbers are paywalled and were not read — stated
   as a limit, not paraphrased.)* This matters for direction: a settlement the seller announces and
   commentators score as a win is not a headwind waiting to land on 10-01.
4. **"The rate changes are a straightforward cut for Apple."** **REFUTED — three of four go the other
   way.** Old EU structure (June-2025 terms): standard App Store IAP **30%**; alternative distribution
   paid a per-install **CTF of €0.50**; link-outs paid **2% initial acquisition + 5–13% Store Services
   + 5% CTC** (the CTC was already scheduled to replace the CTF by January 2026 and slipped to this
   deal). New structure, effective 10-01, from the primary page: App Store with Apple IAP **26%**
   (15% for Small Business / Mini Apps / Video Partner programs and post-year-one auto-renewables);
   App Store with **alternative in-app payment 20%** (10% discounted); **Store Services Commission
   15%** (10% discounted) on out-of-app offers via actionable links, 7-day attribution window;
   **Core Technology Commission 5%** on alternative marketplaces, Web Distribution, and the same
   link-out window. Read against the old terms: the **only** unambiguous concession is 30%→26% on
   standard IAP. The link-out top-of-range is **unchanged** (2+13+5 = 20% old vs 15+5 = 20% new) while
   the *bottom* rises (2+5+5 = 12% old vs 20% new) — a **tightening** for developers who took minimal
   store services. And CTF→CTC converts a per-install fee almost nobody paid at scale into a
   percentage of every transaction outside the App Store.
5. **"The money at stake is small enough to justify `low`."** **SUPPORTED — arithmetically, from
   Apple's own disclosure.** Apple told analysts in 2024 that the **EU is ~7% of global App Store
   revenue** (relayed by Motley Fool, 2026-08-22). The 30%→26% cut is a **13.3% relative** reduction,
   and it touches only the **standard-rate** slice — the 15% small-business/post-year-one rate is
   unchanged under both regimes. Upper bound, assuming *all* EU App Store revenue sits at the standard
   rate: 7% × 13.3% = **0.93% of global App Store revenue**. App Store is itself only part of a
   Services segment that printed **$30.7B in FQ3-2026** (quarter ended 2026-06-27, **+12% y/y**, a
   June-quarter record, >$120B annual run-rate). The `low` tier is correct on the arithmetic, not on
   sentiment. **Deliberately not computed:** a dollar figure — Apple does not disclose App Store
   revenue, and multiplying an aggregator's guess would manufacture false precision. The bound above
   uses only Apple's own 7% and the two published rates.
6. **"This retires Apple's EU regulatory overhang."** **REFUTED — three live matters survive.**
   (a) The **€500M anti-steering fine** (Commission decision 2025-04-23, Article 5(4) DMA) was
   **appealed to the General Court on 2025-07-07** and remains pending — reported as the first test of
   a DMA non-compliance decision on appeal. (b) The **Article 6(7) interoperability specification
   proceedings** (**DMA.100203** connected devices, **DMA.100204** the request-handling process, both
   opened 2024-09-19) are live, and Apple has separately appealed the specification decisions. (c) A
   July-2026 General Court ruling went **against** Apple on its gatekeeper-designation challenge. The
   08-18 settlement is scoped to **business terms and alternative distribution** — Apple's own wording
   — and to nothing else.
7. **"A formal Commission decision closes the loop, and that is dated."** **MIXED — no date, and it is
   the better watch item.** As of 2026-08-19 the Commission had **not published a formal decision** on
   the Article 6(4) alternative-distribution proceeding, and this session found none since. So the
   public record rests on **Apple's** characterisation that differences are "resolved." A Commission
   publication would be a genuine information event; it is **undated**, exactly as the FTC-v-Amazon
   ledger found its real near-term catalyst to be undated while the tracked date was not. Recorded as
   the top watch line rather than filed as a calendar entry, because there is no date to file.
8. **"The effective date is at least measurable, even if it is not important."** **REFUTED — the
   corridor swamps it.** Twelve tracked events fall within ±5 days of 2026-10-01, four of them on
   **09-30** alone (`government-funding-deadline-2026-09-30`, `pce-2026-09-30`,
   `gdp-q2-2026-third-2026-09-30`, `chicago-pmi-2026-09-30`), with **`ism-manufacturing-2026-10-01`
   on the day itself** and `jobs-2026-10-02` the morning after. An FY2027 appropriations lapse would
   dominate the tape outright. Any AAPL excess on 10-01 is unattributable by construction — which is
   why the forward test registered below carries an explicit void condition rather than pretending

### Honest limits

- **Net revenue direction is unresolved, and it is the biggest open question here.** The 30%→26% cut
  subtracts; the CTC and Store Services Commission add. Sizing the addition needs **alternative-
  marketplace and link-out transaction volume in the EU**, and no such figure was findable — searches
  surfaced which marketplaces exist (AltStore PAL authorised April 2024, Epic Games Store launched
  August 2024, Aptoide, Skich, Onside, Mobivention) but **no adoption or share data at all**. This
  session cannot say whether the package is net negative, neutral or positive for Apple, and does
  not pretend to.
- **Above Avalon's financial analysis was not read** — the impact estimates sit behind a paywall.
  Only its publicly-visible rate table and framing are used.
- **The 08-18 excess carries a named confound** — the Redburn upgrade to Buy / $400 PT on 08-17. The
  attribution to the DMA news is contemporaneous press, not a controlled measurement.
- **n=6 on the reaction function, no significance test.** Six regulatory days over 32 months, one of
  them (2024-03-04) a competition-law fine rather than DMA. Directionally consistent and
  benchmark-adjusted, but this is not a base rate.
- **The ~7% EU share is a 2024 disclosure relayed by an aggregator**, not re-verified against a 2026

- **No Commission primary was reached.** `digital-markets-act.ec.europa.eu/apple_en` returned 404 and

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (confirmed — and `confirmed` licenses nothing here).** **Stand aside; watch-only; tier
stays `low`.** The date is now primary-sourced and firm, which removes date doubt and adds no reason
to act: the settlement was announced **2026-08-18**, is already signable by developers, is fully
specified in public, and repriced AAPL **+3.15% excess** that day. October 1 is when a contract takes
effect, not when anything is learned. The exposure is bounded at **under 1% of global App Store
revenue** before offsets that plausibly cancel it, against a **$30.7B/quarter** Services segment —
which is why `low` is the arithmetically correct tier and not a shrug. The value of tracking this
entry is not 10-01. It is that Apple's EU regulatory position now has a **measured reaction function**
(the market pays for settlement surprises, not for fines or effective dates), a **bounded exposure
number**, and a **named list of what the settlement did *not* resolve** — the €500M appeal and two
interoperability proceedings — none of which any AAPL ledger carried before today.

**Kill switches.**

- **The "already priced on 08-18" read dies** if AAPL's close-to-close excess vs QQQ on **2026-10-01**
  exceeds **±3%** with the move attributable to this event rather than the corridor — registered as
  **FT-47**, with the corridor void condition stated there rather than hidden.
- **The "small exposure" read dies** if Apple **quantifies** an EU terms impact in the est. 2026-10-29
  FQ1 guide at a magnitude the ~7%-of-App-Store bound cannot contain — that would mean the disclosure
  the arithmetic rests on is stale, and the tier goes up.
- **The "settlement favours Apple" read dies** if the European Commission publishes a formal Article
  6(4) decision that **imposes terms different from Apple's 08-18 announcement** — the deal would then
  not have been the resolution Apple described, and 10-01 becomes a live date again rather than a
  formality.
- **The overhang thesis changes** if the General Court rules on the **€500M** anti-steering appeal —
  in either direction that is a decision day of the kind the reaction table says actually pays, and it
  should be filed as its own calendar entry the moment a hearing or judgment date is published.
- **The entry goes quiet** on 2026-10-01 + close-out, unless one of the above fires first. This is a
  one-shot effective date, not a recurring print.
