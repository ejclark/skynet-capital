## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **This is the one CPI print a year that is a fiscal event before it is a monetary one, and
almost nobody frames it that way.** The September figure is the **statutory** input to the following
April's uprating — gov.uk's own business-rates letter writes the formula out with *"the consumer
prices index for September 2025 = 139.3"* in it, and April 2026's **3.8%** benefits rise was
September 2025's CPI rate exactly. So the number printed **07:00 London on 2026-10-21** mechanically
sets the 2027/28 business-rates multiplier and the April-2027 benefits and triple-lock prices leg —
and it lands **seven days before the 28 October Autumn Budget** the OBR scores. A hot print is a
larger FY2027-28 spending line the Budget must fund, which routes to **gilt supply**, not to Bank
Rate. On the number itself: **less determined than the August print, not more.** September 2026's
annual rate is **2.9%** (July) **plus two** unknown monthly clears, because August 2026 has not
printed yet either — base case **3.0–3.4%**, central **~3.2%**, the Bank's own July-MPR Q4 peak.
The base month is a **0.0% m/m** held there by two exceptional one-offs — air fares **−28.8%**, the
*third-largest September fall since 2001*, and live music **−8.6%** — which is the tilt up. **One
correction on the record:** the 1p fuel-duty rise a sibling lane put in this print **was cancelled**
(gov.uk, 22 May 2026). **None of this is ours to trade** — `symbols: []`, no rates- or sterling-keyed
playbook. Date is **estimate**; it widens caution and licenses nothing.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This id reached the scanner as **two** proposals, not one — `from-boe-decision-2026-11-05` and
`from-uk-cpi-2026-09-16`, both filed 2026-09-09, both read in full before a line of this was written
(per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md), and both are now shadowed by the
canonical `src/domain/market-events/uk-cpi-2026-10-21.json` written in this PR). They agree on the
placement finding: last CPI before the **10-28 Budget** and the **11-05 MPR**, and the last print
that does *not* contain the 1 October VAT cut. That much is settled and carried forward.

So the question this research had to answer is the one neither proposal asked: **the sibling ledger
[`uk-cpi-2026-09-16`](uk-cpi-2026-09-16.md) already owns "UK CPI is not ours to trade" — is there
anything about *this* month specifically that is not just the same argument five weeks later?**

**One-line verdict:** yes, and it is not a monetary story. **September is the UK's statutory
uprating month**, so this print is consumed by the *fiscal* machinery on a legislated formula and
published **seven days before the Budget that scores it** — while, on the number itself, this print
is *less* mechanically determined than its August sibling rather than more, because two monthly
clears sit between it and the last known annual rate.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The September CPI figure is a statutory input, and the statute is quotable.** SUPPORTED,
   **primary and quantified**, for business rates. gov.uk's *"2/2026: Notification of Non-Domestic
   Rating Multipliers for 2026/27"* (published **10 February 2026**, fetched today) sets the
   multiplier from the formula in **paragraph A10 of Schedule 7 to the Local Government Finance Act
   1988**, defining *"C"* as *"the consumer prices index for September 2025 = 139.3"* against
   *"D"* = September 2024 = **134.2**. That ratio is **1.0380** — the September 2025 annual rate to
   the decimal. The 2027/28 multiplier is the same formula with the **2026-10-21** number in it.

2. **The same month drives benefits and the triple lock's prices leg.** MIXED — statute confirmed,
   the September *convention* relayed rather than read. **legislation.gov.uk** (fetched today) gives
   the Social Security Benefits Up-rating Order 2026 under **sections 150, 150A, 151, 151A** of the
   Social Security Administration Act 1992, and s.150 obliges the Secretary of State to review
   whether *"the general level of prices was greater at the end of the period under review than it
   was at the beginning"* — the measure and the review period are **discretionary in the statute**,
   settled as September CPI only by practice. That April 2026 benefits rose **3.8%**, September
   2025's rate exactly, is the corroboration, and it is **search-relayed from the Commons Library
   briefing that 403'd twice today**. This leg is graded MIXED for that reason and for one that
   matters more: a convention a minister may depart from is a weaker mechanism than leg 1's formula,
   and the departure venue is the **28 October Budget**.

3. **The fiscal timing is the finding, and it runs to gilt supply rather than Bank Rate.** SUPPORTED,
   mechanical. Legs 1 and 2 make the 2026-10-21 print a **direct FY2027-28 spending input**; the
   **2026-10-28 Autumn Budget** and its OBR forecast are **seven days later**, and the ONS publishes
   **UK public sector finances the same 07:00** (`uk-public-sector-finances-2026-10-21`, tracked
   here). So one morning hands the Chancellor the uprating cost and the borrowing run-rate together,
   a week before the package is scored. The monetary read is the *quieter* one: the **11-05** MPC
   sees this print, but its own forecast round already carries a Q4 peak, so a print inside that path
   informs the Bank without surprising it, exactly as leg 6 of the August sibling argued.

4. **The base month is a zero whose composition was exceptional — the tilt is up.** SUPPORTED,
   primary. The ONS **September 2025** bulletin (fetched today): *"CPI was unchanged in September
   2025, as in September 2024"*, with the largest upward contribution *"from the transport
   division"* and the *"largest offsetting downward contributions ... from the recreation and
   culture, food and non-alcoholic beverages, and housing and household services divisions"*. Two
   named one-offs did that work: *"Air fares fell by 28.8% between August and September 2025, which
   was the third-largest September decrease since the collection of airfares changed from quarterly
   to monthly in 2001"*, and *"the largest downward effect came from live music, where monthly prices
   fell by 8.6% compared with a rise of 5.8% a year ago"*. **The honest counter, kept:** a 0.0% m/m
   is *not itself* anomalous — September 2024 was also 0.0%. The claim here is about composition,
   not level: a merely-normal 2026 September needs neither a record air-fare fall nor a live-music
   collapse to repeat, and either shortfall pushes the monthly rate above zero.

5. **The air-fare base effect is LARGER here than in the August print, and it lands inside services.**
   SUPPORTED on mechanism, two-sided on outcome — **this is the ledger's analytical finding**. Air
   fares rose **11.7%** m/m in July 2026 against **30.2%** a year earlier (ONS July 2026, fetched):
   2026's summer spike is roughly a third of 2025's, so there is roughly a third of the altitude to
   give back. The sibling ledger applies that to August; **September 2025's give-back was −28.8%, a
   record-adjacent fall**, which makes the September comparison the bigger one. Air transport sits
   **inside services**, so the same push lifts the line the MPC reads. **The honest other side:**
   the mechanism is symmetric only if 2026's fares *tracked* the shallower spike down — if carriers
   discount hard into a weak autumn, September 2026 could fall as far as 2025 did on a lower base,
   and this leg produces nothing.

6. **Energy contributes nothing to the monthly rate, by construction.** SUPPORTED, primary. Ofgem's
   cap steps **1 January, 1 April, 1 July, 1 October**; the next step is **1 October 2026** (Ofgem's
   26 August release, fetched today: a **4%** rise for a typical direct-debit household, electricity
   **26.32p/kWh**, gas **7.97p/kWh**). Between the July and September collections there is **no cap
   change at all**, so gas — still carrying **14.7%** y/y from the July step, which took housing and
   household services from **2.7%** to **4.1%** — is flat m/m while its y/y carries over unchanged.
   This removes the single largest source of monthly variance from the print.

7. **The 1p fuel-duty rise a sibling lane put in this print does not exist.** REFUTED, primary, and
   this leg is a **correction on the record**. The `uk-cpi-2026-09-16` proposal note reads *"fuel
   duty rose 1p on 2026-09-01 (about 1.2p at the pump with VAT)"*. gov.uk's *"Amended Fuel Duty
   rates: 2026 to 2027"* (published **22 May 2026**, fetched today) extends *"the temporary 5 pence
   per litre (ppl) cut in the rates of Fuel Duty ... until 31 December 2026"*, holds petrol and
   heavy oil at **0.5295/l**, and supersedes the previous plan of *"reducing the cut by 1 ppl on 1
   September 2026, 2 ppl on 1 December 2026"*. The real steps are **0.5595 on 2027-01-01** and
   **0.5795 on 2027-03-01**. **What survives of the fuel argument:** petrol at ~**162p/l** in August
   on a **$99** Brent (press-cited by sibling ledgers) is still a live upward push through the
   *price*, not the duty — and Sep-2025's own largest upward contributor was transport, including
   motor fuels, so the m/m comparison is genuinely two-sided here rather than a clean tilt.

8. **The dated disinflation lands in the NEXT print, not this one.** SUPPORTED, primary and
   issuer-quantified. gov.uk zero-rated VAT on domestic electricity from **1 October** to **31 March
   2027**, *"estimated to reduce CPI inflation by around 0.10 percentage points"*, and Ofgem's 26
   August release confirms the mechanics from its own side: *"The government has removed VAT from
   electricity bills from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027"*, so *"VAT is not included in electricity
   bills for people covered under the energy price cap between 1 October and 31 December 2026"*
   while gas stays at **5%**. October prices publish **2026-11-18** — **after** the 05 November MPC
   vote. **The consequence leg 3 makes new:** the uprating figure is therefore set on a
   **pre-VAT-cut, energy-inflated** month. Benefits, the triple lock and business rates are indexed
   at close to the cycle peak, one month before a known, dated, officially-sized step down.

9. **This print is less determined than its August sibling, and saying so is the honest correction to
   a tempting frame.** SUPPORTED, arithmetic. The August ledger reduced its print to *one*
   subtraction because July's **2.9%** was known and August 2025's **+0.3%** m/m was known. Here the
   chain is **2.9% + (Aug-26 m/m − 0.3) + (Sep-26 m/m − 0.0)** — the August 2026 clear is *itself*
   unpublished until 2026-09-16. So the base case is stated **wider** (**3.0–3.4%**, central
   **~3.2%**) than the sibling's 3.0–3.2%, and half that width resolves on 09-16 at zero cost. A
   ledger that copied the sibling's confidence forward would be borrowing certainty it does not have.

10. **No tracked symbol carries a sterling or UK-inflation channel — and no play is proposed.**
    SUPPORTED, and the null claim is stated as a claim rather than a hedge. `symbols: []`. The house
    playbooks (S1/S2/E1/S3/S4 + G1, [`trade-playbooks.md`](../../plans/trade-playbooks.md)) are
    equity- and earnings-keyed; none is rates-keyed, let alone keyed to a foreign CPI release. Rate
    pricing is unmoved by this print's existence: centralbank.watch (fetched today, data as-of
    **2026-09-08**) has the **09-17** meeting at **hold 93.1% · hike 6.9% · cut 0.0%**, implied
    **3.75%**; the **11-05** meeting's **54.2% hike / 45.9% hold**, implied **3.89%**, is
    **relayed from the [`boe-decision-2026-11-05`](boe-decision-2026-11-05.md) ledger's own
    same-day fetch**, not re-fetched here — that page served only the next meeting. The impact tier
    was reconsidered against `medium` on leg 3's fiscal argument and **left at `low`**: a UK-only
    print with no instrument in this book does not earn a tighter cadence than the decision it feeds.

aggregator**, not a cross-checked strip. The date stays **estimate** despite being read off the ONS
release page, per this lane's no-self-confirm limit. Finally, leg 10 is an argument that *nothing*

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `estimate`):** **stand aside completely, and re-frame this print from a monetary
event into a fiscal one.** The 2026-10-21 release is the **statutory uprating month** — gov.uk's
business-rates letter writes *"the consumer prices index for September 2025 = 139.3"* into a
Schedule 7 formula, and April 2026's benefits rose **3.8%**, September 2025's rate exactly — so the

public finances. That is the channel that matters and it runs to **gilt supply**, not Bank Rate.
Three further positions, all analytical. First, **the base case is deliberately wider than the
sibling's**: **3.0–3.4%**, central **~3.2%** (the Bank's own July-MPR Q4 peak), because this print
is **2.9% plus two** unknown monthly clears and half that width resolves free on **2026-09-16**.
Second, **the tilt is up on composition**: September 2025's 0.0% m/m was held there by a **−28.8%**
air-fare fall (third-largest September decrease since 2001) and a **−8.6%** live-music one-off, and

services**, where the MPC reads it. Third, **the disinflation is dated and lands next**: the
**−0.10pp** VAT step first appears in the October print published **2026-11-18**, *after* the 05
November vote, which means the uprating figure is fixed on a **pre-VAT-cut, energy-inflated** month.
One correction is carried on the record: **there is no fuel-duty rise in this print** (gov.uk,
22 May 2026). This book holds nothing with a sterling or UK-inflation channel and none is proposed.
Estimates widen caution and license nothing.
