## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This calendar now tracks three central-bank meeting accounts. Two have ledgers and both refuse the
same way: the [ECB account](ecb-account-2026-10-08.md) is *"four weeks stale by construction"* and
measured at 1.15× an ordinary Euro Stoxx session, and [FOMC minutes](fomc-minutes-2026-12-30.md) are
three weeks stale with an n=3 record inside ±0.3%. This entry was filed during the
[Tokyo-flash](japan-cpi-tokyo-flash-2027-03-26.md) adjacency sweep on the reasoning that its 03-29
slot is an **attribution hazard** worth naming. So the question is not "is a central-bank account
tradable" — two siblings answered that — but the narrower one this venue actually raises: **does the
staleness refusal even apply to a BoJ Summary of Opinions, which publishes far faster than either,
and if it does not, is the document a market event on its own terms?**

**One-line verdict: the staleness refusal does *not* apply — the BoJ's account is the fastest of the
three majors at a median 10-day lag — and the release is measurably a non-event anyway, on the S&P
(0.84× an ordinary session), on the Nikkei (0.96× trimmed) and on the yen (1.09× against the
decision's 1.83×), with a 48%/50% coin-flip on direction.** The stance is stand aside. What this
session adds beyond the refusal is two structural facts that were in neither the calendar note nor
any sibling: **a second, fuller BoJ document lands five days earlier (the Jan-MPM Minutes,
2027-03-24)**, and the release time is **08:50 JST = 19:50 EDT the previous day**, which relocates
the whole attribution question one session earlier than the note assumed.

**Method:** sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md), plus a

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The staleness refusal the sibling ledgers lean on does not apply here — REFUTED as an inherited
   argument, from the complete population.** Every SoO the BoJ has ever published was parsed today
   from its own year archives, and the publication-minus-meeting-day-2 lag is:

   | Lag (days) | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | 11 | 12 | 13 | 14 | 15 | 21 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | Count | 1 | 26 | 10 | 19 | 14 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 1 | 1 |

   **n=86, median 10, mean 10.05.** The single 21-day observation is the **inaugural** edition
   (meeting 2015-12-18, published 2016-01-08); excluding it the range is a tight **7-15 days**, mean
   9.92, n=85. Against that: the Fed's own convention, quoted verbatim in the
   [fomc-minutes-2026-12-30 ledger](fomc-minutes-2026-12-30.md), is *"three weeks after the date of
   the policy decision"* (**21 days**), and the ECB's, quoted verbatim in the
   [ecb-account ledger](ecb-account-2026-10-08.md), is *"typically published four weeks after the
   meetings"* (**~28 days**). **The BoJ's account is the fastest of the three majors — under half the
   Fed's lag and roughly a third of the ECB's.** Whatever refuses this release, it is not staleness.
   The 2027 case is ordinary: **Mar 18 → Mar 29 is 11 days**, inside the observed band.

2. **The release is a non-event for a US equity book, and it is measurably *quieter* than an ordinary
   session — SUPPORTED, measured, and this is the number this calendar exists to produce.** The SoO
   publishes 08:50 JST (leg 4), i.e. the evening **before** the US calendar date it carries, so the
   first US cash session to see it is that same calendar date. On FRED `SP500`, fetched today:

   | Sample | n | mean \|move\| | median \|move\| | vs baseline |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | All sessions 2016-09-06 → 2026-09-04 | 2,513 | **0.736%** | 0.498% | — |
   | First US session after a SoO | **80** | **0.620%** | 0.402% | **0.84× / 0.81×** |

   Not merely "not a catalyst" — **below** an ordinary session on both the mean and the median.
   **7 of 80** reached 2× baseline, and the tail is American, not Japanese: the largest,
   **2024-01-31 (−1.61%)**, was an **FOMC decision day** — verified against the Fed's own calendar
   parsed today, which shows **three** SoO dates colliding with an FOMC decision in its 2021-2027
   panels (**2021-07-28, 2022-01-26, 2024-01-31**). The other four ≥2× days (2022-10-03 +2.59%,
   2020-06-24 −2.59%, 2024-08-08 +2.30%, 2025-03-28 −1.97%) are not attributed here: this session did
   not read the tape for them, and an unsourced narrative would be worse than the gap.

3. **In Tokyo the same null holds once three global shocks are removed — SUPPORTED, measured, with
   the trimming stated rather than hidden.** On FRED `NIKKEI225`, baseline 2016+ is **0.949%** mean
   |move| (n=2,609); SoO days are **1.147%** (n=86) = **1.21×**. That headline is driven by three
   observations, all of which are famous exogenous events that happened to fall on an SoO date:

   | Date | Nikkei | What the session actually was |
   |---|---|---|
   | 2020-03-25 | **+8.04%** | COVID-crash rebound |
   | 2016-06-24 | **−7.92%** | Brexit referendum result |
   | 2016-11-10 | **+6.72%** | session after the 2016 US election |

   **Drop those three and SoO days run 0.915% = 0.96× baseline** — again below an ordinary day. The
   median never moved much either way (**0.724% vs 0.668%, 1.08×**). This replicates the ECB
   ledger's own finding on a different central bank, a different instrument and a population **6.6×
   larger**: an account day looks eventful only when a global shock lands on it.

4. **The release time is 08:50 JST, and that relocates the whole attribution question one session
   earlier than this event's note assumes — SUPPORTED, primary, and it is a correction.** The BoJ
   schedule page carries, verbatim: *"Note: The time of release will be, in principle, as follows.
   … **Summary of Opinions** It will be released at **8:50 a.m.** … **MPM Minutes** It will be
   released at 8:50 a.m."* Two consequences. **(a)** 08:50 JST is **10 minutes before the 09:00 JST
   Tokyo cash open**, so the Nikkei's same-day close-to-close bar contains the release in full and
   nothing else Japanese precedes it — which is why leg 3 is the clean measurement and not a
   convenience. **(b)** 08:50 JST Monday **2027-03-29** = **19:50 EDT Sunday 2027-03-28** (JST UTC+9,
   EDT UTC−4). The event's own calendar note frames 03-29 as *"the FIRST US SESSION after a four-day
   gap"*; the release actually lands **the evening before that session**, roughly two hours into the
   CME Globex Sunday reopen. The note's conclusion (an attribution hazard) survives; its mechanism
   needed fixing.

5. **The account carries no directional information from its own meeting — SUPPORTED, measured, and
   it is the cleanest kill in this document.** If the SoO revealed how contested or how hawkish the
   meeting was, the account day would tend to extend the decision day's move. Pairing each of the 86
   releases with its own meeting:

   | Test | Nikkei 225 | USD/JPY (H.10) |
   |---|---|---|
   | Same-direction as decision day | **41/86 = 48%** | **42/84 = 50%** |
   | Signed correlation | **+0.142** | **−0.069** |
   | corr(\|decision move\|, \|SoO move\|) | +0.192 | +0.110 |

   **A coin flip on both instruments.** A split by decision-day size does show larger SoO moves after
   the most eventful decisions (Nikkei 1.491% top tercile vs 1.027% bottom), but that is what
   volatility clustering looks like — eventful decisions sit in high-volatility regimes and so do the
   ten days after them — and it is reported here as a caveat rather than promoted to a finding.

6. **Where the BoJ event actually lives is the yen, and the account keeps about an eighth of it —
