## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Most of this number already exists, and it is sitting in a public index today.** September
2025 stands at **102.7** in the live ONS volume index — the **highest single month of the whole 2025
calendar year** — and July 2026 stands at **103.8**. That pair alone fixes **+1.07pp** of the September
2026 annual rate; only **two monthly clears** are still unknown, and those clears have run a mean of
**+0.25%** with a **1.21pp** standard deviation across 29 observations since 2024. Base case: volume
**y/y +0.2% to +2.2%, centred ~+1.1%**, against **+1.6%** in July. **That deceleration is base
arithmetic, not a consumer contraction** — and the way to tell the two apart is the three-month figure
in the same bulletin, which the same arithmetic says stays **positive**. To *hold* July's rate the
index would have to print **104.4**, above June 2026's 104.3 and the **highest since April 2022**, in a

### The question, plainly

This entry arrived by **proposal**. The [`uk-ppi-2026-10-21`](uk-ppi-2026-10-21.md) lane filed

volumes). That proposal was read in full before anything here was written, per
[`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md), and the canonical

proposal also left this research an explicit instruction — *"not verified against a
September-reference-month cadence listing… its own never-assessed research should reproduce the
cadence before relying on it"* — which Leg 1 does.

The question this document actually answers is narrower than "what will UK retail sales do":

1. **How much of the September 2026 annual rate can be computed today, and how much cannot?**
2. **Does this print have a forward proxy?** Its UK PPI sibling concluded of its services half that
   *"there isn't one"*. Retail sales appears to have one. Is it any good?

**One-line verdict:** **the base is the forecast.** September 2025 is the highest month of calendar
2025 in the live index, which fixes **+1.07pp** of the annual rate and makes a deceleration from July's
+1.6% the default outcome without any consumer doing anything — and the three-month figure in the same
bulletin is what separates that arithmetic from an actual contraction. The proxy exists and is
**measured to be a sign indicator, not a level one**.

### Leg 3 — "The September annual rate is a forecast." **REFUTED for most of it. Two-thirds of the distance is already on the shelf.**

The annual rate decomposes exactly:

> **index(Sep 26) / index(Sep 25) − 1** = **[ index(Jul 26) / index(Sep 25) − 1 ]** (the *carry*,
> already determined) **+ [ index(Sep 26) / index(Jul 26) − 1 ]** (the *two-month clear*, unknown)

From `J5EK`, fetched and recomputed here:

| Month | Index | Month | Index |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 Aug | 101.6 | 2026 Mar | 103.2 |
| **2025 Sep** | **102.7** | 2026 Apr | 102.3 |
| 2025 Oct | 101.9 | 2026 May | 103.6 |
| 2025 Nov | 101.5 | 2026 Jun | **104.3** |
| 2025 Dec | 101.6 | **2026 Jul** | **103.8** |
| 2026 Jan | 103.5 | | |
| 2026 Feb | 102.7 | | |

**Carry = 103.8 / 102.7 − 1 = +1.07pp.** Fixed, subject only to revision (Leg 5).

**The clear** is the whole remaining question, and it is close to a random walk. Across the 29
two-month clears since January 2024 the mean is **+0.25%** with a standard deviation of **1.21pp**
(range **−1.86%** to **+3.03%**); the 30 single-month clears over the same window run mean **+0.12%**,
standard deviation **1.08pp**. Unconditionally that gives:

- Centre: 1.07 + 0.25 = **+1.32% y/y**
- ±1 standard deviation: **+0.12% to +2.53%**

Leg 6 tilts the centre down. Two consequences fall straight out of the arithmetic and are the
substance of this ledger:

**First, the base is the hardest of the year, and that is checkable rather than rhetorical.**
September 2025's **102.7** is the highest single month of calendar 2025 — every other month of that
year sits between 99.1 and 102.1. The September 2025 bulletin said so in its own words at the time:
*"the fourth consecutive monthly rise and bringing volumes to their highest level since July 2022."*
A deceleration from July's +1.6% is therefore the **default**, not a call about the consumer.

**Second, holding July's rate would require an extraordinary level.** +1.67% on a 102.7 base needs an
index of **104.4**. The 2026 high so far is June's **104.3**; the last month above 104.4 anywhere in
the series is **April 2022 (104.8)**, fifty-three months ago. That is the shape of the kill switch in
**FT-uk-retail-sales-2026-10-23-1**.

### Leg 4 — "This print has no forward proxy, like the PPI services half." **REFUTED — but the proxy is a sign indicator, not a level one, and that is measured.**

The BRC-KPMG Retail Sales Monitor covers the same economy weeks before the ONS. Its own landing page
claims, verbatim, that it is *"published at least two weeks ahead of official statistics, thus
providing unparalleled and timely insight into current market conditions."* The August edition (BRC's
own ungated page, fetched) covers **2–29 August 2026**: **total UK retail sales +0.7% y/y**, food
**+2.6%**, non-food **−0.8%**, online non-food **−0.2%**, with the BRC's lead economist calling it
*"a disappointing month for retail sales."*

BRC reports **value**; ONS reports **volume**. The natural bridge is BRC's own **Shop Price Index**.
Subtracting it gives a BRC-implied volume series, which can be set against the ONS one:

| Month | BRC total, value y/y | BRC shop price y/y | BRC-implied volume | ONS volume y/y (J5EK) | Wedge |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Apr 2026 | **−3.0%** *(Easter-distorted; BRC's own Mar+Apr combination is +1.5%)* | 1.0% | −4.0% | **+0.20%** | **+4.2pp** |
| May 2026 | +3.7% | 1.2% | +2.5% | **+3.39%** | **+0.9pp** |
| Jun 2026 | +1.9% | 1.2% | +0.7% | **+3.78%** | **+3.1pp** |
| Jul 2026 | +1.3% | 0.9% | +0.4% | **+1.67%** | **+1.3pp** |
| Aug 2026 | **+0.7%** | **1.5%** | **−0.8%** | *publishes 2026-09-18* | — |

*(BRC total and shop-price figures for April, May and June are trade-press secondary — no BRC primary
page for those months was reached today. August's are BRC's own. ONS volumes are computed here from
`J5EK`.)*

The wedge averages **+2.4pp**, or **+1.8pp** excluding the Easter-distorted April, and its range even
excluding April is **0.9 to 3.1pp**. Direction of change agrees in **2 of 3** month-pairs. At that
sample size the honest reading is: **the monitor tells you the sign and the mood, and nothing
reliable about the level.** Four structural reasons it cannot be tighter, all of them known rather
than inferred: BRC covers its member panel (large retailers) while ONS covers all GB retailers *and*
automotive fuel; BRC runs a 4/5-week retail calendar against ONS's calendar month; BRC is
**not seasonally adjusted** and ONS is; and the shop-price basket is not the sales mix it is being
used to deflate.

The consequence for this ledger is a *usable* one rather than a shrug. The September monitor, due in
the first week of October, is the only forward observation this print has — and it should be read the
way its measured accuracy permits: as confirmation or contradiction of the **direction** the base
arithmetic already implies. **FT-uk-retail-sales-2026-10-23-2** puts that on the record as a
prediction rather than a caveat.

### Leg 6 — "August's weakness carries into September." **MIXED — the till says cooling, the survey says the opposite, and the reason given for the cooling has an expiry date.**

**The till, cooling.** BRC's August: total **+0.7%** against a 12-month average of **1.6%** and **3.1%**
in August 2025; non-food **−0.8%**; on the like-for-like measure **+0.5%**, described in secondary
coverage as *"the weakest growth since October 2024."* BRC's stated cause is a **pull-forward**:
unusually warm weather from May onwards brought seasonal purchases forward, leaving less demand later
in the summer, alongside rising household bills.

**The survey, warming.** GfK's Consumer Confidence Barometer for August (published 21 August 2026,
secondary coverage): headline **−14**, up three points, its **highest in two years** and well above
**−25** in April; the major-purchase measure up five to **−7**, its best since **December 2021**;
personal finances for the next twelve months up three to **+4**. Only the backward-looking general
economy measure fell, to **−40**.

These genuinely disagree, and it is worth saying which is the better instrument for *this* number
rather than pretending they reconcile. The till measures spending that happened; the barometer
measures stated intent, and its major-purchase component in particular has a long record of leading
nothing in particular. **This ledger weights the till** — but only to a **−0.2pp** tilt on the centre,
from the unconditional **+1.32%** to **~+1.1%**, because Leg 4 measured the till-proxy's wedge at
0.9–4.2pp and a proxy that loose cannot carry a large tilt.

Two further conditions, both checked and both close to neutral:

- **Weather.** It is the mechanism BRC names in nearly every recent monitor, and the Met Office
  outlook fetched today reads near-average temperatures in early September with short warm spells in
  the south, then a changeable regime from 11 September. There is no repeat of the May–August heat
  anomaly in that outlook, and no cold shock either. Neutral, and — since the pull-forward BRC
  describes was *drawn from* those hot months — the drag it created is by construction fading rather
  than compounding.
- **The Budget.** The Autumn Budget is **2026-10-28**, **five days after** this print. No Budget
  measure touches the September reference month. Only pre-Budget *speculation* can, through
  confidence; the speculation is real (Chancellor John Healey; tax rises not ruled out at PMQs on
  2 September; UK borrowing costs at their highest since 2008 in early September — all secondary),
  but its effect on September volumes is **unquantified here and is not in the band**. Named as a
  limit, not folded in.

### Honest limits

- **The BRC April/May/June figures are secondary.** BRC's own pages for those months were not reached
  today; the totals and shop-price rates come from trade-press coverage. August is BRC primary. The
  wedge in Leg 4 therefore has **one primary month and three secondary ones**, and it is the softest
  input in this document.
- **n = 4 on the wedge, and one of the four is Easter-broken.** BRC itself says the April figure is a
  calendar artefact. Excluding it leaves **three** clean months. Every quantitative claim about the
  proxy in Leg 4 rests on that sample and should not be stated more firmly than it is.
- **The BRC September monitor has no sourced date.** BRC's page states only *"at least two weeks ahead
  of official statistics."* The first-week-of-October expectation is inferred from the August edition's
  own publication, not from a schedule — so **no calendar entry was filed for it**, on the same
  refusal the US retail-sales lane made twice about an unsourced Amazon event date.
- **The clear is treated as a random walk, and it is not quite one.** The 29-observation mean and
  standard deviation ignore any autocorrelation, any seasonal residual left in the SA series, and the
  fact that 2024–26 spans an unusual level trajectory. It is a defensible first-order band and nothing
  more.
