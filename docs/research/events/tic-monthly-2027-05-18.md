## Initial research

### The question

[`tic-monthly-2027-04-15`](tic-monthly-2027-04-15.md) proposed this event to do a specific job: be the
**pure control** for `FT-tic-monthly-2027-01-19-3`'s revision-edition effect, after establishing that the
contested axis is the control class and that an April observation cannot reach it. So: is a May edition
actually a pure control — and does its corridor permit the tape test April's did?

### One-line verdict

**No, and no.** May is the one month Treasury's own note (d) singles out as a survey-carrying release, which
makes it a treatment-suspect on the exact mechanism the dispute is about; and its scored session carries the
FOMC minutes, which is April's structural cleanliness read one calendar step later.

### Leg 3 — "a pure ORDINARY edition" · **REFUTED**, on the primary the series has already quoted twice

The proposal's stated job for this event, verbatim:

> *"May 2027 is a PURE ordinary edition — neither a note (c) revision month, nor a quarterly-cut month…
> nor a February or March vintage. That makes it a cleaner control observation than the Jun/Sep/Dec
> editions FT-tic-monthly-2027-03-18-2 already watches."*

Each of the three exclusions is true. The conclusion does not follow, because there is a fourth category
nobody checked, and Treasury names it in the same note the February sibling used to retire a mechanism.
**Note (d), verbatim from the page this session fetched:**

> *"Beginning December 2011, the data are based on the monthly Form SLT data, and revised as described in
> (c) above. Before September 2011, revised data back to the previous June was usually released in
> **March** to include preliminary data from the annual survey of foreign holdings of U.S. securities at
> end-June of the previous year. **The May release usually included the final results from that survey.**"*

**Two sentences, and this series has only ever used the first.** [`tic-monthly-2027-02-18`](tic-monthly-2027-02-18.md)
quoted the March clause to date the annual-survey mechanism to *before* September 2011.
[`tic-monthly-2027-04-15`](tic-monthly-2027-04-15.md) quoted both clauses and then proposed **May** as the
pure control four paragraphs later. The May clause names the month it was choosing.

**Why that matters here, rather than being a debating point.** The exclusion under dispute is not free-standing
— it has a stated mechanism. The January sibling's grounds for dropping the Feb/Mar vintages were *"footnote
9's series break and the **preliminary annual survey**'s benchmark feed."* If that feed is real post-2011, its
**other half** lands on the **final** survey, and note (d) says the final survey's release month is **May**.
**2027-05-18 stands 18 days after `tic-annual-survey-final-2027-04-30`.** So the event proposed as the class
that is definitionally outside the mechanism is the single strongest candidate *inside* it.

**And the sequencing is the right way round this time**, which is worth stating explicitly given the series'
record. The April sibling's proposal failed on sequence — 04-15 precedes the 04-30 survey and cannot carry
it. 05-18 **follows** it by 18 days, so the benchmark could reach this edition. Three sessions have now
misread this table in three different ways (column, order, and here a *clause*); the correction that
generalises is: **read the whole note, not the sentence that supports the point.**

**What the existing panel already says, stated as inference and not as measurement.** The three excluded
outliers are **2024-02, 2025-03, 2026-03** — no May vintage among them, and the panel spans 2024-01 → 2026-08,
which contains three May vintages. If those are in the ordinary group, the final-survey feed has already
failed three times and note (d)'s May clause is dead post-2011 exactly as its March clause is. **This
session cannot confirm which vintages are in that group** (leg 8), so it is registered forward rather than
claimed — **FT-tic-monthly-2027-05-18-2**, two-sided.

**One more thing the arithmetic says, and it deflates the proposal's premise independently.** Even taking
"pure control" at face value, one such observation is weak. The two readings are `n=10` (Feb/Mar excluded)
and `n=13` (the same ten **plus** the three disputed) — so a **new non-Feb/Mar ordinary vintage enters
both**, and moves both means the same way:

| Outcome of one new ordinary vintage | P under "Feb/Mar is a distinct mechanism" | P under "one population, Feb/Mar is the right tail" | likelihood ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| **small** (like the other ten) | ≈1 | 10/13 ≈ 0.77 | **1.30 : 1** |
| **Feb/Mar-scale** | ≈0 | 3/13 ≈ 0.23 | **decisive against the exclusion** |

**The test is one-sided.** The likely branch buys a likelihood ratio of **1.3**, so about **nine** such
observations would be needed to move a 50/50 prior to 90/10 — at six non-Feb/Mar ordinary editions a year,
a year and a half of releases, and only if a session can rebuild the panel, which four have now failed to do.
The *informative* branch is the large one — which is the treatment reading above. So the honest version of
this event's job is not "one more control"; it is **the final survey's test**, and the pure-control framing
was over-promising the observation it would produce.
