## At a glance

**TL;DR.** Germany sells **EUR 2.5bn of 30-year Bund on 2026-09-16**, the morning of the FOMC's
September decision. **Stand aside — and this time the reason is a null on both sides of the trade.**
Across **213** German 30Y auction dates since 2003, a soft auction moved the US 30Y CMT by
**−0.04bp** more than a firm one (**t = −0.06**) and Germany's *own* 30Y by **+0.35bp**
(**t = +0.66**); the sharper cut — the **47** dates where total bids came in **below the offered
volume** — moves the Bund 30Y **+1.04bp** (t = +1.60) and the US 30Y **+0.25bp** (t = +0.30). The
auction is not a price event even at home. Two primary-sourced findings reframe it. **(1) The
"failed Bund auction" headline is a mechanical artifact** — Germany runs a *multi-price* auction and
**retains** part of every issue for secondary-market use (Finanzagentur's own table: 21.5% of
auction volume in 2025), so bids under the offer happened on **22.1%** of 30Y dates since 2003 and
on **3 of 16** in 2026. **(2) Germany is the mirror image of Japan** — 30Y gross auction supply went
**EUR 11.4bn (2022) → 32.5 → 27.5 → 37.0bn (2025)**, roughly a tripling, while
[`jgb-20y-auction-2026-09-15`](jgb-20y-auction-2026-09-15.md) measured Japan *cutting* its super-long
30–40%. Date **estimate**; it widens caution and licenses nothing.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This entry reached the calendar sideways. It was proposed on 2026-09-08 during the
[`treasury-30y-bond-2026-09-10`](treasury-30y-bond-2026-09-10.md) adjacency sweep — the same sweep
that filed [`jgb-20y-auction-2026-09-15`](jgb-20y-auction-2026-09-15.md) one row earlier — while
testing whether that ledger's "synchronized global long-end" leg was narrative or calendar. It is
calendar, and it is now three sovereigns inside two sessions. So the question is not "will the
auction go well." It is: **a third sovereign sells long duration inside the same 48 hours, on the
Fed's own decision day — does that change anything for a US AI-equity book, and if not, what is the
entry for?**

**One-line verdict:** the spillover is a **measured null on both legs** — the auction result does not
move the US 30-year (t = −0.06) and barely moves Germany's own (t = +0.66) — so the entry is an
**attribution and expectation instrument**; and the two things worth knowing about the German long
end are that its auctions are *designed* to look undersubscribed, and that its supply has roughly
tripled while Japan's was being cut.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The date, size and structure of the auction — SUPPORTED, and one thing is genuinely unfixed.**
The Q3 2026 table carries two lines dated **16.09.2026**: `Bund 30 Y (R)` at **EUR 1.0bn** with the
ISIN shown as `-`, and `Bund (R)` **DE000BU2D012**, coupon **2.90%**, maturity **15.08.2056**, at
**EUR 1.5bn**. The auction-process page explains the dash: *"For the multi-ISIN auctions in the 15-
and 30-year maturity segment, the benchmark bonds to be reopened are not finalised until the
quarterly calendars are updated. The second bond … [is] only announced in a press notice in the week
before the auction."* 2026-09-16 is a **Wednesday**, which is when Germany auctions nominal Bunds of
7 years or more — the calendar is internally consistent.

This is the opposite of the JGB sibling's setup and worth one sentence of process. Japan's terms were
**fully fixed at D-7**; Germany's are **half-fixed at D-7** and complete only at D-4. The rule is
explicit — *"From 2024, auctions and multi-ISIN auctions will be announced four bank business days
before the auction date by press notice"* — and it is observable: the 09-09 Bund reopening was
announced on **2026-09-03**, exactly four bank business days ahead. Four bank business days before
Wednesday 09-16 is **Thursday 2026-09-10**, and as of this session the press index's newest issuance
notices are dated 09-08. That makes the ledger's week-horizon call a *dated deliverable* rather than
a price view.

**2. There is no measurable Bund-auction → US-long-end spillover — REFUTED as a tradeable signal, and
weaker than the JGB null.** This is the leg the entry exists to test. Method: every 30-year German
auction date from **2003-01-01 to 2026-08-26** (213 dates, aggregating the multi-ISIN lines to one
date-level bid-to-offer = total bids ÷ total offered), matched to the **same-date** change in the US
30Y CMT and in the Bundesbank 30Y. Same date, because bidding closes 05:30 ET — before the US cash
open, so the same US session is the first that could react.

| Split (median bid-to-offer = 1.32) | n | US 30Y same-date | Bund 30Y same-date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soft (below median) | 103 / 106 | **−0.55bp** | **−0.06bp** |
| Firm (at or above) | 101 / 107 | **−0.51bp** | **−0.41bp** |
| Difference | — | **−0.04bp** (SE 0.69, **t = −0.06**) | **+0.35bp** (SE 0.54, **t = +0.66**) |

The sharper cut — the headline-generating case — is the one where total bids came in *below* the
offered volume:

| Split | n | US 30Y same-date | Bund 30Y same-date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bids **<** offer | 47 | −0.34bp | **+0.57bp** |
| Bids **≥** offer | 157 / 166 | −0.59bp | −0.46bp |
| Difference | — | **+0.25bp** (t = **+0.30**) | **+1.04bp** (t = **+1.60**) |

Correlation of bid-to-offer against the same-date move is **+0.046** on the US 30Y (t = 0.65) and
**+0.012** on the Bund 30Y (t = 0.17), against daily standard deviations of **4.94bp** and
**3.90bp**. Read it honestly: the *only* measure that gets near a signal is a genuinely
undersubscribed auction moving **Germany's own** 30-year by about **1bp**, at t = 1.60 — sub-noise,
domestically contained, and invisible across the Atlantic. **Verdict: no tradeable spillover, and no
domestic one either.** Registered as `FT-bund-30y-auction-2026-09-16-1`.

**3. The FOMC-day collision is the base rate, and the "the Fed will swamp it" premise is not
supported by the daily tape — MIXED, and this corrects the proposal that created this entry.** The
proposal's own note asserted that "the FOMC dominates any Bund spillover on that date." Tested:
**25 of the 213** German 30Y auction dates since 2003 (**11.7%**) fell on an FOMC decision day,
including **2026-03-18** and **2026-06-17** this year, and **2025-09-17** — the same bond,
DE000BU2D012, on the same September FOMC setup one year earlier. On those 25 dates the US 30Y CMT's
mean absolute move was **3.17bp**, *smaller* than the **3.87bp** on the 181 non-FOMC auction dates.
Widening to every session: US 30Y mean |Δ| is **4.51bp** on FOMC days vs **4.06bp** otherwise
(1.11x) since 2003, and **4.13bp vs 4.37bp** (0.95x) since 2022. The Bundesbank 30Y shows **1.00x**.

So the honest statement is not "the Fed drowns out the auction." It is that **neither event reliably
moves the 30-year by more than an ordinary day's noise**, which lands the stand-aside call from a
different direction than the proposal assumed. The limit is real and stated below: the CMT is a
single daily reading, so this measures the *net* day, not the intraday path a 2:00pm ET statement
obviously produces.

**4. A "failed Bund auction" is a mechanical artifact of the German format — SUPPORTED, and it is the
finding that changes how the 09-16 result should be read.** Germany runs a **multi-price** auction
(bids fill at their own price, not a single stop), and the Finanzagentur *"withholds a certain
nominal volume at each auction (retention quote), which can be gradually released to the market after
the tender process as part of secondary market activities."* Its own published table, in per-cent of
auction volume:

| 2025 | 2024 | 2023 | 2022 | 2021 | 2020 | 2019 | 2018 | 2017 | 2016 | 2015 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **21.5** | 17.0 | 17.0 | 20.3 | 17.1 | 20.8 | 22.1 | 19.4 | 20.7 | 21.5 | 18.4 |

(The page's prose says retention "has been below 20% on average since 2006"; its own table puts 2025
at 21.5%. The table is cited here, not the prose.) Restricting to the 30-year segment and computing
from the workbook gives **14.7% (2022) · 18.5 · 16.6 · 18.9 · 22.0% (2026 YTD)**, and the mechanical
consequence: **47 of 213** 30Y auction dates since 2003 — **22.1%** — took total bids below the
offered volume, including **31–32%** of dates in 2003-2014 and 2015-2021 and **3 of 16** in 2026
(04-07, 06-17, 08-12). **A wire headline calling the 09-16 auction "uncovered" would be describing
the format, not the demand.** The number that carries information is the *allotment*: at the 2022-26
30Y retention rate of **18.6%**, EUR 2.5bn offered implies about **EUR 2.04bn** allotted. Registered
as `FT-bund-30y-auction-2026-09-16-2`.

**5. Germany is the mirror image of Japan on super-long supply — SUPPORTED, and it is the cross-ledger
finding.** [`jgb-20y-auction-2026-09-15`](jgb-20y-auction-2026-09-15.md) measured Japan cutting its

| 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 YTD (to 08-26) |

| 14.9 | 14.0 | 13.1 | **11.4** | 32.5 | 27.5 | **37.0** | **25.8** |

25.8bn (2026)**. Auction *lines* went from 13 a year to 33. **Two sovereigns met the same
generational-high long end with opposite issuance responses** — Japan shrank the auction until it
cleared, Germany tripled it and absorbed the slack through the retention quote. That is why the JGB

### What plays the conditions support

**None.** The refusal is correct four times over: no tracked symbol has euro-rates exposure, no house
playbook is rates-keyed, the transatlantic channel was tested and returned **t = −0.06**, and the
domestic channel returned **t = +1.60** on a 1bp effect. The event's value is entirely diagnostic —

### Honest limits

- **The spillover test is daily, not intraday.** The US CMT is one reading per day, so leg 3
  measures the *net* session. A 2:00pm ET FOMC statement plainly moves the tape intraday; nothing
  here contradicts that, and the ledger does not claim FOMC days are calm — only that they do not
  close with larger long-end moves than ordinary days.
- **Bid-to-offer is not a US-style tail.** A multi-price auction has no single stop, so the metrics
  available are coverage and retention. The two are not interchangeable with the JGB ledger's tail,
  and no cross-sovereign comparison of "softness" is attempted.
- **Retention is a policy variable, not just a demand read.** The Finanzagentur chooses it, and it
  can absorb weak demand or bank paper for later market-making. A high retention print is therefore
  ambiguous by construction; the allotment band in `FT-2` is the honest form of the question.
- **Half the auction's terms do not exist yet.** The EUR 1.0bn line's ISIN is due 2026-09-10. Every
  statement about the 09-16 demand is a prior drawn from 16 prior 2026 dates.
- **The Q3 press-release PDF was read through a raw text extraction** and is quoted only for the
  "one bond … announced by press release in the week prior" rule, which the auction-process page
  states independently.
- **VIX is Yahoo, i.e. secondary**, and 15.72 is the 2026-09-08 close; the 09-09 US session had not
  opened when this was written (03:50 UTC).
- **The date is `estimate`.** The Finanzagentur is the German sovereign debt office and the date is
  not in doubt; the label reflects a gap in `market-events-data.ts`'s prefix taxonomy, the same limit
  `jgb-20y-auction-2026-09-15` and `ecb-decision-2026-09-10` both recorded. Estimates widen caution
  and license nothing.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (2026-09-09, D-7; date `estimate`).** **Stand aside, permanently, and use the event as an
instrument rather than a signal.** No entry, exit or hedge is keyed to the 2026-09-16 auction or to
the 09-16 session. The entry's two jobs are (1) **attribution** — a long-end move on 09-16 is not
automatically the FOMC's, because a third sovereign sells long duration that morning — and (2)
**expectation** — the auction publishes at 05:30 ET with a *computable* allotment band, which is the
one number on the day that can be checked rather than narrated. Both jobs survive the null; neither
becomes a trade if the null broke, because nothing on this calendar is exposed to euro rates.

Three reading rules attach to the day itself, and they exist to stop this ledger drawing the wrong
conclusion from the wire copy:

- **"Uncovered" is the format, not the news.** 22.1% of German 30Y auction dates since 2003 took
  bids below the offer. Judge the **allotment** against the ~18.6% retention norm instead.
- **Do not credit the Fed for the day by default.** Measured: on the 25 prior auction/FOMC
  collisions the US 30Y's mean |Δ| was 3.17bp, *below* the 3.87bp on non-FOMC auction dates.
- **Do not read Germany's soft coverage as Japan's story in reverse.** Japan cut supply until it
  cleared; Germany tripled supply and retains the slack. Same symptom, opposite mechanism —
  [`jgb-20y-auction-2026-09-15`](jgb-20y-auction-2026-09-15.md) owns the Japanese side.

**Kill switches.**

1. **The no-price-channel premise breaks** — a tracked name (NVDA/AVGO/MRVL/CRWV) moves **>2%** on a
   session between **2026-09-09** and **2026-09-16** that the tape attributes to a Bund or
   euro-rates headline. This document is rebuilt, not patched.
2. **The spillover null breaks** — the **2026-09-16** auction prints bid-to-offer **< 1.0** *and* the
   US 30Y CMT rises **≥ 10bp** the same date. Confounded by the FOMC by construction, so only a
   2-sigma reading counts, and it reopens the test rather than settling it
   (`FT-bund-30y-auction-2026-09-16-1`).
3. **The retention reading breaks** — the 09-16 auction allots **outside EUR 1.85–2.20bn** of the
   EUR 2.5bn offered, or the Federal government **rejects all bids** (a right the auction-process page
   reserves). Either would mean retention has stopped being a stable policy dial
   (`FT-bund-30y-auction-2026-09-16-2`).
4. **The terms are not completed on schedule** — no multi-ISIN press notice naming the EUR 1.0bn
   line by **2026-09-11**. The four-bank-business-day rule is load-bearing for the whole "terms are
