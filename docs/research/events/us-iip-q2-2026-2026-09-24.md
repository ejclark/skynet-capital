## Initial research

### The question

The proposal that created this id filed it for a specific, testable reason: BEA's IIP is the
**market-valued** measure of the same as-of 2026-06-30 quarter that face-valued TIC gross external debt
reports six days later, so "the two measures of one quarter diverge by exactly the revaluation a rate
move produces — which makes this release the natural control for reading the TIC print." It recorded one
observation of the link (GED $30.197tn ÷ IIP liabilities $64.64tn = **0.467** at 2026Q1) and said
plainly what would settle it: *"no forward test is registered on it and none should be until the ratio is
measured across a real panel."*

That is an explicit invitation, and it is the question this session took: **measure the panel.** A second
question arrived unbidden on the first scan of the calendar — **is this event even new?**

### One-line verdict

The panel **refutes** the premise — the GED/IIP ratio is a composition series driven by equity, not a
revaluation series driven by rates — and the event is **not** new: it is the **third** slug for one BEA
release, filed by a sweep whose single mis-specified search string also produced its false report of a
failed primary.

### Leg 3 — the wedge exists, has a magnitude, and never needed a BEA print · **SUPPORTED**, and registered

[`tic-quarterly-external-debt-2026-09-30`](tic-quarterly-external-debt-2026-09-30.md)'s D-14 pulse
verified Table A footnote **2/** across five vintages — *"All debts are presented at face value.
Exception: long-term debt securities in the memorandum section are valued at market value"* — and
declined to register anything on it, on the good ground that *"a verified accounting convention is not a
hypothesis."* True of the convention. **Not true of its magnitude**, which nobody measured, and which
turns out to be sitting in the same document.

Table A publishes both sides of the same instrument set. The memorandum line *Debt Securities: By Sector*
is **market-valued**; the main table's per-sector **long-term** *Debt securities* lines are **face-valued**.
Their ratio is a directly-observed bond price, quarter by quarter:

| as-of | memo (market, $tn) | long-term face ($tn) | market/face | 10Y at quarter-end |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015Q1 | 9.457 | 9.765 | 0.9685 | 1.94% |
| 2017Q3 | 10.575 | 10.701 | 0.9883 | 2.33% |
| 2019Q4 | 11.305 | 11.210 | **1.0085** | 1.92% |
| **2020Q4** | 11.843 | 11.307 | **1.0474** | **0.93%** |
| 2021Q4 | 12.637 | 12.572 | 1.0052 | 1.52% |
| 2022Q4 | 11.608 | 13.247 | 0.8763 | 3.88% |
| **2023Q3** | 11.918 | 14.073 | **0.8469** | **4.59%** |
| 2025Q2 | 13.952 | 15.318 | 0.9108 | 4.24% |
| **2026Q1** | **14.438** | **16.128** | **0.8952** | **4.30%** |

It behaves exactly as a bond price must: **above par through the zero-rate years**, peaking at
**1.0474** at 2020Q4 with the 10-year at **0.93%**, and bottoming at **0.8469** at 2023Q3 with the
10-year at **4.59%**.

```
market/face = 1.0655 − 0.04298 × 10Y        n=43 (2015Q1–2026Q1, less 2024Q3)
                                            R² = 0.851, residual sd = 0.0211
corr(market/face, 10Y)  = −0.881            corr(market/face, 30Y) = −0.851
```

Robustness: on the full n=44 including 2024Q3 — the known bad vintage the TIC sibling measured at 7.29%
of level — the coefficient is **−0.04101** at R² 0.776, and dropping the three 2020 ZIRP quarters as well
gives **−0.04182** at R² 0.817. The slope is stable across all three cuts. It implies a reduced-form
duration of roughly **4.3 years** per 100bp, which is low for a book labelled "long-term" and should be
read as an elasticity rather than a portfolio duration — the face denominator also grows with new issuance
at par, so the coefficient absorbs composition. That caveat is in the honest limits and does not touch the
fit.

**Forecast for as-of 2026Q2.** The 10-year closed **4.30%** on 2026-03-31 and **4.44%** on 2026-06-30
(**+14bp**), so the wedge should widen: point estimate **0.8746**, ±2σ band **0.832–0.917**, against
2026Q1's **0.8952**. Registered as **FT-us-iip-q2-2026-2026-09-24-1**, scored on the **2026-09-30** TIC
print. This does not overlap the sibling's register, which contains no row on the wedge at all.

**And the point that matters for this event:** the entire measurement above comes out of one Treasury
document. The revaluation the proposal wanted a BEA companion print to reveal was already published, by
Treasury, in the print the companion was supposed to be a control for.

### Leg 4 — the "market-valued companion" premise · **REFUTED** on the panel it asked for

The proposal's claim is precise enough to test: if GED and IIP liabilities are the same quantity measured
at face and at market, their ratio should move with the face-to-market wedge and with nothing much else.

**It does not.** Over the 43 quarters where both are observable (2015Q1–2026Q1, less 2024Q3):

```
corr( GED/IIP-liabilities , market/face wedge ) = +0.150      → 2.3% of variance
corr( GED/IIP-liabilities , time )              = −0.832      → the ratio is a trend
```

The ratio slid from **0.5972** (2015Q1) to **0.4833** (2026Q1) — **−11.4pp**, monotone enough to fit a
straight line in calendar time at R² **0.671**. A valuation wedge oscillates with rates; it does not
trend for eleven years. **The trend is composition**, and the arithmetic is unambiguous:

| 2015Q1 → 2026Q1 | level | growth |
|---|---|---|
| TIC gross external debt | $17.44tn → $30.20tn | **+73.2%** |
| IIP liabilities (ex-derivatives) | $29.20tn → $62.49tn | **+114.0%** |
| **non-debt residual** (portfolio equity + DI equity) | **$11.76tn → $32.29tn** | **+174.6%** |

Foreign claims on the US grew mostly as **equity**, and equity is not in the TIC numerator at all. So the
ratio is a **stock-market ratio wearing a debt ratio's name**. Testing that directly, on the quarterly
change in the ratio against the S&P 500's quarterly return (n=34, 2017Q1–2026Q1, less 2024Q3):

```
Δ(GED/IIP-liabilities), pp = 0.6977 − 0.28732 × SPX quarterly %
                             n=34, R² = 0.848, residual sd = 0.936pp
```

and the conditional is cleaner than the regression: in the **11** quarters with SPX ≥ +8%, the ratio fell
**11 times out of 11**, mean **−2.30pp**, against **+0.42pp** mean in the other 23. The binomial
probability of 11-for-11 against a coin flip is **p = 0.00049**.

### Honest limits

- **Vintage mixing.** TIC values are **first vintages** as published; FRED `IIPLIABQ` is the **current**
  BEA vintage. The June-edition restatements the TIC siblings censused run ~0.5–1.5% of level — smaller
  than the ratio's 11.4pp slide and far smaller than the ±2σ bands, but not zero, and the ratio panel is
  therefore "as TIC saw it then ÷ as BEA sees it now."
- **The pre-2015 archive is not in the panel.** 21 vintages parse for the headline but not for the
  per-sector short/long split, and **2022Q2 is absent from `ticdata.treasury.gov` entirely**. The wedge
  series is n=43, not the full record.
- **2024Q3 is excluded everywhere**, as the TIC sibling's own +$1,880.6bn bad first vintage. It is named
  rather than silently dropped, and every fit is reported with and without it in leg 3.
- **The duration coefficient is an elasticity, not a portfolio duration.** ~4.3 years is implausibly short
  for a long-term book; the face denominator grows with par issuance, so the coefficient absorbs
  composition drift. The claim registered is the **fitted relationship**, not a duration estimate.
- **The composition regression uses SPX as a proxy for the whole equity half.** Foreign holdings of US
  equities are not the S&P 500, and direct-investment equity is valued on a different basis again. R²
  0.848 on a single crude regressor is a warning as much as a result — it says the driver is obviously
  equity, not that this is the right functional form. The kill switch is written to abandon on sign
  failure rather than refit, precisely because of this.
- **The equity series is a substitute.** The direct index history was blocked by a JavaScript challenge
  (recorded in `probe-ref.blocked`); FRED `SP500` starts 2016-09, which is why the composition panel is
  n=34 where the wedge panel is n=43.
- **The Jaccard numbers are normalizer-dependent.** The 09-15 session reported **0.50** for the `bea-…` ×
  survivor pair where this one reports **0.400** post-annotation, on different stopword handling. The
  finding is the **direction and size of the drop** (0.857 → 0.400 on one fixed normalizer) and the
  **0.190** floor on this id — not any absolute threshold.
- **Leg 2 diagnoses a mechanism, not intent.** That the D-14 sweep searched for the product name is
  inferred from its own recorded source string; no lane's reasoning beyond what it wrote down is available.
