## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **This is the one 2-Year auction on the calendar where the house's −6bp prior should NOT
be applied at face value, and the reason is the calendar, not the demand.** Two of the three
scare-stories the proposing sweeps attached to this date fall apart when measured, and the one that
survives is a volatility argument rather than a direction one. **The compressed-block penalty is a
Monday artifact:** 23 of the 24 shared 2Y/5Y sessions since 2000 are Mondays, and *within Mondays*
sharing costs the 2Y nothing on the tape (−0.09bp vs −0.31bp, permutation p = **0.897**) or on
demand in the current size regime (bid-to-cover z p = 0.394, dealer z p = 0.702) — and **2026 has
already sold this exact configuration twice**, on 04-27 and 07-27, both covering above their local
window. **The December sell-off is a pre-2010 artifact** — the same decay pattern the
[11-23 sibling](treasury-2y-note-2026-11-23.md) found in the Thanksgiving discount: +2.15bp on
2000+ (p = 0.015) falling to p = 0.227 / 0.358 / 0.512 / 0.727 on the 2010+ / 2013+ / 2016+ / 2018+
cuts, carried entirely by 2003, 2006-09. **What survives every cut is that the Dec-24-to-Dec-31
tape simply moves less** — mean |2Y move| 2.57bp vs 4.85bp in 2023+ (ratio **0.53**), 2.55 vs 3.80
in 2018+ (ratio 0.67) — so the regime's −6bp expectation is being applied to a half-amplitude tape.
And unlike 11-23, **this auction day is not attributable at all**: the 5-Year prices in the same
1:00pm session, the bills at 11:30, advance economic indicators at 8:30, the Dallas Fed survey at
10:30 (proposed here), with the FOMC minutes two sessions later. Grade the print, never the day.

## Initial research

**The question, plainly:** two sibling lanes proposed this event on the same day and both attached
the same story to it — that the December month-end block is *compressed*, that the 2-Year and
5-Year sharing one session is unusual and costly, and that a Christmas-Eve announcement leaves a
squeezed when-issued window. On top of that, this calendar carries a measured prior that a 2-Year
auction day rallies the front end ~6bp in the current regime. Which of those four claims survives
contact with the record, and what is the honest expectation for 2026-12-28?

**One-line verdict:** three of the four die — the shared session is a **Monday** in disguise, the
December penalty is a **pre-2010 artifact**, and the when-issued squeeze is a **second null at a
second tenor** — while the one that survives is that the **Dec-24-31 tape moves about half as
much**, which discounts the regime's magnitude without changing its sign; and the practical output
is that this print must be graded on its own auction statistics, because its day is the most
crowded in the block.

**Method:** sourced research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md), rates mode
— the house instruments are symbol-keyed and this event has `symbols: []`, so no `earnings-cycle` /
`intraday-edges` run applies and no cache bust was required. Every figure below is computed this
session from primaries fetched today: the Fiscal Data `auctions_query` dataset (all **320** nominal
2-Year note auctions 2000-01-26 → 2026-08-25, plus **285** 5-Year and **219** 7-Year slots, with
FRNs excluded on `floating_rate` and TIPS on `inflation_index_security`), the daily par yield curve
CSVs for 2000–2026 (**6,674** sessions, 2000-01-03 → 2026-09-08), the Tentative Auction Schedule PDF
(HTTP 200, 17,195 bytes, text layer decompressed stream-by-stream into 909 tokens), press release
`sb0590` (HTTP 200, 75,577 bytes, read as text), CBOE's `VIX_History.csv`, and the Federal Reserve's
own FOMC calendar pages for 2000–2026 (**226** meeting dates harvested from statement/minutes URLs,
plus this repo's three tracked late-2026 meetings). Two fetches failed and are recorded in
`probe-ref.blocked` (both MNI Chicago-PMI calendar URLs). Each claim dated in line.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The date and terms are right, and the entry stays `estimate` — SUPPORTED.** The tentative
schedule carries the row verbatim: `2-Year NOTE · Thursday, December 24, 2026 · Monday, December
28, 2026 · Thursday, December 31, 2026`. No `R`, so a new issue — and the flag column is populated
three rows above in the same December block (`2-Year FRN R`, `20-Year BOND R`, `5-Year TIPS R T`),
so the blank is a signal, not a missing field. Read in the same fetch: `5-Year NOTE` on the **same
Monday 12-28**, `7-Year NOTE` Tuesday 12-29, the `13-Week BILL` and `26-Week BILL` also auctioning
Monday 12-28 off the same 12-24 announcement, and the holiday line `Holiday - Friday, December 25,
2026 - Christmas Day`. `TSY:` is an authorised `confirmed` prefix, but this sale is **110 days out
with its announcement 106 days away** — further out than any sibling that chose `estimate` on the
same reasoning. Holding `estimate` costs nothing: estimates only widen caution and no date-keyed
action follows from this event at any label. **The confirming primary is the 2026-12-24 announcement.**

**2. The shared 2Y/5Y session is neither rare as claimed nor costly — REFUTED, and this is the
load-bearing correction to both proposals.** The `from-treasury-7y-note` proposal states "A 2Y and
5Y sharing one auction date is common overall (53 of the 152 months since 2014)". Measured directly:

| Population | n | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| 2Y auctions sharing a date with a 5Y, 2000+ | **24** | all of them since **2017-08-28** |
| …as a share of the 152 monthly 2Y slots since 2014 | 24 / 152 | **16%**, not the 35% the proposal's figure implies |
| …of those 24, auctioned on a **Monday** | **23** | the configuration *is* a Monday |
| 2026 instances already printed | **2** | 2026-04-27 (b/c 2.65) and 2026-07-27 (b/c 2.66) |

The mechanism is arithmetic, not stress: the 5-Year is pulled onto the 2-Year's session precisely
when the month-end block starts on a Monday. Once weekday is held fixed the effect vanishes — 2017+
Mondays only, shared vs not-shared 2Y auction-day moves run **−0.09bp vs −0.31bp** (t = +0.15,
permutation p = **0.897**). Unconditionally the same comparison looks like something (2017+ far-from-
FOMC: +0.69 vs −2.43, t = +2.04, p = 0.086; $69B era: −1.43 vs −6.45, t = +2.74, p = 0.027), which
is exactly the trap: the shared subset is 96% Mondays and the unshared subset is mostly Tuesdays.

**Demand tells the same story and the `$69B` era is a clean null.** Bid-to-cover and dealer takedown
were z-scored against each auction's own ±12-auction local window, which is the only way to compare
across the 2Y's size regimes:

| Cut | bid-to-cover z (shared vs not) | p | dealer z (shared vs not) | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2017+ | −0.272 (n=24) vs +0.124 (n=92) | 0.116 | +0.365 vs −0.078 | 0.106 |
| **$69B era, 2024-04+** | **−0.324 (n=7) vs +0.108 (n=22)** | **0.394** | **+0.283 vs −0.021** | **0.702** |

One post-hoc cut *does* return a significant number — 2017+ excluding 2020 gives b/c z −0.537 vs
+0.160, p = 0.007 — and it is recorded here as a forking path rather than a finding: dropping a year
after seeing the data is not a test, and the era that actually governs this auction is the null one.
**Practical output: do not discount 2026-12-28 for the shared session, and do not let a soft print
be explained by it.**

**3. December really is the worst month on the 2Y auction-day record, and the effect is pre-2010 —
REFUTED as a live claim.** This is the finding that most changes how a reader should treat the −6bp
prior on this date, and it decays in exactly the pattern the [11-23 sibling](treasury-2y-note-2026-11-23.md)
found in the Thanksgiving-week discount:

| Population (2Y auction-day close-to-close move) | December | All other months | Permutation p |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2000+** | **+2.15bp** (n=26) | −0.54bp | **0.015** |
| 2000+, far-from-FOMC | **+2.56bp** (n=18) | −0.73bp | **0.018** |
| 2010+ | +0.50bp (n=16) | −1.02bp | 0.227 |
| 2013+ | +0.23bp (n=13) | −1.12bp | 0.358 |
| 2016+ | −0.30bp (n=10) | −1.51bp | 0.512 |
| 2018+ | **−1.25bp** (n=8) | −2.06bp | 0.727 |

The whole 2000+ gap is five prints — 2003-12-23 (**+12bp**), 2008-12-22 (**+13bp**), 2009-12-28
(+9bp), 2007-12-26 (+7bp) and 2006-12-27 (+6bp) — spanning the pre-crisis, crisis and immediate
post-crisis year-ends. Every cut that excludes them returns p ≥ 0.227 and the sign flips negative
from 2016 on. **But the honest converse matters just as much:** December has been tested *inside
the live regime* exactly **three** times — 2023-12-26 (−5bp), 2024-12-23 (0bp), 2025-12-22 (−4bp),
mean **−3.00bp** against the regime's −4.83bp on non-December days. That is not evidence of a
December penalty; it is evidence that the regime's December sample is too small to argue either way,
which is why this ledger discounts on **amplitude** (leg 5) rather than on the month label.

**4. The when-issued squeeze is a null, and it is the second tenor to say so — REFUTED.** Announce
Thursday 12-24 (a half-day session), Friday 12-25 closed, auction Monday 12-28: **one trading day**
of when-issued, against the 3–5 the proposals treat as normal. But "normal" is wrong — a ≤2-business-
day announce-to-auction window is the **modal** case for the 2-Year (134 of 320 auctions since 2000),
and it is a null on both demand dimensions: b/c z p = **0.443** (2000+) and **0.649** (2013+); dealer
z p = **0.227** and **0.613**. The `from-treasury-7y-note` proposal measured the same compression as
a null at the 7-Year (29 auctions, residual −0.019bp vs +0.004bp, t = −0.10) and flagged the front
end as untested. It is now tested, and it agrees.

**5. The Dec-24-31 tape moves about half as much, and this is the one leg that survives every cut —
SUPPORTED.** Mean absolute daily 2Y CMT move, sessions dated 24–31 December against every other
session:

| Window | Dec 24–31 | All other sessions | Ratio | Permutation p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2000+ | 3.03bp (n=122) | 3.63bp (n=6,551) | 0.83 | 0.109 |
| 2018+ | 2.55bp (n=38) | 3.80bp (n=2,133) | **0.67** | 0.073 |
| **2023+** | **2.57bp (n=14)** | **4.85bp (n=907)** | **0.53** | **0.073** |

No cut reaches p = 0.05 on its own, but the direction is identical in all three and the magnitude
grows as the sample gets closer to today — which is the opposite of the decay pattern legs 3 and 4
show, and the reason this one is carried forward rather than dismissed. It argues for **scaling the
regime expectation down, not flipping its sign**: a −6bp median applied to a tape running at 53% of
normal amplitude is closer to −3bp in practice. It is also the leg most exposed to the honest limit
below, since n=14 in-regime sessions is thin.

**6. The far-from-FOMC classification holds and the regime replicates exactly — SUPPORTED.**
`fomc-2026-12-09` is **D−19** and `fomc-2027-01-27` is **D+30**, so 2026-12-28 sits squarely in the
far-from-FOMC population, the same half of the model [11-23](treasury-2y-note-2026-11-23.md) tests.
Independently recomputing that sibling's study from the same primaries reproduces it to the decimal:
2023+ auction days **−4.70bp** (n=44) against +0.23bp on non-auction sessions (t = −4.71), and
2000-2022 auction days **+0.38bp** (n=276, t = +1.48) — wrong sign, insignificant. Far-from-FOMC
2023+ runs **−5.85bp** (n=26): min −26 · p25 −8.0 · median −6.0 · p75 −3.0 · max +7, with 21 of 26
at or below zero and **20 of 26 (77%)** at or below −3bp, against **24%** of the 170 pre-2023
far-from-FOMC auctions and **28%** of the 571 comparable 2023+ far-from-FOMC non-auction sessions.
**And the sibling's Monday claim survives this session's harder look at it:** 2023+ far-from-FOMC
Mondays run **−5.22bp (n=9)** against Tuesdays' **−6.18bp (n=17)** — reproducing its figures — and
measured as excess over the *same weekday's* non-auction baseline the two are −5.31bp (p = 0.062)
and −6.27bp (p < 0.001). Monday is marginally the weaker slot and is not distinguishable from
Tuesday at this n; the shared-session result in leg 2 rides on that composition, not on a penalty.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (estimate-dated event; no standalone play):** watch-only, and the contribution is a
**prior corrected in one direction and defended in three**. The house's 2Y auction-day regime
