This entry has its own question, and it is adversarial: **does the sibling's hold-modal read for Q4
actually transfer to December, or is it an artefact of one survey that has since been overtaken?**

**live-not-modal at Medium confidence**, not hold-modal.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The sibling's ~17% Q4-follow-up figure has been overtaken — REFUTED as a current read,
   primary + wire.** On **2026-09-02** the BoJ published Takata Hajime's Sapporo speech. Verbatim:
   *"Partly in light of the fact that the Bank implemented policy interest rate hikes twice a year in
   2024 and 2025, markets until recently factored in semiannual rate hikes through 2028, as suggested
   by Chart 23. I believe, however, that **2026 represents a regime change where rate hikes will not
   be carried out at a fixed pace but will instead be conducted in a nimble and data-dependent
   manner** in response to the domestic price and economic environment, particularly reflecting
   overseas trends."* And earlier in the same text: *"In this phase, the Bank will need to adopt a
   nimble approach to address conditions at the time, including overseas developments."* The same
   day, Reuters reported (read via Yahoo Finance syndication, **2026-09-02**): *"Sources have told
   Reuters the BOJ is set to raise the rate as soon as at its two-day policy meeting through
   September 18, and **is considering hiking more aggressively thereafter than the current pace of
   roughly two times a year**."* **"Thereafter"** has exactly two 2026 venues — 10-29/30 and
   **12-17/18**. Both sources postdate the Reuters Aug 17–24 poll (published **08-26**) that the
   10-30 ledger's hold-modal call rests on. This does not make December modal; it makes the ~17%
   figure the wrong instrument to price it with.

2. **The consensus, read as one consistent model rather than four competing polls — SUPPORTED, four
   dated surveys.** They only look contradictory until the questions are separated:
   - **Level:** *"86% of economists expect the BoJ to raise its key interest rate to 1.25% by the end
     of the fourth quarter (Q4), up slightly from 79% in a June survey"* (Reuters poll, FXStreet
     syndication, **2026-07-23 04:47 GMT**). Same poll: *"70% of economists anticipate the BoJ to
     hike rates to at least 1.50% in Q2 of 2027, while 51% of respondents see 1.50% as the terminal
     rate."*
   - **Pace:** *"An overwhelming majority — 82% — forecast the pace of rate hikes to be roughly once
     every six months, an increase from 71% in a June survey"* (Bloomberg survey, Japan Times,
     **2026-07-23**).
   - **Timing, as of July:** *"Some 50% of 52 economists selected December as the most likely timing
     for the next move after the BOJ raised the rate last month… Another 40% said it would come in
     October"* (same Bloomberg survey).
   - **The August repricing:** 57% of 58 for a September hike, *"a sharp turnaround from a July poll
     when just 5% expected a move this quarter"*, and 10 of 58 for a further hike to 1.5% in October
     or December (Reuters **Aug 17–24**, published **08-26**, carried by the sibling ledger).

   The reconciliation is clean: **August pulled the same single hike forward by one meeting; it did
   not add a hike.** Under an 82%-endorsed six-month pace, a September move puts the next one near
   March 2027 — which is precisely why the Q4-follow-up figure is only ~17%. Note also that the
   82% pace consensus, applied to June's **06-16** hike, lands the next move on **mid-December** —
   this meeting. December was the modal venue right up until August moved it.

3. **December's odds are inversely related to September's outcome — SUPPORTED, structural, and this
   is the branch structure the next pulse should re-derive from.** Four states, not two:
   - **09-18 hikes to 1.25%, 10-30 holds (the sibling's modal path).** December is the venue for a
     *third* hike to 1.5%. Base survey read ~17% shared with October, revised **up** by leg 1. **Live,
     not modal** — this is what every call in the header is written for.
   - **09-18 holds (~16–20% per the 09-18 ledger's OIS read).** The 86% end-Q4 level commitment now
     has only two meetings to deliver in, and the July timing survey already had 40% on October and
     **50% on December**. December becomes **hike-modal**, and `medium` impact would be under-tiered.
   - **09-18 and 10-30 both hike.** December would be a fourth 2026 move to 1.75%, which no survey
     carries. Effectively a hold, and the interest shifts to the 2027-01-22 Outlook meeting.
   - **09-18 hikes and the bank guides at a faster pace.** Leg 1's regime change is confirmed from
     the podium; December goes two-sided and this doc is re-derived, not patched.

4. **This is not an Outlook meeting, and that is the one thing December uniquely lacks — SUPPORTED,
   primary.** The schedule table's Outlook Report column reads **"-"** for Dec. 17-18; March, June,
   September and December all do, while April, July and **Oct. 30** carry dates. So the projection
   channel that the [10-30 ledger](boj-decision-2026-10-30.md) built its FT-2 around does not exist
   here. December's content is the **rate, the statement and the presser** — a narrower surface, which
   argues for *less* headline risk than October, in the one respect where October is genuinely
   richer. Same table, same parse: this meeting's **Summary of Opinions is Dec. 28** and its
   **Minutes Jan. 27, 2027**.

5. **The yen kill switch is low-powered, and this session measured it — MIXED, and it is the
   methodological finding of this ledger.** Both sibling forward tests
   ([FT-boj-decision-2026-09-18-1](../forward-tests/boj-decision-2026-09-18.md),
   [FT-boj-decision-2026-10-30-1](../forward-tests/boj-decision-2026-10-30.md)) predict *no
   close-to-close USD/JPY move beyond ±2.0% on the decision day*. Neither measured what that
   threshold costs. Computed here from `JPY=X` daily bars (2,779 bars, 2016-01-01 → 2026-09-04):

   | Measurement | Value |
   |---|---|
   | Sessions with abs. move ≥ 2.0% | **3 / 176** in 2026 (1.7%) · 2 / 256 in 2025 · 2 / 261 in 2024 |
   | Sessions with abs. move ≥ 1.0% | 10 / 176 in 2026 (5.7%) · 21 / 256 in 2025 · 27 / 261 in 2024 |
   | Trailing 60-session daily sd | **0.514%** (annualized 8.2%) |
   | Direction of all 7 ≥2.0% days, 2024–2026 | **every one yen-strengthening** |

   So a ±2.0% day is a ~1-in-60 to ~1-in-130 event: the test has a very low false-positive rate and,
   correspondingly, **a pass is nearly uninformative** — a randomly chosen session passes it ~98–99%
   of the time. Three findings sharpen that:
   - **On 2026's four cleanly-dated decision days, USD/JPY moved +0.03% (01-23), +0.57% (03-19),
     +0.12% (04-28) and +0.12% (06-16).** Every one is below the 0.514% daily sd — *including the
     June hike to a 30-year high.* This converts the siblings' anecdote (*"the yen barely flinched"*)
     into a measured series, and it is strong support for their underlying claim.
   - **The largest yen event of 2026 would not have tripped the switch.** Around the 07-31 decision
     and the 07-30 → 08-03 joint intervention, the two consecutive bars moved **-1.91%** and
     **-1.62%** (cumulative **-3.43%**) — and *neither single session cleared ±2.0%.* The threshold
     sits **above the observed magnitude of the very events it exists to detect.**
   - **The moves that did clear it came one to two sessions *after* a BoJ hold, not on the decision
     day.** Bracketing each 2026 decision D-2 → D+2: **-2.10% on 01-26** (D+1 after the Jan 23 hold)
     and **-2.00% on 04-30** (D+2 after the Apr 28 hold). Attribution is not claimed — month-end and
     US data are unruled-out — but the *timing* pattern means a decision-day-only window may
     systematically miss the effect it is testing for.

   The registered predictions are not edited (that would be falsification). Instead this venue
   registers **both** the identical ±2.0% decision-day test — completing the pre-stated 2–3
   observation count as **FT-boj-decision-2026-12-18-1** — and a **powered** version at ±1.0% over
   the D-1 → D+2 bracket as **FT-boj-decision-2026-12-18-3**, so the two run side by side at the same
   venue and the power gap is observable rather than argued.

6. **The intervention was a campaign, not a single action — SUPPORTED, and it corrects both
   siblings.** Both carry the **~$59B** joint action of 2026-08-03. CNBC, **2026-09-03**, verbatim:
   *"The move revived speculation about possible currency intervention after **Japan spent a record
   15.4 trillion yen between July 30 and Aug. 26** and the U.S. participated in a coordinated
   yen-buying effort."* At ~159 that is roughly **$97B** — the joint action was the opening of a
   four-week campaign, not the whole of it. The correction matters for December because it raises the
   estimate of how much official flow it takes to move this currency, which is the same question
   leg 5 measures from the other end.

7. **The tape had already repriced by 09-03, and it did so without a decision — SUPPORTED, own
   measurement + press.** CNBC, **2026-09-03**: *"The Japanese yen jumped more than 2% against the
   greenback on Thursday, briefly reaching 155.28 per dollar, its strongest level in a month… Market
   watchers said it was more likely that the move was tied to raised bets on a Bank of Japan rate
   hike this month after hawkish comments from policymakers."* This session's own measurement of the
   same session: **-2.05%** close-to-close (158.923 → 155.660). **The sibling ledgers' ±2.0% kill
   switch would have fired on an ordinary Thursday three days before those ledgers were written, on

8. **Takata's inflation case gives December a concrete pipeline mechanism — SUPPORTED, primary.**
   From the same speech: *"The year-on-year rate of increase in the producer price index (PPI) has
   already risen to **7.2 percent**, as of the July 2026 index, and there are upside risks to prices,
   with more firms passing upstream price increases on downstream… **Through the second half of
   fiscal 2026, the rise in producer prices upstream will likely spread to consumer prices

9. **The BoJ has hiked in December before, and recently — SUPPORTED, primary.** Same speech,

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (date `estimate`):** **stand aside completely — and explicitly decline to inherit the
sibling's hold-modal frame.** The ledger takes four analytical positions and no positional one.

First, **live, not modal, and not ~17%.** The 10-30 ledger's Q4-follow-up figure comes from the
Reuters Aug 17–24 poll, published **2026-08-26**. Seven days later the BoJ published Takata's
*"2026 represents a regime change where rate hikes will not be carried out at a fixed pace but will
instead be conducted in a nimble and data-dependent manner"*, and Reuters reported sourced that the
bank *"is considering hiking more aggressively thereafter than the current pace of roughly two times
a year."* **Thereafter** contains October and December and nothing else in 2026. Stated at **Medium**,
not higher, because leg 10's Takaichi constraint (59% of 52 economists) cuts squarely the other way
and binds harder at this horizon than at September's.

Second, **December is the consensus's deadline, not its date — so its odds move inversely to the
earlier meetings'.** 86% expect 1.25% by end-Q4 (Reuters, 07-23) and 82% expect a roughly six-month
pace (Bloomberg, 07-23), which applied to June's 06-16 hike lands on **mid-December**. A 09-18 hold
