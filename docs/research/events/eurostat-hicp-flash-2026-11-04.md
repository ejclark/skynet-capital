## Initial research

### The question, plainly

What will the October euro-area flash say on 2026-11-04, does it change what a US paper-trading book
should do, and which of the neighbouring ledgers does it feed? Short answer: it prints **after** the
ECB's October decision, so its job is to set up **December 17**; it has no tracked-symbol channel; and
its interest is whether September's energy-hot / core-tame shape survives a second month of a Hormuz
supply shock.

### Method

Primary sources over aggregators, dated: Eurostat's September complete release (`2-16102026-ap`) and
flash (`2-02102026-ap`) for the level and the schedule; Eurostat's `prc_hicp_mmor` / `prc_hicp_manr` API
(fetched 2026-10-20) for the **October-2025 base**; FRED `DCOILBRENTEU` (EIA spot Brent) for crude; the
ECB statistical calendar for the date. The sibling ledgers
[`eurostat-hicp-flash-2026-10-01`](eurostat-hicp-flash-2026-10-01.md) (the same arithmetic, scored) and
[`eurostat-hicp-final-2026-10-16`](eurostat-hicp-final-2026-10-16.md) supply the method and the latest level.

### Conviction legs, tested

**Leg 1 — the date is 2026-11-04 — SUPPORTED.** Two primaries agree (Eurostat's own sentence, the ECB
calendar). The same pair named the 2 October flash exactly
(`FT-eurostat-hicp-flash-2026-10-01-1` PASS), so the pair is a tested dating method, not an assumption. The
proposal's "15:00 CET = 10:00 ET" conversion is wrong by an hour: with both zones on standard time by 11-04,
15:00 CET is **09:00 ET**; and that hour is the ECB series' stamp, not a sourced Eurostat release moment.

**Leg 2 — energy accelerates again, so the headline does — SUPPORTED on direction, MIXED on size.**
The base: October 2025 energy m/m was **−0.2%** (Sept −0.1%), all-items **+0.2%** (Eurostat
`prc_hicp_mmor`). September 2026's energy y/y moved 14.3% → 18.9%, i.e. an energy m/m of about **+3.9%**
(`1.189/1.143 × 0.999`). For October, energy y/y = `1.189 × (1 + m) / 0.998`:

| Energy m/m (October 2026) | Energy y/y | Headline (energy contribution ≈ 9.05% of its y/y) |
|---|---|---|
| −0.5% | 18.5% | ≈3.7% |
| +0.5% | 19.7% | ≈3.8–3.9% |
| **+1.5% (central)** | **≈21%** | **≈3.9–4.0%** |
| +3.5% | 23.3% | ≈4.2% |

Why +1.5% central: Brent (FRED spot) averaged **$114.2** in September against **$86.7** in August (+32%),
and **$127.0** for October 1–14 (+11% over September). Retail fuel is ~45% of the energy basket and passes
crude through at roughly a third of the move with a few weeks' lag; gas and electricity are stickier. That is
this ledger's own model, not Eurostat's. **The mixed half:** the spot series and the futures quotes press
cites disagree materially in October (FRED spot $126.6 on 10-14; press futures $105.6 on 10-13 and $109.9 on
10-16; one 10-19 snippet quoted an expiring contract near $92), so whether October's crude average really
exceeds September's is source-dependent — which is why the band runs from a small energy decline to +3.5%.
The September headline arithmetic closes: contributions energy **+1.71**, services **+1.52**, non-energy
industrial goods **+0.29**, food/alcohol/tobacco **+0.25** = **3.77pp**.

**Leg 3 — services and core stay flat (the shape survives) — MIXED.** The base is ordinary this month:
October 2025 services m/m **+0.1%** (September's −0.9% package-holiday trough is gone), core (ex energy,
food, alcohol, tobacco) m/m **+0.3%**, y/y 2.4%. With no soft base to flatter it, services y/y has no
mechanical push either way: central **3.2%**, band 3.1–3.3%. The risk is up, not down: the September flash
already took services 3.0% → 3.2% and tripped the ECB ledger's supply-side kill, and a second month of
transport-fare pass-through (airfares, package travel) is the channel to watch.

**Leg 4 — no tracked-symbol channel — SUPPORTED.** `symbols: []`; no house playbook is rates- or
euro-keyed; the flash has no US price channel. The honest read-across is the shared energy shock, directional
only: euro HICP has no owners'-equivalent-rent and carries a higher energy weight (~9.05%).
