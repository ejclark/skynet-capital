## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This entry was proposed on 2026-09-09 by the
[`jgb-liquidity-enhancement-5-11y-2026-10-22`](jgb-liquidity-enhancement-5-11y-2026-10-22.md) adjacency sweep,
for one stated job — to be the **confirming observation** for that ledger's four forward tests, because "a
single print can flip on one large bidder, and the coupon-preference reading this family now rests on needs two
prints in the same direction, not one." The proposal attached a caution: 2026-11-27 is the session after the US
Thanksgiving half-day, so "a soft print here is confounded with a thin global bid and should be read with that
stated."

A confirming print is only worth a session if the thing it confirms is measured correctly. So this ledger asks:

**does the family's calibration survive an independent rebuild from MOF's own files, does the coupon finding
replicate outside the zone it was found in, and is the thin-bid caveat real?**

**One-line verdict:** the coupon finding survives everything and gets **stronger** — it replicates out of sample
in a zone nobody here has ever read — but the calibration does **not**: the family's spread-tail numbers are
**ten times too large**, which makes one of its two published day-of triggers unreachable and the other
vacuous; every yen magnitude in the family is ten times too small (no ratio affected); the thin-bid caveat is
**refuted** by twenty years of the instrument's own history; and the "30Y > 20Y > 10Y" tenor ordering, which the
sibling promoted to a reading rule, is a **mid-zone artefact** that does not generalise.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. THE SPREAD-TAIL CALIBRATION IS TEN TIMES TOO LARGE — REFUTED, and it breaks a published trigger.**
MOF's result pages and its own workbook both label the two spread columns **"Highest Accepted Spread (%)"** and
**"Average Accepted Spread (%)"**. The 2026-08-26 print reads **−0.011%** and **−0.014%**; the difference is
0.003 percentage points, i.e. **0.30 basis points**. The family currently records that print at "3.0bp." Every
tail figure it carries is the difference expressed in units of 0.001% and labelled "bp".

Rebuilt from the 32 mid-zone result pages:

| Statistic | Family's figure | **Measured (n=32)** |
|---|---|---|
| Mean tail | 4.4bp | **0.441bp** |
| Median | 3.5bp | **0.350bp** |
| p90 | 6.9bp | **0.690bp** |
| Max | 21.0bp (2025-04-17) | **2.10bp (2025-04-17)** |

Same series, same extremum date, one decimal place. The consequence is not cosmetic. The family's
**"calibrated weak print — spread tail ≥ 6.9bp"** cannot fire: across the **458-auction, twenty-year** history
in MOF's own workbook the largest tail ever printed is **6.70bp** (2010-09-03), the second largest 5.80bp
(2013-04-05), and the largest since 2024 is **3.70bp** (2025-06-12). Its **"calibrated strong print — tail ≤
1bp"** fires on **30 of the 32** mid-zone prints. One trigger is unreachable and the other is nearly always
true. Corrected thresholds are in **Signals & conditions**; registered as
`FT-jgb-liquidity-enhancement-5-11y-2026-11-27-3`.

a reason to doubt the finding.

**3. THE COUPON PREFERENCE REPLICATES OUT OF SAMPLE — SUPPORTED, and this is the strongest new evidence for
it.** The 1–5y zone runs 18 of 35 monthly slates and **this family has never read one**. Sixteen have printed;
fourteen carry both an announcement and a reopened-issues page. Its eligible menu is a different set of bonds
(2Y/5Y/10Y/20Y/30Y at 1–5 years remaining), its offering is **¥700bn**, and its bidder incentives are the money-
market end rather than the belly:

| Zone | n | Coupon-weighted overweight | t | Positive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1-5y (first read)** | **14** | **+0.324pp** | **+3.55** | **13 / 14** |
| 5-11y / 5-15.5y | 32 | +0.181pp | +2.28 | 20 / 32 |
| 11-39y / 15.5-39y | 16 | **−0.056pp** | **−0.64** | 8 / 16 |

Bidders in a zone nobody here had opened take paper **above** the eligible stock's own coupon, on almost every
print, at a t-statistic higher than the zone the effect was discovered in. That is an out-of-sample replication
in the strict sense: different bonds, different auction, different size, hypothesis fixed in advance.

**4. …BUT THE TENOR ORDERING IS A MID-ZONE ARTEFACT — REFUTED, and it is the reading rule that has to change.**
The sibling promoted "**30Y > 20Y > 10Y** overweight" to a day-of instruction. Outside the mid zone it fails:

| Zone | Bucket overweights, by that zone's own coupons |
|---|---|
| 5-11y | 10Y (0.87%) **0.62×** < 20Y (1.26%) **1.32×** < 30Y (2.26%) **1.86×** — monotone |
| **1-5y** | 5Y (0.29%) **0.48×** < 2Y (0.51%) **0.62×** < 10Y (**0.11%**) **1.12×** < 20Y (2.12%) **2.25×** — **not monotone** |
| **11-39y** | 20Y (1.02%) **0.81×** ≈ 30Y (1.25%) **0.84×** < 40Y (1.23%) **2.05×** — **not monotone** |

In both other zones the **highest-coupon bucket is still on top**, and in both the middle of the ranking is
scrambled — in the 1–5y zone the **lowest**-coupon bucket (10Y, 0.11%) outranks two higher-coupon ones. Read
together with leg 3 the resolution is clean and it sharpens rather than weakens the family's claim: the
preference is **continuous and issue-level**, and a **tenor bucket is a poor proxy for it** because coupon
varies enormously *within* a bucket. The continuous measure replicates; the bucket ranking does not travel.
This retires "read the 30/20/10 ordering" as a day-of rule and replaces it with "read the coupon-weighted
overweight," registered as `FT-…-5`.

month-picking exercise. Across the **458-print** history, splitting on whether an auction fell in the Monday–

**2023-11-24** (the only one on the Friday *after* Thanksgiving, cover 4.38× at a 0.20bp tail) and 2024-11-25.
The two mid-zone November prints on record are 2024-11-19 (cover 3.39, tail 0.20bp) and 2025-11-07 (3.25,

caveat, not to invert it**: registered as `FT-…-6`.

### Honest limits

- **The out-of-sample replication is not independent of the mid-zone in every respect.** The 1-5y and 5-11y
  menus overlap in *tenor* (both contain 10Y and 20Y issues) even though they share no individual bond at any
  date. Same issuer, same bidder base, same week of the month — so this is a replication across auctions, not
  across markets.
- **n=9 is a small Thanksgiving sample, and cover is not significant.** Only the tail difference clears
  conventional significance, and one of the nine (2023-11-24) is the only observation on the exact weekday this
  event falls on. What the data supports is "the caveat is unsupported," not "the print will be strong."
- **The month-of-year table is twelve comparisons.** November's top-of-table cover rank is description. The
  Thanksgiving-week split is the pre-specified test and is the only one reported as such.
- **The eligible-stock projection carries three assumptions**: that MOF holds the 5–11 year band, that the
  on-the-run exclusion continues to apply (it reproduced 29 of 34 recent ranges), and that 10-Year No.383
  reaches the standard ¥7.80tn. The ~2026-11-20 announcement settles all three at once.
- **The rotation schedule is arithmetic, not policy.** MOF could redefine the band again, as it did in April
  2026, and the decay clock in leg 6 would restart. The kill switch names that.
- **Coupon and issue age remain collinear**, exactly as the sibling stated. Nothing here separates "high
  coupon" from "seasoned, small float"; both are bond-characteristic stories rather than curve-location ones,
  which is the claim being made.
- **The spread tail is not a coupon tail.** These auctions are bid as spreads to per-issue reference rates, so
  the tail measures dispersion across a **basket of different bonds**. The corrected distribution is internally
  consistent within this zone and transfers to no other.
- **The 458-print history mixes zones.** It is used only for the tail scale, the Thanksgiving split and the
  day-of-week split — questions where the pooled series is the right object. Every zone-specific statistic
  comes from the zone-labelled panel.
- **The curve reading stops at 2026-08-31** because MOF's current-month CSV now serves HTML at both known
  paths (`probe-ref.blocked`).
- **The auction result does not exist**, and neither does its size or its issue list. Everything here is prior.
- **VIX is Yahoo, i.e. secondary**, and is a US reading with no mechanical bearing on a JGB auction; it is
  carried because the probe-ref contract asks for it.
- **The date and the size are both `estimate`.** MOF is a primary and the date is not in doubt; the label
  reflects an unannounced size, an unpublished issue list, and a gap in `market-events-data.ts`'s prefix
  taxonomy, which has no slot for a non-US sovereign debt office. Estimates widen caution and license nothing.
