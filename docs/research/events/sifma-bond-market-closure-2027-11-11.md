## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Stand aside on the date — and note that the thing worth watching in 2027 is not the
closed day, it is which session Treasury moves its 30-year bond auction onto.** The closure itself
reproduces from three primaries fetched first-hand this session: SIFMA's US 2027 card reads exactly
`Veterans Day` / `Thursday, November 11, 2027` with **no early-close note** (the full-closure
shape); NYSE's grid lists **ten** holidays per year for 2026–2028 with **zero** Veterans Day row, so
equities run 09:30–16:00 ET while the recommended USD fixed-income tape is dark; and the OCC's
calendar flags 2027-11-11 as a bond-only **`Bank holiday`**. A fourth, fully-primary US-government
source corroborates by absence: **Treasury has scheduled zero auctions on a November 11 in all 47
Novembers 1980–2026.** What is new versus the 2026 sibling is the **weekday**. 2027-11-11 is a
**Thursday**, and November 2027 is the exact calendar twin of November 2021 — both start on a
Monday, both put Veterans Day on Thursday the 11th and refunding settlement on Monday the 15th. In
**both** prior Thursday-Veterans-Day Novembers of the modern 3y/10y/30y regime (2010, 2021) Treasury
compressed the slate onto Mon/Tue/Wed and the **30-year bond auctioned Wednesday the 10th**, against
a Thursday 30-year in **12 of the 16** Novembers 2010–2025. So 2027 puts the month's largest duration
supply at **D-1** and then goes dark — the mirror image of 2026, where the tracked 30-year sits at
D+1. **The story that shape suggests is refuted here rather than left open.** In both analogs the

## Initial research

### The question

This id arrived as a proposal, not a calendar entry: `proposals/sifma-bond-market-closure-2027-11-11.from-thanksgiving-market-closure-2027-11-25.json`,
filed 2026-09-08 by a sibling sweep at D-14 and honest about sitting outside its own corridor. Its
sources were read second-hand, so leg 0 of this research is to re-fetch every one of them. And the
substantive question is **not** "what is a bond market holiday" — [`sifma-bond-market-closure-2026-11-11`](sifma-bond-market-closure-2026-11-11.md)
already measured that shape to death (half-depth bond ETFs, a flat direction, a refuted
deferred-repricing story) and [`sifma-bond-holiday-close-2027-10-11`](sifma-bond-holiday-close-2027-10-11.md)
carries the 2027 Columbus Day twin. Restating those would be a wasted session. The question that is
actually open is: **does anything about the 2027 instance differ from the 2026 one, and if so does it
change what a reader should do?**

**One-line verdict:** one thing differs and it is structural, not statistical — **2027-11-11 is a
Thursday, which drops the closure inside November refunding week**, and on a 2-for-2 precedent
Treasury answers that by moving the 30-year bond auction to **Wednesday the 10th**, putting the
month's largest duration supply at D-1 instead of 2026's D+1. The tempting corollary — that the
deferred digestion then shows up on refunding-settlement Monday — is **refuted** against 17 November
settlements. Everything else about the 2026 sibling's findings replicates on a fresh pull and is
carried forward rather than re-argued.

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The full-closure shape reproduces from three primaries, first-hand — SUPPORTED (and it stays
   `estimate`).** SIFMA's 2027 US panel, card 78 of the decoded run, reads `Veterans Day` /
   `Thursday, November 11, 2027` with an **empty** note field, structurally distinct from card 79 in
   the same panel (`Thanksgiving Day` / `Thursday, November 25, 2027` /
   `Early Close (2:00 p.m. Eastern Time): Friday, November 26, 2027`). NYSE's grid lists **ten**
   holidays per year for 2026/2027/2028 and contains **zero** occurrences of `Veterans`, `Columbus`,
   `Indigenous`, `November 11` or `October 11` in any of its 109 cells — equities run the full core
   session. The OCC calendar marks `2027-11-11` with `holiday.description` `<p>Bank holiday</p>` — its
   bond-only flag — against `2027-11-25`'s `<p>Exchange/<br />OCC holiday</p>` with `hatched: true`;
   those are the **only two** November 2027 holiday markers in the file. It stays `estimate` on three
   counts: no confirmed prefix exists for a trade association's recommended schedule; this lane may
   not self-confirm an in-sweep discovery it inherited; the source is non-binding by its own terms.

2. **Treasury corroborates the closure by absence, from a fully-primary government source — SUPPORTED,
   and this leg is new to the event class.** `api.fiscaldata.treasury.gov`'s `auctions_query`, asked
   once per year for auctions dated `<year>-11-11` across **1980–2026**, returns **zero rows in all
   47 Novembers**. That is not a holiday calendar and not a recommendation: it is the record of what
   the US Treasury actually did, and it has never once auctioned on a November 11. It does not upgrade
   the status — `TSY:` covers a Treasury *auction schedule* naming a date, and corroboration by
   absence is not a Treasury statement about the holiday — but it removes any residual doubt that the
   USD government market is shut that day.

3. **The weekday is the material difference from 2026, and it puts the closure inside refunding week
   — SUPPORTED, the central finding.** 2027-11-11 is a **Thursday**; 2026-11-11 was a Wednesday. More
   precisely, **November 2027 is the exact calendar twin of November 2021**: both months begin on a
   Monday, both put Veterans Day on Thursday the 11th, and both put mid-quarter refunding settlement
   on Monday the 15th. The November 30-year bond auction's weekday, from Treasury's own record:

   | Nov 11 weekday | Novembers | 30-year auction weekday |
   |---|---|---|
   | **Thursday** | **2010, 2021** | **Wednesday, both** (2010-11-10, 2021-11-10) |
   | Any | 2010–2025 (n=16) | Thursday in **12**, Wednesday in 4 (2010, 2018, 2021, 2024) |

   The two Wednesday years that are *not* Thursday-Veterans-Day (2018, 2024) had Veterans Day on a
   Sunday and a Monday respectively — i.e. an *observed* Monday holiday, which pulls the slate
   earlier by the same mechanism. The routine bill slate moves too: the 4-week auction ran
   `Thu 2021-11-04 · **Wed 2021-11-10** · Thu 2021-11-18 · Wed 2021-11-24` against a clean
   2022 control of `Thu · Thu · Thu · Wed`, the last of which is Thanksgiving week. So the 2027 read
   is **supply at D-1, then a dark session** — the mirror of 2026, whose tracked
   `treasury-30y-bond-2026-11-12` sits at D+1. **Honest boundary:** the 2027 dates themselves are
   pattern-derived. Treasury's tentative auction schedule does not reach November 2027, so no
   proposal file was created for a 2027-11-10 auction and the prediction is registered as
   **FT-…-2** instead.

### Honest limits

- **The Wednesday-30y read is pattern-derived, not sourced.** Treasury's tentative auction schedule
  does not reach November 2027. The precedent is 2 of 2 on the exact shape and 12 of 16 on the
  Thursday norm it departs from, but it is inference, and it is registered as a forward test with a
  falsifier rather than written into the calendar as an event.
- **Leg 7's refutation is a population test on n=17 Novembers**, with the two analogs inside that
  population. It says no *general* settlement-day edge exists; it does not say 2027-11-15 will be
  quiet, and it cannot — which is why the forward test is registered at LOW confidence.
- **The Thursday subset is n=3 for TLT and n=5 for SPY**, and LQD's paired gap does not replicate on
  it (leg 6). Nothing in this ledger rests on the Thursday subset as evidence; the weekday matters
  here for a *structural* reason (refunding week), not a statistical one.
- **The ETF-liquidity finding is about ETFs, not the cash market.** It measures share volume in the
  listed proxies while the underlying is shut, and says nothing about cash Treasury or credit depth,
  spreads or impact on 2027-11-11.
- **SPY thins too**, so the closure-attributable part is the *gap*, a paired median with n=18 / n=24 —
  not a regression with controls.
- **The Veterans Day sample assumes the recommendation held historically.** SIFMA publishes 2026 and
  2027 only; that every November-11 weekday back to 1993 was also a recommended full closure was not
  verified against an archive. Treasury's 47-year zero (leg 2) is strong indirect support for the
  government-market leg of that assumption, and nothing more.
- **Both analogs sat in distinctive macro regimes** — 2021-11-10 carried a 6.2% CPI print alongside
