## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Stand aside — and read this date as a *calendar* fact, not a *tape* fact.** Because
September 2027's 12th is a Sunday, this payroll lands on the **second** Friday of October, and the
one consequence that survives every control is pure arithmetic: it leaves **5 trading sessions** to
[`opex-2027-10-15`](opex-2027-10-15.md) instead of the usual **10** (n=33 vs 371, exact). An October
front-month contract therefore carries the payroll with half the normal runway, and the print's share
of that window's realized variance quadruples in SPY (**21.5% vs 5.7%**, 3.76×, *p*=0.0007, robust in
33 of 33 leave-one-out drops and in both eras). **That is the window shrinking, not the market
changing** — per-session realized vol inside the window is flat (SPY 0.80×, *p*=0.13), the print's own
move is flat (all *p* ≥ 0.13), and matching both sides to a 5-session window collapses the effect to
1.96× (*p*=0.085) and to 1.17× (*p*=0.36) once the March-heavy cases come out. On seasonality this

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This id reached the calendar as a proposal from [`jobs-2027-09-03`](jobs-2027-09-03.md)'s own initial
research, filed at D+35 to make that lane's "last payroll before the September SEP meeting" leg
checkable. The proposal handed forward two things: a warning that **the date looks wrong and is not**
(a second Friday, where every other payroll on this calendar is a first Friday), and an explicit,
honestly-flagged hole — *"its seasonal profile is untested here… October was not measured by either
and must not be assumed to resemble the month on either side of it."*

Four questions follow. **Does the derived date survive an independent re-derivation, and is the
primary retrievable yet?** **What is October's seasonal profile, given that its two neighbours were
measured and came out opposite to each other?** **Does the second-Friday configuration do anything
measurable — and if so, is it the tape or the calendar?** And: **this print's D+3 is a bond-market
closure with the equity tape open; does the sibling's long-weekend duration finding transfer to an
asymmetric one?**

**One-line verdict:** the date reproduces 7-for-7 and stays `estimate` because every BLS route 403s;
October is a **third** profile — equity-side, rates dead last — so neither sibling's instruction
transfers; the second-Friday configuration produces one large, robust, **entirely arithmetic** effect
(half the runway to opex) and **no** behavioural one, which is why it is stated as a constraint rather
than an edge; and the asymmetric closure is the **inverse** of the sibling's structure, with every
duration leg at or below 1.0×.

### Method

- **Attempted and SUCCEEDED.** `federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm` returned

- **Measured, with Yahoo split/dividend-adjusted daily bars** (the source

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The date reproduces independently — SUPPORTED, and it stays `estimate`.** The rule (release on
   the third Friday following the end of the Sun–Sat reference week containing the 12th) was coded
   from its definition and run over 1993–2028 before being applied. It reproduces **7 of 7** knowns:
   the four `confirmed` entries on this calendar (2026-09-04, 2026-10-02, 2026-11-06, 2026-12-04)
   and the three historical Sunday-July-4 releases (2004-07-02, 2010-07-02, 2021-07-02). September
   2027's 12th is a **Sunday**, so the reference week is Sun 09-12 → Sat 09-18 and the third Friday
   after 09-18 is **2027-10-08**. It stays `estimate` on two counts: no primary was retrievable (five
   blocked fetches above), and this lane does not self-confirm.

2. **The proposal's analogue list is short by one — CORRECTED.** It named 1993, 2004, 2010 and 2021
   as the Sunday-September-12th years resolving to an October 8 release. Run over 1993–2027 the rule
   also emits **1999-10-08**, and 1999-09-12 was indeed a Sunday. The analogue set is **five**, not
   four: 1993, **1999**, 2004, 2010, 2021. Small, and banked because a later lane would otherwise
   inherit an undercount as fact.

3. **The rarity framing is overstated in one place and understated in another — MIXED.** The
   proposal's warning that the date *looks* wrong is correct and worth keeping. But a second-Friday
   release is **34 of 419** releases 1993–2027 (**8.1%**), not an oddity — and it is structurally
   confined to five release-months: **March 13**, then May, July, October and December at **5 each**;
   it is impossible in Jan/Feb/Apr/Jun/Aug/Sep/Nov. Meanwhile the **35-day inter-print gap** the
   proposal treated as part of the same anomaly is **146 of 419 (35%)** and is entirely ordinary.
   The unusual thing about this date is the *day-of-month*, not the gap.

4. **The "more accumulated news → bigger print" hypothesis is REFUTED, and the sign runs the other
   way.** A 35-day gap means the tape has gone five weeks without a payroll. The natural inference is
   a larger reaction. Tested on **G35 (n=140) vs G28 (n=263)** print-day \|move\|, **all nine
   instruments came in at or below 1.0×**: IEF **0.72×** (*p*=0.035), TLT 0.79× (0.065), QQQ 0.83×,
   LQD 0.84×, HYG 0.85×, AGG 0.87×, SPY 0.88×, SHY 0.92×, ^VIX 0.98×. One nominal hit in nine at
   α=0.05 is exactly the expected false-positive count, so **no reverse effect is claimed** — what is
   claimed is that the hypothesis in its stated direction finds nothing. Release-month balance was
   checked and the G35 set spreads across all twelve months (10–15 each except April's 1).

6. **The second Friday leaves exactly half the runway to opex — SUPPORTED, and it is arithmetic, not

   **2027-10-08** → [`opex-2027-10-15`](opex-2027-10-15.md), one week. **This is the whole of what is
   special about the date and it needs no statistics at all.**
