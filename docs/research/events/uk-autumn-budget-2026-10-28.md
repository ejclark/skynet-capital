## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **The date is real; the event is not ours.** HM Treasury's own letter to the Treasury
Select Committee — gov.uk, 31 July 2026, fetched direct today — says "The Budget will be held on
**28 October 2026**," and every Budget this session could corroborate falls on a Wednesday, as does
this one. What the date is worth to a US equity book is **measurably nothing**: across the **9**
gov.uk-corroborated Budget days since 2014, against every non-Budget Wednesday since 2014, the S&P
500's mean absolute move was **1.00x** an ordinary Wednesday's and the US 10-year's **1.05x**
(Welch **t = -0.11** and **-0.16** on the matched test); the week after is **1.01x** and **1.07x**.
The gilt→Treasury pipe *is* real — **-6.08bp** of US 10y per **+1%** gilt move, **t = -45.3**,
R² = **0.308**, n = **4,610** — but no Budget has ever delivered a gilt move large enough to open
it. **The one operational fact about 2026-10-28 is that it is also FOMC day** (`fomc-2026-10-28`,
impact high), with the speech at **08:30 ET** and the Fed at **14:00 ET**. So this entry's job is
to be **ruled out** as the cause of a 10-28 move, not to be traded. `symbols: []`, no house
playbook is fiscal-, rates- or sterling-keyed. Date is **estimate**; it widens caution and
licenses nothing.

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This event reached the calendar by **placement**, not by demand: the
[`boe-decision-2026-09-17`](boe-decision-2026-09-17.md) lane discovered it on 2026-09-09 while
sweeping the gilt-supply question, and filed it as
`proposals/uk-autumn-budget-2026-10-28.from-boe-decision-2026-09-17.json`. This session reads that
proposal, re-fetches its primaries rather than inheriting them, and writes the canonical file.

So the question is not "what will be in the Budget" — a US equity book has no way to express a view
on UK income-tax thresholds — but: **is a UK fiscal set-piece ever a US market event, and is this
one?**

**One-line verdict:** a UK Budget is a **gilt** event and, measurably, **not** a US one; the single
modern counter-example broke the *institution* (a Friday statement with no OBR forecast) rather than
merely raising taxes, and neither of its structural markers is present on 2026-10-28 — which lands
on **FOMC day**, making this entry's real job an attribution rule rather than a trade.

**Method.** Sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md), plus a
first-party event study run against this repo's own market-data layer
(`scripts/research/market-data.mjs`, split/dividend-adjusted daily bars). Primaries fetched direct
2026-09-09 (all HTTP 200): HM Treasury's TSC letter and its companion news release on gov.uk, the
gov.uk search API (HM Treasury organisation feed and the `topical_event` index),
`legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2011/4/section/4`, and Chancellor John Healey's 2026-09-07 growth speech.
`earnings-cycle.mjs` / `intraday-edges.mjs` were **not** run — `symbols: []`, no issuer, and neither
has a macro mode. **Six fetches failed and are recorded in `probe-ref.blocked` rather than
substituted silently**: `obr.uk` (403, twice), the Commons Library briefing SN00813 in both HTML and
PDF (403), `ifs.org.uk` (403), and `dmo.gov.uk` (302 into a ShieldSquare bot wall, not followed).
Those five would have been the authoritative *list* of historical fiscal-event dates and the
authoritative source on the DMO financing remit; their loss is the sharpest limit on this document
and is stated again below.

### Conviction legs, tested

**1. The date is right, and it is better-sourced than its `NEWS:` prefix admits.** **SUPPORTED**,
from a government primary rather than the professional-services publishers the proposal relied on.
`gov.uk/government/publications/chancellor-letter-to-the-treasury-select-committee-tsc-budget-2026-date`
(HM Treasury, published **31 July 2026**, fetched 2026-09-09) states: *"The Budget will be held on 28
October 2026."* Its companion release the same day names *"Wednesday 28 October"* and the Chancellor
as **John Healey MP**, adding *"It will be built on fiscal discipline"* and *"It will meet our fiscal
rules."* The label stays `estimate` because `market-events-data.ts`'s confirmed-prefix list has no
slot for HM Treasury — a taxonomy gap, not a doubt (see *Honest limits*).

**2. The weekday convention corroborates it mechanically, without trusting any list.** **SUPPORTED**,
and this leg exists because the authoritative date lists were all blocked. Querying the gov.uk search
API's `topical_event` index gave ten Budget pages whose timestamp is plausibly the delivery day —
2014-03-19, 2015-07-08, 2016-03-16, 2017-11-22, 2020-03-11, 2021-03-03, 2021-10-27, 2024-03-06,
2024-10-30, 2025-11-26. **All ten are Wednesdays** (the post-PMQs convention), and **2026-10-28 is a
Wednesday**. That is the same shape of check the `labor-day-market-closure-2026-09-07` entry ran with
5 U.S.C. 6103: a structural regularity that a wrong date would violate.

**3. "A Budget is a volatile day for US markets" — REFUTED, and the belief traces to one confound.**
The naive read of all ten corroborated Budget days says the S&P 500 moves **1.57x** its baseline and
the VIX **1.71x**. Both numbers are carried entirely by **2020-03-11** — Budget 2020 fell on the day
the WHO declared a pandemic, and the S&P closed **-4.89%**. Drop that one session and, against a
control of **every non-Budget Wednesday since 2014**:

| Series | D-5..D-1 | D0 | D+1..D+5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| S&P 500 | 0.69x | **1.00x** | 1.01x |
| US 10y (^TNX) | 0.86x | **1.05x** | 1.07x |
| VIX | 0.88x | 1.31x | 1.01x |
| UK gilt ETF (IGLT.L) | 1.35x | **1.35x** | 0.91x |

(n = 9 events, n = 643–646 control Wednesdays. Matched Welch tests on |move|: S&P **t = -0.11**,
US 10y **t = -0.16**, FTSE 100 **t = -0.64**, GBP/USD **t = -0.80**.) The S&P around a UK Budget is
**indistinguishable from an ordinary Wednesday at every horizon** — and the run-up is *quieter*.
Only the VIX leans, at 1.31x on the day, and it is gone by the following week.

**4. Gilts do respond — but as a resolution event, not a shock.** **SUPPORTED.** IGLT.L runs
**1.35x** an ordinary Wednesday both into and on the Budget, then **0.91x** in the week after —
*below* a control Wednesday's following week. The scaling makes the point sharper than the ratio
does: an iid random walk predicts a 5-day window ≈ **2.24x** a 1-day window, and the control set
delivers **2.04x**; the post-Budget gilt week delivers **1.38x**. Uncertainty is being *resolved*,
not created. Contrast the same test on the US series, which land on **2.26x** (10y) and **2.25x**
(S&P) — dead on the random-walk benchmark, i.e. **no post-Budget drift in US assets whatsoever**.

**5. The gilt→Treasury pipe is real and strong.** **SUPPORTED**, and this is what makes the null
interesting rather than trivial. Regressing daily Δ^TNX (bp) on IGLT.L return (%), 2008-01→2026-09:
**b = -6.08bp per +1% gilt price, t = -45.3, R² = 0.308, n = 4,610**. Roughly **31%** of daily US
10-year variance co-moves with gilts. In the tail it is louder still: on the **worst 1%** of gilt
days (return ≤ **-1.30%**, n = 46) the US 10-year rose a mean **+6.7bp**, **37 of 46 positive**,
against an all-day mean of **-0.01bp**. A gilt rout does reach the US long end. The question is only
whether a Budget can cause one.

**6. It cannot — no Budget in the sample got near the tail threshold.** **SUPPORTED.** The worst
Budget-day gilt sell-off in the corroborated set is **-1.13%** (2020-03-11, itself the COVID
confound); the next worst is **-1.00%**. The only move past ±1.30% was **2021-10-27's +2.04%** — a
**rally**, i.e. a Budget that delivered *less* supply than the market feared. Nine Budgets, zero
tail-threshold sell-offs. A 1.35x multiple on a 0.41% baseline is 0.55%; the pipe opens at 1.30%.

**7. The 2022 exception, dissected — and why it does not transfer.** **SUPPORTED, with a named
caveat.** The 2022-09-23 "mini-budget" window (2022-09-22 → 2022-10-14) is unambiguously a US
market event: IGLT.L **-7.54%** close-to-close and **-9.53%** peak-to-trough, US 10y **+30bp**,
S&P 500 **-4.65%**, FTSE **-4.20%**, VIX **+5pts**. It also carried two structural markers that
**none** of the ten corroborated Budgets share and that 2026-10-28 does not have: it was delivered
on a **Friday**, and it published **no OBR forecast**. And the transmission was *lagged* — day by
day:

| Date | Gilt ETF | US 10y | S&P 500 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022-09-23 (the statement) | -2.65% | **-1bp** | -1.72% |
| 2022-09-26 | -3.01% | **+18bp** | -1.03% |
| 2022-09-27 | -4.18% | **+9bp** | -0.21% |
| 2022-09-28 (BoE intervention) | +7.19% | -26bp | +1.97% |

The US did **not** move on the day; it moved on D+1 and D+2, as the gilt sell-off compounded. **The
caveat, stated rather than buried:** two markers on one observation is a pattern of size 1. It is a
*tell to watch*, not an established cause — a Wednesday Budget with an OBR forecast can still break
a gilt market, and this ledger does not claim otherwise. What it claims is that the base rate is a
null and the single exception is visibly atypical.

**8. "The most stressed long end in the G7" — MIXED, and the qualifier matters.** The sibling
[`boe-decision-2026-09-17`](boe-decision-2026-09-17.md) ledger records a **30-year gilt at 5.89%**
(early September, highest since March 1998) and a 2056 syndication clearing **5.8168%** on
**2026-09-08**, the highest at any DMO auction since 1998 — all press-cited at their as-of dates in
that document, and inherited here rather than re-fetched. Measured independently, the tradeable gilt
tape does **not** look like a market in trouble: IGLT.L 20-day realized vol **4.9%**, 60-day **5.4%**,
against an **8.3%** full-sample; the index is at the **74th percentile** of its own range since 2024
and the **59th** since 2021, having moved **-1.06%** over the last 60 sessions. A **high yield level
is not a disorderly market**, and the tail leg above needs vol, not a level. **The limit on this
leg, stated plainly:** IGLT.L is an *all-maturity* gilt index, so it structurally under-weights
exactly the 30-year segment the sibling ledger's stress lives in. Both readings can be true.

**9. Political novelty is present, and is not a price signal.** **MIXED.** This is a genuinely
unusual Budget: gov.uk primaries fetched today record *"Keir Starmer's final speech as Prime
Minister: 20 July 2026"* and *"Andy Burnham's first speech as Prime Minister: 20 July 2026"* the
same day, with **John Healey** replacing **Rachel Reeves** at the Treasury within days (his speech
to Treasury staff is dated 2026-07-22; Reeves's Mansion House speech is dated 2026-07-14). So
2026-10-28 is a **new Prime Minister's and a new Chancellor's first Budget, 100 days into a
leadership change**, and the 2022 analogue was also a new-PM/new-Chancellor first fiscal event. That
is the reason to keep watching rather than to stop. But the 2022 damage is attributable to a
specific institutional break — no OBR forecast, off-convention scheduling — not to novelty as such,
and the Chancellor's own 2026-09-07 growth speech commits to *"balancing the books, with a buffer to
protect against uncertainty"* and *"controlling borrowing to bear down on inflation."* Novelty
raises the variance of what is announced; it is not itself a trade.

### What plays the conditions support

**None, and that is the finding.** There is no entry, exit, hedge or sizing decision this event
justifies at any horizon. What it produces instead is one piece of durable machinery:

- **An attribution rule for 2026-10-28.** The Budget speech is at **08:30 ET** (the UK moves to GMT
  on 2026-10-25; the US stays on EDT until 2026-11-01), the FOMC statement at **14:00 ET**. The
  measured Budget contribution to a US session is **1.00x** an ordinary Wednesday. Credit the Fed,
  the 10-29 GDP/PCE pair, or the 11-02 Treasury borrowing estimates before crediting Westminster.
- **A single dated trigger** that would overturn all of the above: IGLT.L ≤ **-1.30%** on the day or
  in the four sessions after.

### Honest limits

- **The five blocked sources are the real limit.** `obr.uk`, the Commons Library briefing (HTML and
  PDF), `ifs.org.uk` and `dmo.gov.uk` were all refused this session. The historical Budget-date list
  is therefore *reconstructed* from the gov.uk `topical_event` index rather than read from an
  authoritative table — which is why leg 2 leans on weekday arithmetic as a check, and why the
  sample is **9–10 events, not the full ~30** UK fiscal events since 2010. Dates I could not
  corroborate (2018-10-29, 2022-09-23, 2022-11-17, 2023-03-15, 2023-11-22 and others) are used
  **only** in the 2022 case study, never in the statistics.
- **n = 9 is a small sample.** A 1.00x point estimate with t = -0.11 is strong evidence *against a
  large effect*, not proof of exactly zero. It rules out the effect sizes that would matter here; it
  does not rule out a 10bp tail.
- **IGLT.L is a proxy, not the gilt curve.** All-maturity, LSE-listed, and it stops trading at 16:30
  London — so a post-16:30 gilt move on Budget afternoon lands in the *next* session's bar.
- **The DMO financing-remit revision is named as the mechanism but not sourced.** The remit is
  conventionally revised alongside the Budget, and that supply number — not the tax measures — is the
  plausible route from a Budget to a gilt move (2021-10-27's +2.04% rally is the shape of it). The
  DMO site is behind a bot wall for this lane, so no remit figure is asserted anywhere in this
  document.
- **The OBR forecast is press-reported, not fetched.** The statutory floor was read direct
  (`legislation.gov.uk`, BRNAA 2011 **s.4(3)**: forecasts *"on at least two occasions for each
  financial year"*), which makes an autumn EFO the norm — it does **not** legally bind the OBR to
  publish on 2026-10-28 specifically. Treat "OBR forecast same day" as expectation, not fact.
- **The confirmed-prefix taxonomy has no slot for a foreign finance ministry.** This is now the
  eighth non-US entry on this calendar filed `estimate` for that reason alone rather than for any
  doubt about its date (`boe-decision-*`, `uk-cpi-*`, `uk-labour-market-*`, `sifma-uk-*`, this one).
  Widening the taxonomy edits `market-events-data.ts`, `event-scan.mjs --validate` and its spec, and
  is out of this lane's one-PR scope — flagged here so the pattern is on the record rather than
  re-discovered a ninth time.

## Stance & kill switches

**Stance (2026-09-09, D-49).** **Stand aside, permanently unless the gilt tail opens.** The UK
Autumn Budget of **2026-10-28** (`estimate`; the date itself is gov.uk-primary and the label is a
taxonomy artifact) is measurably a non-event for this book: **1.00x** an ordinary Wednesday for the
S&P 500 and **1.05x** for the US 10-year across nine corroborated Budgets, with **no** post-event
drift in either. It is carried on this calendar for exactly one reason — **it shares a date with
`fomc-2026-10-28`**, and a session reading a 10-28 tape needs to know that the Westminster headline
crossing the wire at 08:30 ET has a measured contribution of zero. `symbols: []`, no house playbook
is fiscal-, rates- or sterling-keyed, and no position is or will be sized to this.

**What would change it, dated:**

- **The tail opens** — IGLT.L closes **≤ -1.30%** on 2026-10-28 or within the four sessions after
  (through **2026-11-04**). That is the measured worst-1% threshold at which the US 10-year
  historically rises **+6.7bp** (37/46). Registered as **FT-uk-autumn-budget-2026-10-28-1**.
- **The 2022 shape recurs** — cumulative IGLT.L move over **2026-10-28 → 2026-11-04** worse than
  **-3%**, against **-7.54%** in the 2022 window. Registered as **FT-uk-autumn-budget-2026-10-28-2**.
- **The date or its forecast moves** — the Budget is not delivered on **2026-10-28**, or is delivered
  without an accompanying OBR forecast. The second is the 2022 marker, and it would move this entry's
  impact tier the same session. Registered as **FT-uk-autumn-budget-2026-10-28-3**.
- **The quiet-market premise dies** — IGLT.L 20-day realized vol prints **≥ 8.3%** (its full-sample
  rate) before 2026-10-28. Not a forward test; a re-test trigger for leg 8.
- **The supply corridor rearranges** — the **2026-09-17** MPC defers its annual QT decision to
  **11-05**, putting the Bank's supply number *after* the Chancellor's rather than seven weeks
  before it. That changes the sequencing this ledger's "This week" row assumes.
