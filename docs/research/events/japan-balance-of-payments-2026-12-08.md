## At a glance

**TL;DR.** **Stand aside — and bank two things this print is not, plus one thing it might be.**
This session harvested **104 elapsed Balance-of-Payments releases (2013-01-11 → 2026-08-10)** out of
20 captures of the Bank of Japan's own release-schedule sheet and measured them against **3,342
Nikkei sessions**. What looks at first like a real effect — Tokyo's session range **1.39× on release
days, p = 0.005** — is mostly a **day-of-month artifact**: BoP releases cluster on the 8th-14th, that
window is itself **1.18× louder (p = 0.002)** than the rest of the month, and against a
weekday-stratified, day-matched control the effect falls to **1.25× (p = 0.048)** and then to
**1.35× at p = 0.32** on the tightest control of all. It is also **entirely pre-2020** (1.93×,

JST pre-open release — **the opening gap — is null under every control** (1.05×-1.13×, p = 0.28
to 0.75). **The one finding that survives everything is in the yen, and it is a tail, not a
median.** USD/JPY's median move on BoP days is exactly ordinary (**1.01×, p = 0.899**), but
**≥1% USD/JPY days are 2.2× more frequent** — **0.167 vs 0.076** on a matched mid-month control,

## Initial research

### The question, plainly

This event was created on 2026-09-05 by the [December CGPI ledger](japan-cgpi-2026-12-10.md)'s
adjacency sweep, which decoded every serial in the Bank of Japan's release-schedule sheet falling in
the 2026-12-05 … 12-15 corridor and found Japan's current-account print sitting two days before the
one it was researching. Its notes filed this event as *"the yen-relevant half of Japan's December
statistical corridor"* and as *"the LAST full Japanese bar before the December FOMC."* Nothing was
measured about it, and the second half of that description is wrong.

Three questions, and the third is the one this ledger exists for:

1. **Does Japan's Balance of Payments move anything this calendar can see?** It is the one Japanese
   monthly print with a direct claim on the currency — a current-account balance is an FX flow
   statistic in a way a producer-price index is not.
2. **Given that 2026-12-08 sits one calendar day before the December FOMC, could a move on that bar
   ever be attributed to the BoP at all?**
3. **Is the corridor's structure as the parent ledger described it?**

**One-line verdict: no on the Tokyo open, no on the attribution, and no on the structure — but the
yen answer is a genuine "not quite." The opening gap is a null under every control this session
could construct (1.05×-1.13×, p = 0.28 to 0.75 across four nested pools), and the apparent 1.39×
session effect (p = 0.005) degrades to non-significance the moment the day-of-month band is matched
and vanishes entirely after 2019. USD/JPY's median release-day move is likewise ordinary (1.01×,
p = 0.899) — but ≥1% USD/JPY days occur at 0.167 on BoP releases against 0.076 on a matched
mid-month control (p = 0.006), a 2.2× tail that is era-stable and is not a payrolls echo. And the
structural claim this event was filed on does not survive the clock: Tokyo trades 2026-12-09 too, so
12-08 is the second-to-last session before the FOMC, not the last — a distinction that matters
because the quiet cell is measurably the earlier one (D−1 at 0.78×, p = 0.024; D at 0.96×,
p = 0.642).**

**Method:** sourced web research per [`EVENT-RESEARCH.md`](../../process/EVENT-RESEARCH.md) plus

(2026-09-05), never through a summariser:

### Conviction legs, tested

1. **The sample is primary and conflict-free, but it is a *scheduled*-date series — SUPPORTED with a
   named limit.** 114 reference months in four contiguous runs (2012-11→2013-10 ×12,
   2015-05→2016-04 ×12, 2019-05→2020-10 ×18, 2021-05→2027-04 ×72); **104 elapsed**, 2013-01-11 →
   2026-08-10, every one carrying a Nikkei bar. That covers **104 of the 165 months (63%)** in the
   study span. **17 of the 114** carry a capture taken *after* their scheduled date and all 17 agree
   — the Bank replaces this sheet each June and December, and a December replacement is always
   entirely forward-looking, which is why the confirmation count is not higher. No source this
   session could reach publishes a historical *actual*-date list for this statistic. See limits.

2. **The BoP does not move the Tokyo open — SUPPORTED, and it is the headline.**

   | Measure (Nikkei) | BoP (n=104) | Pool (n=3,238) | Ratio | p |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | \|opening gap\| | 0.559% | 0.494% | **1.13×** | 0.280 |
   | \|close-to-close\| | 0.918% | 0.683% | 1.34× | 0.017 |
   | \|intraday\| | 0.639% | 0.459% | 1.39× | 0.005 |

   The last two rows look like a finding. Legs 3-5 dismantle them; the first row survives every
   control and is the one that speaks to an 08:50 pre-open release.

3. **The apparent session effect is largely a day-of-month artifact — SUPPORTED, and it is a new
   seasonal for this calendar.** BoP releases cluster hard: **56 of 104 fall on the 8th**, 84 on days
   8-10, all 104 on days 8-14. Tokyo's own month has structure, measured here on \|intraday\| against
   the rest of the month:

   | Calendar days | n | \|intraday\| | Rest of month | Ratio | p |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | 1-7 | 741 | 0.484% | 0.456% | 1.06× | 0.256 |
   | **8-10** | 339 | 0.525% | 0.457% | **1.15×** | 0.052 |
   | **11-14** | 434 | 0.524% | 0.457% | **1.15×** | 0.036 |
   | 15-21 | 784 | 0.452% | 0.464% | 0.98× | 0.631 |
   | **22-31** | 1,044 | 0.415% | 0.486% | **0.85×** | **0.003** |

   Comparing a mid-month print to the whole year therefore borrows a loud denominator, exactly the
   error class the [CGPI ledger](japan-cgpi-2026-12-10.md) named for December cuts.

4. **Matched, the effect degrades monotonically — SUPPORTED.** Four nested pools, tightest last:

   | Control pool | \|gap\| | p | \|c2c\| | p | \|intra\| | p |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | all sessions (n=3,238) | 1.13× | 0.280 | 1.34× | 0.017 | 1.39× | 0.005 |
   | days 8-14 (n=669) | 1.09× | 0.393 | 1.26× | 0.068 | 1.25× | 0.064 |
   | days 8-14, weekday-stratified | 1.09× | 0.434 | 1.26× | 0.071 | 1.25× | 0.048 |
   | same calendar day (the 8th; n=58) | 1.05× | 0.752 | 1.47× | 0.058 | 1.35× | 0.315 |

   The weekday stratification matters because the sample is Monday-heavy (Mon 34 · Tue 25 · Wed 16 ·
   Thu 15 · Fri 14) against a band that is not, and a Monday session carries a weekend.

5. **And what survives is pre-2020 only — SUPPORTED, and it is why this is MIXED rather than a
   finding.** Against the matched band, on \|intraday\|: **2013-2019 1.93×, p = 0.001** (n=30) vs
   **2020-2026 1.08×, p = 0.653** (n=74). Jackknifing one calendar year at a time holds the pooled
   ratio between **1.21 and 1.45** — no single year carries it — but the era split is not a single
   year, and the loud half is the sparse, discontinuous half of the series.

6. **No weekday cell survives — SUPPORTED.**

7. **The yen's median is a clean null — SUPPORTED, and it is the leg most people would guess wrong.**
   USD/JPY \|close-to-close\| **0.310% vs 0.306% = 1.01×, p = 0.899** (n=96); against the day-8-14

   *The average current-account print is an ordinary day for the currency it measures flows in.*

8. **But the yen's tail is not — MIXED, and it is the single most interesting result here.**
   P(USD/JPY \|c2c\| ≥ 1.0%) on BoP release days is **0.167**, against **0.076** on the matched
   day-8-14 band (**p = 0.006**) and 0.082 pool-wide (p = 0.006). Three robustness checks, all
   passed:

   | Check | BoP | Control | p |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | ≥1.0%, matched band | 0.167 (n=96) | 0.076 (n=723) | **0.006** |
   | ≥1.0%, 2013-2019 / 2020-2026 | 0.179 / 0.162 | 0.090 / 0.060 | — (era-stable) |
   | ≥1.0%, excluding payrolls-echo sessions | 0.165 (n=85) | 0.072 (n=679) | **0.007** |

   The payrolls check was run because BoP days skew Monday and the first Tokyo session after a US
   jobs Friday is a known fat-tail day (0.165 in this pool) — but only **11 of 96** BoP days are one,
   and dropping them changes nothing. The effect is confined to that bucket: ≥0.75% gives
   0.219 vs 0.156 (p = 0.139) and ≥1.5% gives 0.042 vs 0.024 (p = 0.297). **A median-null,
   fat-tail signature is what a release that matters only when it surprises would look like** — and
   this session has no surprise series to test that with, so the mechanism is a conjecture. It is
   reported, registered, and traded on by nobody.

9. **2026-12-08 is the second-to-last Tokyo session before the FOMC, not the last — SUPPORTED from
   the clock, and it corrects this event's own notes and the ledger that filed them.** The
   [December FOMC](fomc-2026-12-09.md) statement lands 14:00 ET Wed 2026-12-09 = 04:00 JST Thu
   12-10. Tokyo's 12-08 cash session runs 09:00-15:00 JST 12-08 = 19:00 ET 12-07 → 01:00 ET 12-08,
   closing ~**37 hours** before the statement. Tokyo then trades **12-09**, closing ~13 hours before
   it. The [CGPI ledger](japan-cgpi-2026-12-10.md) called this bar *"the mirror bar, the last full
   Tokyo session **before** the FOMC"*; the mirror framing is right and the superlative is not.

10. **The distinction is not pedantic, because the two cells measure differently — SUPPORTED.**
    Across 2006-2026, Tokyo sessions keyed to a scheduled FOMC decision date D (pool n≈4,905):

    | Cell | n | \|gap\| | Pool | Ratio | p | \|intraday\| ratio | p |
    |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

    | **D** (last before) | 149 | 0.494% | 0.517% | 0.96× | 0.642 | 0.97× | 0.784 |
    | **D+1** (first after) | 150 | 0.558% | 0.515% | 1.08× | 0.419 | **1.29×** | **0.011** |

    The **D−1** row replicates the CGPI ledger's reported 0.77× at p = 0.026 on n=152 almost exactly
    — on an independently re-derived FOMC list — and so does the **D+1** row (it reported 1.06× and
    1.25×). **The quiet cell is D−1; the session that actually abuts the statement is flat.** A
    December-only cut of D−1 goes further the same way (n=20, **0.63×**) but at p = 0.156 it is an
    observation, not a claim.

11. **This coincidence has no precedent, and there is a mechanical reason — SUPPORTED.** No release
    in the 104 observed ones landed on an FOMC D−1, D or D+1 Tokyo bar. Reconstructing the release
    date for **every** month 2013-01 → 2026-08 from leg 13's rule (which fits 104/104) still yields
    **zero** D−1 hits. Extending the reconstruction to 2006 yields exactly **three** —
    **2007-12-10, 2010-08-09, 2011-08-08** — which are precisely two days before the three CGPI
    post-FOMC coincidences the sibling found, the two releases being locked ~2 days apart by their
    respective rules. The reason the drought ran 15 years: **2026-12-09 is the earliest December
    FOMC decision date in 2006-2026** (the range is the 9th to the 19th; next-earliest is
    2025-12-10), and the BoP prints on the month's 6th trading day.

12. **The three historical instances, reported as an anecdote and not a base rate — SUPPORTED as a
    limit.** Their dates are *reconstructed*, not observed, and one sits inside the 2011 US-downgrade
    week:

    | BoP (reconstructed) | FOMC | Prior US session | Nikkei gap | Nikkei intraday |
    |---|---|---|---|---|
    | 2007-12-10 | 2007-12-11 | −0.18% | +0.32% | −0.52% |

    | 2011-08-08 | 2011-08-09 | −0.06% | −1.40% | −0.79% |

    Two of three gapped past 1% while the US session that preceded them barely moved — which is what

13. **The release date is mechanically determined — SUPPORTED, and it is the tightest date rule this
    calendar has measured.** **103 of 104** releases fall on the **6th Tokyo trading day** of their
    month (one on the 5th). The single exception, **2020-10-08**, resolves exactly: the Tokyo Stock

    for it, and MOF/BoJ counted it as the scheduled business day it was — so on **scheduled** trading
    days the rule is **104/104**. Calendar-business-day indexing fits far worse (6th ×82, 7th ×14,
    8th ×6, 9th ×9, 10th ×3), so the rule tracks the exchange calendar. **2026-12-08 is December
    2026's 6th** (JPX lists no December closure before the 31st), matching serial 46364; **2027-01-12
    is January 2027's 6th** (Jan 1-3 and Coming-of-Age Day Jan 11 closed), matching serial 46399.
    Two independent methods, two agreements — and three primaries on the date itself.

### Honest limits

**This is a scheduled-date series, not an actual-date series — the biggest limit here, and it is
weaker provenance than the sibling had.** The CGPI ledger worked from the Bank's published list of
releases *that had happened*; no equivalent exists for the Balance of Payments that this session
could reach, so all 104 dates are what the Bank and the Ministry said they *would* publish. Twenty
captures spanning 2013-2026 conflict on **zero** of the 114 reference months, the two ministries'
pages agree with the sheet on all five forward dates, and **17** months carry a capture taken after
their own release date — but a release that quietly slipped a day would be invisible to every check
available. **The series covers 63% of the months in its span** (104 of 165), in four runs with two
gaps; leg 11's "no precedent" is therefore stated on the *reconstructed* series, and the three
pre-2013 instances in leg 12 are reconstructions, not observations. **This is a release-day study,
not a surprise study** — no consensus or whisper series for Japan's current account was available, so
every leg pools large and small surprises, and leg 8's fat tail is exactly what a
surprise-conditional effect would look like from the outside. That mechanism is a **conjecture**, and
the honest claim is *"≥1% yen days are twice as common on these dates,"* never *"the BoP moves the
yen."* **Roughly 50 cuts were run and every p is unadjusted**; a Bonferroni haircut over 50 turns
p = 0.006 into 0.3 and p = 0.003 into 0.15, so **no single p-value here carries a claim** — leg 8
rests on surviving three independent robustness checks, not on its p, and the headline (leg 2) is a
**null**, which multiplicity cannot manufacture. **The FOMC cells are associations, not
identifications**, spanning several Fed regimes. **The date rule's one exception is explained, not
tested** — the TSE-outage account fits, and it was constructed after seeing the miss. **The Nikkei
and USD/JPY session calendars come from Yahoo's `^N225` and `JPY=X` daily series**, and `JPY=X` is a
24-hour market whose daily boundary is not Tokyo's close, which makes leg 7/8's close-to-close a
coarse instrument for an 08:50 JST event. **Japan's BoP was restated from BPM5 to BPM6 in 2014**,
inside this sample; pooling across that break is acceptable for a release-day event study and would
not be for a level study. **The date is `estimate`** despite three primaries, per the taxonomy. And
this is **D-94** initial research on a statistic whose reference month has not ended: nothing here
forecasts the print, only what it is worth to a book that does not trade it.
