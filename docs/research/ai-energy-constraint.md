# AI's energy constraint — the map, the survivors, and what the market already paid

**Date:** 2026-08-12 · **Method:** eight supply-chain lenses (generation, grid/transformers,
turbines/on-site power, nuclear fuel, cooling, gas/midstream, a dedicated bear lens, and the
history of completed AI-constraint trades), each researched live (my training data ends Jan 2026 —

## The verdict in one paragraph

The energy constraint is **real, quantified, and mostly already paid.** After 2+ years as the
market's flagship theme, the red team killed 10 of 33 proposed opportunities outright and
downgraded nearly every "unpriced" claim to *priced-and-disputed* or relative value. Whole layers
produced zero survivors (regulated utilities, uranium miners, SMR developers — the last being the
confirmed false-positive class, −65/−78/−48% from their Oct-2025 hype peak). What survives is
**five names enterable now** (SPXC, CMI, BWXT, ET, and CEG at half-size), fourteen more behind
named entry conditions, and one measured, non-negotiable portfolio rule: **all of it is one trade
in eight costumes** — on the Jan-27-2025 DeepSeek efficiency scare, "diversified" picks across
every lens fell together in one session (VRT −30%, VST −28%, GEV −21.5%, CEG −21%) on zero change
in any physical constraint.

## How the sector works (the 5-minute version)

- **Two kinds of power company.** *Regulated utilities* (D, SO, DUK) earn a commission-set ~10%
  return on whatever they build — scarcity doesn't raise their profits, only their construction
  budgets. *Merchant generators / IPPs* (CEG, VST, TLN, NRG) sell at market prices in deregulated
  regions — PJM (13 mid-Atlantic states, home of Virginia's Data Center Alley) and ERCOT (Texas).
  When power gets scarce, the entire windfall flows to IPPs. That asymmetry is why 2024's AI-power
  trade was an IPP trade.
- **Three revenue streams for an IPP:** energy ($/MWh), *capacity* ($/MW-day from PJM's annual
  auction just for promising availability three years out — the purest public price of scarcity),
  and long-term **PPAs** with hyperscalers (the prize — e.g. Microsoft/Crane, Amazon/Comanche
  Peak; the new "bring-your-own-power" variant has the generator building a dedicated plant for
  one customer).
- **Money flow when AI demand hits:** hyperscaler → scarcity rent to IPPs → equipment margin to
  turbine/transformer/switchgear makers (GEV is effectively the only US-listed heavy-turbine
  maker; no US pure-play transformer maker exists) → wages to electrical contractors → bridge
  power to genset/fuel-cell makers → **last**, and only when plants actually burn fuel, to gas
  producers and pipelines. Pricing power sits wherever capacity is sold out furthest ahead.
- **The 2026 twist:** physical scarcity worsened while the *price signal got politically capped*.
  PJM's July auction cleared at the $325/MW-day legislated cap for the third straight year while
  procuring 6,831 MW below its own reliability requirement — PJM's simulation says the uncapped
  price would be $554–777. FERC locked that collar through **May 2030** with the White House,
  DOE, and all 13 PJM governors behind it. **The cap is the base case, not the tail** — any
  thesis that needs it to expire is a lottery ticket.

## Portfolio rules this study forces

1. **One-trade concentration:** cap the whole AI-energy sleeve (proposed: 25% of the paper
   book); size every position for a measured −20/−30% single-day gap. The common factor is
   hyperscaler capex (~$725B combined 2026, +77% YoY) — nothing in the sleeve diversifies it.
2. **The cap is the base case:** merchant cap-expiry legs get lottery-ticket sizing at most.
3. **Counterparty concentration is AI credit risk wearing utility clothes:** Oracle behind ET,
   xAI ~67% of SEI's book, single hyperscalers behind GNRC/NRG/MOD contracts.
4. **Enter on shock days, never on theme-headline strength** — the crowding census (per-name
   drawdown on the last efficiency-shock session) is the entry map.
5. **Master tripwire:** hyperscaler FY27 capex guidance, first read late Oct 2026. First
   guide-down or "digestion" language de-risks the entire sleeve at once. Secondary: the
   debt-financed share of that capex (9% FY24 → 32% mid-2026) — borrowed capex breaks faster.

## Honest limits

Every lens agent researched live against Aug-2026 sources, and the red teams still found a
systematic error pattern: wrong base-date anchors in three lenses, monthly-close "peaks"
understating true drawdowns by 10–15 points, and three theses stale at their own filing date.
All numbers above are the red-team-corrected versions, but treat any figure that matters for a
real decision as needing one more primary-source check. The constraint-clock template itself was
fit ex post on winners (see the method doc's standing caveat) — it is a taxonomy with an
unquantified error rate, not a validated predictor.
