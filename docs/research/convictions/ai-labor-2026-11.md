## The claims in full

- **1 · Salary to AI.** The claim is about employers' motive, and Challenger counts only the reason
  an employer *states*. A cut can fund AI without citing it, so this measures the stated reason, not
  the money. The stated reason has moved from AI (spring and summer) to the economy and falling
  demand (September and October: "The reasons employers give have shifted from technology to the
  economy", Andy Challenger). Tech still leads all industries, so in tech the claim's direction holds
  even as AI's own share falls.
- **2 · Microsoft.** Two programs, and #7033's brief merged them. April's buyout (~8,750 eligible,
  reported) picked by tenure, so it supports "cut salary cost" but not "remove the lower layers". The
  October offers to 60,000 are Eric's figure, as Challenger reports them; their eligibility rules are
  not in the report. Eric called them early-retirement packages; Challenger calls them voluntary
  exit offers. Both mean an offer, not a departure. The FY26 10-K's drop (−5,000) predates the
  October offers.
- **3 · Lower layers.** No instrument reads who is cut by proficiency. The 20–24 gap is the only free
  read on the jobs report and it swings 1 pt in a few months, so it can falsify a squeeze, never
  prove one is AI-driven.
- **4 · Tech layoffs.** Payrolls are falling slowly while announced cuts fall faster: firms are
  shrinking by attrition and hiring less, not by mass layoffs. That fits Challenger's "low-fire"
  reading and Microsoft's voluntary offers.
- **5 · Mass unemployment.** The Sahm rule fires at 0.50 (the 3-month average of U-3 at least 0.5
  pts above its low of the prior 12 months). At 0.00 it is as far from firing as it has been all year.
- **6 · Relief.** The midterm outcome is reported, not certified. Even certified, a relief bill needs
  the president's signature or a two-thirds override, which a ~54-seat Senate does not have. The
  claim may hold after 2029-01-20; this doc's horizon cannot reach it.
- **7 · AI spend.** See [`ai-dip-2026-10.md`](ai-dip-2026-10.md) claim 11. This doc never edits it.

## What this instance taught the format

- **A brief can carry an error the primary corrects.** #7033 "corrected" Eric's 60k to April's
  ~8,750. Challenger's October report, published two days before the brief, names a second
  program of 60,000. The build re-reads every figure against its primary; that rule is what caught it.
- **A conviction with no pair is still worth holding as research.** The format's record type refuses
  it, so the doc carries the claims and a campaign force carries the dates. A record waits for a
  study that names a pair.
