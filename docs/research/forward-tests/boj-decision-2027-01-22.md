| FT-boj-decision-2027-01-22-1 | **The Bank of Japan extends its consumption-tax exclusion convention to a tax CUT rather than booking a ~1pp mechanical drag into its headline FY2027 projection** — registered **2026-09-05 at D-139** of the `estimate`-dated [boj-decision-2027-01-22](../events/boj-decision-2027-01-22.md), and it is the question unique to this venue. The setup: Japan's food consumption tax falls from **8% to 1% for two years from 2027-04-01** (LDP-approved, **pending Diet passage** as of **2026-08-07**, News On Japan; corroborated by vatcalc.com and Japan Today, which add it is *"to be replaced in 2029 by a new refundable tax credit system"*). **2027-04-01 is the first day of FY2027 — the fiscal year this Outlook Report projects, and the cut covers all of it.** This lane's own arithmetic, with the basket weight explicitly unsourced: a 7/108 ≈ **6.5%** price cut on a 16-22% affected CPI share is a **~1.0-1.4pp** one-off drag on year-on-year CPI. Against that, the Bank's Outlook Reports carry a standing note that CPI figures are *"for all items less fresh food, excluding the effects of consumption tax **hikes**"* — wording built for 2014 and 2019 and not literally covering a cut. So the board must either extend the convention or book the drag, and the tell is one cell of a published table: the July-2026 Outlook put the **FY2027 core-CPI median at +2.4%** (revised up from +2.3% in April) with **FY2028 at +2.0%**, so a booked drag would print FY2027 visibly *below* FY2028.
