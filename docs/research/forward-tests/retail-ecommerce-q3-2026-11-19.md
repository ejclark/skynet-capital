| FT-retail-ecommerce-q3-2026-11-19-2 | **The e-commerce share is not inflator-neutral, and Q3 2026 is where that gets tested** — registered **2026-09-09 at D-71**. The proposal that created this event argued the share is the clean metric because *"a share is unmoved by an inflator that lifts numerator and denominator together."* That symmetry fails on this report specifically: per CB26-133's survey description its total-retail denominator is a sample of ~10,800 firms **excluding food services** — NAICS 44–45, which **includes gasoline stations** — and gasoline is ~0% e-commerce. So a pump-price rise lifts the **denominator only** and pushes the published share **down**, mechanically, with no change in online demand. That inflator is live and sized elsewhere in this calendar: Brent **$98.61** on 2026-09-08 after strikes on Saudi facilities, and the sibling monthly ledger books ~**+0.23pp** of August headline lift from pump prices alone. Against it stands a strongly one-sided base rate from `tsadjustedsales.xlsx`: the SA e-commerce share gained ground from Q2 to Q3 in **9 of 11 years since 2015** (+0.1 to +0.5pp), the two exceptions being COVID's 2020 reopening (**−1.4pp**) and the same confounded 2021 (**−0.1pp**). Q2 2026 printed **17.1%** ($340,244M of $1,986,488M).
