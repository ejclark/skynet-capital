The red pass, per shape — the strongest objection before Eric sees it

ShapeStrongest objectionWhat it would take to settle
A · Calendar twin grid Seven filled dots in the Technology tile is a count rendered as a picture at the exact size where counting is slowest. And the grid's resemblance to the month grid is a promise it does not keep: the month grid's cells are days in order, these are sectors in an arbitrary order. Eric reads the Technology tile and says "seven" without hesitating. If he hesitates, the dots become one numeral and the grid loses its twin claim.
B · Coverage rows Eleven rows at 390 is a scroll, and the thing he asked for — "what part of the market we have covered" — is a shape question that a vertical list answers worst. Does he scroll past row 6? If the three gaps at the bottom go unseen, the list buried the finding.
C · Coverage matrix Four mark columns where two are empty for every row today (calendar: 0 everywhere; held: 0 or 1) is a table mostly made of blanks, and a blank cell is the one thing a coverage map must never be ambiguous about. Render the empty columns with an explicit "—" rather than whitespace and see whether the table still reads. It is here in that honest form; judge it as drawn.
D · Sector treemap A treemap's whole mechanic is area = weight, and we have no source for index weights. Equal tiles are a treemap that lies about being one. At 390 the small sectors would be slivers even if we had the weights. Nothing settles it without a weights source. Drawn so the objection is visible, expected to be declined.
E · GICS ring Eleven labels will not fit around a 390px donut, so the ring needs a legend list beside it — at which point the legend is shape B and the ring is decoration above it. Worse, a ring of eleven arcs has no twelfth: Unclassified — where this book's only holding, EEM, actually lands — cannot be drawn on it at all. Is the "8 / 11" in the middle worth the space the donut costs? If yes, it is a summary tile at the top of another shape, not the shape.
H · Ring over matrix It says the same thing twice: the ring and the matrix are two drawings of one table, and on a phone the ring costs half the fold (6 of 12 rows visible, 4 when linked, against 12 for C). And the balance it shows is breadth, not weight. Eleven equal arcs say how many sectors we touch, not how much of the market's value. That is the treemap's missing-weights objection one layer down. The holdings ring is empty for all eleven today, because the only holding sits outside it. Eric names the three gaps from the ring's readout without scrolling, then finds one fact in the matrix the ring could not tell him (Real Estate's 32 reports). If the matrix adds nothing, drop it to the phone rows alone. If the ring adds nothing the readout didn't, drop the ring and keep the readout.

What this lo-fi does not settle

Placement and polish (hi-fi, /design): type scale, the real tokens, where the section sits in the switch order. And four questions the brief already parks: whether "add this stock" is a shared-list request or a personal watchlist (#3811 open question 1), which energy names Eric holds (3), whether "held" shows your positions or every member's (4), and whether depth counts ledgers or also playbook symbols (5). None of them changes which shape wins.
